C1–C2 · Category frame — a thin shelf over a clean anchor

Kyrgyz hair care is the batch's most thinly documented category, with one gift: the anchor is CLEAN. Burdock oil holds the apteka slot at 111–113 сом/100ml (farmamir's НМ blend at 111; beeko's Altai at 113 — two independent snippets agreeing within 2%, the tightest anchor capture in five markets), merchandised therapeutic, exactly the slot the programme knows farmamir/beeko Jul-2026. Above it: mass hair care is Russian/Belarusian FMCG through pharmacies and general marketplaces (beeko, max.kg, agora, Lalafo classifieds); premium is a small beauty-e-tail tier (Dermacosmetics.kg, M Beauty); and the Indian/Ayurvedic niche is TWO Bishkek specialty stores — Izindii.kg (listing Amla 180ml by name) and Muslim-Style (Amla Gold ~670 сом [!] 403-blocked) — plus Parachute at Арабский Мир and classifieds resale. No Vatika listing exists on any .kg domain found; Himalaya's one dated listing (230 сом face cream, ~2021) suggests the niche channel works but nobody is managing it. WB.kg's live storefront may already carry Russian sellers' Dabur stock — the sweep's first question [!].

The carry-over question, framed before the census answers it. WB.kg's live storefront raises a possibility no prior market offered so directly: Russian sellers' Dabur stock may ALREADY serve Kyrgyz buyers through the local rails, invisibly to .kg-domain searches — in which case the "thin shelf" is thicker than it reads, pre-priced in roubles-converted-to-som by sellers optimizing for Russia, and the census's first pull re-frames the whole entry from "extend a toe-hold" toward "consolidate a spillover." The deck plans for the toe-hold reading (the conservative case) and welcomes the spillover one (inherited listings with corpora are assets, as three markets taught) — but refuses to guess: the logged-in WB.kg sweep is G1's first hour, and every downstream number carries its outcome. A file that can name the fork before the data arrives spends the data better when it does.

The small-pack law, derived at its binding market

The format law the batch built across four wallets reaches its full statement here, and the arithmetic deserves one worked page. At $2,470 GDP per head, the relevant purchase unit is not the bottle but the DECISION — the sum a Naryn or Osh household will release for an unproven category on an ordinary week, which the El Aman signal, the poverty share and the remittance rhythm all bracket around tens-of-som, not hundreds. The 45ml at ~170 сом sits inside that bracket; the 200ml at ~550 does not — it is a CONVERT price, released only after the small pack has proven the product in the household's own bathroom. Hence the law's final form, printed for the batch: the entry format is the largest pack the ordinary week's discretionary decision covers; everything bigger is a loyalty format, not an acquisition one. Russia's wallet made the 200ml the entry; Uzbekistan split the difference; Kyrgyzstan's makes the 45ml the entry and the 200ml the ceiling. Same product, same frame, three format architectures — the wallet writing the SKU list, as it should.

The lapsed-listing lesson, generalized

The Himalaya teardown's finding — one listing, dated 2021, then silence — deserves its general statement before the position work, because it names this market's real failure mode. Small markets don't reject brands; they FORGET them. A listing lapses, a reseller moves on, a shelf gap goes unrefilled, and three years later the search index shows a ghost. Nothing about Kyrgyz demand killed that Himalaya cream; inattention did. The extension's answer is structural: supply routed through the KZ corridor's standing rotation (not a one-off consignment), the warm doors contractually re-supplied, and ONE named owner for the KG file inside the importer's organisation — the single-owner rule at its most literal, because in a market this size the difference between presence and ghosthood is whether anyone's job description mentions it. The deck writes that sentence into the G3 terms.

Two shelves, witnessed — the file's registers made concrete

The Jayma stall (the south, wave 3). The design implication the deck accepts rather than fights: in bazaar retail, TRADE marketing IS consumer marketing — the stallholder brief (one laminated card, Kyrgyz, per-ml math and three honest claims) is the south's real creative, and the Instagram plan is the north's.

The campus shelf (Bishkek, wave 1). A mini-market two streets from the medical academy: instant noodles, SIM cards, and — if the walk confirms the pattern — a shelf of Indian goods the students asked for. Amla belongs there on footfall logic, and its presence does quiet double work: supplying the corridor without targeting it, and letting the Kyrgyz flatmates and neighbours meet the bottle in passing — the second-hand seeding the dignity rule permits because geography, not copy, does the introducing.

The warm-door conversation, worked — a smaller room than Almaty's

The Izindii conversation, staged for its actual scale. This is not the Almaty importer meeting with four categories' assortment lists — it is a small Bishkek wellness shop whose owner listed a Dabur oil because her customers asked, sources it through whatever CIS chain answers her emails, and stocks out when the chain hiccups. The conversation's three parts fit her size. FIRST, respect: she found this demand before Dabur did; her read on who buys Amla (the corridor's students? Kyrgyz wellness shoppers? both?) is the cheapest market research in the country, and the meeting opens by asking for it. SECOND, the offer: reliable supply at direct terms — not a distribution empire, just never stocking out again — plus first access to the small-pack formats her price-sensitive walk-ins keep asking about. THIRD, the honest scale: she may not want to be an importer of record, and the deck doesn't need her to be — the G3 decision has the KZ-extension option precisely so small warm doors can stay small and supplied while the paperwork lives elsewhere. Two such conversations plus a campus walk, one week — and the extension either has its spine or knows it must import one.
