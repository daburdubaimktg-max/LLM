C1 · Category frame — three wings, one estate, and a seat already taken (by us)

UK hair care (~£2.2bn, +7.7% — Mintel 2025 vintage, index-derived) hosts the programme's deepest Dabur hair position, in three distinct wings. Wing one — Vatika, the mults house brand: ranged simultaneously at Superdrug (8+ SKU sub-lines live: Coconut, Castor-Rosemary, Argan, Egg Protein, Olive, Almond, Black Seed, Red Onion), Tesco and Asda (oils, shampoos, masks, styling creams) — not an ethnic-aisle guest but a full haircare house in the health-and-beauty aisle, at £3.95/200ml on the aggregator feed. Wing two — Amla, the platform volume brand: four Amazon pack sizes plus the new "Amla Miracle" line, with the 100ml carrying ~20,940 ratings at 4.2★ — the trial size as the volume anchor, a pack-architecture datum in itself. Wing three — ORS via Namaste: the Black-haircare arm distributed through PAK Cosmetics (28 London-area stores, ~6M customers/year) plus Beautizone/Modaps — present, channelled, and firewall-governed. No market in 38 has given the census three operating hair wings; the deck's job is to run each at its own grade and never blur them.

C2 · The corridor diagnostic — run as assigned, with a first-time finding

The US skin deck's diagnostic asks: is the category's value tier commodity-built (a corridor seat exists) or credibility-built (the seat is taken)? UK hair's answer is the programme's first of its kind. The value tier IS commodity-built — KTC coconut oil at £1.99–4.00/500ml (kitchen-grade, dual-use), Parachute at £2.59–6.29, Bajaj at £2.99–5.79: price-led staples with no brand story beyond familiarity. The ceiling IS founder-keyed — Chāmpo (1.5M+ units, +220% YoY, Selfridges' fastest-growing hair brand) and Fable & Mane (Cult Beauty, Sephora UK) monetise the Ayurvedic story at prestige prices. Between them sits the credibility-value corridor — branded botanical hair care with heritage truth at mass prices — and Vatika is already sitting in it, at £3.95, in three mults, with eight ingredient sub-lines. The diagnostic's finding: the corridor holds, and for the first time in the programme, the estate is the incumbent the diagnostic finds in the seat. The strategy that follows is therefore incumbency strategy — widen the seat, defend its price identity, and take the growth channels before a challenger reads the same map.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown

A framing note for the five cases: this teardown set reads differently from every prior market's because the usual question — how do we enter against these? — is replaced by the incumbent's questions: who could take the seat, which gates matter, and which neighbours teach operating lessons. Chāmpo and Fable & Mane answer "who monetises our story above us"; Mielle-at-Boots answers "how the gates actually open"; Superdrug's BC&S answers "which retailer invests alongside us"; the floor brands answer "where the boundary holds"; PAK answers "how an institution wants to be treated." None is an enemy; all are instruments.

1 · Chāmpo — the founder key, UK edition (fifth refusal, sharpest case)

Founded 2019 by Kuldeep Knox on her grandmother's Ayurvedic haircare ritual: 1.5M+ units, +220% YoY, the fastest-growing hair brand at Selfridges, funded (€1M pre-Series A, Dec 2021) — the UK's proof that the Ayurveda story commands prestige shelf. The teardown's edge over the US cases: Chāmpo is BRITISH South Asian — the founder key here is not just biography but the exact community voice the with-not-about rule centres. The refusal stands (Dabur cannot manufacture a founder), but the UK adds a nuance: Chāmpo's success EXPANDS the corridor's value rather than threatening it — every Selfridges shopper who learns the ritual at £30 is a future £3.95 repeat-buyer of the original. The prestige tier is the corridor's unpaid education budget.

2 · Mielle at Boots — the gate's mechanism, decoded

Boots' textured-hair strategy runs through ranging proven third-party brands (Mielle's Rosemary Mint range live across four SKU pages) rather than building own-brand ranges. The lesson for wing three: the Boots gate opens for brands with demonstrated velocity and community credibility — which describes ORS's PAK-channel record precisely. The lesson's boundary: Mielle arrived on a viral wave with P&G's muscle; ORS's pitch is steadier — heritage, range depth, and the beauty-supply channel's proof.

3 · Superdrug's BC&S — the retailer as category investor

Superdrug expanded its own textured range 15→23 SKUs, added curl-type navigation icons, and platforms Black-owned brands (Nylah's Naturals, Afrocenchix, Flora & Curl) — 188 curl/afro products store-wide. Superdrug is spending its own money growing the category wing three lives in, and it already ranges Vatika next door. The synthesis opportunity: Dabur is the only supplier that can bring Superdrug BOTH sides of the textured/botanical conversation (Vatika's South Asian oils + ORS's Black-haircare lines) — in two separate conversations, per the firewall, but through one commercial relationship's trust.

4 · KTC and Parachute — the floor's honest lesson

The commodity floor is not down-market Vatika; it is a different product doing a different job (kitchen-grade multi-use oil, bought on price-per-litre). Its lesson is the boundary it draws: Vatika's £3.95 buys formulation (herbs infused, cosmetic-grade), format (cosmetic packaging, usage occasions) and brand memory — the floor can never claim these, and Vatika must never price-war toward it. The 200ml at £3.95 vs KTC's 500ml at £2.50 is not a price gap; it is a category boundary, held by story.

5 · PAK Cosmetics — the institution

Twenty-seven years, 28 large-format London stores, ~6M customers a year: PAK is the UK Black-beauty channel's Melcom — retailer, wholesaler and community institution at once. ORS holds three sub-brand shelves there (Olive Oil, HAIRepair, Curls Unleashed). The teardown lesson is stewardship: this channel's trust is generational, its owners watch brands' conduct closely (the US channel-politics file reads across), and wing three's growth is earned by supply excellence and stylist education inside the institution — never by routing around it to chase mults glamour.

6 · SELF-teardown

Strengths: the seat held (corridor incumbency with mults distribution the diagnostic has never found before); range breadth (eight Vatika sub-lines); the deepest UK review base in ethnic beauty (20,940); three wings covering three consumer worlds; D2C and official storefront infrastructure. Weaknesses: the structure deficit underneath everything (the macro's finding — no named distributor, ungoverned resellers); zero TikTok Shop presence while the platform became the 4th-largest beauty retailer and hair care is named among its best-selling categories; Amla's absence from the mults unexplained (opportunity or decision? — the G1 conversation answers); no visible UK media spend (the estate may be growing on zero marketing — an operating-deficit signature); and the appropriation-tension terrain, where the brand best entitled to speak has the most to lose from speaking carelessly.

The channel inversion, priced properly

The census's most counter-intuitive number deserves its own economics. In 36 entry markets, the traditional channel was the value channel and modern trade carried the premium; the UK inverts it — the same Vatika bottle sells at £3.95 in the mults and £4.99–5.79 at South Asian independents. The inversion's causes are structural (smaller order quantities, higher unit logistics, no supplier trade support) and relational (the independents sell trust, proximity and the full basket of home — the premium is partly a service charge the community pays willingly). The strategic consequences: first, the independents are the estate's HIGHEST-margin retail partners per unit and its oldest — the consolidated wholesale map (macro option A) should IMPROVE their buying terms, converting some of that 25–45% gap into either their margin or shelf-price relief, their choice; second, no Dabur promotion should ever weaponise the mults' price against them ("cheaper at Tesco" content is barred); third, the inversion is a quiet moat — a challenger brand cannot replicate forty years of corner-shop trust, and the estate should stop treating its independent channel as legacy plumbing and start treating it as the defensive asset it is.

The walk, UK form

One day, four decks served: Southall morning (a cash-and-carry sweep — which wholesaler actually racks Dabur, at what trade prices; then two independents' shelves photographed — the full Dabur/competitor ladder); Leicester afternoon (the Golden Mile's grocery and beauty shelves — the corridor institution's Dabur depth); the mults leg (one Tesco Extra, one Asda, one Superdrug — facings, adjacencies, price tags, the BC&S set's layout); the evening screenshot session (every Amazon UK listing's live price/seller/S&S state; the TikTok Shop in-app search; the D2C shop's checkout experience tested with one real order). Every price card in the set re-bases on that day.

Two scenes that anchor the plan

The Superdrug aisle, read closely. Eight Vatika bottles on one bay, each label telling its own ingredient story, no header card, no education, no connection to the trend exploding on the phones of the women walking past them. Two metres away, the BC&S set has curl-type icons, brand stories, a designed journey. The gap between those two metres is the whole hair plan: Vatika has the range, the price and the truth; it lacks only the telling. A header card, a QR to the creator series, a "botanical hair house" narrative — retail theatre at trade-budget prices — converts the bay from eight accidents into one destination.

The Southall wholesaler's rack. Dabur cartons stacked beside Parachute and Bajaj, trade-priced by a wholesaler whose own supply route nobody at the estate can name with certainty. The G1 morning that photographs this rack also asks its owner three questions — who do you buy from, what terms, what moves — and the answers begin the consolidated wholesale map (macro option A) from the ground up. The estate's oldest channel gets mapped before its newest (TikTok) gets built; both happen in the same quarter.

C8 · Ad & comms decode + LEAP

The UK's creative terrain is the programme's most culturally intricate: the oiling ritual is trending in mainstream media (Stylist, Refinery29, trade titles naming scalp oiling a 2025 trend) under vocabulary ("hair slugging," "clean girl") that the South Asian community documents as its own practice renamed. The with-not-about rule therefore governs every asset: British South Asian creators as co-authors and first voices; the origin stated as living memory, not marketing archaeology; the mainstream buyer welcomed into a practice, never sold a discovery. All assets AUTHORED; the gates are the community review AND legal (CAP/ASA claims standards).

The decode's structural note: UK beauty advertising's claims regime (ASA/CAP) is stricter in practice than the US's — "helps strengthen" needs substantiation on file, before/after imagery invites challenge, and the ASA acts on single complaints. The estate's advantage is that its truthful register (heritage, ingredients, price) needs almost no risky claims: the creative work above sells memory, ritual and value — three things no substantiation file is required for and no challenger can dispute. The one claims file worth building properly is Amla Miracle's ("97% natural," "dermatologically tested") — both statements need their evidence organised UK-style before the storefront stages them.

DEEP THINK — incumbency without knowing it

The deck's strangest fact: Dabur holds the UK corridor seat and gives every sign of not knowing it — no media, no platform storefront, no velocity story told to buyers, a range that grew SKU by SKU without an architecture. The steelman this invites: maybe unmanaged incumbency is evidence the seat doesn't need managing — the diaspora replenishes, the mults reorder, why spend? The answer is the trend cycle: the oiling wave has made the seat VISIBLE — Chāmpo's growth, Mielle's Boots landing, TikTok's hair category, and the K-beauty-style wave logic all advertise the corridor's value to every strategist in London. Unmanaged incumbency in an unwatched category is thrift; in a watched one it is an invitation. The first challenger to build what Dabur hasn't — a governed, platform-native, community-credible botanical hair house at mass price — inherits the seat's growth while the incumbent keeps its base. The plan's urgency is not decline; it is that the seat's rent went up when the category got famous, and the incumbent is still paying the old rate.

The review corpus, UK edition

Twenty-one thousand ratings on one SKU is the UK estate's own free consumer study, and the corpus discipline (born in the US hair deck) runs here with a UK-specific coding frame: heritage-continuity language vs trend-arrival language (the two cohorts' split), scent and texture commentary (the US found scent the top objection — does the UK corpus agree, or does the diaspora-weighted base mute it?), pack-size behaviour (does the 100ml's dominance reflect trial, price points, or bathroom-shelf habits?), and any fairness-adjacent vocabulary appearing in REVIEWS (which the brand cannot control but must know about). The corpus read lands before the range review, because a buyer conversation armed with "21,000 customers, here is what they say, coded" is the velocity file's qualitative twin.
