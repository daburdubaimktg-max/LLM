C1 · Category frame — a market you enter through one door, speaking one grammar

UK wellness retail concentrates in a way no prior market has shown: Holland & Barrett — 809 stores, £524.2M FY24 gross profit, three consecutive growth years, a £1bn sales target, new Morrisons concessions, an "ingestible beauty" pilot running at +7% — is the category's gatekeeper, flanked by Boots' wellness aisles, Amazon UK, and the fastest riser (TikTok Shop, supplements among its best-selling categories). And UK wellness marketing speaks one legally-fixed grammar: with ashwagandha, turmeric and ginkgo efficacy claims held "on hold" in the GB Nutrition & Health Claims register, the permissible register is "traditionally used for…" language plus named botanicals — which happens to be the register a 140-year Ayurvedic house speaks natively. The frame's third wall is the borderline line: MHRA's updated guidance (17 Oct 2025) reclassifies over-claiming supplements as unlicensed medicines — Honitus sold as "cures cough" is a violation; Honitus sold as a traditional honey-tulsi remedy is a product. The whole deck lives inside these three facts.

The corridor diagnostic — wellness's run

The fourth UK run returns a shape the instrument hasn't logged before: the corridor here is a GRAMMAR, not a price band. The value tier is commodity-built (own-label bulk botanicals), the ceiling is claim-keyed (PL/THR registrations and "science-backed" own-label), and the ground between is held by whoever can say something TRUE and DIFFERENT inside the "traditionally used" vocabulary. Dabur's classical range is precisely that — formulations with documented centuries where competitors offer single-herb capsules with marketing heritage. The seat exists, is grammar-shaped, and is Dabur's to take by shipping the right formats with the right words — which is the whole playbook in one sentence.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown

A framing note: the teardown set reads five operators as five answers to the UK wellness question — the gate (H&B), the registered rung (A.Vogel), the cautionary retreat (Pukka), the live engine (the shilajit challengers), and the structured peer (Himalaya). As with oral, none is attacked; the strategy occupies the ground their five lessons jointly leave open: portfolio economics + tradition grammar + proof artifacts + the affiliate engine + door-one wholesale.

1 · Holland & Barrett — the gate, read as a counterparty

H&B is simultaneously the pitch's target and its risk: 809 stores plus concessions make it the only conversation that puts Dabur wellness in front of mainstream Britain at scale — and its own-label engine (1,000+ new SKUs in two years, "science-backed" positioning) makes it a competitor that studies every successful supplier's velocity. The teardown lesson from watching its behaviour: H&B ranges brands that bring a STORY its own label can't copy (origin, heritage, practitioner credibility) and commoditises everything else. Dabur's pitch therefore leads with the uncopyable (the 1884 house, the classical formulations, the practitioner layer's recognition) and prices with eyes open. The ingestible-beauty pilot (+7%) is the adjacency door: Chyawanprash's amla-and-honey composition is ingestible-beauty-adjacent by nature.

H&B's Morrisons concession deal (Sept 2025) adds a mechanism note: the gate is extending itself INTO the mults — a supplier ranged by H&B may inherit supermarket reach through the concession footprint without a separate grocery pitch. The pitch's value therefore compounds beyond the 809 stores, and the deck's H2 2027 timing (velocity-armed) aims at a counterparty whose distribution is growing while we build the record.

2 · A.Vogel — the pharmacy-herbal proof

The Swiss house's UK operation holds what no Ayurvedic brand does: THR registrations (Atrosan) and a full Product Licence (Echinaforce), distributed through independent pharmacies and serious herbal retail. The lesson is the ladder itself: PL > THR > food supplement is the UK's trust hierarchy, and A.Vogel's decades-long climb shows the registered rungs are reachable for a foreign herbal tradition — at dossier cost. Option F's benchmark.

3 · Pukka — the retreat that marks the boundary

The UK's Ayurveda-inspired champion built mainstream distribution on TEA, discontinued its supplements range (mid-2023), and holds no THR — under CVC's Ekaterra it is a beverage business with herbal branding. The lesson cuts precisely: Ayurveda-inspired BRANDING clears every UK gate; Ayurvedic SUPPLEMENT economics defeated even the category's best-distributed local player. Whatever Dabur ships must win on product economics (portfolio SKUs at import scale, not UK-built formulations) — the Pukka path of premium local manufacturing is marked as the way a bigger brand than ours already lost.

4 · The shilajit challengers — the affiliate engine, studied

The TikTok-native brands building UK shilajit (one case study: 145,000+ affiliates in 12 months, affiliate-led sales at 86% of GMV) prove two things: the demand for Dabur's heritage categories is real and platform-shaped, and the go-to-market is an AFFILIATE ENGINE, not a media plan — thousands of micro-creators on commission, live sessions, resin-scooping content. The teardown boundary: these brands' claims run hot (vitality/testosterone adjacency) and will eventually meet the ASA; the estate's version runs the same engine with claims discipline — slower, safer, and still early enough, because the category is two years old.

5 · Himalaya UK — the structured competitor, again

The macro's governance benchmark holds in wellness specifically: Himalaya's UK footprint (official storefront, Tropicana Wholesale into gyms/sports-nutrition, Rio Health, ethnic wholesale) reaches channels Dabur's equivalent products don't. The wellness-specific lesson: the sports-nutrition wholesale lane is a real route for ashwagandha (the gym consumer is the ingredient's UK growth buyer) that bypasses the H&B gate entirely — a second door the pitch-shy can open first.

6 · SELF-teardown

Strengths: captured price architecture already coherent at the core (the £9.35 jar is sensibly positioned); the honey claim pre-positioned for the scandal's aftermath; the classical range's authenticity unmatched by any UK player; the clinical layer's implicit endorsement; the 34-year estate as potential THR dossier evidence (the 15-years-in-EU/UK traditional-use bar may be answerable from Dabur's OWN UK market history — a derivation no younger brand can copy). Weaknesses: the trend formats absent (the census's mis-shape); Honitus's unlicensed exposure under the October 2025 borderline guidance; the 2005 MHRA file naming Dabur's product CLASSES (churna, shilajit) — the badge programme is not optional here; the FSA review hanging over the flagship's hero ingredient; and no H&B/Boots wellness listing found — the gate unattempted.

The claims workshop — UK wellness sitting

The workshop convenes against three desks at once (the ASA, the MHRA borderline assessors, the FSA) and produces the house rules. Rule one — tradition is the claim: "traditionally used in Ayurveda for over a century" is legal, true and differentiating; every asset leads there and stops there. Rule two — the product never promises, the certificate always shows: outcomes language is replaced by evidence artifacts (the COA, the QR, the batch number) — proof performs the persuasion claims cannot legally attempt. Rule three — the borderline is patrolled by presentation, not ingredients: the same Honitus bottle is legal as "a traditional honey-tulsi preparation" and illegal as "relief from cough" — every listing's NAME, imagery and claims are audited as a set, because MHRA reads them as one presentation. The rules' enforcement is structural: the monthly affiliate claims-audit (T10b) exists because the engine's thousand voices are the likeliest breach vector — a commissioned creator's enthusiastic "cured my cold" is the estate's liability, and the programme's terms make claim-compliance a payment condition.

The practitioner layer — the quiet asset, specified

The UK's Ayurvedic clinical infrastructure (the London clinics' decades, Leicester's establishments, the APA's national directory) is a credibility layer no other Western market offered, and the deck specifies its use precisely because the temptation is misuse. What it is NOT: a distribution channel (the Banyan ceiling holds), an endorsement pool to be purchased (professional ethics and ASA rules both forbid it), or a marketing asset to name-drop. What it IS: the community whose quiet regard makes the brand's classical claims credible when journalists check; a source of formulation-literate feedback; and — for option F — the bibliographic and expert-witness layer a THR dossier would draw on. The relationship programme is therefore old-fashioned: the products available to practitioners at trade terms, the company present at the APA's events, the estate's regulatory work (COAs, the claims pass) shared transparently — respect expressed as conduct, compounding at the pace such things compound.
