C1 — The ask (executive summary)

Macro anchor (LEBANON_MACRO decision board): Skin ranked DEFENSIVE/FLANK — big, beauty-obsessed, but a French-derma fortress with a local-natural hero. This deck sharpens the flank into three shelf-tested lanes and corrects the footprint record: not pure greenfield — Fem live, Herbolene listed-OOS — which re-orders the whole playbook around distribution repair first.

The play in one screen. Four truths define the flank. First, the doors exist and are broken: Carrefour lists Herbolene (OOS, price hidden) and sells Fem in four variants at $2.66 — proof a Levant route already ships Dabur skin SKUs into Lebanon, and proof the constraint is replenishment discipline, not listings. Fixing this costs almost nothing and generates real rate-of-sale data — the cheapest first move in the entire Lebanon programme. Second, the fortress and the hero are both real, and neither is the target: the French-derma wall holds $9–65 with pharmacist endorsement (boycott lists touch L'Oréal and Beiersdorf but Lebanese derma shelves show no bite — clinical trust wins), and Beesline owns natural-pride at a $5.99–21 face range whose floor keeps drifting up — leaving the $3–7 band beneath it structurally open as the crisis pushes shoppers down. Third, the culture writes the claims: Lebanon's beauty ideal is olive, sun-warmed, dewy glow — the local champion Cosmaline sells tanning oils, and even whitening-king Beesline is de-fairnessing its own franchise — so DermoViva's fairness legacy is not just off-culture, it is behind the local incumbent's own retreat; the paying brightening niches are underarm and spot/melasma correction, both approachable later on tone-unification language. Fourth, the Gulabari question splits the evidence and gets a test, not a bet: rosewater-on-face is documented Lebanese behaviour and nothing cosmetic-grade exists between the $4 kitchen bottle and Beesline's $11.64 toner — but the grandmother's Bekaa ma' ward owns authenticity absolutely. The honest resolution: a format-differentiated, dermato-coded rose toner/mist tests at $4.99–5.99, selling spec and format (never rose heritage), behind the two confident lanes — and parks after one failed season. The moves: (1) repair Carrefour replenishment + extend Herbolene/Fem to Spinneys via the live Vatika account; (2) launch the Herbolene twin-pack at ≤$3.29 under Vaseline's $3.39; (3) deepen Fem loudly on skin-kindness (no bleach, no fairness words); (4) test the rose toner; (5) hold DermoViva face wash as a later contested play (must beat local Cosmaline $3.68 on herbal story, not price) and never lead sunscreen. Do not contest French derma or prestige-artisan, do not use تبييض on any face product, do not ship Fem's bleach variants, and do not let the flank grow into a fortress assault. Reputation Bet (C18): by summer 2027, in-stock Herbolene takes ≥8% of the jelly shelf and Fem holds its Chamsa parity with wider distribution — or skin reverts to a Fem-only maintenance line.

C2 — Category frame, size & the barbell structure

Skin is Lebanon's largest BPC segment by value (Euromonitor: "skin care leads by value; facial and body dominate") inside the ~$353.5m BPC market that grew +20% in 2025; Statista projects skin at ~US$98–100m (~28% of BPC) with a flat-to-negative volume CAGR — a value-resilient, volume-constrained category. The shape is the now-familiar Lebanese barbell, but with skin-specific evidence: 86.2% of Lebanese women prioritise quality over price (aspiration intact) while the market "shifted dramatically away from internationally recognized brands toward lower-quality products" (the wallet reality), and — decisive for Dabur — 79.9% trust herbal-based over chemical formulations [PMC 2023, n=1,051].

C4 — Demand engine: glow, not fairness — and grooming without the veil (Module — consumer truth)

The demand truths re-derive sharply from both Jordan and the Gulf. The beauty ideal is olive, sun-warmed, dewy — Lebanon "chases glow rather than glass-skin," summer means "sun, sea and that golden tan," the local mass champion sells tanning oils, and Beirut's aesthetic economy (the "Lebanese facial," the cosmetic-surgery-capital inheritance) exports glow region-wide. Fairness-cream demand in the Gulf/South-Asian sense is commercially dead on the face; what pays instead is نضارة (radiance), توحيد اللون (tone unification), spot/melasma correction (a real need in a UV-12 country — LRP Pigmentclar $41.80, Eucerin Thiamidol distributed) and underarm brightening (Beesline's empire).

The Gulabari split-evidence resolution — a test, not a bet. This deck's two research passes returned honestly opposed reads on the rose toner. The case against: flower water in Lebanon is a $2–4 pantry commodity with Bekaa terroir and grandmother authority (Cortas, Maalouf, Mymouné, Terroirs du Liban's women's-co-op rosewater) — an Indian rose brand has no room below it and no credibility above it. The case for: the behaviour is already cosmetic (rosewater as DIY toner is documented mainstream practice), and nothing cosmetic-grade exists between the $4 kitchen bottle and Beesline's $11.64 toner — a real format gap. Both are true, so the strategy is conditional: a cosmetic-grade rose facial mist/toner at $4.99–5.99 — spray format, dermato-tested claims, نضارة language — that sells format and skin-spec, never rose authenticity (the grandmother wins that contest before it starts). One season on Feel22 + two grocery doors; clear velocity threshold; parked without sentiment if it misses. Orange-blossom remains the more distinctive NPD cue for a later hydrating line — the first mass-skincare claim on Lebanon's own signature botanical — but that is a formulation opportunity, not a Gulabari rescue.
