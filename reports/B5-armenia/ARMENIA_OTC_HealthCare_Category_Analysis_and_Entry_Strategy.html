ARMENIA · OTC HEALTH CARE — THE SISTER SCIENCE AND THE GRANDMOTHER'S JAR

Armenia holds a DOCUMENTED ancient herbal science — Mkhitar Heratsi (12th c., "father of Armenian medicine") and Amirdovlat Amasiatsi (15th c., ~3,500 plants catalogued, translated into four languages) — and a LIVING folk-herbal practice (Smithsonian-documented grandmother remedies; honey+herbs as the default winter immunity answer): the SISTERHOOD form's second full run, with a precedent already trading (Nairian "mines Armenia's ancient manuscripts" for cosmetics) · Chyawanprash — itself an amla+honey+herb preserve — is literally the BRANDED JAR of the remedy Armenian households already make · The regulatory file is the batch's most double-edged: EAEU SGR carries by document-review (no lab test), while enforcement is provably thin — only 16% of social-commerce supplements are registered [Hetq] — making "state-registered, pharmacy channel" a real differentiator · Honey verdict #20 executes into a REFINED finding: local supply is fragmented and UNBRANDED — the whitespace is the branded jar itself · GAP RULE: Armenian double-gated · 7 July 2026
FOCUS · One line: run the sisterhood form at full protocol — Heratsi and Amasiatsi honoured FIRST, Ayurveda introduced as the sister science that catalogued its own three and a half thousand plants — and land Chyawanprash as the formal jar of Armenia's own folk remedy (honey+herbs for winter), carried by the pharmacy channel where the state-registered claim separates it from the 84%-unregistered social-commerce tide, priced against its own corridor listings under the two-door law, with honey verdict #20 executing into the unbranded-supply refinement (the first mover in BRANDED jarred honey, distinct-origin, never against the beekeeper), the yoga-studio wedge used as warm ground rather than assumed awareness, and the January wellness box aimed per the macro's clock.

C1–C2 · Category frame — a sister tradition, a living remedy, a thin referee

Three structural facts organise Armenian OTC wellness. FIRST, the heritage: Heratsi's fever treatise fused scholarly and folk-herbal medicine in the 12th century; Amasiatsi's "Useless for the Ignorant" catalogued ~3,500 plants in the 15th and travelled into Persian, Arabic, Latin and Greek [Smithsonian Folklife; CSMBR; academic profiles] — a named-physician, textual, scholarly herbal science, which is to say the Ibn Sina configuration: SISTERHOOD substrate, and this time with commercial precedent, because Nairian already built a cosmetics brand on "mining the manuscripts" [Smithsonian Magazine]. The amended protocol's slot-reading: the manuscript story is CLAIMED in cosmetics (Nairian's ground — the skin deck's problem), OPEN in wellness — no supplement or food brand tells it. SECOND, the living practice: Smithsonian documents a continuing folk-herbal tradition, and honey+herbs is the region's default winter immunity remedy, made at home, unbranded. Chyawanprash — an amla-honey-herb preserve — is structurally the JAR VERSION of what the household already believes in. THIRD, the referee: supplement registration is EAEU document-review (fast, administrative), and enforcement is thin to the point of documentation — 16% of social-commerce БАД registered; five enforcement actions in three years [Hetq [!] 403-blocked, search-synthesised]. The formal channel is therefore both an obligation and an ARGUMENT: on this shelf, "registered, at the pharmacy" is a claim most of the volume cannot make.

THE SISTERHOOD GATE — the form's second full run

Uzbekistan's oral file built the sisterhood form on Ibn Sina: honour the local lineage FIRST, introduce the foreign tradition as kin in scholarship, never imply ownership or precedence. Armenia's substrate is arguably stronger — the local physicians are named, dated, textual, and already commercially warmed by Nairian — and the gate therefore runs at full protocol. Step 1, LINEAGE VERIFICATION: the Heratsi/Amasiatsi facts are academically solid [Smithsonian/CSMBR], but their USE by a foreign brand needs a Armenian cultural-history reviewer's sign-off — the Nairian precedent proves the story can be told commercially; it does not prove a FOREIGN teller is welcome [!] panel-pending. Step 2, the ORDERING RULE: every asset names the Armenian physicians before any Indian text — «Ամասիացին գրանցեց 3,500 բույս. Այուրվեդան՝ իր հազարավորները» (Amasiatsi catalogued 3,500 plants; Ayurveda, its own thousands) — the sister introduced by the elder sibling's name. Step 3, the COHORT TEST: 12–15 pharmacy-going wellness buyers meet the framing; the kill word is any variant of «մերը բավական է» (ours is enough — the frame read as unnecessary import) dominating unprompted. Step 4, the FALLBACK, pre-built: if the sisterhood frame fails, Chyawanprash ships on the FOLK-REMEDY register alone — the jar of the honey-herb remedy, composition-forward, no manuscripts invoked — a launch that loses a narrative and keeps its structure, exactly the gate-with-fallback configuration the batch now runs as standard.

C5 · Consumer decode — the remedy-maker, the pharmacy buyer, the warm ground

The remedy-maker. She stirs honey into thyme tea when the family coughs; her mother did the same. The jar does not replace her practice — it FORMALISES it for the weeks she's busy and the family is away from her kitchen. The register speaks WITH her knowledge: «այն, ինչ գիտեք՝ պատրաստ բանկայում» (what you already know, in a ready jar) — the folk-remedy fallback register that also happens to be the launch's safest lead.

The pharmacy wellness buyer. The Solgar/Vichy shopper — reads labels, pays for paper, distrusts the Instagram tide. For her the registered claim and the composition ARE the product; the sisterhood story (if gated through) is the reason she tells a friend. The counter card leads with the 40-botanical composition and the one-line register: «գրանցված է, դեղատնից» — registered, from the pharmacy.

The ~2,600 Indian students are the dignity rule's Armenian application: campus-adjacent pharmacy stocking, zero targeted creative — the standing law, tenth market.
