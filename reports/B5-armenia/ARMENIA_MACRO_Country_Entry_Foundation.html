M1 · Demography — small, concentrated, honestly shrinking

Armenia is ~2.93M people with ~37% in Yerevan metro [Worldometer/WPR 2026 [!] armstat primary] — the batch's smallest market and its most city-concentrated after Azerbaijan's output-weighted Baku. GDP/head ~$9,474 (2025) is the batch's second-richest on paper, but the deck refuses the flattering read: the 2022–24 numbers were a relocation boom (≈110,000 Russians arrived 2022 [!] widely repeated, never verified; retention materially lower), and the unwind is measured — ~3,750 IT specialists left in 2025, 69% of them the relokants themselves [Pravda Armenia, Feb 2026], growth stepping 8.9% → 5.2% → 4.0% [WB/Armstat]. Underneath, the native population is in long-term decline. The design consequence is the note's own phrase, adopted as the file's frame: Armenia is a beachhead and reputation play, not a volume play — a ~$1.7bn consumer-goods market [CMI 2025 [!]] where the mini-gate (born in Kyrgyzstan for exactly this scale) is the default instrument and every forecast wears the post-peak haircut.

M2 · Economy — the boom read honestly, the dram watched

The macro read the batch must not flinch from: an entry underwritten on Armenia's 2022–24 consumption story would be two years too late. The re-export/relocation model is visibly decelerating, analyst commentary openly questions the domestic production base beneath it [News.az 2026], and the dram's stability — one of the CIS's best since 2022 — is partly a FUNCTION of the relocation FX inflows now reversing [CBA data]. What survives the haircut is still a real market: the batch's second-highest income per head, a hyper-concentrated modern-trade city, an IT-salaried consumer class that remains (the outflow is a trickle against the stock), and cross-border transfers still running $728M in four months [ArmBanks Jun 2026 [!] composition unclear]. Pricing architecture notes: AMD-stable enough for annual price lists with a quarterly dram watch; the post-peak haircut applies to VOLUME forecasts, not to price positioning — Yerevan's premium tier (SAS's shelves) did not un-learn its tastes when the boom slowed.

The lane arithmetic, worked once. What the Georgia base case means in operating terms: a consignment leaving the RAK hub books sea freight to Poti (the Black Sea leg), clears Georgian transit, and trucks the Yerevan highway — 18–28 days door-to-door on forwarder estimates [!], against Kyrgyzstan's one-quarter shelf promise off an adjacent rotation and Azerbaijan's BTK home lane. The consequence cascades: safety stock carries a 28-day tail (working capital the thin-market P&L must absorb); promotional calendars book freight eight weeks out, not four; and a stock-out repairs slowly, which makes the supply-before-demand law (seventh market) bind harder here than anywhere since Mozambique. The air option (Dubai–Zvartnots, 4–10 days) exists precisely to break that arithmetic for the SKUs whose margins afford it — Chyawanprash and rose water fly; shampoo sails. The split is written into the wave plan, not left to the forwarder's quote sheet.

The normalization file — real progress, no dates, zero plan-dependence

The macro's most consequential watch item deserves its own ledger, because its pieces move at different speeds and a plan that averages them errs twice. MOVING: Turkish Airlines flies Istanbul–Yerevan since March 2026; Turkey has eased customs restrictions and declared direct-trade bureaucratic preparations complete; the Alican–Margara land crossing is ~90% built; the Ani Bridge MOU is signed [Al-Monitor/Modern Diplomacy, May 2026]. NOT MOVING: Turkey's foreign ministry explicitly denied the reported 4 June 2026 opening date and states there is NO fixed timetable — the border remains politically hostage to a final, RATIFIED Armenia–Azerbaijan peace treaty, which itself waits on Armenia's constitutional referendum (expected 2027) [Turkish Minute; Armenian Club, 6 Jul 2026]. TRIPP (the 42km Syunik corridor under a 99-year US concession) began project rollout April 2026 but runs Azerbaijan-to-Azerbaijan — it is not an Armenia import lane and never becomes one in this file's horizon. The planning law, stated once: every storyline that would transform this market's logistics — the Turkey border, TRIPP's spillovers, reduced Georgia dependence — is DOWNSTREAM of one unratified treaty, so the entry plan carries zero dependence on any of it, a monthly watch on all of it, and a pre-written re-cost trigger: the day a ratified treaty sets a border date, the entire lane file re-opens and the Turkey hub's economics get computed for the first time in earnest.

M4 · Regulation — the lightest paper file in the batch, with one real line

EAEU membership makes Armenia's regulatory file the batch's lightest: EAC conformity marks and TR CU certificates from the Russia set extend by direct effect, no national re-registration. The one real localisation line is LANGUAGE: TR CU labeling law requires the destination state's language ALONGSIDE Russian — and Armenian is a unique 39-letter script (Mesrop Mashtots, 5th c.), not Cyrillic, not Latin. Bilingual RU+HY labels are a legal requirement, not a courtesy [armenian-lawyer/alba-translating/trade.gov aggregation]. Cosmetics fall under TR CU 009/2011, whose December-2025 amendment tightened testing/labelling standards [!] pull the exact amendment text before any label artwork. The standing checks: each SKU's registry entry read for territory restrictions (the KG lesson — W0 check #1), and the Dec-2025 amendment's delta mapped against existing Russia-set artwork.

M5 · Culture — no halal file, and the batch's best bridge instead

Armenia is the world's first Christian nation (301 AD); the halal file does not exist here, exactly as the kit brief ordered — and what replaces it is the batch's richest OTC substrate. The Matenadaran holds a dedicated Medieval Medicine hall; Mkhitar Heratsi (12th c.) fathered Armenian medicine; Amirdovlat Amasiatsi (15th c.) wrote a ~3,500-plant encyclopedia translated into Persian, Arabic, Latin and Greek; mountain monasteries preserved the herbal-science manuscripts [Matenadaran/Smithsonian Folklife]. This is a documented ancient herbal-science tradition — scholarly, textual, named-physician — which is to say: a SISTERHOOD substrate, the Ibn Sina configuration, not the occupied-story configuration. The macro arms the OTC deck's reading and adds the occupied-story caution from Baku: whether Armenia's story slot is open to a bridging narrative ("two ancient herbal sciences meet") or owned the way naftalan owns Azerbaijan's is precisely what the amended protocol now reads FIRST. National symbols — apricot (the flag's orange, the duduk's wood) and pomegranate (blessed each Jan 6) — are ingredient-story anchors Dabur's portfolio can genuinely speak to. The gifting calendar: New Year (Jan 1, Gaghant Baba) is the summit; Christmas+Epiphany Jan 6 carries the pomegranate ritual; Vardavar (12 Jul 2026) is the youth-skewing midsummer water festival — a summer-care window no other batch market offers.

M10 · Synthesis — the 20th honey verdict, the calendar, the batch position

The honey file — verdict #20, the exporter's-pride case

Armenia produces ~2,000 tonnes (2024), EXPORTS at national-pride scale for its size (80t/$397k, 2024), and prices its own honey at ~$12.48/kg (~4,820 AMD) domestically while imports transact around ~$3.07/kg [OEC/Tridge/Selina Wamucii [!] both price points re-validated before any economics]. The structure rhymes with Azerbaijan's (the local jar premium, a value gap beneath) but the derivation — as the taxonomy requires — runs on local facts and lands differently: Armenia's import figure proves cheap foreign honey ALREADY flows at volume, so the value lane is not white space, it is an existing trade Dabur would join anonymously at commodity margins. The 20th verdict is therefore SELL-NICHE (DISTINCT ORIGIN): if honey ships at all, it ships as Indian/Himalayan-forest honey — a named-origin specialty beside Armenian mountain honey, never against it and never into the commodity import lane — gated on one G1 shelf question (does ANY branded origin-story import honey exist here?) and one panel read (does "Himalayan" carry romance or nothing in Yerevan?). Small, honest, killable — and the taxonomy's width grows again: twenty verdicts, no two derived from precedent.
