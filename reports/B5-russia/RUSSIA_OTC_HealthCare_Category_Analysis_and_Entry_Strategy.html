C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown — five entries, one self-read

1 · Evalar — the provenance mirror

Evalar is Dabur's mirror image wearing Altai instead of Ayurveda: Biysk-headquartered, "every third Russian" a claimed user, 1bn+ packs over 20 years, ~500 registered SKUs, pharmacopeial-grade manufacturing claims, ~47 own pharmacies plus franchises, 40.7% of revenue online, #1 in Eastern Europe/Russia/CIS by Euromonitor corp.evalar.ru/Forbes 2025. Teardown lesson: provenance-led natural wellness at scale is PROVEN here — and Evalar's single-herb, low-unit-price format leaves the multi-herb daily-ritual JAR genuinely unoccupied. Dabur does not fight Evalar; it sells the format Evalar never made, priced as a ritual, not a remedy. The respect clause: Evalar's marketplace and apteka.ru ranking machine is the best in the category — study its card craft before writing a single Dabur card.

2 · Zandu — the premium cousin

Zandu (Emami) trades on the same shelf with a premium flourish — Sona Chandi's gold/silver infusion at the ceiling band via Balarama and Ayur-Fresh specialist listings. Lesson: the sub-category already has a premium tier, built by an Indian rival — evidence the Russian shopper will pay UP for Chyawanprash variants, and a warning that the formalization race has a competitor with FMCG discipline and export experience. Zandu formalizing first is the single worst competitive outcome available; the wave plan's tempo is set by this row.

3 · Patanjali — the volume cousin

Patanjali holds its own Ozon category page — the value-leaning, swadeshi-coded rival ozon.ru taxonomy. Lesson: the informal niche is deep enough to sustain THREE brand taxonomies, which is the strongest demand evidence in the file; and Patanjali's price posture caps how far the reference brand can premiumise without leaving a value flank open beneath it.

4 · The Soviet adaptogen shelf — the incumbent that taught the market

Eleuthero, rhodiola ("золотой корень"), ginseng, schisandra, leuzea, mumiyo: decades of lay usage as general tonics against fatigue, stocked in every apteka, covered by mainstream media as household knowledge, with ginseng and eleuthero alone ~30% and ~21% of adaptogen-labelled SKU supply farosplus/aif/kp coverage. This "brand" is the category's real founder: it taught Russians that plant tonics for resilience are normal, pharmacy-approved behaviour. Chyawanprash inherits the belief wholesale — its 40-herb jam is legible as the same idea at maximum breadth. What it must add: WHY breadth beats a proven single herb — the panel's core messaging brief.

5 · The post-COVID immunity wave — the demand engine, dated

Self-supplementation runs on immunity anxiety layered over the tonic tradition: vitamin D/C, magnesium, omega-3 lead; 58% start supplements "from fatigue" rather than diagnosis gmsclinic survey via vkpress 2024-25. Lesson: the demand engine is emotional-preventive, not clinical — exactly Chyawanprash's home register ("daily family resilience ritual") — and it is a WAVE, not a floor: the deck's projections use the tonic tradition as the base and the immunity wave as upside, never the reverse.

6 · Self-teardown — the race, the blend review, and the sugar question

Three honest exposures. FIRST, the race: Dabur's review-corpus lead is real but rented — corpora accrue to LISTINGS, and grey listings can be out-formalized by a rival's official entry regardless of stars. SECOND, the compositional review: a ~40-herb blend meets Russia's permitted-ingredient and dosage lists, and the note's sources flag this as the likeliest source of friction or a reformulation demand — the SGR filing is therefore the deck's critical path, started before any commercial motion. THIRD, the sugar question: a sugar-free variant already exists on the Russian shelf (someone is listing it), and the health-literate cell that anchors the category reads sugar lines the way the hair deck's naturalists read состав — the variant strategy is a launch decision, not an afterthought.

C5b · Consumer decode — the spoon, the tincture, and the leaflet

The tonic traditionalist. She keeps eleuthero drops in the medicine cabinet the way her mother did, buys them at the apteka in autumn, counts them a habit rather than a health programme. Chyawanprash reaches her ONLY through the pharmacy window — a marketplace card is invisible to her category behaviour. When she meets the jar on an apteka shelf beside the tinctures, the leaflet has one job: translate "40 herbs, one daily spoon, the whole family" into the register she already trusts — the course-of-use grammar (курс применения) her tinctures speak. Her question is not whether plant tonics work (settled decades ago) but whether THIS one is serious: the СГР number on the box and the pharmacist's nod do more for her than any influencer can.

The immunity-anxious parent. Post-COVID, reads Telegram health channels, gives the family vitamin D all winter, starts supplements "from fatigue" like the surveyed 58%. The daily-spoon family ritual is DESIGNED for this household — one jar replacing a shelf of single-purpose bottles — and the 4.9★ corpus is her due diligence already done by strangers. She is the marketplace phase's engine and the sugar question's judge: her feed literacy means the sugar-free variant's existence may decide whether the brand enters her basket at all.

The Ayurveda devotee. Already shops the specialist layer, knows the Dabur name from years of niche buying, may already own opinions about which Chyawanprash brand is "authentic." She is numerically small, strategically loud — the review corpus's most prolific author and the formalization race's commentariat. The protected-specialist rule keeps her channel alive; the official entry must read to her as the brand ARRIVING, not the brand being taken away from her shops and repriced.

The per-ritual arithmetic, worked — the only price argument the category respects

The deck's pricing pitch is a division problem, and it deserves to be shown rather than asserted. A RUB 599 jar of 550g at the recommended daily spoon (~12g adult dose) is roughly 45 servings — about RUB 13 per family-member day, cheaper per ritual than a pharmacy vitamin gummy and in the same order as the tincture regimen it sits beside: an Evalar ginseng tincture at RUB 77 for 25ml delivers roughly a month of drops for one person (~RUB 2.5/day) but covers ONE herb and one user, and the households the decode describes stack two or three bottles per season. The honest comparison is therefore not jar-versus-bottle but CABINET-versus-jar: three stacked singles (~RUB 250–350 a season per person) against one jar covering the family's daily habit at ~RUB 13 a day with forty botanicals in one motion. The arithmetic wins politely — it never claims the tinctures don't work (the beside-grammar, again), only that the jar consolidates the ritual. Every number in this paragraph is AUTHORED from captured price points and label dosing [!] re-run with G1 actuals and the printed dosage table before any card copy uses it — but the STRUCTURE of the argument survives any re-pricing, which is exactly what the relationship-pricing doctrine asks of a pitch.

The apteka pitch, worked — one meeting, three exhibits

Move 5 compressed to its real shape: a category manager at a national chain gives an unfamiliar supplier twenty minutes, and the deck's whole apteka thesis either survives that room or doesn't. Exhibit one is the CERTIFICATE — the СГР number, the marking codes, the EAEU registry entry a pharmacist can check from the counter: in a channel whose authority rests on being the place that doesn't sell nonsense, the paper is the product. Exhibit two is the VELOCITY PRINT — two quarters of marketplace data showing the jar's repeat-purchase curve and the 4.9★ corpus, which answers the category manager's actual question (will it turn?) with the only currency he trusts. Exhibit three is the SHELF LOGIC — a one-page planogram argument placing the jar beside the adaptogen tinctures rather than the vitamin wall, with the per-ritual arithmetic printed on it, because the pitch that names its own shelf neighbour saves the buyer the work of inventing a category. What the pitch deliberately omits: any request for promotion, any discounting, any claim the claims wall wouldn't clear — the chain's compliance officer reads sell-in decks too. If the answer is no, the deck's cap clause executes without drama: the jam-format precedent question (T18 #8) gets its answer, the marketplace P&L stands on its own floor, and the race is still won on paper. One meeting, prepared for a year, cheap at the price.
