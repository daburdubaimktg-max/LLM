FOCUS · One line: close the Russia set with its only build-from-zero entry — Herbolene as the herbal modernisation of a barrier-cream archetype Russians already love (never a correction of it), priced above the RUB 13–55 vaselin floor on an honestly-argued texture-and-botanicals delta; Gulabari as an origin-story rose water in a market where origin IS the rose-water argument; both riding the importer, container, marking ops and review machinery the first three decks paid for — with the tone-adjacent grammar locked to пигментные пятна/постакне correction (the whitening frame does not exist here and will not be imported), and the set's close-out delivering Batch 5 its instruments.

C1–C2 · Category frame — a real-need category with a nostalgic incumbent

Russian mass skin care is anchored where the climate put it: in BARRIER care. Petroleum jelly (вазелин) retails from RUB 13–55 across pharmacy chains in both cosmetic and medical grades, made by domestic plants (Muromsky Instrument Plant, Tula Pharma Factory) and marketed beside Unilever and Nevskaya Kosmetika product on the same shelves apteka.ru/asna/megapteka, accessed 2026 [!] inflation-sensitive band. Above it sits the archetype: Детский крем (Detsky krem — "children's cream"), the thick zinc/lanolin barrier cream in the simple tube, near-universal in Soviet households for babies and adults alike, still manufactured by legacy brands with Nevskaya Kosmetika's «Детский» line its direct descendant nevcos.ru. The iconic mass hand/body brands — Чистая линия (Chistaya Liniya — "pure line") and Бархатные ручки (Barkhatnye ruchki — "velvet hands") — fill the middle; the natural-premium storey belongs to a distracted Natura Siberica and the fast-growing domestic challengers; and the pigmentation-correction lane is led by K-beauty actives with domestic players (Siberina ~RUB 950 night cream, KRASSA) beneath them kp.ru/siberina/maskshop. The category's defining fact for an entrant: the need is real, universal and seasonal-peaking — and its cheapest answer is beloved.

So what. Two products, two different games, one discipline. Herbolene plays a VALUE-ADJACENT game against a beloved archetype — it must be legibly better (texture, botanicals, non-greasy absorption) at a price the trusted-mass shopper will stretch to, and it must NEVER mock the tube it sits beside. Gulabari plays an ORIGIN game in a lane that already speaks origin fluently — Indian rose, Ayurvedic tradition, against Bulgarian heritage and Crimean patriotism. Neither game needs the whitening register, and the deck locks the door on it in writing: the fence's fifth environment holds by reframing, and the reframe is total.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown — five winners, one self-read

1 · Детский крем (the archetype) — the incumbent made of memory

The teardown's first entry is a product-shape, not a company: the zinc/lanolin tube whose buyers include people who were themselves the babies it was bought for. Its strengths are total trust, universal availability and a price near the floor; its weaknesses are the honest ones of age — greasy occlusion, a scent remembered rather than chosen, zero innovation story. The lesson writes Herbolene's brief verbatim: modernise, never correct. "The barrier cream you grew up with, grown up" is the emotional geometry (panel-rendered or not shipped); herbal actives and a dry-touch finish are the functional delta; the archetype keeps its dignity and Herbolene takes the stretch purchase. Attacking the tube would be attacking the shopper's grandmother — the deck bars it.

2 · Nevskaya Kosmetika — the keeper of the archetype

The St Petersburg legacy house holds the «Детский» descendant line — fragrance-free, plant-extract formulated, recommended across generations for sensitive skin nevcos.ru. Lesson: the archetype is DEFENDED by a competent, low-cost manufacturer with national distribution — the modernisation lane must be real product superiority, not marketing varnish, because the keeper can reformulate cheaply if provoked. Second lesson: its fragrance-free sensitive-skin grammar is the trusted-mass register Herbolene's card must speak.

3 · Natura Siberica — the premium proof, still convalescing

The natural champion proved Russians pay mass-premium for botanical heritage stories; the succession crisis, store closures, and the Sistema takeover left it functioning but strategically distracted meduza/RBC/Interfax 2021-23 [!] 2026 posture. Lesson: the natural-premium storey has a window — and a warning. The window: shelf and mindshare the champion isn't defending with full attention. The warning: Sistema's ownership means recapitalisation could end the window abruptly; the deck's wave plan treats it as a bonus, never a plan.

4 · The rose-water field — Bulgarian memory, Crimean patriotism

Розовая вода is a legible, established category: Bulgarian rose water carries Soviet-era trade nostalgia (Kazanlak's Rose Valley was the Eastern Bloc's perfume garden), while Крымская роза brands built a domestic provenance claim after 2014, marketing soothing/bactericidal/anti-aging benefits — near-identical to Gulabari's own claim set krimroza/mirbolgarii/master-om. Lesson: Gulabari needs ZERO category education — it needs an origin argument. "The rose of Indian tradition" enters a conversation already structured as Bulgaria-versus-Crimea and offers a third seat: panel-tested, because a third origin can read as exotic richness or as an answer to a question nobody asked. The e-commerce lane decides cheaply.

5 · Himalaya — the benchmark, skin edition

Himalaya's ~179-SKU Russian footprint spans face washes, creams and children's lines through the specialist layer and its dedicated importer site ayurvedastore/himalaya1.ru — proof an Indian herbal SKIN range sustains multi-retailer Russian distribution through exactly the channel stack Dabur now owns via three categories' formalization. Lesson: the skin entry does not pioneer; it PLUGS IN — and Himalaya's face-wash-led assortment hints at the adjacency Dabur should watch (cleansing) without chasing it this cycle.

6 · Self-teardown — the cold start, priced honestly

Skin is the set's only entry with no inherited listings, no review corpus, no market-cleared price — every advantage the other decks inherited must be BUILT here, in the set's most seasonal category, against the programme's most emotionally-defended incumbent, under the set's least-settled compliance track (cosmetics marking still phasing to July 2026). The honest consequences: skin launches LAST in the set's sequence (the macro's T18 order stands); its forecasts carry the widest bands; its wave 1 is deliberately narrow (two SKUs, one season, marketplace-only); and its kill-switch is the cheapest in the set — a cold start that misses its first winter simply doesn't reorder for the second without a minuted re-argument. The set's rails make the attempt cheap; the discipline makes the failure cheap too.

C5 · Consumer decode — the tube, the toner, and the winter

The winter pragmatist. Buys barrier cream the way she buys gloves — before the cold, without romance, biased to what worked last year. Her tube costs RUB 90 and she has no complaint except the grease on her phone screen. Herbolene wins her stretch purchase only with a delta she can FEEL in the store aisle or read in three review lines: absorbs dry, smells clean, herbs she can name. Price above ~RUB 220 and she shrugs back to the archetype; promise "luxury" and she distrusts the whole card. She is the reason the deck's Herbolene copy is functional-first, heritage-second.

The toner ritualist. Built a K-beauty-inflected routine from Telegram channels and marketplace bundles; rose water is her toner step and she knows the Bulgarian-vs-Crimean debate the way the hair deck's naturalist knew состав. Gulabari's third seat is pitched to her curiosity: a NEW origin with an old tradition, an ingredient list she can audit, a price inside her routine's band. She writes reviews; the winter pragmatist reads none for barrier cream — which is why Gulabari carries the set's review-velocity machinery into skin and Herbolene leans on aisle legibility instead.

The gift-season buyer. Russian winter gifting (New Year dominates) moves beauty minis and care sets; the specialist layer already merchandises Ayurvedic gift baskets. A Gulabari + Herbolene winter duo is the set's cheapest seasonal test — riding the season the category peaks in anyway [!] gifting mechanics unresearched this pass — pilot-scale only.

Channel doctrine — the fourth passenger's discipline. Skin's channel plan is deliberately parasitic, and the word is used with respect: every rail it rides — the importer, the container, the marking ops, the review team, the official storefronts, the eventual apteka relationships — was paid for by the first three categories, and skin's economics are designed around NOT paying for them twice. The discipline this imposes is real. Skin does not get its own distributor conversations, its own container schedule, or its own media team; it gets the set's, on the set's calendar, which is precisely why the seasonal lock matters — the one thing the shared machinery cannot do is move winter. The compensating freedom is that skin's failure is the set's cheapest: a category that borrowed its rails can stand down for a season without stranding infrastructure, which is what makes the two-SKU, one-winter, kill-cheap design honest rather than timid. The set built the road; skin is the first proof the road carries traffic it wasn't built for — and that proof, more than skin's own P&L, is what the batch's remaining markets are watching for.

The delta test, worked — before a word of copy exists

Move 4's precondition deserves its own scene, because it is the deck's version of the oral file's taste gate and just as unskippable. February, Yekaterinburg — chosen over Moscow because the Urals winter is the category's honest examiner and Ulybka Radugi's shelf territory besides. Ten households from the pragmatist cell, each already using the archetype or vaselin, each given an unbranded Herbolene tube and a two-week diary with exactly four questions: does it last the walk to the metro (the barrier question), does the phone screen smear (the texture question), do cracked knuckles improve by week two (the repair question), and — the only marketing question in the file — would you pay double your current tube for this, yes or no, and why in your own words. No moderator romance, no concept boards: the product either survives a Urals February in the hands of people who did not choose it, or the modernise-the-archetype thesis is a Delhi conference-room opinion. If the diaries say the delta is real, their sentences become the panel's raw material and the phone-screen test graduates from hypothesis to content device. If they say it is not, the deck's cheapest failure executes before a rouble of media exists: reformulate for the climate or stand down for a year — both minuted, neither improvised. Ten tubes, two weeks, one honest answer to the only question the winter aisle asks: better enough, or not.
