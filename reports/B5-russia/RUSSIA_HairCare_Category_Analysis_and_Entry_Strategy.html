C1–C2 · Category frame and size-of-prize

Russian hair care is a mature, fully-laddered category that never stopped being an OIL category at its functional end. The post-2022 map has three storeys: a value/apteka storey owned by folk-remedy oils and legacy domestic brands (Mirrolla burdock oil RUB 99–141, Zolotoy Shelk, Nevskaya Kosmetika); a mass-natural storey where the Kalina-heritage brands (Chistaya Liniya) and the Natura Siberica group trade herbal credibility at scale; and a premium/professional storey (L'Oréal Professionnel, ex-Henkel Schwarzkopf lines now under Lab Industries) that survived 2022 in reduced form Kommersant/RBC/retail listings, 2023–26. Dabur's captured listings sit at RUB 465 (Amla 45ml, WB #18059040) and ~RUB 575–650 (Vatika coconut/almond via specialist e-tailers) — the mid storey [!] all prices pre-G1. The strategic fact of the category: oils are not a niche to educate — they are a folk category to join.

C3 · Brand-by-brand entry teardown — five winners, one self-read

1 · Репейное масло / Mirrolla — the incumbent that isn't a brand

The functional-oil shelf's true owner is a FOLK CATEGORY, not a company: burdock oil, sold by Mirrolla, Evalar, Zolotoy Shelk and apteka own-labels at RUB 99–141, framed in narodnaya-meditsina terms — scalp circulation, root strength, dandruff apteka.ru/gorzdrav/samson-pharma listings. Its lesson for an entrant is the deepest in the deck: Russians already believe exactly what Dabur needs them to believe — that a traditional plant oil, applied patiently, grows stronger hair. The belief predates every brand on the shelf and belongs to none of them. Amla does not need to import the claim; it needs to import a PEDIGREE for it. The framing writes itself and must be panel-verified, never assumed: for shoppers who trust burdock oil, Amla is the same idea with a 5,000-year Ayurvedic passport.

2 · Natura Siberica group — the natural champion, distracted

The group (Natura Siberica, Organic Shop, Рецепты бабушки Агафьи/Grandma Agafia's Recipes, Planeta Organica) built Russia's biggest wild-botanical story, survived a founder's death, an inheritance war, a RUB 4.5bn lawsuit and a near-bankruptcy, and sold 100% to AFK Sistema (May 2023); ~RUB 10.2bn revenue, self-declared ≤4% of the cosmetics market with top-3 ambitions RBC/Interfax/Vedomosti 2023; zdrav.expert. Its teardown lesson is double: (a) provenance-led natural positioning WORKS at scale in Russia — the Agafia line in particular proves shoppers pay for folk-herbal authenticity at mass prices; (b) the champion has spent four years on lawyers and ownership, not innovation — a window, exactly as the macro read Natura Siberica's distraction.

3 · Zolotoy Shelk — the domestic value engineer

Zolotoy Shelk (Золотой шёлк — "golden silk", Narodnye Promysly LLC) built a wholly-Russian complex-actives range (the "Shelkosil" follicle-stimulation story) across shampoos, masks, oils and sprays, distributed apteka + mass retail at value-mid prices beloris/fabriz listings. Lesson: the pseudo-clinical "active complex" register is the domestic value tier's upgrade language — Amla's answer is not a competing molecule story but a HERITAGE story; do not fight Shelkosil on lab diagrams the panel cannot verify.

4 · Lab Industries (ex-Henkel) — the transferred portfolio

Henkel sold its Russian business (April 2023, RUB 54bn) to a domestic consortium; the renamed Lab Industries kept producing the portfolio — Taft, Shamtu, Schwarzkopf lines among them Kommersant/Vedomosti/RBC 2022-23 [!] current shelf branding needs an aisle check. Lesson: the premium-mass shelf did not empty in 2022 — it changed OWNERS while keeping brand codes, so the "Western exodus vacuum" narrative oversells the opening. The genuine opening is narrower: innovation cadence and marketing spend fell, and no incumbent owns the AYURVEDIC oil slot specifically.

5 · Parachute (Marico) — the graduation map, drawn by a rival

Parachute coconut oil has traded in Russia since ~2009 and now sits in ordinary pharmacy chains (Stolichki, 366.ru) and across the Ayurveda e-tailer layer greencardamon/irecommend. This is the single most important teardown entry: an INDIAN hair oil that crossed from the Indian-goods niche into mainstream apteka distribution — proving the lane Amla must travel exists and is survivable. The route it demonstrates: years of niche persistence → review corpus → pharmacy listing on the folk-oil shelf. Dabur's advantage over Parachute's slow walk: the FORMALIZE machinery (official storefront, marking, media) can compress the timeline the moment the paper clears.

6 · Self-teardown — the risk the entrant carries in

Dabur's Russian hair file arrives with a wound already documented: the review corpus alleges adulteration on some batches — the kosmetista thread's "ни грамма натуральности" is the exact sentence a formal entrant cannot leave unanswered. The risk is not reputational abstraction; it is a RANKING mechanic — WB weights the most recent 15 reviews heavily, so a run of authenticity complaints on grey stock can bury a listing the official importer now owns. The mitigation is the deck's whole design: authorized stock, batch-verifiable Chestny Znak codes ON the pack and IN the ad copy, and the seller census so grey inventory stops arriving beside official inventory on the same card. The self-read's honest clause: formalization may RAISE prices (marked, insured, properly-routed stock costs more than grey stock), and the deck's margin architecture must absorb that without abandoning the mid storey.

The census, worked — one listing end to end

What move 1 looks like in practice, on the deck's best-documented card. WB #18059040, "Индийское масло для волос DABUR AMLA 45ml", RUB 465. The analyst opens the card from a logged-in session and records five fields. FIRST, the seller-of-record name — Wildberries displays it on every card; the name is checked against the Russian company register (an ИНН/INN lookup) to learn whether this is a one-person reseller, a specialist importer with a portfolio of Indian brands, or a serious distributor with warehouse addresses. SECOND, the price history via the public price-tracker layer Russian shoppers themselves use — a card that has re-priced eight times in six months is a reseller surfing FX; a stable card is managed inventory. THIRD, the review corpus: count, average, and the last fifteen read one by one, because that is exactly what the algorithm and the next shopper will do — every authenticity complaint is logged with its date and its batch reference if the reviewer photographed the pack. FOURTH, marking status: does the card's imagery show a DataMatrix code; does the seller's assortment suggest marking-compliant operations. FIFTH, assortment breadth: a seller carrying forty Dabur SKUs is a candidate partner; a seller carrying three is noise. One analyst-hour per card, perhaps forty cards in the full census — a week of work that replaces the single most expensive mistake available in this market: choosing a partner, a price or an enforcement posture before knowing who is actually behind the shelf. The census file then carries ONE named owner, per the single-owner rule, because five more Batch-5 markets will ask to borrow it.

C5 · Consumer decode — two shoppers, one anxiety

The apteka loyalist. She has bought репейное масло for years — RUB 120, red-and-yellow cap, works if you are patient. She meets Amla not in a pharmacy but on Wildberries, searching "масло для волос от выпадения" (hair oil for hair fall). The listing says India, says Ayurveda, costs three times burdock. Her first question is not "what is amla?" — the folk-oil belief already answers why oils work. Her question is the one her culture trained her to ask of anything imported and unfamiliar: is it real? She scrolls to the reviews and finds both answers — the five-star hair-fall testimonies and the adulteration allegation — and the sale hangs on which she reads last. That is the whole consumer problem of this deck, met in one scroll: the barrier is not belief, it is authenticity.

The marketplace naturalist. Younger, VK- and Telegram-fed, reads composition lists (состав) line by line, already buys Agafia or Organic Shop, treats "натуральная косметика" as a default filter. For her Amla is legible as an exotic upgrade within an existing habit — IF the состав scan survives her reading (mineral-oil listings are a known Vatika criticism in the review corpus) and IF the brand speaks the ingredient-literate register her feeds taught her. She is the review-writer; the loyalist is the review-reader. The launch sequence targets her first because her reviews unlock the loyalist's trust — the review-velocity programme (T9b) is aimed squarely at her.

Channel doctrine. The saliency table hides one structural novelty worth stating plainly: this is the programme's first market where the DIGITAL shelf is the primary shelf rather than the modern-trade proxy for one, and where the specialist layer — the Ayurveda e-tailers — functions the way Batch 8's warm-door grocers did: small in volume, decisive in credibility, and deserving of formal protection. The protected-specialist rule this deck adopts: official marketplace cards never undercut the specialist layer's prices on like-for-like SKUs, and the specialist accounts get supply terms that survive the formalization. The reasoning is the same as Mozambique's protected-grocer price re-derived for Russia: the channel that kept the brand alive before the company arrived is not a competitor to squeeze but an asset to keep warm — it holds the category's priesthood, its forums seed the review language, and its buyers are the panel's best recruiting ground. The rule costs a few points of specialist-channel margin and buys the one thing no media budget can: a corps of credible insiders with no reason to resent the brand's formal arrival.

DEEP THINK · Formalizing versus free-riding, argued honestly. A sceptic at the board table should ask: Dabur already sells in Russia at zero cost — grey sellers buy export stock, carry every risk, and the review corpus grows without a rouble of A&P; why pay the formalization premium at all? The deck's answer has three parts. First, the free ride pays nothing: no contribution reaches Dabur beyond the export invoice, and the T11 grey column's profit accrues entirely to intermediaries. Second, the free ride is DEGRADING the asset: the adulteration narrative in the corpus attaches to the BRAND, not to the seller who caused it, and the recent-15 review mechanic means the damage compounds at the exact point of sale. Third, the free ride is closing on its own: marking enforcement since July 2025 squeezes non-compliant stock regardless of what Dabur does. The question is not whether the grey era ends — it is whether Dabur owns what replaces it. That said, the sceptic wins one clause: if the census shows official economics cannot beat grey economics even at steady state, the deck's kill-switch is to STAY an exporter — keep selling to whoever buys, formally decline the market build, and say so. An honest FORMALIZE file carries its own refusal branch.
DEEP STRAT · The first category deck of a seven-country batch. Hair goes first in Russia for the same reason it went first across Batch 8: it is the category where the entry MODE is most visible, and whatever instrument the mode requires gets built here for the siblings to inherit. Botswana's hair deck built the champion-door method; Russia's builds the seller census — the programme's first instrument for markets where the brand precedes the company. Expect the census to be this batch's relay curve: Kazakhstan and Belarus see the same marketplaces, the same mirrors, possibly the same sellers. One census method, run seven times, is cheaper than seven improvisations — share tools, never engines, exactly as the Lusophone verdict wrote it.

Programme perspective. Ninety-seven files into the programme, the shape of this one is genuinely new. Every prior hair entry asked "how do we get onto the shelf?"; Russia asks "the shelf already stocks us — now what?" The honest answer the deck settles on is that arriving uninvited is neither a head start to celebrate nor a problem to litigate: it is EVIDENCE — the cheapest, most credible demand study the programme has ever received, funded entirely by strangers. Grey sellers risked their own capital on the proposition that Russians would pay RUB 465 for Indian amla oil, and the review corpus is the study's findings, adulteration complaints included. The entry design simply refuses to waste what it was given: ratify the price the market already accepted, answer the anxiety the market already voiced, and pay the formalization premium that converts someone else's experiment into Dabur's position. That discipline — read the inherited evidence before spending on new evidence — is this deck's contribution to the batch's method.
