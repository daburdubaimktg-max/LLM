C1–C2 · Category frame — a repricing shock inside a three-register market

Russian toothpaste is not a category with a gap; it is a category with a GRAMMAR. Three registers own the shelf: the folk-herbal register (Лесной бальзам's oak-bark-and-herb-decoction gum-health story, ~3.8% share but 25 years of category education paid for by Unilever/Kalina); the clinical register (R.O.C.S. — apteka-led, dentist-endorsed, #1 in children's at 53%; Sensodyne ~15.7% on sensitivity); and the natural-design register (SPLAT — RUB 14.8bn FY2025, +25%, ~13.4% unit share and rising, a genuine Russian premium export story) ruhim/zdrav.expert/tadviser/dp.ru 2024-25 [!] shares. Above them all sits Colgate — ~23.3% units, the stayed-but-reduced #1 with its Stavropol plant running. The category's live event is a PRICE SHOCK: the average tube reached RUB 327.86, +51.9% y/y at end-2025 retail trade data — a repricing that stretched the ladder and widened the exact mid-tier band where Dabur Red's inherited RUB 273 already sits.

The repricing window, read closely

A +51.9% average-price year deserves a paragraph of its own, because it is the only cyclical fact in this file that favours the entrant. When a staple reprices that hard that fast, three things happen in sequence. First, the habit buyer LOOKS at the shelf again — the autopilot that protects incumbents switches off, and every brand on the shelf gets re-auditioned whether it asked to be or not. Second, the tiers SMEAR: a mass tube that was RUB 90 now costs what mid used to, and the shopper who refuses to follow her old brand upward goes looking for "the same seriousness for the money I used to pay" — which is precisely the sentence Red at 273 answers inside a repriced ladder whose average is 328. Third, the window CLOSES: repricing shocks normalise within four to six quarters as wages and habits catch up, and the re-audition ends. The wave plan therefore treats the window as a dated asset — wave 1 and the taste gate run inside it — while refusing to let urgency skip the gate itself: a shock window with a rejected taste is just a faster way to lose. The entry rides the window; it does not bet on it staying open.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown — five winners, one self-read

1 · SPLAT — the natural-design template

SPLAT is what a local challenger looks like when it executes for twenty years: #1 Russian oral-care brand by volume since 2009, ~13.4% unit share and climbing (+3.09pp in 2025), RUB 14.8bn FY2025 revenue (+25%), exports to 60+ countries including 2025 shipments to Saudi Arabia zdrav.expert/kommersant 2025. Its method: design-led packaging, ingredient storytelling, taste innovation, and a premium-but-attainable price that made "natural toothpaste" an upgrade rather than a compromise. Teardown lesson for Dabur: SPLAT proves Russians pay mid-premium for natural ORAL efficacy with a story — and simultaneously closes the generic-natural lane. Red cannot out-natural SPLAT; it must out-PROVENANCE it: Ayurveda is the one story SPLAT cannot tell.

2 · R.O.C.S. — the apteka clinician

R.O.C.S. (DRC group) owns the pharmacy lane and the children's segment (53% share; ~24% overall per 2021 data [!] refresh) on dentist endorsement and clinical formulation claims. Lesson: the apteka door for toothpaste is GUARDED by a clinical incumbent — unlike hair oil's folk shelf. Red's apteka entry, if any, rides the gum-health/herbal-medicinal frame (its genuine Ayurvedic register), never a head-on clinical pitch.

3 · Лесной бальзам — the register-builder

Lesnoy Balzam spent 25 years teaching Russians that a toothpaste built on herb decoctions (oak bark, chamomile, sage) is a serious gum-health product, not folk quaintness — first under Kalina, then Unilever, now produced by Arnest YuniRus in Yekaterinburg lesnoybalzam.ru/company.unipack.ru. Its ~3.8% share understates its gift to Dabur: the REGISTER it built is exactly the one Red speaks — herbal actives for gum health — with a different herbarium (clove, neem, miswak vs oak bark and sage). The teardown's key insight: Red does not need to explain WHY herbs belong in toothpaste; Лесной бальзам already paid that education bill. Red needs only to explain why INDIAN herbs — a provenance question, answerable, cheap.

4 · Colgate — the intact ceiling

Colgate suspended investment and advertising in March 2022, then confirmed in February 2023 it would stay, balancing income protection and staff retention; the Stavropol facility runs and the brand holds ~23.3% of units RBC 2022; dp.ru/Bloomberg 2023. Lesson (same as hair's Lab Industries row, harder edition): there is no vacuum at the top — there is a QUIET ceiling. The opportunity is the leader's silence: with marketing suspended, Colgate defends by shelf inertia, not by argument. A preference brand can out-TALK a silent leader without ever out-spending it — but must never mistake silence for weakness in distribution.

5 · Himalaya — the graduation proof, discount edition

Himalaya toothpaste reached Fix Price at RUB 39.50–99 and ordinary supermarkets at ~RUB 160 irecommend threads — an Indian Ayurvedic paste in true mass retail. The proof cuts both ways and the teardown holds both edges: it proves the ROUTE (Indian herbal toothpaste can graduate beyond the Ayurveda-shop niche into national value retail), and it warns about the DESTINATION (Fix Price pricing brands the origin as cheap — a position Red, at 273, must not drift toward). Dabur takes the route, refuses the destination: graduate into Magnit Kosmetik and grocery mid-shelf, not the fixed-price bin.

6 · Self-teardown — the taste question and the census inheritance

Red's honest risk is in the mouth, not the market: it is a PUNGENT paste — clove-forward, warming, medicinal — entering a market whose herbal register was built MILD (chamomile-mild, oak-bark-astringent at most). The review corpus on Indian pastes here skews positive among niche buyers, but niche buyers self-selected for exoticism; the mid-tier shopper did not. A designed taste trial (T13) therefore gates every scale decision — the deck refuses to discover taste rejection in the national roll-out numbers. Second self-read: the seller census from the hair deck covers Red's cards in the same sweep (same marketplaces, plausibly the same sellers) — this deck inherits that instrument rather than rebuilding it, and its own twist is regulatory: Red's formula must be checked against the 0.15% fluoride threshold BEFORE filing, because the declaration-vs-registration fork changes cost and timeline.

C5 · Consumer decode — the tube in the basket, three ways

The habit buyer (the ceiling's subject). Buys the same Colgate her household always bought, now RUB 40 dearer than last year. The +51.9% category repricing is HER event, not the strategist's: she is re-reading the shelf for the first time in years, which is the only reason a preference brand gets an audience at all. Red's one sentence to her must do three jobs — same seriousness, different tradition, worth the same money: the panel's hardest brief in this file.

The natural upgrader (SPLAT's subject, Red's target). Already pays 300+ for SPLAT because design and ingredient stories persuaded her natural is BETTER, not just gentler. She scrolls marketplaces, reads состав, watches Telegram beauty-health channels. Red at 273 is legible to her instantly — herbal, serious, exotic-but-credentialed — IF the first squeeze doesn't shock her. She is the taste trial's primary cell and the review-velocity programme's first audience, in exactly the hair deck's naturalist-then-loyalist sequence.

The gum-health worrier (Лесной бальзам's subject). Buys herbal toothpaste for a REASON — bleeding gums, dentist's warning — and treats the herb list as a treatment plan. Red's clove-neem-miswak grammar reads to her as a stronger dose of the register she already trusts, and the pungency the upgrader must be warned about is, for her, EVIDENCE of potency. The decode's finding: the same sensory fact is a risk in one cell and an asset in the neighbouring one — which is why the taste trial (T13) segments before it scores.

Channel doctrine — the basket problem, named early. Toothpaste differs from the set's other categories in one behavioural fact this table must respect: it is a BASKET staple, bought with groceries on autopilot, while marketplaces are DESTINATION shelves, browsed with intent. The FORMALIZE lane therefore carries a structural handicap in oral that it never carried in hair — the shopper who would choose Red on a browsed shelf may simply never browse for toothpaste at all. Three design answers, priced into the plan rather than hoped around. First, the marketplace phase targets the two cells who DO browse the category — the natural upgrader and the gum-health worrier, both destination shoppers by need — and measures itself against their volume, not the nation's. Second, subscription mechanics (both platforms run repeat-purchase programmes) convert one browsed decision into an autopilot annuity — toothpaste's repeat physics are the marketplace's best friend once the first conversion lands. Third, the graduation to Magnit Kosmetik and grocery mid-shelf is not an expansion luxury but the posture's COMPLETION: preference brands in staple categories live or die on physical adjacency to the habit aisle, and the deck's marketplace phase is honestly framed as the evidence-factory that buys that adjacency, not as the end-state. The tight waterfall and this doctrine point the same direction: oral is the set's volume game, played patiently.

The taste gate, worked — one cell, one evening

What T9b looks like on the ground, so no one mistakes it for a formality. A rented room in Nizhny Novgorod — deliberately not Moscow, because the graduation channels are regional — and twelve women from the gum-health cell, each a current herbal-paste buyer, each paid for a two-week diary, not an evening's opinion. Evening one: the blind squeeze. The moderator records the first three seconds on video — the flinch, the pause, the second sniff — because first-contact shock is a datum the diary will later contradict or confirm. The paste is scored against the participant's own current brand on five scales, and the only score that matters tonight is "would you finish the tube?" Then the tubes go home. Day seven, the diary check-in: the adaptation curve — did the warmth become the point? Day fourteen, the branded reveal and the money question: at 273, beside your usual paste at its real shelf price, which goes in the basket, and why, in your own words. Those recorded sentences — not the scores — are the panel's raw material for the card copy, because the register this deck needs is the one its own converts already speak. Twelve women, three cells, two cities, four weeks, a minuted read-out: the cheapest instrument in the file, guarding the thinnest waterfall in the set.
