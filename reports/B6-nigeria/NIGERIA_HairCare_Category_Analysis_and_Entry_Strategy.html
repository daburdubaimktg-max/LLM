NIGERIA — HAIR CARE: CATEGORY ANALYSIS & ENTRY STRATEGY

Dabur India · Batch 6 (SSA West Africa) · Category deck 2 of 4 — the oils & treatment BUILD

Prepared 6 July 2026 · Public sources only · Regional lens: africa-consumer-insight · English ad copy verified / Pidgin+Hausa gap rule applied · Cites NIGERIA_MACRO

THE ASK: Nigeria's US$1.16–1.46bn hair-care market is inflating on FX, not volume (2024 value up on a >60% unit-price jump while volumes fell) — which is the exact opening for a naira-priced, locally-blended play. Dabur is already a named hair incumbent here through Amla ("world's largest hair-oil brand"), but the position is imported, premium-priced and unmerchandised: Amla 200ml sits at ₦16,000 on Jumia against local amla oils (Tara, Zoya) at ₦3,000–7,500 — a ~3× wall in which only ~15–25% of the shelf price is true product cost (the rest is the 20% CET, FX-inflated CIF and stacked informal-trade margins). The deck's spine is three moves. (1) Don't fight the extensions fortress — Darling/Godrej (new Lagos wet-hair factory, Dec 2023), X-Pression and Lush/Tolaram own synthetic fibre; Dabur has no right-to-win there. (2) Own the unowned lane — herbal strength + scalp/hydration for natural & braided hair, the fastest-growing oils/leave-in segment with no dominant herbal owner, on Amla's latent "strong, long, thick" equity bridged to the North's chebe/henna heritage. (3) Break the price wall by blending at Festac/Lagos — a local-fill Amla can plausibly hit ₦5,000–7,500, inside the local band, while carrying the Dabur trust mark. The route to the shopper is the salon/braider — the shared node between care and extensions, and the proven local playbook (Nature's Gentle Touch trained ~3,000 stylists via its "Hair Institute"). Category verdict (macro T16): BUILD — a disciplined oils-and-treatment beachhead, not an assault on fibre or a dying relaxer.

C1 — The ask (executive summary)

Macro anchor (§M8, §M11): Nigeria is a re-acceleration; Dabur is a named hair incumbent via Amla but the range is unmerchandised (macro T13b). Hair is ranked BUILD — oils franchise management + refuse the Godrej/Tolaram extensions fortress; the Vatika range decision is a Dabur International question.

The play in one screen. Three consumer behaviours co-exist in Nigeria and the deck must not confuse them: women still relax (though penetration is falling to ~3.4%), the young are going natural, and nearly everyone buys extensions/protective styles — a ~US$6bn/yr "dry hair" economy across Africa. Extensions is the structural winner but it is a fibre-manufacturing and social-vendor game owned by Darling, X-Pression and Lush — not a lane Dabur can win. Dabur's right-to-win sits in the care layer that every one of those three behaviours needs: oils, leave-ins and scalp treatment, the fastest-growing sub-category, with no dominant herbal owner. Dabur already holds the ingredient equity (Amla = "strong, long, thick"), a heritage bridge (amla ↔ the North's chebe/henna "long hair" tradition) and — critically — a local factory that can collapse the ₦16,000 import wall to a ₦5,000–7,500 naira-priced product. The three fixes: re-cost via Festac local-blend (+ LUP sachets), re-merchandise Amla and resolve Vatika's apparent NG absence, and build the salon/braider engine Dabur has never had (the NGT model). Waves: W1 Amla local-blend re-launch + salon attach (South) & halal-North (chebe/henna adjacency, Sabon Gari); W2 Vatika treatment + scalp/leave-in for natural & braided hair; W3 kids' Amla + a stylist-certification platform. Reputation Bet (C18): by 31 Dec 2028 a locally-blended Amla reorders across ≥4 states at a naira price inside the local band, salon-detailed, with a halal-North SKU — or the imported-premium position is falsified and Hair reverts to an e-com harvest.

So what: the claim ladder is the discipline that keeps Dabur's most tempting message legal. Nigeria's single most powerful hair claim is "growth" — and it is exactly the one that trips drug reclassification. Dabur's escape is that its ingredient truth does the work the banned word can't: "amla for strong, long, thick hair" and "reduces breakage" are cosmetic-legal and carry the same consumer promise. Small local players print "growth" with impunity because enforcement is weak, but a scaled Dabur launch will be held to the letter — so the claim architecture is a competitive moat, not just a compliance chore.
