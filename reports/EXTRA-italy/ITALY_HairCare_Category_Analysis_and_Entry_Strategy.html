THE DECK IN ONE PARAGRAPH: Italian hair care (€1.096bn retail, 2024, +6.6% — plus a >€620M salon economy) is 77% a grocery-channel category — and, uniquely in this directive, a market whose OWN traditions already mainstreamed the hair-oil ritual: castor oil (olio di ricino) and argan sell as standalone pure oils at €10–15 per 100–120ml through pharmacy, erboristeria and natural brands (Marco Viti, Alkemilla, Bionoble), marketed in language nearly identical to Amla's Ayurvedic grammar — keratin affinity, split-end prevention, scalp nourishment. The corridor diagnostic returns its thirteenth finding: BRIDGE-BUILT — nobody needs to teach Italy oiling (Germany's trend), and no community holds it exclusively (France's estate); the practice is indigenous, the price band is established, and Amla is simply an untranslated synonym sitting BELOW the band at a grey €6.99/100ml with a review corpus flagging the exact objections Italy's INCI-literate natural mainstream punishes: strong odour, liquid paraffin, greasiness. The prescription: translate Amla INTO the Italian oil grammar, price INTO the €8.99–9.99 band [AUTHORED], and answer the corpus honestly — including the deck's sharpest portfolio question: whether Italy's natural-mainstream hero should be the classic (paraffin-based) Amla or a natural-oil-forward variant, an internal NPD/assortment question ASSIGNED, not assumed. The Vatika range gap (effectively absent from Amazon.it) is the census's cheapest fix; the Afro crossover has already happened organically (Bellapil/AfroItalia lists Amla) and is governed with the group's ORS order intact — coexist where the categories differ (ritual oil vs styling/relaxer care), never push onto the sibling's shelf.

A framing paragraph on why this deck's tone differs from its two EU hair predecessors. France's hair deck managed an estate's politics (fifteen doors, a recognition doctrine, a district ordinance); Germany's managed a laboratory's clock (audits before listings, a test that was coming). Italy's manages neither — its work is closer to craftsmanship than governance: write the listing well, price with dignity, decide one formulation question honestly, and route a portfolio that already exists. The directive's method scales DOWN as gracefully as up: where the market's structure is gentle, the plan is permitted to be simple, and padding it with imported heaviness would be its own kind of transplant error. The depth below goes into evidence and craft rather than machinery — because that is what this market's entry actually consists of.

C4 · THE CORRIDOR DIAGNOSTIC — THIRTEENTH FINDING: BRIDGE-BUILT

Set Italy beside its two EU predecessors and the finding names itself. France's hair corridor was TAUGHT-BUT-UNCLAIMED — third parties (prestige, DIY-mass) were educating a middle nobody had claimed, and a shelf had just opened. Germany's was RETAILER-OCCUPIED — the educator owned the shelf. What separates Amla from the band is not demand, teaching or access; it is TRANSLATION: an Italian-first listing that speaks the ritual's local grammar, states the usage (pre-shampoo, the greasiness fix), names the scent honestly, and answers the INCI question the corpus already asked. BRIDGE-BUILT corridors have the lowest entry cost in the taxonomy — and one specific trap: a bridge built by others carries their tolls. The band's residents priced 100ml at €10–15; entering beneath the band (the grey €6.99) reads as import-cheap, not ritual-worthy. The governed price walks INTO the band's lower rungs, dignity intact.
