C1–C2 · Category frame

Kazakh hair care is a three-storey market with a familiar shape and one novel fact. The storeys: global mass/masstige (L'Oréal's KZ subsidiary leading, P&G, Unilever, Henkel lines; mass shampoo ~₸600–1,200/400ml), Russian natural crossovers (Natura Siberica with full four-city delivery via siberica-kz.kz), and a small but real LOCAL tier — Jain Labo (Almaty premium-professional), ASIYA (natural), Qazaq Qyzy Men (positioned explicitly on "Kazakh hair and climate"), Telli Ondiris (the country's first GMP cosmetics contract manufacturer) kursiv.media May-2026; retailer catalogues Jul-2026 [!] no shares found for any local brand. The novel fact is the channel: one super-app carries the category's decisive shelf, and Dabur is already on it — partially. Vatika's shampoo/conditioner/mask range is listed in depth; Dabur Amla oil — the global flagship — was not confirmed present; only a Dabur castor oil 50ml (Kaspi ID 116337917) and a rival "Ayusri Amla" oil surfaced. Meanwhile the functional-oil slot is anchored, as in Russia, by burdock oil — an apteka staple at ₸275–4,225 across packs and blends, contested between Russian Evalar and local FitOleum apteka.103.kz/i-teka.kz/daribar.kz Jul-2026.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown — five winners, one self-read

1 · Burdock oil (the category that isn't a brand) — the anchor, second market running

The Slavic apteka-oil tradition trained Kazakhstan's shopper to expect a functional hair-oil shelf, exactly as in Russia — same product, same folk-clinical claims, same pharmacy placement, here contested between a Russian brand (Evalar) and a local one (FitOleum) apteka aggregators Jul-2026. The Russia deck's conclusion transfers with its reasoning intact and is NOT re-argued from zero: Amla enters BESIDE the anchor as the Ayurvedic equivalent, priced at a disciplined multiple, never against it. What IS new: the anchor's price spread here (₸275–4,225) is far wider than Russia's tight RUB 99–141 band — pack-size and blend chaos that G1 must normalize before the multiple is set, and a hint that the apteka tier itself premiumises blends (burdock + nettle at ₸3,285) in a way Russian pharmacies don't. The multiple may therefore anchor on the PLAIN 100ml (~₸640–790), not the blends.

2 · L'Oréal Kazakhstan — the resident professional

Unlike Russia's stayed-but-shrunk posture, L'Oréal runs a full local subsidiary here — mass to premium, salon to shelf eurobak membership; retailer catalogues. Lesson: the top of the market is DEFENDED by a fully-operational multinational, not a distracted one — no vacuum narrative survives even a day in this file. The consolation is the same as Russia's: nobody in that stack owns the Ayurvedic oil slot.

3 · The local quartet — the import-substitution signal

Jain Labo, ASIYA, Qazaq Qyzy Men and Telli Ondiris are individually small but collectively a SIGNAL: Kazakh trade press celebrates local beauty brands, a GMP contract manufacturer now exists, and "made for Kazakh hair and climate" is a live positioning kursiv/weproject 2026. Two lessons. First, the NEIGHBOUR RULES from B8 apply — these are the Fro Girls of this market; coexist, never crush; their growth validates the natural register Dabur speaks. Second, Telli Ondiris' existence puts local contract manufacturing on the table as a year-3 option if volumes ever justify tariff-free EAEU-local production — noted for the batch owner, not planned.

4 · Parachute — the distribution lesson, again

Marico's coconut oil sits in six pack sizes across seven-plus independent Kazakh e-tailers — beauty shops, pharmacies, the specialist layer beautyhood/ganga/biosfera/mirkos etc.. In Russia Parachute taught the graduation route (niche → apteka); here it teaches BREADTH — the independent-retailer mesh matters even in a super-app market, because it is where the specialist credibility and the pharmacy adjacency live. Dabur's oils trail Parachute's spread badly; the wave plan's second motion (after the Kaspi fix) is a six-account independent-retailer sweep using the same specialist relationships that already stock Chyawanprash.

5 · Himalaya — the benchmark, third market running

Himalaya's anti-hairfall oil 200ml is ON Kaspi; its toothpastes are on Kaspi; its skin range spreads through mir-vostoka.kz — the same importer-led structure found in Russia and now visibly ahead in Kazakhstan kaspi/mir-vostoka Jul-2026. C5 · Consumer decode — three shoppers, one super-app

The apteka mother. Buys burdock oil at the pharmacy with the paracetamol, for her daughter's braids and her own winter-dry scalp. The braid is not incidental: қос өрім carries meaning her grandmother enforced, and hair strength reads as family care, not beauty. Amla reaches her on the pharmacy shelf (wave 2's independent sweep) or not at all — she does not browse Kaspi for oils. Her one question is the Russian loyalist's, softened: what is this, and does it do what burdock does? The shashmai hook speaks to her — IF the panel confirms the reference reads as heritage rather than marketing archaeology.

The Kaspi browser. Twenty-six, Almaty or Astana, buys everything on instalments, reads both review layers (product AND seller), follows K-beauty TikTok. For her the Ayurvedic oil is legible as a routine upgrade — the same slot K-beauty hair essences occupy, at a friendlier price, financeable in three taps. She is the review engine and the launch's first audience; the instalment mechanics mean her price tolerance stretches one storey higher than her wallet — a structural difference from every prior market in the programme.

The southern traditionalist. Shymkent, more observant, more Kazakh-speaking, warier of Russian-labelled goods and warmer to halal-certified ones. Vatika's black-seed (қара сасыр/чёрный тмин) conditioner — already listed — speaks to her directly: black seed carries its own Prophetic-medicine resonance. The deck notes the asset, prices the halal-mark question into the macro's G4 test, and refuses to lead nationally with a frame the secular north would shrug at. Bilingual pack copy is her non-negotiable.

The instalment mechanic, decoded properly. One behavioural note deserves separation from the shopper portraits because it cuts across all of them: on Kaspi, a ₸1,790 oil can be bought in three interest-free monthly slices of ~₸600 — and the platform merchandises this on the product page itself, beside the price, as default furniture. The programme has never met this at FMCG price points. Its consequences are subtle: the relevant psychological price is the SLICE, not the ticket, so the mid storey's effective ceiling sits higher than wallet arithmetic suggests; but equally, instalment normalcy trains shoppers to compare slices across categories — a hair oil competes for the same ₸600 slice as a phone case. The deck's read: price to the ticket (relationship rule, burdock multiple), let the slice do its widening work unpriced, and never advertise the instalment — the platform already does, and a brand that leads with financing reads as expensive, which Amla must not.

Channel doctrine — one flywheel, not two shelves. The saliency table's deepest difference from Russia is not the names but the TOPOLOGY: Russia's marketplace duopoly let a brand hedge across two ranking systems, two review corpora, two fee schedules; Kazakhstan's super-app concentration means one algorithm's opinion of one seller account is, to a first approximation, the brand's national commercial health. The doctrine this imposes has three clauses. Concentrate: the converted seller's account gets ALL the operational investment — split-listing across multiple weak sellers is how brands stay invisible on Kaspi. Diversify deliberately, not reflexively: WB.kz/Ozon.kz mirrors and the independent mesh exist as insurance and credibility, sized at maybe a fifth of the effort, never allowed to fragment stock or price. And instrument the dependency: the monthly SLA audit is the deck's single most important recurring meeting, because on this topology a partner's shipping discipline is indistinguishable from brand equity. The batch should note the general form — in super-app markets, channel risk concentrates into COUNTERPARTY risk, and the census's new SLA field exists because of exactly this.

The anchor normalization problem, shown once

Before the matrix, one methodological paragraph the G1 analyst needs: the burdock anchor's captured spread (₸275–4,225) is not a price — it is a basket of incomparables. The ₸275 end is a 50ml pepper-blend promo pack; the ₸4,225 end is likely a multi-pack or a premium nettle blend at full list; the honest anchor for Amla's multiple is the PLAIN 100ml at its everyday price, which the snippets bracket at ₸640–790 across two cities. The G1 protocol therefore records, for every anchor candidate: pack size, blend vs plain, promo vs list, city, and channel (pharmacy vs marketplace) — five fields that turn a noisy spread into a usable per-100ml number. This matters beyond hair: the same normalization discipline applies to the toothpaste ladder (bulk-lot noise polluted the aggregator ranges) and the honey band (₸1,200–4,500/kg spans grades). The Russia set never needed this paragraph because its captures were single-listing prices; Kazakhstan's snippet-reconstructed data class does — and the protocol travels to every remaining 403-blocked market in the batch.

The conversion conversation, worked — one seller, one afternoon, four categories

Move 1's real shape is a meeting in an Almaty office with whoever runs the seller account currently listing Dabur on Kaspi — plausibly one of the specialist importers already stocking Chyawanprash, since the Kaspi Pay history requirement means the account is an operating business, not a dropshipper. The conversation has three parts, and the deck scripts them because the same meeting serves all four category files. FIRST, the audit, offered respectfully: their SLA metrics, their stock provenance, their sense of what sells — they know this shelf better than any research pass; the census's fields become interview questions. SECOND, the offer: authorized status, direct supply at import economics instead of whatever chain they currently buy through, the flagship SKUs they've never been able to source reliably, marketing support their listings have never had — the argument is that authorization makes THEIR business bigger, not that Dabur needs a favour. THIRD, the terms that protect both sides: MAP discipline so their margins hold, the protected-specialist clause so their non-Kaspi channels stay theirs, an SLA improvement plan if the audit found soft spots, and a review clause — because if their ops sink below Kaspi's thresholds, Dabur's whole KZ ranking sinks with them. One afternoon, prepared with four categories' assortment lists, and the entry's most important single relationship is either founded or ruled out. If ruled out: the second-seller scout begins, and the deck's timeline honestly slips a quarter — recorded in advance so nobody calls it a surprise.

Set position. Hair opens Kazakhstan as it opened Russia — the census market's first mover — but its real deliverable to the siblings is the SELLER: whoever converts here becomes the candidate seller-of-record for oral, OTC and skin on the same Kaspi account, which is why this deck's G2 decision is taken with all four categories' assortments in the room, not hair's alone. What hair leaves open on purpose: the halal-mark question (the macro's G4 test, reported by oral's Miswak pilot more than by hair), and the apteka door (the Parachute route is mapped but the pitch waits for velocity).

Set-position note, continued. Two more items this deck hands the siblings. The WATERFALL COMPARISON in T11 — the batch's first side-by-side lane costing (ordinary KZ vs sanctions-adjacent RU, netting ~1–2 points better here despite the tariff give-back) — becomes the template row every remaining B5 market fills in, because by Georgia the batch will have five lane costings and a real empirical curve. And the DOUBLE GATE for Kazakh copy (language panel + cultural-tone review) previews the batch's Turkic-language discipline: Uzbekistan and Azerbaijan will need the same two-layer check with their own languages, and building the review workflow now, on Kazakhstan's bilingual shelf, is cheaper than improvising it twice more.

DEEP THINK · The gap as information. Why would resellers list seven Vatika SKUs and skip the world's best-selling amla oil? Three hypotheses, each with a different consequence, none assumable. (1) INDEXING MISS — it's listed, search didn't surface it: then move 1 is a price-ratify, not a listing fix; G1 answers in an hour. (2) ECONOMIC SELECTION — resellers stock what moves, and masks/shampoos at ₸5,200 carry better absolute margin per unit than a ₸1,500 oil: then the gap is a rational reseller choice that an OFFICIAL entrant with portfolio logic should override — the flagship builds the brand that sells the masks. (3) DEMAND SIGNAL — amla oil genuinely doesn't sell here because the burdock loyalist never searched for it and the browser cell doesn't know the word: then move 1 still stands but the media plan carries the education load and the volume forecast halves. The deck budgets for (2), tests for (3) in the first quarter's velocity, and lets G1 eliminate (1) before anyone repeats the phrase "flagship gap" to the board. A gap is only an opportunity after it has been interrogated as a warning.
DEEP STRAT · The second run of the FORMALIZE playbook is the real test of whether the programme has a playbook. Russia's hair deck built the census, the beside-anchor frame, the protected- specialist rule; Kazakhstan's ran them in half the space and spent its freed hours on what is genuinely new — the super-app's flywheel, the flagship gap, the bilingual double gate, the instalment psychology. That ratio (inherited scaffolding : local thinking) is the batch's efficiency curve made visible, and it should IMPROVE again in Kyrgyzstan where the anchor, the language pair and possibly the very sellers repeat. The standing caution, carried from the Russia set's close: instruments answer HOW, never WHETHER — and this deck's whether (does the flagship sell at the authored multiple?) is bought by one quarter of live velocity, not by any amount of inherited method.

Programme perspective. The 103rd file records something the programme has not seen before: a market where the brand's SUPPORTING cast arrived and its lead never did. Every prior FORMALIZE finding — Russia's hair, oral and OTC shelves, Kazakhstan's own oral and wellness listings — showed informal demand pulling the obvious flagship first. Here the resellers built a Vatika business and left the amla slot to Himalaya, which is precisely why the deck refuses to treat "we're already on the shelf" as one fact: presence is a PORTFOLIO question, and the flagship-gap check now joins the standing W0 list for every remaining market. The deeper lesson for the batch owner: informal channels optimize for reseller economics, not brand architecture — margin per unit, sourcing convenience, whatever cleared customs last month — so the shelf a brand inherits from the grey tier is arranged by someone else's P&L. Formalization is not just legitimizing that shelf; it is REARRANGING it to the brand's own logic, and the two jobs cost differently and should be budgeted separately.
