M1 · Demography — young, urbanising, re-Kazakhifying

Population 20.4–21.1M (Astana Times Jan-2026 vs Worldometer 2026 — the defensible public range [!] stat.gov.kz direct), median age 29.7–29.8 — a full decade younger than Russia and the sharpest single contrast in the batch so far: this is a cohort-growth market again, after Russia's replacement-and-persuasion market. Urbanisation 65.1% and rising; three tier-1 cities hold ~26% of the country — Almaty ~2.35M (commercial capital, e-commerce fulfilment, the Indian-diaspora base), Astana ~1.64M (political capital), Shymkent ~1.29M (southern anchor, more observant/halal-forward) Astana Times/worldpopulationreview, Jan 2026. Ethnic structure (2021 census): Kazakh 70.4% and rising (63.1% in 2009), Russian 15.5% and falling — a structural "re-Kazakhification" with direct pack-and-copy consequences. Language: Kazakh is the sole state language (80.1% proficiency), Russian officially usable (83.7%) and still dominant in retail UX — bilingual Kazakh+Russian packaging is the safe default, and the Kazakh-language share of the work rises the further the plan reaches beyond Almaty stat.gov.kz 2021 census.

M2 · Economy — growth with a soft currency and dear money

The macro shape is favourable-with-cautions: growth 4.5–5.5% (2026f) makes Kazakhstan one of the strongest stories in the region, but inflation is double-digit (10.4% May 2026, NBK band 9–11%), the policy rate is 17% even after its first cut since 2024, and the tenge carries a multi-year depreciation trend (~514 → ~535 forecast) NBK/FocusEconomics/Interfax 2026. Three planning consequences, each familiar from the batch's toolkit: value/mid price-pack architecture over premium at launch (double-digit inflation compresses real wallets); asset-light marketplace-first entry over owned warehousing (17% money makes inventory carry punishing — the same FBO-financing logic as Russia's 16%, one point worse); and relationship pricing over fixed tenge lists (the crawl is structural). One deliberately un-Russian note: this is an OIL economy — the state budget and consumer discretionary both ride crude, so the risk register carries an oil-price line no Russia deck needed for consumer demand.

M3 · Entry lane — ordinary trade, deliberately kept ordinary

After Russia's hard case, Kazakhstan's lane section is short because the lane is CLEAN — and the deck's one strategic instruction is to keep it that way. M5 · Health & culture — two heritages, one shopper

Kazakhstan's cultural profile is a dual inheritance — Hanafi Sunni Islam practised with a notably secular, Soviet-shaped pattern, layered on 70 years of Russian administrative and medical culture. For Dabur this duality is an asset with two handles. The Soviet handle: the adaptogen tradition (eleuthero, rhodiola, ginseng) is normalised pharmacy behaviour here exactly as in Russia — the tonic mental model is pre-built. The Kazakh handle: kumys and shubat (fermented mare's and camel's milk) are LIVING daily wellness products — doctor-recommended for post-illness recovery, including post-COVID — a nomadic-heritage functional-food tradition the Slavic markets don't have lada.kz/bizmedia/tengrinews 2020-26. Chyawanprash's "ancient formula, daily family tonic" story can stand beside BOTH without contradicting either — the deck's core cultural finding. Halal turns positive here for the first time in the batch: ~70% Muslim population, an active certification infrastructure (SAMK's Halal Damu — 1,800+ companies certified since 2014, explicitly covering toothpaste, creams, shampoos; KazStandart, AKHIK, Halal Quality Center beside it), consumer interest expanding from food into personal care, and DUMK's own published endorsement of miswak as sunnah mu'akkadah halaldamu.kz/muftyat.kz/6wresearch 2024-26. The honest caveat the OTC/skin note argued and this file adopts: halal in Kazakhstan is a regulatory-readiness asset and a mild trust signal, not yet a proven shelf-conversion lever — the secular practice pattern is not the Gulf's. The move: certify (cheap, portable across the CIS, a real differentiator against SPLAT/Evalar/Natura Siberica who carry nothing), test the mark's shelf impact in-market, and only then decide packaging prominence. And the Bollywood/India-familiarity question is an honest source gap — no KZ-specific penetration data found; routed to the Kazakh-language queue rather than assumed.

The two-heritage shopper, met in one aisle

What the dual inheritance looks like at the shelf, in one composite the panel must pressure-test: a thirty-four-year-old mother in Almaty finishes Ramadan having fasted some days and skipped others without guilt; her medicine cabinet holds eleuthero drops her own mother swears by AND a jar of honey her aunt brought from an Almaty-region apiary "because the Prophet praised it"; she buys her toothpaste on Kaspi in instalments without thinking of it as credit; her feed is Instagram in Russian and TikTok increasingly in Kazakh; and when her daughter's school braids need taming she reaches for whatever the pharmacy stocked — burdock oil, most likely, because that is what the pharmacy stocks. Every Dabur category in this file speaks to her through a DIFFERENT one of her inheritances: Chyawanprash through the tonic cabinet (both traditions at once), Miswak through the faith she carries lightly but genuinely, Amla through the pharmacy's oil shelf, Herbolene through the winter that cares about neither heritage. The design discipline this imposes: never bundle the frames. A single "Ayurveda + halal + tradition" mega-message flattens her into a stereotype she does not recognise; four category messages, each keyed to the one inheritance that actually governs that purchase, meet her as she is. The panel's standing brief is to verify each frame separately — and the halal register most carefully of all, because overclaiming religious salience to a secular-practising majority is the file's most avoidable own-goal.
