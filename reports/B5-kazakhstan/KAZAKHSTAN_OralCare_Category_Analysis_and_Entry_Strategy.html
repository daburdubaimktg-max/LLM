C1–C2 · Category frame — a validated herbal register with a priced gap

The Kazakh toothpaste shelf is the Russian shelf's younger cousin, with the same three registers and friendlier arithmetic. Colgate anchors mass (Propolis Whitening 100ml ₸400; Gentle Whitening ₸625; Triple Action ₸845 [!] snippet prices); SPLAT owns premium-natural with a 27-SKU catalogue (₸614–1,714 aggregator band; Ultrakompleks 100ml ₸2,195; and — the deck's favourite fact — Lechebnye Travy ["Medicinal Herbs"] 100ml at ₸1,990, a mainstream herbal SKU that proves the register Dabur speaks is already paid for at premium prices) cosmart/tomas Jul-2026; R.O.C.S. holds its kids/clinical ground on Kaspi; Sensodyne is present (no clean price); Lesnoy Balzam is thin here; and — unlike Russia — no local champion exists: the search found no Kazakh national toothpaste brand at all [!] absence flagged, not asserted. Between Colgate's ₸845 top and SPLAT's ₸1,100 floor sits a lightly-occupied ₸850–1,100 seam, and beneath it the broader ₸600–1,100 value-herbal band. Dabur is already IN the field: Red 100g/200g, Herbal Salt+Lemon, activated-charcoal whitening, Miswak and Miswak Gold all listed on Kaspi, with Miswak separately anchored at pharmacies (aptekaplus ₸570/75g; ₸1,280/190g bundle) and at anfal.ru kaspi/aptekaplus indexed Jul-2026.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown — five entries, one self-read

1 · SPLAT — the register-validator, second market running

SPLAT's Kazakh catalogue (27 SKUs, herbal flagship at ₸1,990) proves the premium-natural register crossed the border with its Russian equity intact cosmart/tomas. The Russia teardown's conclusion transfers: the generic-natural lane is CLOSED at the top, and provenance is the open flank. What's new: SPLAT here is an IMPORT playing an away game — no Novosibirsk factory-gate economics, no home-market media halo — which is why its Kazakh prices sit at the top of its Russian relative band. The gap beneath it is therefore wider here than in Russia, and Red's landing zone is roomier.

2 · Colgate — the anchor without a silence

Unlike Russia's marketing-suspended Colgate, the Kazakh operation is ordinary — full range, normal trade support. The lesson inverts Russia's: there is no silent ceiling to out-talk; there is a NORMAL leader to flank. Consequence: Red's copy works harder here for attention (no incumbent quiet to fill), and the deck budgets the creator programme accordingly.

3 · R.O.C.S. — the clinical fortress, kids' edition

R.O.C.S. travels its apteka-clinical authority into Kazakhstan with a visibly deep children's range on Kaspi. Same ruling as Russia: the children's segment is REFUSED (fluoride tiers + the fortress + trust walls), and the gum-health frame is the only apteka-adjacent door worth testing — later, on velocity evidence.

4 · The miswak field — Dabur's own inherited outpost

The most interesting teardown entry is Dabur's own SKU, examined as if a competitor's: Miswak trades on Kaspi AND at pharmacy retail AND at a specialist Almaty e-tailer, in two variants (regular + Gold), price-anchored where nothing else Dabur sells is. Someone — resellers, pharmacies, shoppers — built this without a brief. The honest read: the demand it evidences may be functional (a good herbal paste at a good price), diasporic (Indian and Central-Asian familiarity), or religious (the sunnah association), and the deck does not know the mix. The pilot is designed precisely to find out — because if the religious component is real, Miswak is not a SKU here, it is a FRANCHISE, and the batch's three remaining Muslim-majority markets inherit the finding.

5 · Himalaya — the benchmark, oral edition

Himalaya toothpastes trade on Kaspi in at least three SKUs (charcoal whitening, Xylitol) — a more diversified Indian oral shelf than Dabur's in variants, though without the miswak resonance kaspi indexed. The clock argument applies but softer here: Dabur's oral footprint is its DEEPEST Kazakh presence, and the race in this category is Dabur's to lose.

6 · Self-teardown — the mix question, the fork, the palate

Three exposures. FIRST, the demand-mix question above — the deck's core unknown, bought by the pilot. SECOND, the fluoride fork: Red's and Miswak's formulas must be checked against the 0.15% threshold before filing — the same W0 formula check as Russia, reused. THIRD, the palate: Red's clove-forward pungency meets a market whose herbal reference (SPLAT's Lechebnye Travy) is Russian-mild; the taste gate re-runs here with locally recruited cells — the instrument travels, the mouths don't. One softener: Kazakhstan's cuisine runs spicier and more herb-forward than Russia's median, so the gate may read friendlier — a hypothesis for the gate, not a reason to skip it.

C5 · Consumer decode — three mouths, two engines

The value-herbal pragmatist (Red's cell). Astana, thirty-five, buys Colgate at ₸625 on autopilot, notices SPLAT at ₸1,990 and files it under "someday." Red at ₸850 is the first herbal paste priced inside her consideration band — and her question is Russia's question (is strong herbal BETTER or just stronger?), which is why the taste gate re-runs here unchanged in design. Her instalment reflex barely applies at these prices; she pays the ticket.

The practising professional (Miswak's northern cell). Almaty, twenty-nine, prays most days, wears her faith lightly at work, keeps halal at home without ceremony. She knows what miswak is — her grandfather kept the stick — and a good modern paste that honours the practice quietly appeals; a paste that LECTURES her about sunnah from a shelf-wobbler repels her instantly. She is why rule 1 exists, and why the pilot's creative is observational, not exhortative: the product in an ordinary morning, the tradition present, unexplained.

The southern household buyer (Miswak's core cell). Shymkent, buys for six people, more observant, more Kazakh-speaking, price-conscious in a way the instalment culture doesn't reach at toothpaste prices. For her the ₸1,280 family bundle (190g + brush) is the offer — the pharmacy already stocks it, the mosque community already knows the word, and the bundle's value math beats Colgate per-brushing. Her conversion is the pilot's primary metric, and her repeat rate its honest one.

The bleed risk, named before it happens. The never-bundle rule sounds procedural until one imagines the failure concretely: a well-meaning creator holds up both tubes in one video — "the strong herbal one AND the sunnah one, from the same Indian house" — and in nine seconds Red has borrowed faith it has no right to and Miswak has been reduced to a flavour variant. Both registers are damaged; neither cell trusts the brand more. The rule's enforcement is therefore contractual, not aspirational: creator briefs carry one SKU each, the two campaigns never share a hashtag, and the brand page architecture separates the lines the way the position grid does. Where the two legitimately meet — the converted seller's storefront, the pharmacy shelf — they meet as neighbours with their own cards, which is how every multi-line house on every shelf in the world coexists with itself. The discipline costs nothing and buys the only thing that matters in a two-engine play: each engine's audience believing the brand built that engine for THEM.

The gap, stress-tested before it is banked

One analytical honesty before the matrix: the ₸850–1,100 "seam" is derived from snippet prices whose noise the hair deck's normalization protocol was built for, and a gap derived from noisy data can be an artefact of missing rungs rather than an open lane. Three checks run at G1 before any board slide draws the ladder: FIRST, the missing rungs — R.O.C.S., Sensodyne and Lesnoy Balzam all lack clean prices this pass, and any of them could be sitting exactly in the seam; SECOND, the promo layer — Kazakh marketplaces discount visibly (the Vatika mask's 5,200-vs- 6,500 list-promo spread), so the EVERYDAY ladder may compress against the list ladder; THIRD, the pack-size trap — Colgate's ₸845 is a 100ml and SPLAT's ₸1,100 floor is a 40ml, which per-100g inverts part of the story. If the seam survives all three checks, Red's move-2 price banks it; if it narrows, the relationship rule (Miswak-anchor based, per the set-position note) reprices without drama. A deck that finds a gap should try to close it itself before a competitor demonstrates it never existed.

The pilot on the ground, worked — one pharmacy in Shymkent

What cell (b) actually looks like, so nobody designs it from an office chair. A mid-sized pharmacy near Shymkent's central bazaar, already stocking the Miswak bundle because aptekaplus's network reaches here. The Dabur rep's entire intervention is three objects: a shelf-strip beneath the existing facing carrying the certified halal mark and the per-brushing arithmetic (₸1,280 ÷ a family's quarter of brushing — the math, nothing else); a one-page leaflet by the till describing the miswak TRADITION in the panel's triple-gated Kazakh and Russian — what the stick was, what the paste keeps, no verse quoted, no duty implied; and a stock-depth commitment so the bundle never gaps during the measurement quarter. No promoter, no discount, no shelf-talker exclaiming anything. The measurement is till data: units per week versus the pre-period, versus cell (a)'s mark-less twin pharmacy two districts over, versus cell (c)'s untouched control. Twelve weeks later the repeat signal arrives in the reorder pattern. The whole cell costs less than one Almaty billboard month — and it answers a question three countries' packaging committees would otherwise debate from conviction. The design's quiet virtue: everything in it is REMOVABLE. If the read is negative, the strip comes down, the leaflet runs out, and no shopper ever knew an experiment happened — which is exactly the reversibility a faith-adjacent test owes the community it runs in.
