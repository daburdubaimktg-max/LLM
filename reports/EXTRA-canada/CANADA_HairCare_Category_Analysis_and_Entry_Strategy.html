The peer already proved it: Parachute crossed the ocean WITH its company — the corridor is open, and its toll is operational, not narrative
Prepared for the Dabur International board · 7 July 2026 · Public-web evidence only · Inherits the Canada Macro (ENTITY-ABSENT; ACCOMPANY; the litigation fence; no TikTok runway) · Gap rule in force · All platform prices [!] re-validate at G1 (Canadian session)

THE BANNER. Canadian hair care hands the corridor diagnostic its twenty-first finding: PEER-PROVEN. The question every European market asked — does the oiling corridor exist, who taught it, who owns it — is already answered here by a same-origin rival: Marico's Parachute sits on Amazon.ca in multiple SKUs, on Iqbal Foods' shelf in the 1-litre format, and markets "expeller pressed in Marico's state-of-the-art factory" purity copy to the exact shopper Dabur Amla serves. The corridor needs no proving, no education budget, no vocabulary bridge — a peer proved it, and in doing so set its price of entry: accompaniment. Parachute crossed WITH its company (reliable supply, compliant packs, real distribution); Dabur's Amla crossed in a suitcase (one third-party seller, 403-walled prices, a Vatika listing reading "currently unavailable — no restock date"). PEER-PROVEN corridors prescribe: match the peer's operational standard, then differentiate on what the peer cannot copy — portfolio breadth (amla is not coconut; the treatment register vs the base-oil register; the wellness wing behind it) and Brampton-density retail theatre on the directive's best geography (52.4% South Asian; Punjabi the largest non-official tongue). Above the diaspora shelf, Fable & Mane at Sephora Canada has already measured the premium ceiling — an ayurvedic hair-oil story at prestige pricing — giving the premium file a live comp. And one pool is fenced regardless of its size: the textured-hair market (Shea Moisture/Cantu at Walmart; the Feb 2026 locked-case controversy) is REFUSED this cycle under the macro's litigation fence. This deck's work is the diaspora shelf, done properly.

The three-tier stack deserves a paragraph before the diagnostic formalises it, because its strangest feature is easy to miss: the tiers do not feed each other. In most markets a brand climbs — grocery credibility funds beauty-aisle entry, beauty-aisle cachet lifts grocery velocity. Canada's stack is segmented by SHOPPER, not by price alone: the Iqbal aisle's buyer and the Sephora wall's buyer are different people with different registers, and Fable & Mane's success created no visible lift for grocery-tier Indian oils (nor drew from them). The practical consequence shapes the whole deck: winning tier 1 is a complete, self-justifying business — not a stepping stone that must "graduate" to justify itself — and the premium file is a SEPARATE venture with separate packaging, channel and economics, to be green-lit on its own case. Plans that treat the stack as a ladder overspend on the middle; plans that treat it as three markets spend precisely.

C4 · THE CORRIDOR DIAGNOSTIC — TWENTY-FIRST FINDING: PEER-PROVEN

Run the series against Canada and the failures are instructive. Not TAUGHT-BUT-UNCLAIMED (France): the education is not orphaned — a rival actively harvests it. Not BRIDGE-BUILT (Italy): no translation is needed at all — the shopper reads the label in the language it was written in. Not AFTERCARE-SHAPED (Spain): the ritual is home-administered here, not salon-priced. And not any estate-governance shape, because the blocking fact is COMPETITIVE, not structural: the corridor's proof of concept belongs to a peer. Marico did for coconut oil in Canada exactly what this directive proposes Dabur do for amla — arrived with an entity, kept shelves stocked, marketed purity in the diaspora's own register — and now holds the default position. PEER-PROVEN corridors carry a two-line prescription. First, the toll is operational parity: reliable supply (the "currently unavailable" listing is the single most damaging line in the census), compliant packs, official seller status — the accompaniment the macro already mandates. Second, differentiation must run where the peer cannot follow: Parachute is a one-note brand (coconut, base-oil register); Dabur's flank is the TREATMENT register (amla as conditioning/strengthening ritual — a different product moment than coconut's base oiling), the two-brand rack (Amla + Vatika covering both registers), and the wellness wing behind it that Marico's Canadian shelf lacks entirely. The finding also disciplines ambition: a peer-proven corridor is a SHARED corridor — the goal is the second seat at a proven shelf, not the peer's chair.

C11 · Ad & comms decode + LEAP (mandatory module 6)

The register that wins the Canadian diaspora aisle is not discovery copy — the shopper knows the product better than the copywriter — but RELIABILITY-AND-RESPECT copy: the brand from home, now properly here, in stock, honestly priced, legally labelled. Punjabi leads the vernacular layer by arithmetic (667k speakers, the aisle's plurality language); English and French carry the statutory layer; Hindi/Urdu/Gujarati/Tamil rotate in POS by store catchment. The treatment-flank copy speaks the champi register — the ritual's own vocabulary — without museum-glass nostalgia: these are RECENT immigrants; the ritual is current practice, not heritage recovery.

Objection 1 — "If Parachute already won, why is the second seat worth taking?" Because the corridor is bigger than one chair and the peer's win PROVES the economics rather than exhausting them. Objection 2 — "The deck spends a fence's worth of discipline on a market (textured hair) it was never going to enter — why?" Because the fence must be VISIBLE to survive handoffs. Objection 3 — "Density theatre is a nice phrase — does festival merchandising really replace a missing commerce platform?" Not equivalently, and the deck doesn't claim it. TikTok Shop converts REACH into purchases; the density programme converts PROXIMITY into purchases — different physics, similar arithmetic in this one geography, because Brampton's 52.4% concentration means a single end-cap's weekly footfall rivals a mid-tier creator's Canadian-South-Asian impressions, with a checkout twenty feet away. Density is the bridge, not the doctrine.
