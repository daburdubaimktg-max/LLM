THE BANNER. The corridor diagnostic closes the Canada set with its twenty-fourth finding: PROVEN-PATH-UNWALKED. Every element of Gulabari's mainstream crossover is already demonstrated — separately. The CATEGORY is proven: Thayers rose-petal witch-hazel toner holds a dedicated brand shop-in-shop at Shoppers Drug Mart, selling provenance copy ("grown exclusively for Thayers on a family farm") to mainstream Canada — rose-water tonics are an accepted drugstore format, not an exotic pitch. The DOOR is proven: Shoppers belongs to Loblaw, the same landlord whose Superstore shelf already carries two Dabur SKUs — the relationship the oral deck secures is the skin deck's warm-up. The PRODUCT is proven: Gulabari sells steadily across the diaspora channel (Amazon.ca 120ml/250ml ASIN families; southasiancentral, cartly, Motherland, distacart) on pure replenishment loyalty. What nobody has done is ASSEMBLE the walk — and the census's sharpest irony says the assembly is overdue: Meswak, a lower-cultural-fit product, crossed into mainstream Loblaw retail while Gulabari, the obvious-fit product, never did. PROVEN-PATH-UNWALKED corridors prescribe exactly what they sound like: no education budget, no new story, no category creation — govern the floor, join the family pitch at the grocery banner, then pitch Shoppers with comps all the way down (Thayers proves demand; Kannauj-class rose provenance matches the family-farm register; the price sits honestly below). Two fences hold throughout: the whitening fence's 19th market in its proactive form (hydroquinone BANNED outright in Canadian cosmetics; CBC Marketplace patrols the beat; the semi-annual audit of Dabur's own grey flows transfers from Spain), and the litigation fence's brand-architecture rule: Gulabari — clean architecture — carries Canadian skin; DermoViva never leads. This deck closes the Canada set.

The ledger's pattern is the finding. Crossovers in an ENTITY-ABSENT market happen when an importer takes initiative — and importers pick by THEIR economics (case velocity in grocery), not by brand-fit logic. C4 · THE CORRIDOR DIAGNOSTIC — TWENTY-FOURTH FINDING: PROVEN-PATH-UNWALKED

The neighbours fail in order. Not PEER-PROVEN (hair): Thayers is not a same-origin peer whose operations set the toll — it is a CATEGORY incumbent whose existence removes the education cost; nothing about matching Thayers' operations is required. Not BEACHHEAD-UNMANNED (oral): Gulabari holds no mainstream listing to secure — the beachhead is TO BE MADE, from proven parts. Not LADDER-GAPPED (Spain skin): Canada's rose seat is not a vacant rung in a living tradition — it is an OCCUPIED seat in a proven category, and the entry is share-within-category beside the incumbent (the Thayers decode: their provenance register legitimises provenance competition — a brand whose whole story is "our farm" cannot object to "our roses, since 1884"). The finding is the set's friendliest: every risk normally priced into a category entry has already been retired by someone else — category demand (Thayers), retailer door (the Loblaw relationship), product-market fit (the diaspora's replenishment loyalty), even the compliance machinery (the macro's CNF/IoR work covers cosmetics wholesale). What remains is assembly: sequence the proofs into one pitch and walk it. The prescription's discipline is therefore about ORDER and PATIENCE, not invention: floor first (governed, official, bilingual), grocery second (Gulabari joins the family-assortment pitch at Superstore — the warm door), Shoppers third (the beauty pitch, made with grocery velocity data in hand), and the premium/prestige tier never this cycle. The one strategic choice the deck owns is REGISTER: Gulabari enters as VALUE-PROVENANCE (honest origin at drugstore price, below Thayers) — never as prestige cosplay and never as exotica.
