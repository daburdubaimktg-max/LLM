C4 · THE CORRIDOR DIAGNOSTIC — TWENTY-SECOND FINDING: BEACHHEAD-UNMANNED

The neighbouring findings fail precisely. Not BELIEVER-BUILT (Spain OTC): that was a NETWORK of small self-selected doors; this is ONE large door — the hardest in Canadian grocery — opened by a single unknown actor, with nothing behind it. Not CROSS-ROUTED: the routes have crossed (the product reached the right mainstream shelf); what failed is occupation. Not PEER-PROVEN: no rival proved this seat — Dabur's own SKU did, unaccompanied. The finding is new: a beachhead exists at maximum-value ground, and it is unmanned. The prescription is an ORDER OF OPERATIONS, and the order is the strategy: (1) SECURE — the supplier trace (macro W0 #5) followed by supply-continuity terms, because an unmanned listing's default fate is delisting on a single stock-out or compliance query, and losing the Loblaw seat would cost years; (2) CLAIM — the halal certification and the devotional register, because the asset is free, differentiating, and no incumbent can copy it; (3) EXTEND — the family assortment pitch (Red, Herbal beside Meswak), made to a buyer whose own data now shows an ayurvedic SKU performing. Extending before securing risks the seat; claiming after extending wastes the meaning that would have carried the extension. The sequence is not preference — it is the finding.
