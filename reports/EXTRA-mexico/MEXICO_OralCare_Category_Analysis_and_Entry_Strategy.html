The teardown's central row (the absent local prover) carries the deck's deepest analytical question, and it deserves the fuller treatment: WHY did no Mexican house ever build a natural toothpaste? Three hypotheses survive first contact. The CHANNEL hypothesis: Mexican natural brands grew through TV-mass marketing (Genomma's machine) and pharmacy shelves — both channels reward claim-led products, and toothpaste's claims are locked by the fortress's dentist programmes; a natural paste had no channel that wanted it. The MARGIN hypothesis: toothpaste's mass price points (MXN$25–45) leave no room for natural COGS without the deliberator premium, and nobody tested whether Mexican deliberators would pay it. The CULTURE hypothesis: Mexican folk oral care ran on non-paste formats (salts, herbs, rinses), so the tradition never pointed at the tube. Each hypothesis implies a different experiment reading — channel says the DIGITAL shelf changes everything (the test's own laboratory), margin says the premium question IS the test, culture says the Sonora week's findings matter most — and the fieldwork's WHY interviews are designed to weight all three before the window opens.

DEEP THINK · Three objections answered

Objection 1 — "An experiment with a pre-signed pass is a plan designed to fail politely." Inverted: it is a plan designed so that FAILURE CANNOT BE HIDDEN and success cannot be faked. The pre-registration cuts both ways — a weak reading executes the pass without a rescue meeting, but a STRONG reading opens the pharmacy file automatically too, with the winning register attached and no committee able to slow-walk it. The architecture removes human discretion at exactly the two moments discretion performs worst: admitting a miss and moving on a win. Politeness has nothing to do with it; the protocol is the programme's honesty made mechanical.

Objection 2 — "Three test cells split a tiny signal three ways — why not one strong listing?" Because the register question is worth more than an early significance reading. If Mexico's natural-oral demand exists, the programme needs to know WHICH story carries it (botany-specific, family-classic, or the herbolaria bridge) before spending a peso on the pharmacy pitch — and a single listing answers volume while leaving the register a guess. The cells share every fixed cost, the window is long enough for small signals to accumulate, and the worst case (all flat) is itself the cleanest possible reading. The design trades speed-to- significance for decision-quality at scale-up — the right trade wherever year 2 costs more than year 1.

Objection 3 — "The deck leans on herbolaria culture for its hypothesis while refusing to market to herbolaria traditionalists — inconsistent?" Sequenced, not inconsistent. The tradition's role in year 1 is EVIDENCE (the Sonora week's folk-practice questions calibrate whether herbal tooth-care has roots here) and VOCABULARY (the «árbol farmacia» register), not a target segment — because marketing a foreign paste at traditionalist practice before the demand test reads would be both premature and presumptuous. If the experiment proves demand and the fieldwork finds real folk oral-care traditions, year 2's register can deepen the bridge respectfully. The tradition is consulted first, sold to later, maybe — the same manners the programme has kept at every sacred-adjacent boundary.
