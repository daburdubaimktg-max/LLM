C1 · Category frame — four sub-markets, one habitual centre

Zambian hair care runs the Southern African four-part structure: relaxers (salon-led; a Lusaka relaxer treatment services at K750), braids/extensions (Darling's kingdom — synthetic-fibre dominant; installs from K200 cornrows to K1,200 knotless; bundles from ~K100 informal [! TikTok-sourced floor]), texture/protective care (upkeep for braided/relaxed hair), and — the centre of gravity — hair food and oils: petroleum-based pomades and vegetable/herbal oils used daily for scalp and shine, the cheapest and most habitual purchase in the category, on every kantemba shelf in some brand's colours. Dark & Lovely markets a dedicated Gro Strong hair-food line continent-wide; Vaseline Blue Seal ships into Zambia among its named SADC markets; the braider economy (K150–300/day earners) consumes oils and pomades as bulk working inputs. No ZMW shelf ladder could be built from public sources — the deck's architecture is authored on regional geometry and the macro's corridor economics, labelled as such, and rebuilt at the browse.

C2 · Demand architecture — the tub, the bottle, and the chair

Engine one: the daily scalp habit (the tub). Hair food is bought like soap — habitually, in small quantities, at absolute-kwacha points, from the nearest shelf. The habit needs no creation; it needs a better-formulated, honestly-priced occupant with a story the radio can tell in Bemba. Amla's proposition (a century-old herbal oil, strengthening, for the scalp under any style) enters the habit as an upgrade WITHIN it, not a replacement of it. Engine two: the protective-style upkeep (the bottle). Six weeks under braids creates the same under-install care demand the SA file mapped — scalp oil with an applicator, refresh between washes — served today by improvisation. The braider is this engine's gatekeeper AND its bulk buyer: the B2B channel (wholesale packs, braider WhatsApp groups, salon reps) reaches her as a professional, and her chair recommends to every client. Engine three: the aspirational urban (the routine). Lusaka's Facebook-taught under-30s build routines with imported content; small in volume, loud in direction — served at Shoprite with Vatika's enriched formats, never allowed to define the launch's price architecture. The refusal engine: relaxers — declining, chemical, salon-locked; observed, not entered (the programme's standing call, third market running).

The consumer decode in one story

A month in Matero. Week one: a school cook buys her hair food — a K12 tub of whatever the kantemba stocks, no brand loyalty because no brand has ever asked for it — and oils her scalp nightly under a headwrap, as her mother did. Week two: her daughter comes home from the braider with fresh knotless braids and a K10 note's worth of loose oil decanted into a jam jar, because the chair's tub was running low and the braider sells splashes to clients as a sideline. Week three: on the minibus, the Nyanja presenter reads a promotion for a soap between song requests, and the cook, who has never clicked an ad in her life, buys that soap on Saturday. Week four: at Shoprite's month-end special she considers, for the first time, a bottle with a tree on it — if such a bottle existed on that shelf, at a price the flyer respected, with a name the minibus radio had said.

Read together: the tub, the jam jar, the presenter and the flyer are the wedge's four moments, and every one of them is currently unclaimed by any storied brand. The deck's whole design is to be present at all four in the same season — the sachet for the kantemba, the R4 bulk for the braider's sideline (formalised into the starter kit), the radio sentence for the minibus, and the honest bottle for the flyer.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown

3.0 Self-teardown: equity in the region, nothing on the soil

Dabur Amla is a documented pan-African force — Kenyan distribution through Unisel, East and West African shelf presence, decades of ethnic-hair-oil equity — and, on all available evidence, has never formally traded in Zambia. The self-teardown is therefore a question of TRANSFER: does equity built in Nairobi's and Lagos's retail systems arrive in Lusaka as recognition, as vague familiarity from cross-border media and travel, or as nothing? The Kamwala check answers part of it (grey stock would prove pull); the wedge's first two quarters answer the rest. The deck's honesty: the brand is proven-elsewhere entering a habit that is proven-here, and the join between them is the entry's actual bet — stated in the macro, priced in the reputation clause, and never dressed up as a certainty.

3.1 Dark & Lovely (Godrej) — the hair-food shelf's brand power

D&L's Gro Strong hair-food line (Anti-Dryness/Anti-Breakage) is the branded benchmark in exactly the tub segment the wedge targets, backed by the relaxer-era equity and Godrej's African distribution machine. Its exposure: the hair-food format is petroleum-first, function-first — the herbal-oil story (amla as an ingredient with a name, a tree, a century) is a differentiation D&L's portfolio does not naturally tell. Priced [!] at the browse; fought on story and small-pack accessibility, never on tub-for-tub price war with Godrej's economics.

3.2 Darling Zambia — the landlord, third file running

"Zambia's biggest hair extension brand", physically based in Lusaka, Godrej-owned: the install economy's landlord, again adjacent to (not occupying) the scalp-care layer. The SA file's braid-column logic transposes with one Zambian upgrade — here the braider B2B channel is DEFINED as the wedge's second lane from day one, not a later-wave pilot, because the informal salon economy's share of category throughput is structurally larger. The race risk recurs (Godrej could bundle care SKUs with Darling installs), and the same kill-switch stands: if outflanked, become the column's best ingredient supplier rather than its brand owner.

3.3 Vaseline Blue Seal — the multi-purpose incumbent

Blue Seal ships into Zambia as the multi-purpose jelly that serves skin AND scalp — the true volume incumbent of the tub economy, unpriced pending the browse (the K89.99-class artifact suggests the 400ml band [!]). The lesson from every prior market holds: never attack the floor; sell the SPECIFIC (a hair oil formulated for hair, with an ingredient story) above the GENERAL at an honest premium.

3.4 The kantemba unbrandeds — the real competitive mass

Loose pomades, repackaged jellies, unlabelled oils: the informal shelf's own economy, price-led, quality-variable. Not an enemy — a demand proof and a quality opening. The counterfeit adjacency (regional evidence only [!]) argues for tamper-evident small packs and authenticity cues sized for a K5–K20 purchase, not for premium serialisation theatre.

The channel doctrine in one paragraph

The wedge's channel order inverts the batch's modern-trade habit: wholesale-to-kantemba LEADS because that is where 70% of the economy shops daily, Shoprite anchors the formal proof and the flyer culture, and the braider B2B lane runs parallel from day one rather than waiting its turn — three lanes filled BEFORE the radio speaks, because a presenter's read that sends listeners to empty shelves spends the unrepeatable first impression on a stock-out. The pharmacy tier waits for R5; the secondary chains mirror in wave 2; and Kamwala is handled with intelligence-first respect — if the check finds grey stock, its shopkeepers become the formalisation conversation's first invitees, not its casualties. Distribution precedes voice: the doctrine in three words, and the wave design's whole geometry.

Reading the ladder against the street

A worked example, in the Mauritian style. Suppose the kantemba census finds loose oil selling at K8–12 per splash-equivalent and the cheapest branded hair-food small format at K18–25. The R1 sachet at K10 then sits exactly where it must: above the loose oil's quality doubt, below the branded tub's commitment, at the note that changes hands. If the census instead finds the informal floor higher (K15-class splashes), the whole ladder shifts up a rung and the R2 bottle gains headroom; if lower (K5-class), the sachet leans on its tamper-evidence and name as the premium-of-pennies. As with Mauritius: the ladder is relationships wearing provisional numbers — floor respect, note-psychology, honest curves between rungs — and the census corrects the numbers without touching the relationships. What the ladder never does, in any census outcome: undercut the loose economy to death (the informal trade is the distribution system's own bloodstream) or price the bottle past the flyer's respect.

The sachet question, answered with more care than usual

Every African file in this programme has said "sachet economics apply"; this deck owes the phrase its arithmetic. A K10 sachet at kantemba margins returns roughly K1–1.5 of contribution per unit at the authored stack — thin enough that the sachet tier only works as a SYSTEM: high-velocity replenishment through wholesaler routes that already visit the stall weekly, print runs long enough to amortise tooling, and the sachet's real job understood as TRIAL AND REACH rather than profit (the 100ml bottle is where converted triers land, and its margin carries the pair). The design consequences: sachets launch WITH the bottle, never alone; the wholesale terms bundle them so the stall stocks both; and the sachet's face carries the same tree and name as the bottle at one-tenth the size, because the K10 buyer and the K42 buyer are the same woman in different weeks of the month. The kantemba census verifies the price points; the system stands regardless of where exactly they land.

C11 · The flag-moves

DEEP THINK — the transfer bet, stress-tested. The wedge assumes Amla's pan-African equity survives a border it has never crossed. Three failure shapes, each with a plan. SHAPE ONE: zero recognition — the brand lands as a true unknown. Then the radio register does the work introductions always did in this country, and the entry economics were priced for it (the launch plan buys awareness, not reminder). SHAPE TWO: recognition without preference — consumers know the name from travel/media but default to habit. Then the sachet tier is the converter: a K10 trial is the cheapest preference experiment ever run, and the braider channel supplies social proof at the chair. SHAPE THREE: the grey market already answered — Kamwala stock proves pull AND sets a street price the formal entry must respect (the Mauritius grey-ceiling logic, miniature). The check distinguishes the shapes BEFORE the container manifests; the plan flexes by shape rather than praying for one. Kill-test: if two quarters of full distribution + radio + chairs produce velocity below the authored floor, the wedge thesis itself — not the execution — goes under review, and the country sequencing (oral riding hair) re-times honestly.
DEEP STRAT — the braider programme, designed as infrastructure. (1) FIND: the rep network maps braider clusters via wholesalers' existing routes (they already know every market's chairs); (2) TERMS: R4 bulk packs priced so per-use beats the current tub, with a starter kit (bulk oil + client-size minis for resale at the chair — the braider becomes a micro-retailer, margin hers); (3) VOICE: WhatsApp groups seeded with usage content by braiders, for braiders — the brand supplies product and facts, never scripts; (4) PROOF: chair-visible results are the category's oldest medium — a client's scalp under fresh braids is the ad; (5) SCALE: the programme's census (chairs served, reorder cadence) becomes the informal economy's best proxy dataset — market research the formal sector cannot buy, gathered by selling. Works if: wholesaler routes reach the chairs and the arithmetic genuinely beats the tub. Fails if: Godrej bundles first (the standing race). Kill-switch: the ingredient-supplier pivot, third market running — and this time with a Lusaka-based rival whose bundling decision is one meeting away, which is why the programme launches in wave 1, not as a later experiment.

The window and the wedge, timed together

The macro's window argument lands hardest on this deck: the kwacha's strength prices the import stack today; the launch calendar (W0 in the rains, launch in the dry season) means the wedge's first full trading year runs inside the window's most probable duration; and the two-quarter review gate falls before any plausible copper-cycle reversal could force a repricing. The deck is, deliberately, a fair-weather launch with a foul-weather plan: honest sizes while the window holds, a pre-priced sachet-lean pivot if it closes, and the re-timing minute as the honest exit if it slams. Speed here is not appetite — it is the arithmetic of entering during the good year the country just paid four hard years to earn.

C16 · What does not transplant

Mauritius's fairness launch stays home (no grey ceiling — unless Kamwala writes one); SA's amla claim-back stays home (no genericisation to fight — the ingredient arrives unknown rather than unowned); the GCC's everything stays home. What arrives: the SA braid-column logic UPGRADED to wave-1 status; the Mauritius authored-ladder protocol at full stretch; the batch's small-pack law at its deepest application; and the programme's oldest lesson in its plainest form — the habit is the asset, the brand is the guest, and the guest earns the house by speaking its languages, literally: this is the first deck in forty-two countries whose launch lives or dies on radio copy in languages the founding documents never named. The panel is not a compliance step here; it is the launch team.

What this deck teaches the batch

Three exports. The RADIO-LAUNCH skeleton (T11b) — the sentence, the presenter-read format, the copy gate, the test-station rehearsal — becomes the template for every low-connectivity market remaining in the programme, with Mozambique and Angola's files (radio-heavy, Portuguese-language) adapting its structure to their panels. And the DISTRIBUTION-PRECEDES-VOICE doctrine, obvious in retrospect, gets its first explicit statement here because this is the first market where the launch medium's power (the presenter's trusted read) is strong enough to be dangerous if the shelves aren't ready for it.
