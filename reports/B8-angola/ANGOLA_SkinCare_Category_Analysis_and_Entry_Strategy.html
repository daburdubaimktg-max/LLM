C5 · The private law — T15b-6 written in full

Seven clauses, binding on every SKU, copy line, shelf negotiation and influencer brief in this country, effective before the first container and independent of any regulatory change in either direction. (1) No lightening, brightening, whitening, tone-evening or "glow" claim, in any language, on any Dabur skin product sold in Angola — including soft constructions; the panel's barred list opens with iluminador and clareador and extends by panel authority. (2) No shelf, bundle, display or e-commerce category adjacency with the lane: placement briefs specify DISTANCE, and the trade terms say so in writing. (3) No ingredient winking — no niacinamide-tier actives marketed at even-tone effect under cosmetic vocabulary. (4) The census wears gloves: the W0 lane-map documents vocabulary, prices and placements for avoidance only, photographs no vendor, names no stall-holder, and stores nothing that could make a person at a stall into a strategy's data point — the Zambia protocol, verbatim. (5) Influencer and creator briefs carry the law as a contract clause, because the register risk here lives in commissioned content more than packs. (6) The law survives evidence of commercial cost: no quarterly review may reopen it on revenue grounds; it is reviewable only for STRENGTHENING. (7) If Angola ever regulates, Dabur complies with the stricter of the two texts. One paragraph of reasoning, once: in five markets the programme argued this fence from law, health, or both; here the only available argument is the oldest one — that a brand whose home market taught it the cost of tone politics does not need a regulator to know where its products end.
