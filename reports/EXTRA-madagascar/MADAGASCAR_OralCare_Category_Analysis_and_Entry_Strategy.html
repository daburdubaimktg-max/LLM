C1 · The finding: MEMORY-SHAPED

South Sudan's oral file (#199) taught the programme that a heritage claim has an OWNER, and that a border can strand a true story in someone else's culture-space. Madagascar presents the grammar's third configuration, and the friendliest yet: the heritage is the market's own, it was abandoned within living memory, and the market's own scientific literature blames the abandonment for a public-health catastrophe. The documented chain: press-reported Ministry of Health figures put caries at ~90% of under-6s (2–3 carious teeth already) and ~90% of under-15s (CAPTURED — santetropicale/APIDPM, vintage unshown → G8); and a JOMOS-published study of Malagasy schoolchildren links the rise explicitly to abandonment of the traditional chew-stick (brindille), inadequate brushing technique and frequency, and the sugar-shifted diet (CAPTURED — jomos.org, 2006). The market, in other words, holds a MEMORY of an herbal oral practice — grandmother-aged, not ancient — and a scientific record that says the memory was healthier than the present.

The fifty-first corridor finding, stated for the register: where a market abandoned its own herbal tradition within living memory AND its own literature mourns the abandonment, the herbal entrant's positioning is REVIVAL — "the tradition, kept" — and the ownership check passes by inheritance: the story being revived is the customer's own. This is the configuration Meswak/Dabur Herb'l was designed for, and it differs from every prior oral file: in South Sudan the stick's homeland was across a border (severed — national register shut); in CAR there was no documented stick tradition at all; in the Gulf batches the miswak is a living devotional practice (a register to respect, not revive). Here the brindille is neither foreign, nor absent, nor sacred — it is a LAPSED HABIT with a scientific eulogy, and a tube that says "what the brindille knew, in a form school mornings allow" speaks WITH the literature, the grandmothers, and the Ministry's own alarm at once. The fluoride law then draws the hard line this warm story must never cross: the revival register celebrates the herbal tradition; it NEVER disparages fluoride, the toothbrush, or Colgate — because the same literature that mourns the brindille also blames poor brushing, and an entrant that undermines brushing to sell herbs would be arguing against the country's dentists with the country's dead teeth.

C4b · The lane's economics of inheritance — what riding the rails is worth

Oral is the first deck to cash the set-inheritance cheques the hair file wrote, and the cashing deserves numbers rather than sentiment. The corridor's fixed costs — the AMM establishment licence, the BSC machinery, the Tsaralalàna signing, the Malagasy voice, the audit — were paid once by the hair lane; oral's incremental cost of entry is a purchase order and a print run. Concretely: the shared container means oral's freight line is marginal (pallets in space already booked); the shared house means no second negotiation, only an annex to terms that already name the lane; the shared voice means the revival campaign's production cost is one photo shoot and two reviewer-signed lines; and the shared audit means ★1 and the floor question cost one enumerator-hour inside a walk already funded. The deck's honest estimate: oral reaches its first shelf for well under a tenth of what a standalone oral entry would price, and the two-cycle read that governs its family-tube decision runs on the same ledger infrastructure hair already operates. The strategic consequence is sequencing discipline rather than haste: BECAUSE the lane is nearly free to open, the only real cost of waiting one extra cycle is time — so the plan waits for hair's corridor to demonstrate one clean cycle before oral's first carton, buying certainty with the cheapest currency the set owns. Marginal-cost entries are the reward for building sets instead of launches; this page is what that reward looks like in ariary.

C7b · The bread test — the mini's real arithmetic

The épicerie-till scene deserves its arithmetic spelled out, because it is the deck's actual demand model. A household at the 300,000 Ar wage anchor spends its till coins on bread, tea, oil, soap — and the mini-tube's ~1,550 Ar asks for roughly a day's bread money once a month. The question that decides the oral lane is not "will Madagascar buy herbal toothpaste?" but "in which weeks does the till allow a toothpaste purchase at all, and is 1,550 Ar inside that window?" The audit's till-watching hour answers it empirically, and the format ladder is designed around the answer's likely shape: the mini at the bread-day threshold for trial and tight months; the family tube at ~5,000 Ar for the fortnights after payday and the crop-cheque weeks (the macro's vanilla/clove calendar applies to oral exactly as to hair — provisioning follows the harvest pulse in SAVA and Analanjirofo). One more consequence falls out of the arithmetic: the sachet-vs-tube question that decided the hair lane's format war is NOT symmetric here. Hair oil's loose-pour competitor forced the sachet; toothpaste's competitor is non-consumption, which the mini fights better than a single-use sachet because a tube on the sink builds the daily habit the whole category needs. Format follows the competitor — a rule worth one sentence in the instrument stack's margins.

C12b · The second-order board — reactions priced before launch

Colgate: the mini-at-the-till play targets non-consumption, a segment the incumbent's listed formats structurally ignore; its rational response is nothing, its possible response is a Tsaralalàna trade squeeze, and the house's own two-supplier interest blunts that lever — priced, not feared. C13b · The docking protocol — if a national programme exists

G8's answer changes the deck's public-health posture, so both futures are pre-written. If the MoH/WHO pull surfaces a national oral-health programme (school brushing schemes, fluoride policy, dental days), the protocol is: the brand's school-adjacent presence is LIMITED to unbranded donated brushes routed through the programme's own machinery, with Dabur's name on the invoice and nowhere else; no branded curriculum, no logo'd murals, no sponsored screening days — the reputational asymmetry (a branded health programme in a country with 90% pediatric caries invites the "profiting from disease" reading at the first journalistic touch) outweighs any awareness gain. If NO programme exists, the brand does not build one: a toothpaste entrant constructing national dental policy is the harvest-of-system-failure error in a different costume (the OTC shadow map's ethics clause, translated). The lane's public-health contribution is then the humblest possible: an affordable tube, a brush in every frame, and the page's brushing-normalisation content in Malagasy — demand-side norm support at the speed the household, not the ministry, chooses. Both futures cost almost nothing; both are auditable; and the bet's clause 6 makes the chosen one verifiable.

C15b · The listening question — one sentence the instruments cannot ask

The set's standing practice: each deck names the one question that listens rather than measures. Hair asked the braiding mother what would make her switch. Oral's is asked at the épicerie till, of the mother who did NOT buy toothpaste this week: "what would have to be true for the tube to make it into the basket?" — not "why didn't you buy" (which extracts an apology, not a fact) but a question that lets her design the offer. The likely answers — a smaller size, a payday week, the child asking for it, the neighbour having it — are each a different strategy, and the deck deliberately does not guess among them: the mini bets on size, the calendar bets on payday, the school card bets on the child, and the épicerie's own shelf bets on the neighbour. Four small bets, one question, and the reorder ledger will say which one the market voted for. The instruments measure what happens; that one question hears why.

DEEP THINK · Three objections answered

Objection 1 — "Reviving an abandoned habit is education spend by another name — the hair deck just told us education budgets lose." Answer: the two decks describe two different jobs and the distinction is the finding pair's whole point. Hair joins a ritual that RUNS (zero education, spend on availability); oral revives a habit whose INFRASTRUCTURE survived even though the practice lapsed — the memory is alive (grandmother-aged), the motivation is alive (every mother knows the teeth are failing), and the literature is alive (the market's own scientists wrote the sales argument in 2006). Revival copy therefore does not TEACH anything; it gives permission to return to something the audience already believes was better. That is transcription's cousin, not education's — and the LEAP cards' scores reflect it. Where true education WOULD be needed — building a brushing norm from nothing — the deck explicitly assigns the job to the public-health system and takes only a supporting role (the brush in frame, the donated-brushes protocol). The brand never pays for what only a ministry can build.

Objection 2 — "10–15% under Colgate is a knife-fight with the category's owner." Answer: it is a LADDER position, not a fight, and three design choices keep it one. The copy never names or references the incumbent (the no-argument law covers competitors in spirit); the herbal register differentiates on KIND, not on degree ("the tradition, kept" vs "more paste for less"); and the mini-tube leads, which competes with NON-CONSUMPTION — the households the caries statistics actually describe — rather than with Colgate's loyal base.
