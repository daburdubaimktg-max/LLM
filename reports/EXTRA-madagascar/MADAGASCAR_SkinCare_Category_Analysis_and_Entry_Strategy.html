C1 · The finding: OWNERSHIP-TRANSFERRED

The ownership check (minted in South Sudan's oral file, #199) sorts a heritage claim into classes: the market's own, a community's private inheritance, a neighbour's culture-space, or contested. Madagascar's skin category forces a class the check had not yet needed, and it is the most delicate the programme has recorded. Masonjoany — the ground-wood face paste worn by Sakalava and Vezo women on the north and west coasts, functioning as sunscreen, insect repellent, skin treatment and decoration (yellow from tabàky/Coptosperma madagascariense; white from aviavy/Ficus grevei) — was, per the captured record, introduced by Indian traders and then indigenised over ~150 years with endemic woods, ritual collective grinding on stone, and mother-to-daughter transmission (CAPTURED — Wikipedia "Masonjoany"; theamerican.mg; Peace Corps). For a Gujarati-rooted trade community (the Karana) and an Indian ayurvedic entrant, this looks at first like the programme's dream: an authentic 150-year Indian-beauty bridgehead, documented, still worn daily.

The fifty-third corridor finding, stated for the register: a heritage that an entrant's culture GAVE a market generations ago, and that the market made wholly its own, is OWNERSHIP-TRANSFERRED — the gift belongs to the receiver now, and the giver's descendant has no more right to repossess it than a stranger. The bridge may be spoken as shared history in private; it may NEVER be claimed as license in public. This is the mirror of South Sudan's SEVERED case and the completion of the check's geography: there, a true story sat in a neighbour's culture-space (register shut because it was never the market's); here, a true story that ORIGINATED in the entrant's culture-space has MOVED permanently into the market's (register shut because it is no longer the entrant's). The seduction is precisely the danger — "we brought you masonjoany, let us modernise it for you" is the single most tempting and most disqualifying sentence the set could utter. The coast's women grind tabàky on stone as their mothers did; a Mumbai-made "masonjoany-inspired" tube that repossesses their gift would be read exactly as what it is, and the 150-year bridge would become a 150-year debt called in.

The verdict, consistent with the macro's ENTER-LOW discipline and the set's three prior skin holds: skin ships essentially NOTHING that touches masonjoany at seed. One generic functional SKU is held and priced (a dry-season moisturiser, the neutral band the whole set has used); masonjoany is approached, if ever, ONLY through the guest-gardener's front door — a formal sourcing/benefit-sharing conversation with the coastal cooperatives and the tradipraticien-adjacent keepers, on the Compagnie-du-Miel model, governed by the Nagoya question (G12), and never as a repossession dressed in R&D. The blind fence handles lightening in parallel. Two disciplines, one closed category, and a set that ends — as it began — by taking less than it could. That closing symmetry is deliberate: the Seychelles set closed on a prune, South Sudan on a both-ways fence, and Madagascar closes on a returned gift — three different disciplines of restraint, each derived from its own market rather than transplanted, which is the East wave's through-line and the instrument system's proof that restraint is a measured verdict, not a house habit.

With TRANSFERRED added, the ownership check now covers every heritage situation the queue has met — five classes, each with a register verdict that follows from the class, not from creative appetite. The instrument's whole value is that it answers the question BEFORE the brief: a heritage a brand may love is not therefore a heritage a brand may use, and the class tells the copy desk which love is licensed. The five classes are worth stating as a single rule for the programme: the register a brand may use is decided by WHERE a tradition lives and HOW it got there, never by how much the brand admires it or how well it would sell. Admiration and commercial fit are the two forces that push a brief toward heritage misuse, and the check exists precisely to overrule both with a question about ownership. Burundi inherits the completed five-class map.

C7b · Why NEIGHBOUR-FLOWERED is a hold, not a lane

The rose instrument's tenth reading deserves one paragraph of its own reasoning, because the temptation to enter is real. Madagascar HAS a floral-water culture — the distilleries produce geranium, neroli, ylang and basil hydrolats at export scale, so a consumer accustomed to floral waters exists. The naive read says "floral-water habit present → Gulabari has a market". The instrument corrects it: the storehouse is present but STOCKED WITH SOMEONE ELSE'S FLOWERS, and rose specifically has no captured local constituency. Gulabari would arrive not as a familiar category filling a gap but as a foreign flower asking a room full of geranium and ylang to make space — competing against locally-distilled hydrolats that carry both a price advantage (no 24% import wall) and a provenance advantage (the distilleries are a national pride). The geometry's verdict is HOLD, and the reasoning generalises: a present category habit is a green light only when the SPECIFIC product shares the habit's vocabulary; a habit for floral waters is not a habit for ROSE water any more than a habit for tea is a habit for a specific estate's leaf. The hold is cheap (Gulabari simply does not ship) and the door is not bolted — if a floral play ever matters, it buys from the distilleries' own flowers rather than importing rose past them, which is the guest-gardener discipline applied to the floral bed.

The two-fence governance stack — as built
