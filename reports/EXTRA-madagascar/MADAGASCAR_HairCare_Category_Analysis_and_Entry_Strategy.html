C1 · The finding: RITUAL-UNBRANDED

Most hair markets in this programme required Dabur to fight for a slot in someone's routine — against Movit's owned mass tier in Kampala's shadow, against Darling's braid empire in West Africa, against premium salon gates in the Gulf. Madagascar presents the opposite and rarer configuration: the routine already exists, is nationally distinctive, and no brand owns any step of it. The documented traditional sequence — wet the hair, detangle, seal with coconut oil, braid (CAPTURED — naturalsobsessed 2011; nanoil "Hairs of the World") — is precisely the use-case Vatika Enriched Coconut Hair Oil was built for. Around it sits a hair culture of unusual depth: braiding as near-universal female practice transmitted mother-to-daughter; named signature styles (tanavoho — the Sakalava women's long tight braids wrapped circularly around the head, worn for weddings and ceremonies, a symbol of femininity; kipetaka — Betsileo; both [check]); a long-hair beauty ideal strong enough that "why do Madagascar women have among the longest hair in Africa" is a whole social-video genre (CAPTURED — hairstylesoftheworld 2016; TikTok/YouTube discover pages); and prestige traditional alternatives (baobab-seed oil, moringa oil) that confirm the oil slot is a QUALITY ladder, not a commodity one.

The fiftieth corridor finding, stated for the register: where a category's core ritual is (a) universal, (b) named, and (c) brandless, the entry's demand engine is the ritual itself — the work is packaging, pricing and distribution, and the education budget is zero. The habit-match teardown below grades all three conditions on captured evidence. This is the cheapest demand configuration the programme has recorded: cheaper than the Seychelles' parcel proof (which proved demand for a BRAND), cheaper than Juba's corner (which bought an information position). Its dangers are correspondingly specific: a ritual is owned by its practitioners even when no brand owns it — so the copy must join the ritual as a servant, never claim to improve or correct it; and a dual-texture nation (Highlands wavy-straight, coastal tightly-textured — INDEX-DERIVED) means the ritual wears two faces that the brand must serve equally without ever mapping them politically (the macro's casting rule, operationalised in T9d).

C3b · The dual-texture map — served equally, mapped never politically

The macro's INDEX-DERIVED axis deserves its own careful paragraph, because it shapes the portfolio while being forbidden to shape the marketing. Madagascar's population sits on an Austronesian–African spectrum: Highlands communities (Merina, Betsileo) trend wavy-to-straight; coastal communities trend tightly textured. For FORMULATION this matters mechanically — a sealing oil serves both worlds (oil is the ritual's universal step), but the eventual second wave splits: leave-in and curl-definition formats serve textured hair; light-oil and anti-breakage formats serve the straighter Highlands types. For MARKETING the axis is untouchable: it correlates with the Merina/côtier political fault line, the oldest and most sensitive in the country's politics, and any brand asset that codes texture by region, class or aspiration has chosen a side in a conversation no entrant survives. The deck's resolution is the casting rule (T13b) plus a portfolio principle: wave 1 ships only the universal step (oil), which needs no texture segmentation at all — the dual-texture question is deferred until the brand has earned the right to answer it, and even then it is answered in formats, not in faces. The census question stays open honestly: the audit's regional reorder patterns will show where each format family belongs, and that evidence replaces the ethnographic inference this paragraph is built on. An entry that begins at the one step both worlds share enters the whole nation at once — that, and not convenience, is why oil leads.

C6b · The trader-shopper conversations — three sentences the audit buys cheap

The set's standing practice of naming the cheapest evidence carries here as three short conversations the W0 audit conducts for the price of tea. C10b · The race clock — armed on Parachute

The race-clock discipline (Seychelles, #194) requires naming the competitor whose arrival would close the window, estimating their trigger, and pre-writing the response. Here the name is obvious: Marico's Parachute, the world's dominant branded coconut hair oil, already at scale across East Africa and the Gulf, with exactly the same RAK-to-Indian-Ocean logistics available to it. No Parachute presence in Madagascar was captured this session (absence unverified — the audit's stand photographs answer, ★2's sibling question), and the structural read is that Madagascar's 24% wall, thin modern trade and 403-walled invisibility keep it below Marico's radar exactly as long as the category stays unbranded. Dabur's entry itself is therefore the likeliest trigger: a visible Vatika launch tells the category's other conglomerate that the slot pays. The pre-written response has three clauses. First, the founding-claim discipline: whoever brands the ritual FIRST owns the transcription — «volo lava, volo matanjaka» class copy, the Analakely strips, the épicerie penetration ledger are worth more than any counter-campaign, so the plan front-loads distribution breadth over margin in the first two cycles. Second, no panic pricing: if Parachute lands, the ladder answers on evidence (their entry price becomes a bracket row, not a fire alarm), and the enrichment story (amla, henna, the herbal register) is the moat oil-purity claims cannot cross. Third, the window is not infinite and the file says so: every quarter of pre-launch deliberation is a quarter of founding-claim value spent — the bet's clause 6 makes the discipline auditable in both directions.

C17b · The channel economics of zero education — where the saved budget goes

Finding #50's "education budget zero" line has a constructive half the board should see priced.
