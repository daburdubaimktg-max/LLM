M1 · Thesis — the kin-keyed gate, entered low, fronted local

Madagascar is the Africa queue's strangest combination: a bottom-of-pyramid, sachet-economy, ariary-fragile market (GDP/capita US$453, 2024 — CAPTURED, World Bank/TradingEconomics; ~75% below the poverty line, 2022 WB estimate) that nonetheless offers Dabur the structurally easiest route to market in the entire programme. The reason sits in one word: the Karana — an Indo-Malagasy trading community of Gujarati (Kathiawar) origin, ~20,000–25,000 people, arrived by dhow via Mahajanga from the 1860s–1880s, which today owns the import-wholesale layer outright. Antananarivo's trading quarter Tsaralalàna — groceries, droguerie, cosmetics — is "almost all run by Karana" (Petit Futé; EchoGéo, CAPTURED). The community's top houses are national-scale: Sipromad (Akbaraly family, ~US$350M revenue) owns Madaphar, the country's largest pharmaceutical distributor; Axian (Hiridjee family, in Madagascar since 1866, ~US$3bn revenue) owns Telma telecom, MVola mobile money and BNI bank (Forbes 2016; Wikipedia — CAPTURED). For an Indian herbal house shipping from RAK-UAE down the Dubai–Mombasa–Toamasina lane — the exact lane these houses already use — the gate is not merely open; it is family-shaped.

The forty-ninth corridor finding, stated for the register: KIN-KEYED — when the market's gate is kin to the entrant, the key turns easily but the door is watched. The Karana's dominance is real and so is its fragility: many remain stateless after four to five generations; the community periodically faces kidnapping-for-ransom waves and resentment politics; and the country is nine months out from a military coup (CAPSAT, 11–14 Oct 2025; Rajoelina fled; Col. Michael Randrianirina interim president; transition ongoing — CAPTURED, France24/UN News/Wikipedia). An entry that reads as "an Indian company arriving through the Indian merchants" is commercially optimal and politically legible in exactly the wrong way during a nationalist transition. The thesis therefore has three clauses: (1) ENTER — this is a real entry market, not a watch (the first since the Seychelles); (2) LOW — sachet-economy formats priced against a 300,000 Ar (~US$65)/month minimum wage; (3) FRONTED LOCAL — the Karana house is the importer and wholesaler, but the route-to-market face (merchandising, field team, brand voice) is Malagasy, in Malagasy and French, with the Karana relationship structured as plumbing, never as positioning. The kill-switch is political, dated to the transition's election calendar — the #1 macro watch-item.

Species classification: SEED-ABSENT, KIN-GATED — the estate taxonomy's twelfth configuration. No Dabur distributor, subsidiary or listing was found (CAPTURED-ABSENCE, multiple null searches EN+FR, 7 Jul 2026; residual chance of informal Karana-imported Vatika on Tsaralalàna shelves → G1, the audit's first question). The nearest Dabur infrastructure is Egypt, Nigeria (African Consumer Care) and the 2016 Discaria Trading acquisition in South Africa; the practical hub is RAK-UAE. Unlike South Sudan there is no own rail one corridor away — but there is something the programme has never had: a wholesale aristocracy that speaks Gujarati, prays in Khoja and Bohra mosques, and has moved Indian-Ocean goods for 150 years. The affinity-alignment map logs its strongest ALIGNED cell yet — with the political caveat welded to it.

M5b · The wallet's calendar — vanilla, cloves, and the rural cash pulse

A macro fact no deck may ignore: rural purchasing power in Madagascar is not a flat line but a PULSE, and its clock is the export-crop calendar. Vanilla is the island's signature — ~75–85% of world supply, US$389M exported in 2023 (63.8% of global vanilla exports, the #2 national export), contributing ~62% of agricultural-sector income (CAPTURED — OEC/capmad/apl vanilla). Cloves add US$120M (2021, 21,164t) rising to $224M (2022), with Madagascar the world's #1–2 exporter and ~50% of world clove-OIL supply besides (CAPTURED — OEC; Madagascar Matin). The SAVA region (vanilla, north-east) and Analanjirofo (cloves) therefore experience FMCG demand spikes when the crop cheques land — the campaign seasons put real money into smallholder hands on a schedule the trade knows by heart (read-through INDEX-DERIVED). For the entry this yields three planning facts. First, the épicerie tier in the crop belts is seasonally RICH — sachet volumes there should be provisioned against the harvest calendar, not divided evenly across quarters. Second, the wholesale houses already run this rhythm; the Tsaralalàna conversation should ask for the seasonal order curve as a free datum (the Kampala-question discipline travels — one more question, zero new invoices). Third, the essential-oil economy (ylang-ylang ~20–25t/yr [!] re-validate, ~25% of the world market; geranium's 1,000+ farmer-producers; 95% of production exported to ~40 countries) means Madagascar's countryside is professionally familiar with the idea that PLANTS ARE VALUE — a herbal house does not need to teach this market that botany pays; it needs to respect that the market already knows, and that its own distilleries, cooperatives and IMRA got there first.

M8b · Demography, language and the street's registers

The consumer nation is young (median ~20; two-thirds under 25), majority-Christian interwoven with ancestral practice (famadihana and the wider razana complex — INDEX-DERIVED), and linguistically DUAL: Malagasy (Austronesian, universal) beside French (official, spoken by ~25% ≈ 8.5M on 2024 francophonie data — CAPTURED). The pack-copy law for the set follows directly: Malagasy leads, French follows, Arabic never — French alone reaches a quarter of the country and skews urban-elite; Malagasy-first copy is both the majority's language and, in the current "Malagasy brands rising" mood, the politically graceful register. The ethnic geography carries marketing consequence and political sensitivity in equal measure: the Highlands (Merina, Betsileo — Austronesian-leaning, straighter hair types) versus the coastal côtiers (more African-leaning, textured hair) make this a genuinely DUAL-TEXTURE hair market in one country (INDEX-DERIVED, flagged for the hair deck's census rather than asserted as segmentation truth), and the Merina/côtier axis is an old political fault line the brand must never touch in imagery choices — the hair deck inherits a casting rule, not just a formulation brief. Dialect matters at the edges (Merina standard in the Highlands; Sakalava along the Mahajanga–Morondava coast where the masonjoany practice and the Muslim minority both concentrate), and the 2–5% Muslim share (official IRF range; community claims of 7–15% recorded and unused) clusters exactly where the Karana entered: Mahajanga, Ambanja, Antsiranana. The entry's language map and its gate map are therefore the same map read twice — the north-west coast is simultaneously the kinship shore, the Sakalava register, and the halal-relevant pocket; and the capital's Analakely stands are where all of it is priced.
