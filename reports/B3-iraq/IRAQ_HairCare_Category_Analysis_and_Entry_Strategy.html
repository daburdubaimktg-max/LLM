THE ASK: Hair is Dabur's strongest right-to-win in Iraq — because the one lane no incumbent owns is the one Dabur already leads regionally: herbal hair oils + the hot-oil treatment ritual (حمام زيت, ḥammām zeit) + henna. The Western majors own efficacy/repair (Head & Shoulders = شعر خالٍ من القشرة بنسبة ١٠٠٪, Pantene = لمعان/shine, L'Oréal = repair), and the Turkish value flood (Elidor, Morfose) owns the mass-shampoo price tier via the land border — but none occupies the oil/treatment/henna space that maps onto genuine Iraqi ritual (oiling between washes, the henna wedding night, black seed). Dabur owns that space: Amla is "the world's largest hair-oil brand" (35M+ consumers, 80+ yrs) and Vatika (فاتيكا) is a ₹1,000-cr International Power Brand whose region-exclusive Hammam Zaith line was engineered for Arab hair/rituals (argan/black-seed/olive-almond) — and whose Vatika-Arabia campaign reached 30M+ viewers. Yet in Iraq specifically, Amla and Vatika are absent from organized retail — they reach the shopper only via GCC cross-border sites and OpenSooq classifieds [!] confirm. So the play is to convert that established Arabic-script equity into a managed Iraqi position, led by Hammam Zeit as a salon-and-social-selling hero and Amla as the "شعر طويل وقوي — long, strong hair" staple — priced value-herbal (~4,400–5,100 IQD, dead-center mass vs Garnier Ultra Doux), federal via Umm Qasr and KRG via the Turkey lane. Category verdict (macro T17): BET / BUILD. One hard claim rule: "reduces hair-fall due to breakage," never "growth/regrowth" (which reads therapeutic).

C1 — The ask (executive summary)

Macro anchor (§M8, §M11): Iraq is a re-activation of established Arabic-script equity, not a cold entry; Amla already imports (~70 shipments to Dec 2025), Vatika is a MENA powerhouse. Hair is ranked BET/BUILD — own the oils/treatment/henna lane, not the mass-shampoo war; served from RAK (no local make); FX + COSQC CoC are the gates.

The play in one screen. Three consumer truths make hair Dabur's best Iraqi category. First, Iraq is an oiling culture — women oil hair between washes (olive, coconut, argan, amla), the حمام زيت hot-oil treatment is a named ritual, and henna is a wedding-and-conditioning staple — so Dabur's herbal-oil equity is native, not imported. Second, Dabur already owns this space regionally but not operationally in Iraq: Amla and Vatika carry deep Arabic-script equity yet sit only in grey/classifieds channels, absent from Miswag/Feel22/Elryan — pure activation whitespace. Third, the competitive set leaves the lane open: H&S/Pantene/L'Oréal fight efficacy/shine/repair and the Turkish brands fight the mass-shampoo price floor, but the oil/hot-oil/henna territory is nearly uncontested branded space. The move is therefore not a shampoo launch but an equity conversion: appoint a compliant distributor; lead with Vatika Hammam Zeit as a salon-and-social-selling hero (the salon is the fattest, most treatment-linked margin tier, and the hot-oil is a demonstrable service) and Amla as the "long, strong hair" oil staple; ride the henna heritage with a PPD-free natural colour; and price value-herbal against Garnier Ultra Doux, not against the premium argan tier. Waves: W1 Hammam Zeit + Amla activation via salon + social-selling/COD; W2 Vatika shampoo/henna range + KRG (Sorani) track; W3 treatment/ serum extensions + occasion (Eid/wedding/Nowruz) grooming. Reputation Bet (C18): by 31 Dec 2028, Vatika Hammam Zeit and Amla are officially listed and reordering across ≥3 governorates + the salon channel, with grey stock <15% of observed shelf — or the equity stays grey and the lane is left for the Turkish flood to fill.
