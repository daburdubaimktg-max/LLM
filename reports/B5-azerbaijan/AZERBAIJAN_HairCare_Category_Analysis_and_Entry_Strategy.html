C1–C2 · Category frame — a shelf that already voted, a band that tore

The Azerbaijani hair market did something no other market in this programme has done: it stocked Dabur without being asked. Twenty-two hair SKUs — twelve Vatika shampoo variants, six masks, henna powders, and four oils including Amla, Amla Gold, Amla Anti-Dandruff and Maha Bhringraj — trade on Umico/Birmarket via three third-party resellers (DR LAILA COSMETICS, REYKHAN BOUTIQUE, AVENA BEAUTY) [birmarket.az/brands/Dabur, captured 7 Jul 2026 [!] stock status and seller registration unverified]. The demand proof is free; the damage is visible in the same capture. Every listing carries a slashed "was" price — Vatika 200ml at 2.94 against 4.12; the argan 400ml at 5.99 against 10.00; Amla Gold at 8.18 against 15.00, a 45% cut. This is a TORN BAND: resellers discount-churning against each other with no principal to hold a floor, teaching the Baku shopper that Dabur is whatever this week's coupon says it is. The macro's trilemma (ratify / reprice / undercut) resolves on this evidence toward REPRICE-BY-TAKEOVER: the official entry must restore a held price architecture, and the grey band's LOWER edge becomes the promotional floor the brand never again crosses, rather than the list price it accidentally taught.

Emami Kesh King — the Ayurvedic ceiling and the cautionary comp. The only other Indian-heritage hair oil on shelf: 31 AZN/300ml, premium-positioned, one SKU deep [birmarket, 7 Jul]. Dabur's grey Amla at less than half Kesh King's per-ml is accidentally positioned as the value Ayurvedic — a position the takeover should KEEP deliberately, because the essence-trend tail (next) shows where volume lives.

The essence tail — the category's actual grammar. The anti-hairfall oil tag runs 23 SKUs: Chinese growth ampoules (Disaar 2.98–6.21 AZN/30g), rosemary-mint trend SKUs (Purc 9.99, NatuCare 15.29, Sadoer 14.85/50ml), Korean premium (La'dor 35.84/100ml), Pakistani Hemani coconut (10.89/400ml), and — the batch's quiet echo — Floresan's Репейник burdock-pepper oil at 6.00 AZN/100ml, the Russian burdock rung reappearing on a Caucasus shelf. It is NOT the Indian pre-wash ritual, and the deck's no-transplant line is drawn exactly here: Amla's Azerbaijani copy sells shine and anti-hairfall in essence grammar; the champi ritual appears nowhere until a local panel says it reads as care rather than curiosity [!] strategic judgement, flagged as such.

The naftalan flank — the local story nobody foreign owns. Fitoton's tar-free Naftalan shampoo (Herba Flora) trades on Azerbaijan's own scalp-therapy heritage — one SKU found, niche-medical today [!]. The teardown files it as the market's dormant heritage asset: not Dabur's to claim (the membership gate would fail — no formulation truth), but a signal that scalp-HEALTH language has local roots the essence trend hasn't touched.

Self-teardown — the company that arrived second. Dabur's Azerbaijan hair position is unique in the programme: brand present, prices public, demand proven, and NOTHING under management — no distributor, no price floor, no AZ-language sentence, no stock guarantee. Strengths: 22 SKUs of free market research; a value-Ayurvedic position already minted; the Turkish supply halo waiting. Weaknesses: the torn band has taught discount-hunting; the reseller stock is likely Russia/Turkey-routed with uncertain labelling compliance [!]; and the takeover must either recruit the existing resellers into an official structure or out-serve them — displacing one's own grey tail without a stock gap is the operation's hardest week.
