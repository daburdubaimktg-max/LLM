M1 — Demography

So what: a young (median 30.1), two-thirds-urban, 37–39M-person market with 92% internet penetration and a diaspora financial artery — the first B4 country where the constraint is not the wallet's existence but its defended occupancy: French and local brands already own every shelf Dabur wants.

Population sources conflict in a way that matters for TAM math: Worldometer extrapolates 38.70M (Apr 2026) while the official HCP census (RGPH, Sept 2024) counted ~36.8M [!] re-validate against HCP.ma — this deck uses the census as the conservative base. Urbanisation 67.9% (~26M urban); median age 30.1; internet penetration 92.2% (35.5M users, ANRT via DataReportal Digital 2026, Oct-2025 data). The consumer geography is a coastal spine: Casablanca-Settat produces 32.2% of GDP, with Rabat-Salé-Kénitra 15.7% and booming Tanger-Tétouan-Al Hoceïma 10.6% — the top three regions hold 58.5% of GDP and the regional gap is widening (HCP regional accounts 2023, publ. Sept 2025). Rural Morocco (~32%) lives at SMAG wages (~2,533 MAD/month from Apr 2026) on hanout-and-souk supply — a separate price-pack problem, not an afterthought.

M3 — Politics & stability

So what: the most institutionally stable market in Batches 2–4: monarchy-anchored continuity, activist competition regulator, World-Cup-2030 investment cycle. The political risks that shaped Yemen/Sudan planning are absent; the live "politics" for Dabur is regulatory (halal mandate, AMMPS transition) and trade-policy (the Turkey FTA punishment).

Constitutional monarchy; the King is Commander of the Faithful (أمير المؤمنين) — religious authority is centralised, Maliki, and moderate, which keeps halal an institutional standard (IMANOR) rather than a street contest. Government is technocratic-coalition (Akhannouch); protests are economic and localised; no expropriation history in FMCG. Two governance facts matter operationally: the Conseil de la Concurrence is genuinely activist (Glovo settlement Jul 2025 — 30% commission cap, exclusivity ban; GMS sector study 2024-25) — distribution agreements will be read; and Western Sahara logistics are workable (Casablanca–Agadir–Laâyoune trucking; Dakhla Atlantique port building) but origin-labelling for EU-linked flows needs care [!] re-validate distributor coverage south of Agadir. India–Morocco relations are warm and trade-oriented (fertiliser/defence axis) — origin India is neutral; origin UAE is positive (CEPA signed); origin Turkey is politically penalised at the tariff line (M5).

M4 — Regulation (the entry gate)

So what: Morocco is the most regulated-formal entry since Saudi Arabia: a two-step cosmetics regime (PCHC establishment licence + per-SKU declaration), a pharmacy-monopoly OTC lane with state-fixed retail prices, and — the market-structure event — a reported halal-certification mandate for all imported cosmetics from 17 Oct 2026. Compliance is a moat here: whoever is certified, registered and on-shelf when the mandate bites inherits share from whoever is not.

The halal mandate deserves board attention as strategy, not compliance. If confirmed, from 17 Oct 2026 every imported cosmetic needs IMANOR- recognised halal certification. M5 — Trade & tariffs (the entry economics)

So what: the origin decision is pre-made by treaty arithmetic: India-direct pays ~30% duty; Turkey is punished at 90% of MFN (to ~2027); RAK-UAE rides GAFTA (plus the signed UAE–Morocco CEPA when in force); Dabur Egypt rides the Agadir Agreement duty-free and already serves Morocco. Every downstream deck's waterfall starts from the RAK-or-Egypt fork — with Egypt holding a freight edge and RAK holding a portfolio-breadth edge.
