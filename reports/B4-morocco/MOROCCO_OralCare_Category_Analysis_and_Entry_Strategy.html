C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: WEDGE — "own the word both ways, and sell to the mouth nobody serves." Morocco's oral category looks walled — Signal is the most-scanned brand (Sagaci Mar 2026 [!]) with a 9.50-to-49-MAD pyramid and two Élu Produit de l'Année 2025 badges; Colgate holds the floor at BIM (15.90). But three facts open the wedge. (1) The market's growth is penetration, not share: Moroccans use ~1 toothpaste tube/year vs the WHO-recommended 6, only 18.5% own a toothbrush, 95% of adults under 35 have caries (MoH via Aujourd'hui le Maroc, 2013 — the canonical figure; no newer national survey) — the biggest competitor is the un-brushed mouth, and the incumbent behaviour it defends with is siwak and water. (2) Dabur already holds the deepest value position in the corridor: Miswak 120g+50g at 36–39 MAD = 0.21–0.23 MAD/g vs Colgate 0.38 and Signal 0.47 — but 100% grey, fake-list discounted, reseller-sold. (3) The word is contested but unowned: Signal and Colgate both field Herbal Miswak tail variants (9.50/12.60 at mymarket.ma) — yet only Dabur has miswak as the brand itself, and nobody at all has touched Morocco's second siwak tradition: swak beldi (السواك البلدي) — walnut bark, the women's tooth-whitening ritual bought at the attar. The wedge: go official through the halal gate, keep the value format as the family workhorse, and open the whitening flank with the story only Morocco can tell — "the whitening your grandmother rubbed on with bark, in a tube, without the burn."

The macro's guardrails apply with force here. Religion is a certificate, not a register — in the Commander-of-the-Faithful's institutional Maliki frame, fatwa-marketing is culturally and regulatorily off-limits; Miswak sells heritage + nature + efficacy, with the Sunnah resonance carried silently by the word itself (Yemen's mosque-mat register would misfire here — the explicit anti-transplant). And the halal mandate (17 Oct 2026, IMANOR) is the forcing event that converts Dabur's grey problem into Dabur's official opportunity: the same customs gate that blocks uncertified consignments purges the grey sellers who currently cheapen the brand (strategist read).

Shopper vs consumer: the hanout buyer purchases toothpaste like a staple — small format, low absolute price (Signal fields a 9.50 MAD 50ml Herbal Miswak exactly there); the GMS family shopper buys grams-per-dirham multipacks; the para shopper buys therapeutic claims at 50–99; the young whitening shopper follows TikTok hacks and buys charcoal. Dabur's Miswak family format (170g at 36–39) serves the second; the missing pieces are the hanout single (≤12 MAD 50ml) and the whitening flanker with the swak-beldi story. The register rule: Darija warmth for family/heritage, French for efficacy supers; the piety resonance stays in the product name, never in the claim — Morocco's institutional-religion frame (macro M3) rewards discretion and punishes exploitation (bespoke-expert judgement).

The anti-transplant register (what is re-derived, not imported): Yemen's oral wedge rode a literal arak heartland and a state-made handover; Morocco's rides under-consumption and an unowned female ritual — different physics entirely. Three explicit flips. First, the religious space inverts: Yemen's deck deployed the mosque-mat channel and the "سنّة وعِلم" register at trade level; Morocco's institutional frame makes that register unusable — the piety content lives ONLY in the brand name and the Ramadan calendar timing, never in copy. Second, the whitening story flips gender and source: B3 markets framed whitening through bridal/khalta economies; Morocco's swak beldi is an everyday female self-care ritual with an attar supply chain — the register is beauty-heritage, not bridal-event. Third, the value law changes denominators: Yemen priced against the qat-note (absolute dirhams per day); Morocco's family shopper reads grams-per-dirham on a monthly staple — hence the 170g platform leads where a Yemen-style coin-price lozenge logic would miss. What DOES carry over intact from B3/B4-hair: the folk register's proof=2 signature, the go-official-through-the-gate governance sequence, and the rule that the certificate is the ticket, never the pitch.

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

The space maps on two axes: price-per-gram and story ownership. Signal owns the trust/distribution centre; Colgate owns the discount floor and the charcoal shelf; Haleon owns therapeutic premium. The open cell is heritage-natural at family value — where the miswak WORD is the brand, the grams are the proof, and the whitening flank speaks swak beldi rather than laboratory charcoal. Both incumbents have conceded this cell by fielding miswak as a 50ml tail SKU: they validate the claim while declining the story. Dabur Miswak's existing corridor position (0.21 MAD/g, the deepest value on the shelf) is already IN the cell — ungoverned. The play is not to create the position but to claim it officially, loudly, and with the second story no multinational can tell: a walnut-bark whitening heritage that belongs to Moroccan women, not to a Salvadora orchard in the Gulf (strategist read).

The Signal asymmetry, stated precisely. Signal's strength and its constraint are the same fact: it is a portfolio brand whose miswak SKU exists to close a flank, not to lead a story. For Unilever to hero Herbal Miswak nationally it would have to promote a 9.50-MAD tail SKU at the expense of its 49-MAD White Now premium ladder — trading up its own shoppers DOWN. Dabur faces no such cannibalisation: miswak IS the ladder, from the 10–12 hanout single to the 39 whitening flanker, so every dirham of miswak media compounds one brand. This is the structural reason the wedge can survive an incumbent response: Signal can match the claim but cannot match the concentration — its economics punish the very story Dabur's economics reward. The same asymmetry played out in reverse for Dabur in Yemen (where Siwak-F's slot-holding forced an internal architecture memo before any external move); in Morocco the fleet is clean — one oral brand, no fratricide, full media concentration (strategist read, opinion).

Three consequences: the wholesale spine's terms matter more than consumer media for the first two years (a 1-point wholesaler margin beat moves more tubes than a TV flight); the 50ml single's job is to EXIST at the counter, because the hanouti will not stock a 37-MAD family tube for a clientele buying 10-MAD units; and out-of-stock is fatal in a channel with no shelf — a hanout that asks twice and gets no Dabur stops asking forever. This is why the playbook routes oral through the same importer, the same wholesalers and the same WhatsApp price-discipline broadcasts as hair: the two categories share one trade relationship, and the hair relaunch's Derb Omar credibility is oral's cheapest asset (strategist read).
