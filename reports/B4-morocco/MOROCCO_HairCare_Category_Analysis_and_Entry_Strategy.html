C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: LEAD — "govern the anarchy, take the loose third slot, bring the hammam home." Three facts make hair Dabur's Morocco spearhead. (1) The demand is already Dabur's and being wasted: Amla and Vatika sell today across Jumia (226 Vatika results), marjanemall and parapharmacies — but at a 5.5× price spread on a single SKU (Amla 100ml at 18 / 25.30 / 35 / 99 MAD, 7 Jul 2026), with fake "was-79" anchors training Morocco that Dabur is a discount-bin brand. No multinational ranges a ghassoul/clay format in Morocco (Garnier's clay SKU is France-only). The strategy writes itself: reset governance behind the 17 Oct 2026 halal gate, then launch the only branded range that speaks the hammam's own language — الغسول، الحمّام، أركان — at Ultra Doux-corridor prices.

The macro's heartland-inversion guardrail governs every word: Morocco invented these rituals. Dabur does not bring argan or clay to Morocco; it brings format, dosing, halal certification and a governed price to rituals the market already performs weekly — and it brings back, with receipts, the hammam oil the Gulf has been buying from Dabur for years. "Selling Moroccan heritage back to Moroccans" is Garnier's exposed flank (Richesse d'Argan sourced from Moroccan co-ops, sold under a French brand) — Dabur's Indian-herbal honesty plus visible co-op sourcing can flank it (strategist read, opinion).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

The space has two occupied poles and one empty seam. Pole 1 — French-natural mass: Ultra Doux owns "douceur" pantry-naturals at 26–55 MAD with co-op argan stories. Pole 2 — clinical/premium: Elseve science ladders, para brands (Klorane/Furterer/Phyto/Ducray), Kérastase at 395. The seam: ritual-native herbal at mass price — the brand that doesn't borrow the hammam as a variant name but IS the hammam system (pre-oil + clay wash + finish oil), halal-certified, honestly dosed, at 26–49 MAD. Nobody holds it: Garnier names ingredients but not rituals; locals hold authenticity without scale or formats; the attar holds the ritual without brands. That seam is Dabur's natural shape — the same position Vatika holds in the Gulf (Hammam Zaith is literally the category) transposed to the ritual's home country (strategist read).

Two occupancy details sharpen the seam's edges. Garnier's pantry ladder (olive, almond, honey, castor, argan) proves the ingredient story sells at mass — but every variant is an ingredient, never an occasion: there is no "hammam routine" architecture anywhere on the Ultra Doux shelf, no pre-wash/wash/finish system, no clay format. And the curl economy proves the opposite end: Moroccan women will follow a multi-step system (the CGM sequence) when someone teaches it — they simply have no mass-priced brand to buy the system from (Mielle imports at 220 MAD fill the gap badly). The seam, precisely stated, is system-at-mass-price: the only player who can brand the pre-oil → clay-wash → finish sequence as one architecture, at 25–69 MAD rungs, with halal certification and honest dosing, is a herbal house with existing oil equity — which in Morocco, today, is Dabur alone.
