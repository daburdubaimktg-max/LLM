C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: FLANK — "the storefront with eight followers." Skin & sun is Morocco's LARGEST cosmetics segment (40.6% of a $1.82–2.04bn market, Grand View 2024 → derived ~$740–830M estimate, flagged) — and this deck's research corrected the macro's own premise twice. Correction 1: Fem is not absent. It is live on Jumia at 33.99 MAD (Crème Dépilatoire à la Rose, 120g, UAE-made), sold by an official "DABUR MAROC" storefront — with eight followers: Dabur's only owned retail presence in the entire Moroccan market is selling its depilatory to an audience of eight, with zero marketing, while the brand's other 100+ listings ride grey resellers. Correction 2: Herbolene is not mispriced — it is mis-formatted. The macro read 45 MAD as "above the Vaseline anchor (29)"; the like-for-like is 225ml vs 50ml — 0.20 MAD/ml vs Vaseline's 0.58, a 65% per-ml discount the shelf never communicates, because the entry-format 50ml rung that would make the comparison visible doesn't exist. The flank, therefore: wake the official storefront (Fem with a halawa-respectful rose story), tell the grams truth (Herbolene value format + a 50ml entry rung at ≤29), hold the glow line absolutely (DMP bans hydroquinone/mercury outright; Vaseline's GlutaGlow proves the compliant glow route; Nivea's 2022 "Le blanc, c'est la pureté" backlash marks the landmine), and park Gulabari behind a toner-format test in the rose heartland. No fortress is attacked: the French dermo tier (250+ MAD) and the beldi pride tier (Nectarome, named-valley provenance) stay unfought.

The Morocco set's closing lesson lands here: across four categories the pattern held — Dabur's Morocco problem is never demand and always governance. Hair had 107 ungoverned listings; oral had five price points and no official store; OTC had a clean cold start; skin has the strangest version — an official storefront that nobody, including apparently Dabur, remembers to run. The flank prices that fact: the cheapest asset in the whole set is a storefront that already exists, already holds the halal-friendly UAE origin, and already sells the right SKU at a defensible price — it lacks only attention (strategist read).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

Three tiers are defended: value-mass (Vaseline's ladder + Nivea's trust), dermo-premium (250+, white coats), and beldi-pride (provenance). The open ground is precisely mapped by the incumbents' own prices: (1) the grams tier below Vaseline's per-ml curve — a 225ml jelly at 0.20/ml with an argan story, IF the shelf learns to read per-ml (the 50ml entry rung teaches it); (2) the 100–250 mid-tier between Nivea's 101 ceiling and dermo's 250 floor — thin, and where a certified herbal glow line could later sit; (3) the ritual-occasion shelf (post-hammam, bridal-prep, winter-Atlas) that no mass brand architectures — Vaseline sells moments of dryness, not moments of ritual; and (4) the depilatory story space — Veet sells function; nobody sells the halawa-respectful, rose-scented, hammam-embedded version of the same ten minutes. Dabur holds live SKUs adjacent to all four. The flank is not a launch; it is a re-presentation of assets already through customs (strategist read).

The review economy is the real battlefield. Morocco's skin shopper buys on verification: Nivea's 4.9★ with dozens of reviews IS its shelf argument, and the Darija trust words — مضمون (guaranteed), مجرّب (tried) — are review-language before they are advertising-language. Dabur's 0-to-1-review counts across every skin SKU mean the brand is currently invisible to the verification habit regardless of price or story. This reframes the flank's first-quarter priorities: before any occasion film or négafa content, the machinery that converts buyers into reviewers (COD follow-up flows, scratch-card review prompts, creator unboxings that seed the first fifty reviews) is the highest-ROI spend in the category. The KPI hierarchy is deliberate: reviews before followers, followers before reach, reach before NPD — because each layer is the next one's proof (strategist read, opinion).
