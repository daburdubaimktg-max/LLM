C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: WEDGE — "charcoal first, clove second, miswak parked; sell the second brushing." Three findings force the shape. First, the herbal white space does not exist: Colgate Herbal is the region's MOST-USED variant (51.88% of actual usage in the Douala proxy thesis), made 500km up the coast at Colgate-Palmolive Cameroun's Douala plant and already promoted on Park'n Shop Brazzaville's own Instagram — Dabur's default herbal positioning would walk into the incumbent's strongest room. The wedge goes where the local evidence points instead: charcoal is the living natural register (Colgate charbon in Cameroon, Aichun Beauty whitening paste live in Pointe-Noire at 3,250 FCFA, SIVOP's DENTYGEL charcoal variant) — so Dabur Herbal Charcoal leads, the same SKU that led Libya but chosen here by different evidence. Second, affordability is the category's wall, certified by the WHO itself: fluoride toothpaste is officially rated UNAFFORDABLE in Congo (7.4 labour-days to buy a year's supply; 27 dentists for 5.7M people; children's untreated caries at 37.6%) — so the entry's second arm is the small-format ladder (35–50ml under 500 FCFA), riding a regional precedent that already exists in oral (DENTYGEL's 50-FCFA sachet; Signal's 375-FCFA 35ml). Third, the campaign writes itself from Brazzaville's own school data: caries prevalence falls from 53% (once-daily brushing) to 12.8% (twice-daily) to ~zero (three times) — «le deuxième brossage» is a habit platform where the commercial interest and the health outcome are the same sentence. Dabur Red waits as the wave-2 ingredient-specific differentiator (clove = genuine white space but zero local education — a story to build, not assume), and Dabur Miswak is PARKED: no Salvadora tradition exists, the name carries zero recognition plus unwanted signalling in a Christian-majority market, and the nearest local "tradition" the search found — bâton de manioc — turned out to be a cassava dish, the programme's best false-friend exhibit yet (strategist read on agent evidence, 7 Jul 2026).

C2 — Market size & growth

Sizing honestly: no Congo-B oral-care market value exists publicly; the deck sizes by burden and structure. The burden is the WHO's own country profile (2022, the strongest single source in this deck): untreated caries in 37.6% of children aged 1–9 and 28.8% of everyone over five; severe periodontal disease in a quarter of adults; 27 dentists in the whole country (0.1 per 10,000 — a quarter of the already-thin African average); no national oral-health policy, no oral-health staff at the ministry, per-capita dental spend of $1.0 a year. In that system, toothpaste IS the oral-health infrastructure — and the WHO rates the fluoride tube officially unaffordable at 7.4 labour-days per person-year. The structure: a mass shelf supplied from Douala (Colgate's regional plant, importing inputs from China, Turkey and India), a value floor held by an AFRICAN maker (SIVOP's DENTYGEL at 251–675 FCFA — the deck's revision of the "Chinese value floor" assumption), a novelty/whitening tier on the classifieds (Aichun at 3,250), and a branded-tube band of 800–2,000 FCFA in Brazzaville per the blog source. Growth logic is therefore not premiumisation but PENETRATION: the second-brushing platform grows the category's volume and its outcomes in the same act, and the small-format ladder converts the WHO's affordability finding from an indictment into a product spec (strategist read).
