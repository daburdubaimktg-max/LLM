C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: BUILD-NARROW — "enter as food, stay off the medicine shelf." The research maps a three-shelf medicine market and all three are wrong for Dabur. The first shelf is the pharmacist guild: 59% of medicine supply moves through private pharmacies fed by two pharmacist-owned wholesalers (Laborex Congo, 233 pharmacies served, and UbiPharm's COO.PHAR.CO — between them the channel IS the profession), at formal prices ENACT documents at up to 7× international reference levels for generics. The second shelf is the street: the bana mangangas hold 28% of national medicine supply (ISS/ENACT 2025 [!] study year), selling a 6-tablet dewormer at 150 FCFA against the pharmacy's 1,250-FCFA branded equivalent — a tier fed from Kinshasa, partly counterfeit, and priced where most of this 51.7%-poverty country actually lives. The third shelf is the state, at 13% and falling over. A medicine entry would mean filing a French-language CTD dossier into a 9–18-month AMM process to reach shelf one, and then being undercut 5–10× by shelf two. The deck refuses all three and builds the fourth shelf: food. Congo publishes no «complément alimentaire» category — food-framed products (honey, lozenge-confectionery, digestive candy) appear to clear as foodstuffs through DGDA customs, which means Park'n Shop, Géant, the boutiques and even the table-top street vendors can all carry them LEGALLY, because food needs no pharmacy licence. That last clause is the thesis's quiet weapon: the 28% informal tier that no pharma brand can touch without counterfeit adjacency is a lane Dabur's food SKUs can enter openly — the sachet doctrine's third application, and the first one that converts the grey market into a legal shelf (strategist read on agent evidence, 8 Jul 2026).

C2 — Market size & growth

Sizing honestly: no OTC market value for Congo-B exists publicly; the deck sizes by spend structure and channel volume. The spend structure: households carry >40% of total health expenditure directly (World Bank via WHO/AFRO ceremony note, Brazzaville, 24 Apr 2023), government health spend sits near 4% of GDP against the WHO's 5% floor, and under half the population is described as having healthcare access [!] re-validate methodology. Universal insurance exists on paper — Law 19-2023 (27 May 2023) created CAMU, four implementing decrees landed 24 March 2024, a 0.5% CAM levy applies to salaries above 500,000 FCFA — but reimbursement was still "projected for 2025" at last public report: the state is collecting before it pays, so cash self-medication remains the operating health system for the 2026–2028 entry window. The channel volume: 59% private / 28% street / 13% public, with the informal tier's price points (150-FCFA dewormer courses) defining where mass-market health money actually clears. Demand is ailment-led — malaria/fever first (39.9% of Moungali caregivers treated child fever with antipyretics; 19.7% still reach for quinine), digestive and respiratory next — and the growth story for a food-lane entrant is not category growth at all but trust substitution: every shopper who buys a sealed 500-FCFA branded product that does what it says is a shopper the 20×-margin counterfeit economy loses (strategist read).

C3 — Consumer & ritual decode

The health consumer here is a triage economist. The documented pathway for a child's fever runs: home remedy first (citronnelle infusion, papaya leaf, the bitter Kongo bololo), then a street or boutique purchase of an antipyretic (39.9%) or quinine (19.7% — a generation behind ACT protocol, itself a finding), then — only when those fail — the CHU, by which point 34.8% of severe-malaria admissions have already been self-treated. Money decides each step: the pharmacy's 1,250-FCFA branded dewormer and the street's 150-FCFA course are the same decision to different wallets. Two registers matter for Dabur. The first is the bitter-equals-medicine register: Kongo bololo means literally "bitter" in Lingala, and bitterness is the taste of efficacy — which is precisely why Hajmola's salty-sour-pungent profile and honey's sweetness must be sold as FOOD pleasure with health adjacency, never as medicine that suspiciously tastes nice. The second is the kimia register — peace/wellbeing, the church blessing «Kimia ezala na bino» — the emotional territory where family health lives, adjacent to but distinct from the nganga/nkisi spiritual-remedy register that a foreign brand must never touch (nkisi carries dual medicinal-spiritual meaning; it is the fifth untouchable ceiling of this programme — salute, never appropriate) (register read on agent evidence; Lingala items flagged for native validation).

C4 — Competitor teardown (Module 1)

Five named players define the OTC landscape — and the most instructive one never filed an import declaration. Shalina Healthcare is the teardown's centre: 40+ years in the DRC, 200–250 SKUs across pain, digestive, skin and dental, 30+ distribution points across DRC cities, India/China-sourced — and its products reach Brazzaville by informal river trade and counterfeit imitation (the 52-carton seizure, including tramadol, valued >100M FCFA). Shalina proves the demand for exactly Dabur's proposition — affordable, Indian-made, everyday health — while demonstrating the channel NOT to use. Laborex Congo (est. 1985, Eurapharma/CFAO, >150 Congolese pharmacist shareholders, a 7,000-m² automated Brazzaville warehouse built 2015, ~4,500 references, 233 pharmacies served from two agencies) and UbiPharm/COO.PHAR.CO (950M FCFA capital, pharmacist-majority, ZI Mpila) are not competitors but the guild's gatekeepers — parapharmacie references in their catalogues are the wave-2 door. CAMEPS (the public purchaser; new Global-Fund-financed 900-m² warehouse inaugurated 26 Dec 2023) defines the public tier Dabur will never sell into wave 1. And the bana mangangas — ex-combatant-staffed, Total-market-clustered, simultaneously prescriber, dispenser and wholesaler — are the volume channel whose PRICE POINTS (not products) Dabur must answer (structural read on agent evidence).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

Six positions map the space. «Le moins cher» (cheapest) is held by the street and unwinnable compliantly. «La marque de confiance pharmacie» (pharmacy-trust) is held by the guild's European-reference brands at 7× prices — winnable only by joining the price structure that feeds the street. «L'indien abordable» (the affordable Indian) is Shalina's — occupied in reputation, vacant in legal supply. «Le naturel de la forêt» (the forest-natural) belongs to Likouala honey and the plant pharmacopoeia — a terroir ceiling to salute, never claim. «Le certifié constant» (the certified constant — sealed, batch-coded, same jar every month, NABL/export-certificate paperwork visible) is EMPTY: no honey brand, no lozenge, no digestive product in the capture holds it. And «le remède de grand-mère en format moderne» (grandmother's remedy, modern format) is half-open — the register exists (honey-lemon for cough is already folk practice) but no brand has packaged it. Dabur's territory is the last two combined: certified constancy carrying familiar remedies in food formats — the honey the household already believes in, with the certificate the market has never seen (strategist read).

C6 — Whitespace map

Crossing need-states against the shelf evidence produces four fundable white spaces and two traps. The white spaces: (1) certified honey — a live 3,600–6,000-FCFA market with zero branded incumbent, where the WHO's own honey-lemon cough guidance gives health adjacency without a claim; (2) throat/cough confectionery — the respiratory need is documented, the pharmacy syrup shelf is priced out of mass reach, and no lozenge brand shows in any capture (Honitus lozenges enter as candy); (3) post-meal digestive candy — Hajmola's lane, no incumbent found, perfectly suited to the 50-FCFA street-legal sachet; and (4) the diaspora health-gift basket — Care242 proves the remittance-for-health behaviour; a sealed Dabur family pack is a natural object for it. The traps: glucose powder (a 3,000-FCFA/500g single Facebook data point suggests a market, but it is commodity-priced and counterfeit-attractive) and multivitamins (Jiji's 10,000-FCFA floor marks a premium-import niche the poverty line cannot widen). Chyawanprash has NO local schema — no tonic-paste tradition surfaced (the bsissa-twin logic that deferred it in Libya applies with more force here) — and Lal Tail waits on evidence: the infant-massage-culture question is a declared source gap, not an assumption (strategist read).
