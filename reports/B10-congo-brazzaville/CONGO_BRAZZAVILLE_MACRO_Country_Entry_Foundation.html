M1 — Demography, geography & the two-city gift

The Republic of the Congo is ~6.1M people arranged as a strategist's simplification: Brazzaville (2.64M city / 2.81M metro) plus Pointe-Noire (1.34M) hold 58.1% of the nation, connected by the Congo-Ocean Railway (CFCO, ~510km) that returned to full operation in 2024 after rehabilitation. Urbanisation is ~72%, and 47.2% of the population is under 18. The rest of the country is thin, poor and infrastructurally dark (rural electricity 12.4%), which is not a tragedy for an entry plan — it is a boundary: Phase 1 IS the two cities, and "national coverage" is a phrase this deck refuses. The consumer is young, urban, Franco-Lingala/Kituba-speaking, cash-and-mobile-money-based, and — decisively for a grooming portfolio — heir to la SAPE, the world's most codified street elegance culture (M9). The two-city concentration also collapses distribution capital: one western port hub (Pointe-Noire, the commercial capital and oil HQ) and one political/consumer capital (Brazzaville) — with the 510km corridor between them being the entire national supply chain that matters (strategist read on agent evidence, 7 Jul 2026).

M2 — Economy, FX & THE PLUMBING FINDING

The FX architecture is a paradox this deck names early: the currency is the calmest in the programme and the payments are among the hardest. The XAF is pegged at €1 = 655.957 (since 1999, French-Treasury-guaranteed) — no spread, no parallel market, no Algeria-style two-price economy; EUR invoicing is mechanically riskless (USD-sourced goods carry only the EUR/USD cross). But the PLUMBING is the constraint: CEMAC's 2018/2019 forex regime requires domiciliation bancaire — an authorized local bank must verify documentary compliance (SGS import declaration, invoices, supplier coordinates) BEFORE any transfer abroad is permitted, and the bank guarantees nothing; export-revenue repatriation rules carry conflicting public readings (70% general vs 35% extractive-minimum [!] re-validate the operative rate for FMCG); banking penetration is 10–12%; and the sovereign is debt-peaked (97.2% of GDP end-2025, arrears accumulating, IMF ECF graduated Mar 2025 with vulnerabilities "elevated," China the largest single creditor). A US legislative push even targets BEAC forex policy as suppressing $45B of regional investment — outside confirmation that the plumbing, not the peg, is the wall. The entry answer repeats the programme's tested shape with a CEMAC accent: prepaid EUR-invoiced terms, the distributor owns the domiciliation file, small-lot cadence, zero XAF receivables — financing beats tariffs, fourth market running (strategist read).

The plumbing doctrine, stated as structure. Each row of T3 maps to a clause of the entry design. Domiciliation bancaire means the transfer approval lives in a Congolese bank's compliance queue — so Dabur never stands in that queue: goods are prepaid in EUR before loading, and the queue becomes the DISTRIBUTOR'S operational problem, priced into his margin the way Libya's LC backlog was priced into the 15% rate. The repatriation ambiguity (70% or 35%, nobody's public documents agree) is not a research failure to apologise for — it is a market signal: where the operative rule cannot be read, the safe architecture is the one that never needs the rule, which is what zero-XAF-receivables means in practice. The 10–12% banking penetration explains the rest of the design: the consumer economy runs on cash and MoMo, so the revenue side of the distributor's book is physical-cash-heavy, his FX conversion runs through the same thin banking layer, and his proven history of paying foreign suppliers on time — checkable through references, not promises — becomes the single most important diligence item in G2. And the debt clock hangs over all of it: a sovereign at 97.2% with accumulating arrears will eventually squeeze import allocation the way every fiscally-cornered state does; small lots and prepaid terms mean that squeeze, when it comes, costs Dabur a pause rather than a balance sheet (strategist read).

Operating inside controlled stability. The political read for an FMCG operator is counter-intuitive: a 95% election with an opposition boycott is, commercially, a LOW-VOLATILITY environment — policy continuity is near-certain, the ministries do not churn, and the rules (such as they are) stay put. The risks are not instability but its specific instruments: the demonstrated willingness to switch off the national internet for a day (which makes any digital-only channel plan a hostage), the fixer economics at administrative interfaces (which make the distributor's relationships the real regulatory capability), and the succession — the one event that could convert controlled stability into contested transition overnight. The design answers are proportionate, not paranoid: media plans that lead with radio and physical POS and treat social as amplification; a distributor whose standing spans both cities' administrations; an asset footprint that consists of inventory and relationships rather than plant; and the G4 standing check that simply asks, before each shipment, whether the political window is open. The 510km separation of political and commercial capitals, unusual in this programme, is handled the same way every structural fact in this deck is handled — by making it the distributor's problem to be good at, and a signing criterion for choosing him (strategist read).

Why the doctrine holds here, argued rather than assumed: at 66% prevalence the lightening shelf is not a niche — it is the skin category's centre of gravity, and walking past it costs real revenue. The deck holds the ban anyway on three grounds. First, the harm is documented LOCALLY, not imported from elsewhere: the Brazzaville clinical series (steroid and hydroquinone injuries — acne, mycoses, paradoxical hyperpigmentation) is exactly the evidence class that made Libya's scandal a safety register, minus only the press cycle. Second, the counterfeit economy already implicates India as a source vector for falsified products (M8) — an Indian brand selling lightening actives into this market would be one lab test away from a reputational fire that burns the whole four-category entry. Third, the commercial loss is smaller than it looks: the natural/health lane (herbal oils, jelly-class occlusives, scalp care) plus the SAPE grooming tier is precisely where an Ayurvedic house differentiates, while the bleaching shelf is a knife-fight among incumbents with no brand trust to win. The ban is policy; the skin deck will build around it (strategist judgement, flagged as doctrine).

No preferential lane — so the format IS the tariff strategy. For the first time since the programme entered Africa's north, there is no GAFTA, no Agadir, no COMESA card to play: the CEMAC CET taxes a Dabur container identically whether it sails from Mundra, Jebel Ali or Alexandria. That flattens the supply-hub question into logistics and invoicing (RAK's consolidation competence and EUR-billing flexibility likely win) and moves the entire economic contest into the stack itself — where the 20–60% IT-fee range is the difference between a viable mass ladder and a premium-only niche. The sachet doctrine is the pre-answer: single-use and small-format SKUs compress the absolute landed cost per unit to street-coin levels (the 8× counterfeit price gap in medicines shows exactly how price-elastic this market's bottom is), carry the brand into Marché-Total wholesale on the Lebanese network's own economics, and leave the full-size formats to the Park'n Shop/Géant tier where the SAPE-adjacent premium consumer absorbs the stack. The batch overlay strengthens the case rather than deciding it: with Cameroon already live on the harmonised CEEAC-CEMAC tariff and Congo's adoption pending, the medium-term picture is one customs area from Kribi to Pointe-Noire — meaning whatever architecture this deck builds should be specified as CEMAC-portable from day one, because Gabon (next in batch) and Cameroon (added to B6) will reuse it (strategist read).

The distributor decision, argued. Regal Group is the first call for reasons that compound: it is the Sindhi-diaspora import-retail template this programme learned to value in Ghana's bank (the Melcom pattern) already operating at Congo scale; it covers all three cities that matter plus its own shelves (Park'n Shop), collapsing the distribution and listing conversations into one; and its brand portfolio proves it already runs the exact FMCG mechanics — import, domiciliation, wholesale, retail — that the plumbing section says are the entry's hard part. The counter-considerations are equally plain: a house that owns retail may under-serve competing retailers (the Géant/Casino and Franprix accounts need their own terms), its wholesale reach into the Lebanese-run open-market tier must be verified rather than assumed (the sachet volumes live there), and single-partner concentration in a succession-risk market argues for contractual portability. Hence the shape: Regal-first soundings with the Lebanese-network wholesale access as a named diligence item, UbiPharm as the parallel OTC track, the whitening-ban clause written into whatever agreement signs, and prepaid EUR terms as the non-negotiable filter that any serious house will accept and any overstretched one will reveal itself against (strategist read).

The SAPE register, handled with the ceiling geometry. La SAPE is the most commercially interesting cultural fact in Central Africa for a grooming portfolio — a century-old, self-organised movement in which meticulous personal care is not vanity but PHILOSOPHY, practised visibly by men whose grooming spend defies their income statistics. It is also exactly the kind of register the programme has learned not to touch directly: like Kamaana's curls, Jabal Akhdar's honey and the hammam's glow, la SAPE belongs to its practitioners, and a foreign brand that costumes itself in sapologie will be read the way every costume is. The workable geometry is the accompaniment position: products that serve the sapeur's standards (hair oils and creams for sculpted styles, skin care for the presentation ritual), creator partnerships in which SAPEURS speak and the brand supplies, and premium formats whose packaging respects the aesthetic without imitating it. The Kinshasa overlay doubles the prize: la SAPE and rumba are JOINT Brazzaville–Kinshasa cultures with a shared Lingala register, so creative built respectfully here pre-amortises the DRC deck's launch — the shared-creative, separate-logistics doctrine this deck names and the batch will reuse. All of it is native-gated: no sapologie-adjacent copy ships before Congolese voices have shaped and approved the register (bespoke-expert judgement; native validation queued).

O-audit synthesis — the greenfield with a warm handshake. The portfolio arrives at zero, as it has in every market since Tunisia, but Congo's zero has a distinctive texture. On the demand side, the audit's assets are cultural rather than commercial: a traditional-medicine fluency that receives herbal positioning as common sense (the OTC deck's ground is pre-tilled), a grooming philosophy (la SAPE) that no incumbent has ever branded to, and an oral/hair/jelly need-set that the climate and the street economy make obvious. On the supply side, the one asset is the handshake: the Regal template means the first partner conversation happens inside a diaspora trust network that has run Congolese FMCG for a generation — a warmer start than Libya's cold-call bench or even Ghana's Melcom option, relative to market size. The audit's constraints are equally clear: the counterfeit economy has already spent India's name (the origin-judo mandate is not optional here), the whitening ban removes the skin category's largest segment before the skin deck is even briefed, and the Godrej-Darling absence — the nearest peer declining this market — is the one datum that says "size modestly" louder than any macro number. The four category decks inherit these as standing orders: build on the herbal-fluency and SAPE lanes, seal everything, price for the street in sachets and for the sapeur in full formats, and treat the two-city boundary as the strategy rather than its limitation (strategist read).

The case FOR: Congo-Brazzaville is the programme's purest two-city play — a market where 58% of the country shops within two distribution hubs joined by a working railway, where the currency cannot surprise you (the only peg in the programme guaranteed by a G7 treasury), where the competitive clock is as still as Libya's (no major invests; the nearest Indian peer never entered), and where the culture hands a grooming brand two gifts no adjacent market offers: la SAPE's codified elegance and a traditional-medicine fluency that makes herbal positioning native rather than exotic. The Regal template means the first distributor conversation starts inside the Indian diaspora's trust network. The case AGAINST: the landed-cost stack is the heaviest visible in the programme (a possible ×2.6 before margins, with a 20–60% wild card no one can price from a desk), the sovereign is debt-peaked with arrears accumulating, the payment plumbing is hostile to everyone including the compliant, poverty is 52% and stagnant, the registration authority cannot even be NAMED from public sources, and the political system just demonstrated both its stability and its failure modes (95% re-election; election-day blackout) in the same week. The honest resolution: this is a GO whose SIZE is decided by four numbers the desk cannot see — which is why the gate sequence prices the ladder before it ships a container, the sachet doctrine pre-absorbs the stack's downside, and the whole position is built exit-light against the succession tail. GO — small, sealed, two cities, and priced only after G1 answers (strategist judgement, argued both ways).
