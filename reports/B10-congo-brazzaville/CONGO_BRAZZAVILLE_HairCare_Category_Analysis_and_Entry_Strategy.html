C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: BUILD — "lead with the African house, not the Indian one." The anti-transplant method reaches Dabur's own portfolio: in twenty-plus prior markets Vatika was the hair spearhead by default; in Congo-Brazzaville the evidence says otherwise. The shelf's demand structure — documented braiding economics (2,000-FCFA mèche packets under 13,000-FCFA labour; extensions 12,000– 40,000), a BIFURCATED texture market (the nappy movement arrived ~2012 and built Kinshasa infrastructure by 2016, while a documented «retour au défrisage» counter-trend runs concurrently), growth-serum demand at 10,000 FCFA, and salon-led retail — maps point-for- point onto ORS (Organic Root Stimulator), the ethnic-hair house Dabur has owned since 2011 via Namaste Labs, and not onto Vatika's Indian-herbal oil-massage register. The honesty that gates the claim: BOTH brands are greenfield here (ORS's only "presence" is an EU e-tailer's shipping dropdown; Vatika has zero footprint of any kind) — so ORS leads as a product-register bet, not a revealed-preference capture, with Dabur Amla riding as the value oil into a tier whose 60–80% documented retail margins leave room for an honestly-priced branded entrant. The channel thesis is the deck's second original: salons ARE the retail (documented 50–80% resale margins on mèches/oils/défrisants as standard salon business model), so the entry builds a salon-POS network under the macro's distributor architecture rather than fighting for supermarket facings first. Everything ships sealed, printed-priced, and WhatsApp-catalogued — the trade's own 60–70%-of-revenue channel (strategist read on agent evidence, 7 Jul 2026).

C2 — Market size & growth

Sizing honestly: no Congo-B hair-care market value exists publicly; the deck sizes by structure and captured tickets. The two-city consumer (macro: 58.1% of the nation) spends on hair as identity infrastructure: braiding labour at 3,000–10,000 FCFA per session (1–3 hours), full extensions at 10,000–30,000, mèche retail from 5,000 to 100,000 by grade, monthly beauty budgets of 15,000–40,000 FCFA for engaged consumers [!] single blog source for the budget figure — against a $1,680 GDP-per-capita economy, which is the point: hair spend here defies income statistics the way la SAPE's tailoring does, and the macro's SAPE reading now carries a hair-specific citation («la coiffure n'est pas un luxe, c'est un besoin quotidien»). Category dynamics: the mèche/extension economy is the volume heart (Kinshasa's Darling factory feeds the river's other bank; Brazzaville's formal retail for it is thin — the execution gap C4 names); oils/serums are the margin heart (purchase 1,000–3,000 → retail 3,000–7,000); relaxers persist through the «retour au défrisage»; and chébé's journey from "rare diamond" (Les Dépêches, Oct 2018) to a dedicated e-commerce vertical (Mbote's Gamme Chébé, 2026) shows the natural-strengthening register maturing into a real category over eight years (strategist read).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory
