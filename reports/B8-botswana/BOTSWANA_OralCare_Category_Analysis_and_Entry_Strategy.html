The consumer decode in one scene — the tube that slipped a rung

Month-end at Choppies Game City: a teacher whose ministry payslip has stopped growing puts back the P32 mainline tube and takes the P17 mass one — the downturn in a single gesture, repeated across ten thousand baskets a week. She is not poorer in habit, only in month: she still reads labels, still wants the better choice, and feels the small indignity of the trade-down every time. A herbal tube at P24 — visibly more considered than the mass anchor, visibly kinder than the mainline price — offers her a third option the ladder currently lacks: trading SIDEWAYS instead of down. That single gesture is the seam's whole market thesis, and it is why the copy brief says dignity, not discount. Out in Mochudi her cousin's tuck shop sells the smallest tube by the unit to customers who never see a leaflet — the other Botswana, served in wave 3 by the wholesale sheet, in Setswana, at the wage floor's arithmetic. Two gestures, two boards, one deck.

The seam's two shoppers — one more precision

The decode's teacher is the seam's TRIAL shopper; its RETENTION shopper is slightly different, and the copy must serve both. The trialist arrives mid-trade-down, catches the sideways option, and buys once on the dignity frame. The retainer is whoever finds, at week three, that the clove-and-neem taste has become the morning's preference — retention in oral care is sensory, not narrative, and no talker can manufacture it. The design consequence: the launch invests in TRIAL (the talker, the leaflet slot, the facing) and lets the formulation carry retention unassisted — which is also why the trial's benchmark reads week-13-to-26 velocity rather than launch-quarter spikes: the read is designed to see the retainers, not the curious. A seam entry that measured only trial would flatter itself; this one is built to catch the quieter, decisive number.

DEEP THINK · The seam in a squeeze, argued both ways. The obvious objection: a downturn is the WORST time to ask a premium-to-mass price — the trade-down current runs against the seam. The counter the deck stakes its posture on: trade-down shoppers are the seam's BEST audience, because they are actively re-deciding a habitual purchase (the rarest moment in FMCG) and feeling the loss of the better choice as they do it — a sideways option at a small premium to their new floor catches a decision already in motion. Namibia's trial tested the seam in calm; Botswana tests it in weather — and the two reads together give the programme something neither alone could: the seam's sensitivity to the cycle. If both hold, the posture is robust; if only Namibia's holds, the seam is a fair-weather instrument and the batch's files say so. Either way the pre-fixed benchmark keeps the answer clean — the experiment's integrity IS the deliverable, third market running.
