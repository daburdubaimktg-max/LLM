C1 · Category frame — SA lineage, local sprouts, an unwalked shelf

Botswana's hair category runs the Southern-African grammar — relaxers/weaves/braids on SA-brand lineage (Motions, Dark & Lovely, TRESemmé, Sofn'Free through the SA-fed channels), a rising natural-hair current — with one feature no prior batch market showed: local natural-hair brands already trading — Fro Girls (Gaborone, plant-based positioning, "oils indigenous to Botswana"), AfricNatural, Ludo (mango/castor/shea detangler) eVoke Digital roundup (403 [!]); Fro Girls socials Jul 2026. The shelf itself is unwalked: no BWP price for any hair SKU survived capture (Choppies/Sefalana catalogue pages 403-blocked), no Dabur or Himalaya listing confirmed anywhere — with the standing possibility that the Indian grocers hold parallel Amla stock TODAY [!] the seeding check = this deck's first fact. The salon economy shows one point (boho braids ~P750, Fresha — upscale tier) and a gap below it [!] the bairro ladder, seventh market. Frame, honestly: a small category in a squeezed market, entered at near-zero infrastructure cost through doors that already exist, with the entry's one genuinely new obligation being GOOD NEIGHBOURHOOD — the local naturals brands are young, proudly Botswanan, and exactly the kind of businesses a clumsy multinational entry tramples; the deck's register rules make trampling impossible.

The Indian grocers (Big Basket/Curry Leaf tier) — the preview incumbents. The decant/tuck-shop tier — the tail's incumbent. The batch's standard read, seventh application: beaten on seal and honesty at a wage-anchored price, via the franchise tail's single door.

Self-teardown — the champion entry's own risk. The champion door is powerful and singular: one desk (Choppies) controls most of wave 1's reach, which concentrates negotiating risk the extension markets never faced — if the desk demands margin the value posture can't carry, or private-label ambitions, the entry's shape changes. Mitigations: the negotiating TRIANGLE (Choppies + Sefalana + Clicks — three doors, no monopoly), the seeding channel's independence (the grocers sell regardless), and the honest fallback (a Sefalana-first entry reaching the tail before the towns — slower, cheaper, workable). The bet prices all three.

The consumer decode in one scene — the leaflet, the braid, the grocer's shelf

Three Gaborone moments. Thursday: a bank clerk collects the Choppies leaflet with the week's specials — her category entry point is a PRICE she recognises as fair, on a shelf she visits twice a week; a new oil earns her trial by appearing in that leaflet at a number that respects the month she is having. Saturday: her sister sits four hours for box braids at a bairro salon whose prices no platform lists; the oil that eases the take-down and calms the first week's scalp is whatever the salon keeps — a kit conversation, not an ad conversation. Sunday: their neighbour — Motswana, not diaspora — walks Big Basket's aisles for the spices she learned to cook with and passes a shelf of hair oils that has quietly served this city for years; if an Amla bottle stands there at a fair price with a label she can read in two languages, the brand's first Botswana customer may already own its second bottle. Three moments, three doors, one design — and the deck's insistence that none of the three shoppers ever meets a version of this brand that behaves like a stranger.

C5 · Register architecture — the neighbour rules

Three rules, one new. (1) The licensed transplant, fourth market: Dabur SA's English creative reuses post-audit; the Setswana renderings ride the market's own dual-language norm through the commissioned panel. (2) The fences: dingaka (care, not cure — at state-recognised height), the policed lightening lane (no glow-adjacent drift in any hair copy), no crisis framing. (3) THE NEIGHBOUR RULES — this deck's addition: Dabur copy never claims localness, never references the local brands, never bids for "Botswana's own" register; trade conduct never uses scale against the sprouts (no exclusive-shelf asks, no below-cost promos in their segment); and if a retailer proposes displacing a local naturals brand for Dabur facings, the answer is no — written here so the account team never improvises it. Good neighbourhood is cheap, permanent, and the only posture that survives a small market's long memory.

The worked example — the seeding month, run end to end

Week 1: two calls and a visit. Big Basket confirms outcome A — a dusty but steady facing of parallel Amla 100ml at, say, P45 (a gouged price by conversion arithmetic; the SA list crosses to ~P28). Week 2: the formalisation meeting — Dabur SA supply at a protected wholesale price, the retail suggestion at P32, the grocer's margin BETTER than the parallel lane gave him, honest stock, no more suitcase logistics. Week 3: the shelf-talker goes up ("the real price" frame, panel-rendered), and the grocer's regulars — the brand's only existing customers in the country — get their loyalty rewarded rather than taxed. Week 4: the velocity data (even four weeks of it) enters the champion pitch as the only slide that matters: THIS BRAND ALREADY SELLS HERE. Cost of the month: one trip, one agreement, one talker. What it buys: real prices, real customers, a protected ally, and a pitch no import competitor in this market can match — evidence. The alternative (straight to the Choppies desk with SA velocity printouts) was the Namibian play; this market offered a warmer first move, and the method's whole discipline is noticing which market offers what.

The salon file's honest paragraph

The kit mechanic arrives at its seventh market with six read-outs behind it and one honest admission: this deck knows less about Botswana's salon economy than any sibling knew about theirs at this stage — one upscale price point, no bairro ladder, no product-habit probe. The response is sequenced humility: the kit is a WAVE-2 decision made on wave-0 fieldwork, the probe rides the standard fortnight, and the arithmetic rule (verified or refused) needs no local adaptation because it never contained local assumptions. If Botswana's salon economy turns out thinner than the batch's norm — plausible in a 2.6M-person market where home braiding may carry more of the load — the kit scales down to an anchor-salon handful or waits entirely, and the oils' case rests on the retail doors alone, which is where this market put its strength anyway.

The channel doctrine's small print

One operational note: the three doors have three different PAPER shapes. The grocers trade on invoices and handshakes — the formalisation agreement is one page, and its protected-price clause is the whole point. The champion desk trades on standard supplier terms — rebates, promo participation, returns — and the value posture's floor must be written into the FIRST agreement because renegotiating restraint later is twice as hard. The franchise tail trades through Sefalana's wholesale sheet — Dabur never touches the 400 banners directly, so the tray-economics audit (the batch's standing rule) runs on that ONE sheet, and the tail's whole health is visible in a single monthly document. Three doors, three papers, one owner reading all three — the anti-neglect package's Botswana edition.

The neighbour rules in programme perspective

The rules deserve their wider frame, because they answer a question the programme will meet again. Across thirteen markets the batch's competitive reads have faced multinationals (fought by design), unbranded economies (out-trusted), and heritage walls (respected at distance) — but never before LOCAL STARTUPS in Dabur's own lane: young brands doing with Botswana's plants what Dabur did with India's a century ago. The easy institutional reflexes both fail here: crushing them is cheap, effective and poisonous (a small market watches, and the story "the Indian giant flattened our girls' brand" costs more than the segment is worth); patronising them ("we welcome local innovation") is worse. The rules thread it: full commercial competition on shelf — same categories, honest prices, may the better product win — with three specific abstentions (no localness claims, no displacement asks, no comparatives) that keep the competition clean. The B8-SUMMARY should carry the doctrine forward named: every maturing African beauty market will grow its own sprouts this decade, and the programme that wrote its manners down in Gaborone won't have to improvise them in Nairobi.

C16 · What does not transplant

Namibia's pure extension does not transplant — the champions are NEGOTIATIONS with their own equity, not slides on an SA agenda; Mozambique's door-first grammar arrives miniaturised (grocers as seeders, not the spine); Zambia's radio lead stays home a third time (leaflets and Facebook are this market's media); the Brazilian code never reached this side of the continent. What transplants whole: the kit mechanic (7th adaptation), the two-anchor tail pricing, the sealed-bottle brief, the anti-neglect package (extended to the champion listing), the licensed-transplant audit. What this deck exports — its last, since the batch's research closes behind it: the NEIGHBOUR RULES (the programme's first written coexistence doctrine for local micro-brands — every future market with sprouting local naturals inherits it), and the SEED-CHAMPION-TAIL sequence as the champion entry's operating order.

DEEP THINK · The windfall question, argued against margin instinct. The conversion lands Amla at value prices with mid-tier economics — and every commercial instinct says take the margin: price at the local mid band, bank the difference. The deck refuses, and the argument deserves its paragraph because it will be re-litigated quarterly. A downturn market is a TRUST market: the shopper reading specials leaflets first is auditing every brand's conduct, and the import that arrives visibly fair buys a reputation asset no later campaign can purchase — while the import that prices to the ceiling in a squeeze is remembered too, longer. The arithmetic seals it: at this market's size, the margin difference is small money; the velocity difference compounds into the recovery. And the governance lock: the bet's clause 8 audits the RESTRAINT, not just the result — because a windfall held for three years by policy beats one held for three quarters by inattention.
DEEP STRAT · The batch's last worked fortnight. Day 1–2: the seeding calls and the grocer visit — the branch chosen, possibly the first prices in hand. Day 3–5: the Gaborone walk (champion shelves, Clicks aisle, the naturals stockists) — the converted board rebuilt. Week 2: the bairro salon ladder, the leaflet-archive read, the Sefalana C&C introduction, the Setswana panel's commissioning, and the Francistown day-trip. Ten days, two people, and the batch's seventh country is designed — the same fortnight that cost Angola a research expedition and Zambia a browse-rebuild now runs on inherited checklists and warm doors. The B8-SUMMARY should price this: the batch's W0 cost curve fell roughly as fast as its relay curve, and for the same reason — machinery, finished properly, travels.
