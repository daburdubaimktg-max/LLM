C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: BUILD-NARROW — "enter as food, arrive as the proof." Three Tunisian facts define the lane. First, the gate is real but narrow: cough syrups and lozenges sit on med.tn as classified médicaments (Humex, Strepsils, Pectamol all carry pharma-register listings), the Pharmacie Centrale's import monopoly (Loi 90-105; scope fixed by the arrêté conjoint of 25 Nov 2020 [!] full text unpulled) guards the medicine door, and officines are numerus-clausus rationed — so Honitus SYRUP queues behind a DPM/ANMPS registration and does not lead. Second, the supplement lane is a documented legal vacuum: the pharmacists' union told Parliament's health commission on 7 May 2026 that compléments alimentaires operate in a «vide juridique» — no dedicated law, open-entry parapharmacies, uncontrolled online sale — which means low formal barriers TODAY and a legislative clock ticking over the entry window. Third — the deck's defining fact — the honey market broke into open crisis the day before this deck was written: La Presse (6 Jul 2026) carried the Bizerte farmers' union denouncing industrial sugar-syrup "honey" from China and India, sold cheap online to consumers who buy honey «à des fins thérapeutiques». Honey in Tunisia is a REMEDY (per-capita consumption ~12g/yr, overwhelmingly medicinal [!] single-source) whose trust has just been publicly burned — and the burner named India. The entry therefore leads with certificate-forward Dabur Honey (lab analysis on pack, batch traceability, NT 56.12 conformity — the answer to the scandal, never "Indian honey"), rides Hajmola as a confiserie-format digestive into the state-validated post-iftar discomfort conversation, formats Honitus as winter lozenges through the para lane, and holds Chyawanprash for wave 2 under the one local story that can carry it — bsissa, Tunisia's own honey-bound fortifying paste (strategist read on agent evidence, 7 Jul 2026).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

The shelf sorts into four occupied registers: French-clinical capsules (Vital, Opalia, the European imports), médicament cough formats behind the officine counter, fragmented-trust honey with a 3× per-gram spread and a fresh scandal, and the attar/traditional register living in medinas and tisane blogs — plus one register nobody occupies: proof-forward natural food formats. That empty register is precisely Dabur's home turf: honey with a batch certificate, a digestive that tastes like confectionery and works like grandmother's rules, a honey-herb lozenge that bridges the tisane and the pharmacy. The structural insight is that Tunisia's wellness trust is BROKEN exactly where Dabur is strongest (honey authenticity) and its regulation is OPEN exactly where Dabur's formats live (food and confiserie) — while the two doors Dabur doesn't need (médicament, capsule-supplement) are the only guarded ones. The entry does not fight Vital for the capsule shelf or Adwya for the officine; it sells proof where proof just became the product (strategist read).

C14 — Lead-brand cost-to-profit value chain (Module 7)

The waterfall is in C11 (T13): ~16% contribution on certificate honey at 34, ~18–20% on Hajmola's confiserie economics, ~17% on Honitus lozenges — a THREE-SPEED portfolio in which the lead SKU deliberately runs the thinnest margin. That inversion is the strategy: honey's job is trust-building velocity inside the live 30–36 band (a premium price would contradict the "affordable authentic" macro thesis and hand the fraud tier its counter-argument — «the real one costs double»), while Hajmola and Honitus monetise the trust at confectionery margins. Three value-chain specifics. First, the certificate is a COGS line, not an A&P line — batch lab analysis and QR serialisation sit in the landed stack (~4–6% of landed cost estimate) because proof is the product's core feature, not its promotion. Second, the food lane's VAT and BCT treatment need one decisive check each: honey may carry a reduced food VAT rate (the waterfall conservatively uses 19%), and whether honey/confiserie fall on the BCT 2026-4 deposit list at all — the circular's named categories are cosmetics-leaning — could hand this portfolio a financing advantage the hair and oral lines don't have [!] both = gate-1 checks. Third, the Hemani benchmark disciplines the plan: 11 years in-market through one niche platform proves the register survives, and warns that niche-channel economics alone never fund a brand — the GMS blanket is what Dabur adds, and the grossiste-en-parapharmacie wholesale layer (open, less regulated than pharma wholesale) is the asset-light spine that carries it (estimates all).
