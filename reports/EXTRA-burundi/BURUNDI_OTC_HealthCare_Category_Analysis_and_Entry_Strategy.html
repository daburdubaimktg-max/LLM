C1 · The finding: POLICY-INVITED

Across the Burundi set every structural fact has cut AGAINST Dabur: the parallel FX rate (RATE-RATIONED) re-prices everything upward, and the 35% EAC wall (WALLED-OUT-OF-PRICE) hands the price lane to duty-free rivals. The OTC/herbal-wellness category is the one place where a structural fact cuts FOR the entrant, and the reversal is the finding. Burundi's state has, by presidential decree of 20 November 2020, created a Ministry of Health structure to promote the local production and use of "médicaments traditionnels améliorés" — improved traditional medicines (ID — afro.who.int). It maintains a network of ≥1,000 tradipraticiens across all 18 provinces; ethnobotanical inventories list 936+ medicinal plant species in use; the Buta traditional-medicine centre sees ~5,000 patients a year; and the public-procurement medicine supply (CAMEBU) is largely Indian-origin, seeding an "India = medicines" credibility. In other words, the herbal register that the tariff wall cannot tax is also the register the government actively LEGITIMISES and PROMOTES.

The fifty-seventh corridor finding, stated for the register: where a state's own policy legitimises the entrant's core register (here, improved traditional medicine), the category is POLICY-INVITED — the structural walls that close every other lane are, in this one, either irrelevant (differentiation beats the tariff) or actively favourable (the state promotes the space). Enter here first and hardest; this is the lane the market's own government holds the door for. This is why the macro sequenced OTC FIRST despite template deck-numbering, and why this deck carries the set's real entry thesis. The lane has three converging supports no other Burundi category enjoys: the DECREE (policy legitimacy), the CREDIBILITY (India = medicines via CAMEBU), and the DIFFERENTIATION escape (herbal register the wall cannot erase, GARDEN-KEPT guest-gardener discipline governing the courtesy). The entry is a papered OTC seed — a Honitus-type honey-based cough format riding the intact medicinal-honey belief where the under-supplied local sector cannot make the format (honey verdict #37) — routed through ABREMA, distributed via the pharmacy/parapharmacy tier (Super Pharma, c² Négoce) and the Dar-corridor rail, priced at the parallel rate. The seed is small by design, but its purpose is positional as much as commercial: it is the beachhead the whole Burundi presence is built from, entered where the state itself keeps the door.

C3b · The wall-becomes-door mechanic — stated precisely

The finding's core claim deserves precise statement because it inverts the set's logic and a board will rightly ask how a wall becomes a door. The mechanism has two steps. First, the 35% CET wall closes the PRICE lane for every Dabur category (WALLED-OUT-OF-PRICE) — which forces Dabur off price and onto DIFFERENTIATION as the only viable axis. Second, for the herbal-wellness category specifically, that forced axis (herbal differentiation) happens to be the exact register the state's decree promotes and the tradipraticien system embodies — so the move the wall FORCED is the move the policy REWARDS. In every other category the forced differentiation is a consolation (hair's Amla niche, oral's care register — real but marginal); in OTC it is a convergence, because the differentiation axis and the policy preference are the same axis. The wall did not literally become a door; it herded Dabur toward the one door the state was already holding open. That is why the finding is POLICY-INVITED and not merely "OTC is less walled": the category is not just less penalised, it is positively favoured, and the favour comes from the same structural force (the wall) that penalises everything else. A board that understood only "OTC has a better margin" would miss the point; the point is that Burundi's hostile structure, followed honestly, DELIVERS Dabur to its single most welcoming lane. Structure, read all the way through, is not only the set's obstacle but its guide.

C7b · The ABREMA weight — why OTC pays it and oral did not

The set runs two regulatory lanes deliberately, and the contrast is worth making explicit because it is a strategic choice, not an accident of paperwork. Oral chose the LIGHTER BBN cosmetic lane (the "herbal + fluoride" care register needs no health claim, so it does not trigger ABREMA's authorisation-plus-inspection regime). OTC chooses the HEAVIER ABREMA medicinal lane, and pays its weight willingly, for three reasons the oral paste could not invoke. First, the OTC format IS a health product — a cough remedy makes an implicit efficacy claim the honest register cannot avoid, so the ABREMA path is not optional the way it was for a plain herbal dentifrice. Second, the ABREMA authorisation is itself a QUALITY SIGNAL in a market whose street-medicine tier is proliferating and whose regulator is publicly alarmed — being visibly, inspectably papered is exactly the differentiation the papered-walk discipline prizes, and it is worth more here than the speed BBN would have bought. Third, the register EARNS the weight: the ~22% margin (the set's healthiest) can absorb the inspection cost that a thin commodity lane could not, so the regulatory burden the wall would make unbearable on price is bearable on differentiation. The two-lane split is therefore the set's regulatory intelligence in miniature: match the lane to the claim and the margin — light BBN where the register carries without a health claim, heavy ABREMA where the health claim is real, the quality signal is valuable, and the margin can pay. A set that ran everything through one lane would either over-paper the dentifrice or under-paper the syrup; running both correctly is what lets each category enter at the right regulatory weight.

C10b · The listening question and the honest-limits gathering

The set's two closing instruments. The listening question is asked of the pharmacist, the lane's keystone: "when a mother asks for something for her child's cough, what do you reach for now, and what do you wish you could hand her that arrives papered and affordable?" — the gap between those answers is the honey-cough format's exact whitespace, described by the person who stands in it and who will, if the format earns her trust, become its advertising. And the honest-limits gathering: the deck does not know the imported-syrup price ceiling or the honey-remedy floor (★1); it does not know the ABREMA procedure, fees or inspection timeline (★2 — the lane's critical path); it does not know whether any phyto/ayurvedic product has been registered at ABREMA before (★3 — the precedent that would ease the path); it does not know local honey coop capacity (the sourcing watch's denominator); and it treats the waterfall as parallel-rate AUTHORED, audit-replaced. Those three support a firm positive — enter OTC first, papered, on the herbal register — and the audit fills in the price and the path. The knowns pick the lane; the ABREMA pull and the shelf audit make it operable.

Objection 1 — "The whole Burundi set has been holds and caution. Now suddenly OTC is 'the real entry'. Which is it?" Answer: both, and the distinction is the set's spine.
