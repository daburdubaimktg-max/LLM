C1 · The finding: WALLED-OUT-OF-PRICE

Every prior hair deck in the queue could at least contemplate a mass price entry. Burundi forecloses it structurally, and the foreclosure is the finding. Under the EAC Common External Tariff's 2022 fourth band, hair preparations from OUTSIDE the bloc face 35% duty (plus 18% VAT), while goods MANUFACTURED in an EAC state enter duty-free — and the mass hair market's confirmed player, Movit (Kampala, Uganda), is EAC-origin (ID — movitproducts.com; distributed in Burundi among 8 markets). Layer the parallel FX premium (RATE-RATIONED) on top and the arithmetic is brutal: a RAK-origin Vatika tin is taxed 35%, paid for in dollars bought at an +80–160% premium, and set beside a Movit lotion that dodged the tariff entirely and was made closer to the shelf. Dabur cannot win a mass hair-oil price fight in Burundi; the wall decides it before the first stall visit.

The fifty-fifth corridor finding, stated for the register: where a customs union gives in-bloc rivals duty-free entry and taxes the entrant's origin at a punitive band, the price lane is closed by structure — the only viable entry is DIFFERENTIATION the tariff cannot erase (a herbal register no commodity rival occupies), or a supply-chain move that changes the entrant's origin (become in-bloc). Everything else is subsidising the customs union. For hair specifically this is a hard verdict, because hair oil is exactly the commodity the wall punishes most: a Vatika-vs-Movit contest is a price contest, and price is the one axis the wall has already lost. Two doors remain, and the deck sizes both. Door one: the HERBAL-DIFFERENTIATED niche — Amla-as-strength, the ayurvedic register, a small premium the wall's victims can still command where the buyer wants the herbal story specifically and Movit does not tell it. Door two, and the higher-leverage one: the EAC-ORIGIN solve (macro option C), which sits above the country team and would delete the wall for hair and every other category at once. Until one of those is real, the deck's verdict is HOLD — reinforced by a second, independent reason: the mass hair category's leader, ex-Godrej Darling, is in post-divestiture flux (Godrej sold the Kenyan Style Industries assets to Hair Manufacturing Kenya Ltd, CAK-approved March 2024), and whether the Burundi operation is a plant, a depot, or disrupted is unverified (G7). No hair commitment is made on a guessed competitive map behind a wall that already lost the price fight.

C7b · Why the salon gate is the wall's one hair workaround

The professional gate deserves its own reasoning because it is the single mechanism by which hair could ever enter Burundi at all. The wall closes the MASS shelf — the isoko stall where price is the only question and Movit has already won it. But the salon operates on a different economic logic: the braider is paid for a SERVICE, the finishing product is a small input to a larger transaction, and the customer's question there is not "which oil is cheapest" but "which oil does my braider trust for my hair." That question has a herbal answer the wall cannot tax into irrelevance, because the price of the oil is a minor line in the service bill and the differentiation (Amla-for-strength, the ayurvedic register) is exactly what a service-buyer will pay a small premium for. The parapharmacy channel extends the same logic into retail: a "care/herbal" shelf prices on register, not on commodity parity. So the wall's one hair workaround is to LEAVE the mass axis entirely and live where price is not the question — the professional gate and the care shelf. This is not a large business (the ~14% waterfall says so), and it is held rather than launched, but it is the only hair position the structure permits, and naming it precisely is what stops a future team from wasting the wall's victims on the mass shelf where they cannot survive. The gate is the sensor, the channel, and the wall's exception, all at once — the East wave's GATE-PROFESSIONAL contribution to the screen library earns its place here.

C10b · The option-C escalation — specified, because it is the real decision

The deck keeps routing the highest-leverage move to Dabur International, so it owes a specification of what that escalation actually asks. The question is NOT "should Dabur build a factory for Burundi" — Burundi alone could never justify it. The question is: as Dabur International plans its East Africa manufacturing footprint (already relevant for Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, South Sudan and DR Congo — the whole East wave), does routing key personal-care SKUs through an EAC-origin facility clear the 35% CET across ALL of them at once? Framed that way, Burundi hair is not the business case; it is one of seven data points in a regional case that may already pencil. The Burundi deck's job is therefore narrow and specific: supply the wall's cost in BIF (the T3 index), flag that the same wall taxes every non-EAC-origin category Dabur ships to every EAC market, and hand the aggregated number upward so the footprint decision is made with Burundi's ~38% disadvantage counted in the regional total rather than dismissed as one small market's problem. If the answer is yes, hair (and oral, and OTC, and skin) all re-price overnight; if no, the herbal niche is the ceiling and the hold stands. Either way, the escalation is the deck's most valuable single output — a country deck that identifies a regional supply-chain lever and routes it correctly does more than one that optimises within a wall it should be trying to remove.

DEEP THINK · Three objections answered

Objection 1 — "Holding the whole hair category because of a tariff is defeatist — brands overcome tariffs all the time." Answer: brands overcome tariffs by DIFFERENTIATION or by moving PRODUCTION, and the deck names both routes explicitly — the herbal niche and option C. What brands do NOT do profitably is fight a 35–40% landed-cost disadvantage on a COMMODITY against an entrenched duty-free rival on the rival's own price axis; that is not grit, it is subsidising the customs union out of the P&L. The hold is not "give up on Burundi"; it is "do not enter hair on the one axis the structure has already lost, and do enter the moment differentiation shows a pulse or origin deletes the wall." Defeatism would be ignoring the wall and launching into it; the deck does the opposite — it prices the wall in BIF so the board sees exactly why price is closed and exactly which two moves open it.

Objection 2 — "A 14% margin niche in the poorest market is not worth the effort — why even hold the option?" Answer: because the option costs almost nothing to hold and its value is contingent on a move (option C) that is being made for OTHER reasons anyway. If Dabur International solves EAC-origin manufacturing to serve Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and the rest of the East wave — a decision far larger than Burundi hair — then Burundi hair's wall vanishes as a free side-effect, and the held, pre-priced niche becomes a normal entry overnight. Holding a priced option against a contingency someone else may trigger is the cheapest strategic posture there is; dropping it would forfeit a free call option on a supply-chain decision already in Dabur International's interest. The 14% is the WALLED margin; the un-walled margin (option C) is a different, healthier number the same SKU earns the day origin changes.

Objection 3 — "You keep deferring to 'the audit' and 'option C' — is there any hair decision this deck actually makes?" Answer: yes, three, and they are real. It decides NOT to enter the mass price lane — a firm negative that saves the P&L from a structural loss and stops a well-meaning country team from "buying share" into a wall. That single averted mistake may be the deck's largest financial contribution to the set. It decides the ONLY viable hair axis is herbal differentiation through the professional gate — a positive that tells any future entry exactly where to stand. And it decides to escalate origin above the country team — routing the highest-leverage move to where it can actually be made.
