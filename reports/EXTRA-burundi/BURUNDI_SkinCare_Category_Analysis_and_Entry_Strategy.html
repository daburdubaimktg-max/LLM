C7b · Why "healthy glow, not bleach" is the fence's only opening — and why it stays shut here

The one lane the blind fence could conceivably leave open is the non-tonal "healthy/nourished skin" register — and the deck is deliberate about why even that stays HELD rather than launched in Burundi. The distinction matters: refusal would mean the lane is forbidden (as lightening is); hold means it is permitted but not yet justified, priced and waiting for three specific conditions. The deck writes the niche so that if a prevalence map appears, the fence eases, and the wall drops (option C), activation is a decision about evidence rather than a scramble — the held-and-priced instrument's whole purpose, applied to the queue's last category.

DEEP THINK · Three objections answered

Objection 1 — "Four skin holds in a row (CAR, Seychelles, Madagascar, Burundi). At some point the instrument is just a habit of saying no." Answer: the four holds land on four DIFFERENT sets of grounds, which is the instrument working, not defaulting. CAR held for poverty-and-blindness (no measurable market, no map); the Seychelles for focus-and-wealth (a prune, entering the strong lanes well); Madagascar for ownership-transferred (a gift not Dabur's to sell); Burundi for floor-and-wall-and-blind (a local champion, a tariff, and unmeasured lightening). A habit would produce the same reasoning four times; these produce four distinct structural reads, each re-derived from its own market's evidence. And the holds are not uniform in shape — the Seychelles pruned to enter three strong lanes, Madagascar opened a sourcing door, Burundi rides the OTC beachhead — so "no" means something different each time. The health check the instrument runs (does the hold's grounds change with the market?) passes a fourth time, which across a 12-country queue is the evidence that the tool measures rather than projects.

Objection 2 — "Holding the whitening fence in the poorest market in the queue, where 'volume' arguments bite hardest, sounds like a luxury Dabur can't afford here." Answer: it is the opposite — the fence is cheapest to hold exactly where it is most tempting to breach, because the alternative is catastrophic and the market is small. A lightening SKU in Burundi would earn thin walled margin on a poor wallet while re-pricing the entire 27-market zero-breach record at zero and betting against a regional regulatory tide (EALA, Rwanda's enforced ban) that is visibly turning. The "volume" the breach would buy is small, walled, and legally doomed; the reputation it would spend is the whole programme's. The fence is not a luxury Dabur affords in rich markets and sacrifices in poor ones; it is a discipline that is IDENTICAL in every market because the downside is identical, and closing the queue's 27th market with it unbroken is the proof that it was never negotiable.
