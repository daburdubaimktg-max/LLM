C4 · THE CORRIDOR DIAGNOSTIC — ELEVENTH FINDING: LAB-KEYED

France's OTC corridor was translator-held, and the prescription was to be what the translators translate. Germany's corridor contains the same translators — Cosmoveda, Classic Ayurveda, Amla Natur, each selling its own Chyavanprash — but their moat is built of different material, and the difference changes everything. The French translators sold French custody (manufacture, certification, national trust); custody cannot be bought by an Indian original without ceasing to be one. The German translators sell German lab proof — "every batch tested post-import to European standard" is Amla Natur's whole pitch — and proof, unlike custody, is purchasable without identity loss. The macro's audit file, built for the gatekeeper defence, turns out to be this corridor's entry key: once Dabur's Chyawanprash carries the same per-lot German certificates the translators wave, the tie-breaker reverts to what only Dabur has — the reference recipe, made where the tradition lives, since 1884, at a price the translators' small-batch economics cannot follow (their €22.95–37.90 band against Dabur's governed €19.99-class 500g [AUTHORED]). LAB-KEYED corridors reward exactly the investment PROVE-THEN-PLACE already commits. The eleventh finding is the first one the mode was built for in advance.

VERDICT #26 — GERMANY: ABSTAIN-METHODS-AT-WAR. Dabur honey does not enter German retail while the market's proof currencies (DNA sequencing vs NMR vs import surveys) are publicly contradicting each other — because in a proof-culture market, an entrant whose origin story carries a searchable 2020 adulteration controversy cannot win an argument the referees themselves are having. The abstention is ACTIVE, with three standing files: (1) the existing Amazon.de listing's self-certifying copy ("no sugar adulteration") is amended to neutral compliance language NOW — it is a claims exposure, not an asset; (2) the 2020 German-lab pass and a dual-method (NMR + DNA) per-lot protocol are maintained as the re-entry dossier; (3) the re-entry signal is defined: an EU-settled reference method (or the Breakfast-Directive enforcement wave forcing methodological convergence) plus a Destatis-confirmed India import lane. Verdict #26 deserves one more paragraph of honesty about what it costs. Abstention forfeits nothing measurable today — no India honey lane exists to lose, no open seat closes — but it does spend TIME: France's #25 builds an origin story next door while Germany waits, and if the methods war settles quickly, competitors with cleaner tails could move first. The verdict accepts that risk knowingly, because the alternative risk is asymmetric: a German honey launch that collides with one more adulteration headline — in a market already primed by the 2020 CSE story — would not merely fail a SKU; it would hand the gatekeepers a narrative that contaminates the audit-file credibility every OTHER category's plan depends on. DEEP THINK — the deck's two hardest calls, attacked. First: holding the flagship (Chyawanprash) is over-caution — France leads with it, the translators sell it freely, and nobody has banned anything. The counter-evidence is specific: the BfR named ashwagandha in a formal communication; the Verbraucherzentrale's market-check is dated THIS April; Denmark's RASFF stream shows enforcement appetite in the neighbourhood; and Germany's arbiters read ingredient lists before stories. The translators sell German-tested, German-bottled products under German names — lightning rods a tenth Dabur's height. The HOLD is not fear; it is sequencing: the file clears (recipe answer, Novel-Food re-run, Article 8 status), and move 4 executes with certificates in hand. The cost of six months' patience is a season; the cost of a Marktcheck naming the flagship is the register. Second: verdict #26 forfeits a first-mover origin story (India honey) that verdict #25 just built in France. Correct — and deliberate. Same principle, opposite weather, opposite verdicts — that is the taxonomy working, not contradicting itself.
