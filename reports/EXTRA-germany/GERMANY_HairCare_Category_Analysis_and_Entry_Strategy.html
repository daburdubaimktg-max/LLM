The retailer owns the corridor — and the original still has three doors the retailer cannot walk through
Prepared for the Dabur International board · 7 July 2026 · Public-web evidence only · Inherits the Germany Macro (AUDIT-DEFICIT; PROVE-THEN-PLACE; the khadi precedent) · Gap rule in force · All platform prices [!] re-validate at G1 (DE session)
THE DECK IN ONE PARAGRAPH: German hair care (~USD 3.4bn, 2024, INDEX) is the programme's first market where the corridor's incumbent is the retailer itself. dm and Rossmann do not merely gatekeep the mass shelf — they run the category's education (both sites merchandise the 2024–25 hair-oiling/Rosmarinöl trend with named landing pages and an influencer programme), own the certified-natural middle tier (alverde, Alterra) at store-brand prices, and hold the trust lead outright (Balea beats Nivea on YouGov's 2025 value score, 43 to 31). Above them sits the translated-Ayurveda premium (khadi's BDIH-certified Strong Amla in 50ml treatment formats; Weleda's Rosmarin oil at €11.95/50ml ≈ €239/litre); below them, Dabur's own de facto German price list at india-store.de (Vatika €3.99–4.99/200ml) and a grey Amazon tail whose 2×200ml Amla at €16.90 prices ~70% above the grocery ladder with a review corpus complaining of "very strong oriental scent" and expired bottles — the audit-deficit species visible in a single listing. The corridor diagnostic returns its ninth distinct finding: RETAILER-OCCUPIED — and its prescription: never fight the retailer's own label; enter through the three doors the retailer cannot occupy: originality (the Indian original beside the German interpretation), community (the diaspora ladder and — via the group's own ORS — an Afro channel already served), and ritual scale (200ml oiling bottles against 50ml treatment vials — a use-case the trend is teaching Germans right now). One wing is refused outright: no Vatika push into the Afro shops, because the anchor brand on those shelves is ORS — Dabur's own subsidiary — and the brand-architecture firewall plus simple portfolio sense say the group does not compete with itself.

C4 · THE CORRIDOR DIAGNOSTIC — NINTH FINDING: RETAILER-OCCUPIED

Run the diagnostic and German hair care produces a shape none of the prior eight runs contained: every layer of the corridor between Dabur's €4.99 grocery ladder and khadi's certified premium is occupied by the same two companies that decide mass-market listing. dm and Rossmann write the trend content (their own hair-oiling landing pages ARE the category's education), manufacture the certified middle (alverde, Alterra — including Alterra's own rosemary scalp oil), and command the trust economics (Balea's 43-to-31 win over Nivea means the shelf's cheapest credible option is the shelf-owner's). In France the taught middle was unclaimed; in Germany the teacher, the landlord and the middle-tier occupant are one entity. The prescription follows from what a retailer's own label structurally cannot be: it cannot be the original (no dm label will ever be the 1884 Indian Amla), it cannot be the community's brand (diaspora and faith channels carry brands, not store labels), and it is slow to follow format innovation that contradicts its price architecture (a 200ml ritual-oiling bottle at €6.99 undercuts the €239/litre treatment-vial economics the premium tier depends on). Three doors; the playbook walks them in order.

A word on why the LEAP set leads with format and freshness rather than heritage. Every prior market's hair creative earned its highest scores on community voice (France's 18/20) or heritage recognition; Germany's corpus and culture argue the opposite order. DEEP THINK · DEEP STRAT · THE REPUTATION BET

DEEP THINK — the finding stress-tested. The strongest objection to RETAILER-OCCUPIED as a distinct finding: it is just "strong private label," a feature of every developed market, and the diagnostic is inflating a commonplace. The answer is in degree becoming kind: nowhere else in the programme's forty markets does the same actor simultaneously hold the trust lead (Balea over the global brand leader), the certified middle (own-label seals), the category's education (retailer-owned trend media with named influencers) AND the listing decision. In the UK, Boots gatekept but Vatika still held the seat; in France, GMS gatekept but taught nothing. Germany's duopoly performs every corridor function at once — which changes the prescription from "find the gap in the ladder" to "enter through what an own label structurally cannot be." That is a different strategic geometry, and the three-doors prescription would be WRONG in every prior market — the test of a real finding. Second objection: the 3.7★/32-rating Amla Gold suggests the German consumer simply does not like the product — maybe the market is telling Dabur no. The honest answer: 32 ratings of a grey-imported, possibly-stale, English-labelled SKU measure the supply chain, not the product; France's 6,618-rating corpus on the same formula reads 4.2★. The German test is whether governed freshness, honest scent copy and a German label move the corpus — move 2 is designed as exactly that experiment, and if two quarters of governed listings still read sub-4.0, the mild-variant NPD question opens formally and the deck's assumptions get re-argued in the open.

DEEP STRAT — what this deck deposits. Programme-level: the corridor taxonomy's ninth finding names a market structure (retailer-as-corridor) the remaining directive countries should be screened for — Mexico's Walmex and Brazil's pharmacy chains are candidates. The education-budget clause logs its fourth geometry (teacher-owns-shelf: the clause pays out only through originality and format, never price-tier harvest). Set-level: this deck hands Oral the review-corpus lesson (German buyers punish surprise — the fluoride conversation must be equally surprise-free), hands OTC the freshness-governance system (Chyawanprash's jar dates matter as much as the oil's), and hands Skin the scent-honesty template (Gulabari's rose register will meet the same fiche-clinical reader). And one portfolio note for the board, filed without a recommendation: the German Afro channel is anchored by a brand the group owns, in a market where the group's other hair brand is refused entry to that channel by this deck's own logic. Whether ORS's German importer route becomes a template for Vatika elsewhere, or Vatika's audit infrastructure serves ORS's German stock, is an internal synergy question — flagged, firewall-respected, never guessed at here.
