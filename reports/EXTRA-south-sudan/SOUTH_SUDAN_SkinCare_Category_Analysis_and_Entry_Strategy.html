C1 · The finding: FENCED-BOTH-WAYS

Every previous fence market gave the fence one job: keep Dabur out of the lightening conversation. Kinshasa (#186) drew a fence through a MEASURED centre; CAR (#191) drew one through unmapped territory and named the blind-fence protocol; the Seychelles (#196) ran the mild form with an annual watch. South Sudan presents a configuration the fence has never faced: the skin-tone conversation here is not a private consumer practice to be quietly avoided — it is a LIVE PUBLIC IDENTITY DEBATE with two loud, opposed currents, both of them culturally owned by the market itself.

Current one: bleaching is widespread and commercially embedded. CGTN Africa (12 Oct 2020) documents it as "a popular practice with many South Sudanese"; most of Juba's markets host several stores selling agents; the typical user profile is women 18–40, with teens sometimes parent-encouraged; dermatologists flag mercury-containing creams; the cheapest whitening cream cost 450–500 SSP at the time of the report. Radio Tamazuj has reported a government ban on dangerous skin-lightening creams — the ban's text is uncaptured, its scope and enforcement unknown (macro T16 #7). Current two: the counter-current is equally real and internationally celebrated — the "Queen of the Dark" moment around model Nyakim Gatwech, the Al Jazeera identity feature, a body of South Sudanese essay-writing on colourism. Dark skin is, for a visible part of this nation's public voice, an asset of identity — not a problem to be solved.

The forty-eighth corridor finding, stated for the register: where skin tone is a live public identity debate, the fence closes BOTH registers. The lightening vocabulary is shut by the standing fence (25th market, zero breaches). But the PRIDE vocabulary is shut too — by the ownership check minted three decks ago (#199). "Embracing dark skin" is South Sudan's own story, authored by its models, essayists and daughters. A foreign herbal house that borrows that story to sell lotion commits the severed-heritage error in mirror image: a true story deployed by the wrong owner. The street would hear it precisely as what it is — an outsider monetizing the nation's most personal conversation. The fence therefore has two wings here, and the buildable ground between them is deliberately narrow: the neutral functional band. Dryness. Protection. Moisture. Nothing about tone, in either direction, ever.

The verdict follows the macro's pre-commitment: skin ships NOTHING in the seed phase (the macro bet's clause 5, now executed by its own deck). One SKU is held and priced — both endings written — with activation bound to three events, none of them a date: the re-review (election resolved + corridor stable), the ban text captured and read, and the seed's trust ledger showing two clean cycles. This is the CAR close-out grammar (HOLD) re-derived on a third set of grounds: CAR held skin for poverty-and-blindness, the Seychelles pruned it for focus-and-wealth, South Sudan holds it because the category's public conversation is a minefield the brand must never enter, and the functional band beneath it cannot be priced until the ban's text and the market's first price ladder exist. Three holds, three different reasons — the instrument keeps proving it measures markets, not habits.
