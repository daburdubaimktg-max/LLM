South Sudan — OTC Health Care: Category Analysis & Entry Strategy

Private OTC lives in the aid system's shadow: where NGOs deliver most of the country's health care free, the sellable cells are exactly the ones no clinic dispenses — the pocket balm, the comfort format, the everyday relief the humanitarian system was never built to carry
Prepared for the Dabur International board · 7 July 2026 · Public-web evidence only · Routing: africa-consumer-insight-expert · Research base: SS_FULL_NOTE.md §4; macro #197 + #198–199 standing orders · The latency scale (#190) INHERITED VERBATIM · Honey verdict #35 honoured · The DFCA/CRIA gate governs · The disclosure carried: nothing herein is CAPTURED-grade

THE FORTY-SEVENTH CORRIDOR FINDING — AID-SHADOWED. South Sudan's health economy is humanitarian by construction: NGOs and faith-based organisations deliver ~70–80% of all health services, the state's health allocation runs at 1.3–4% of a collapsing budget, donor funds hold up ~50% of functional facilities while 18% stand closed, and the whole apparatus — from the Health Pooled Fund to MSF's gap warnings — is built to dispense ESSENTIAL care free at the point of need (all ID). The finding names what that architecture does to a private OTC entrant: the free system defines the commercial map by negation. Competing with donated essentials — antimalarials, ORS, antibiotics — is commercially hopeless and ethically wrong at once; but the aid system was never built to carry comfort: the muscle balm for the man who carried loads all day, the pocket relief a clinic queue never dispenses, the small daily formats of feeling-better that fall below every essential-medicines list. Private OTC's viable cells are the aid system's SHADOW-COMPLEMENT — and the shadow is where this deck builds. The architecture follows from the instruments this market inherits whole: the latency scale runs verbatim (a counterfeit-documented, trust-thin market buys what proves itself on the skin in seconds — the balm leads; syrups and tonics wait at their tiers), the DFCA/CRIA gate governs with the conservative pre-commitment (the balm's classification answered in writing before any carton; a «covered» ruling means it waits — no CRIA adventure at seed scale), the drug-shop tier — formally licensed «community medicine shops», a paper-formalized channel no prior market offered — is mapped as the shadow's own retail, and verdict #35 is executed as absence: no honey jar anywhere in this file, the coop watch logged beside the lulu file at the annual review.

DEEP THINK · The objections, answered

"Selling comfort beside a failing health system — isn't the whole category unseemly?" The opposite, if the boundary holds. The shadow's goods are the ones no donor will ever fund and no clinic should stock — a porter's shoulder rub, a worker's evening relief — and selling them takes nothing from the system while returning something the system cannot: ordinary consumer dignity, the small purchase that isn't charity. What WOULD be unseemly is named and fenced twice over: competing with donated essentials, marketing through the aid economy, or building a plan that profits from the system's further collapse — the sensitivity table's last row answers that edge case in advance, and the annual re-read audits it. The boundary is the ethics; the deck's job was drawing it exactly.

"The balm carried CAR's OTC deck too — is the programme just shipping the same answer to every poor market?"
