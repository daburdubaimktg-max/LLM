THE FORTY-FIFTH CORRIDOR FINDING — NEIGHBOUR-SHELVED. The strangest structural fact in the South Sudan file is an e-commerce model: Shop Ninja «buys in Kampala and delivers to Juba in 2–3 days» — the market's own entrepreneurs have formalized what the whole consumer economy already does informally. With Uganda supplying 40.6% of imports, the Nimule artery carrying ~90% of trade, and Juba's retail too thin to hold a full assortment, the reference shelf for Juba's consumers and traders is partly IN KAMPALA: the trader restocks there, the diaspora relative shops there, the price expectations form there, and «available in Juba» often means «carried from Kampala last Tuesday». The finding names the consequence for any entrant: visibility in the neighbour's wholesale row is a precondition for credibility on the home stall — a brand absent from Kampala's Indian-goods wholesalers and duka suppliers will read as a stranger in Konyo Konyo even if a carton lands there, because the trade's own buying trips won't have seen it. The entry design follows: the seed (Amla small formats, the macro's SKU #1) ships through the SAME Kampala consolidation the Juba trade already rides; the Kampala wholesale row's Dabur presence (real today — the Kenya/Uganda Indian-goods channel) becomes a marketing asset the file audits and, where thin, inexpensively thickens; and the Juba read (SEED-AS-SURVEY's second named application — stall reorders, WhatsApp SSP prices, the salon DM census) is interpreted knowing that some of Juba's demand will express itself as KAMPALA purchases the sensors can't see. One documented player anchors the category's map: Movit Products (Kampala) formally lists South Sudan among its eight markets — supermarkets, cosmetics shops, pharmacies, salons — and this deck studies its model as the structural template while selling into a different cell (named-active oils above the jelly-and-pomade mass tier).
