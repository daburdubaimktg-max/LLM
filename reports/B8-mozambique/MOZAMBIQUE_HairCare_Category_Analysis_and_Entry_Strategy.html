MOZAMBIQUE · HAIR CARE — "A TERCEIRA VIA": THIRD-LANE OILS

The lane nobody shelved: not the relaxer aisle (South Africa's), not the novela code (Brazil's, culturally present but commercially absent) — natural oils for braided and natural hair, entered through the warm door at the batch's lowest ladder · All MZN numbers AUTHORED at the 64–72 MZN/USD plan band unless flagged · Portuguese copy panel-gated (shared panel, Changana extension) · 6 July 2026
FOCUS · One line: put Dabur Amla and Vatika oils into a market where the hair category has two occupied lanes (SA-lineage relaxers on the chemical shelf, Brazilian aesthetics on the TV screen) and one empty one — the natural-oils lane currently held by unbranded coconut and castor decants at Xipamanine — entering through the Indian trading houses' shelves and the trancista's chair, priced against the decant plus trust, and honest that in a US$623-per-capita economy the branded premium itself is the entry's central bet.

C1 · Category frame — two lanes taken, the third one loose

Mozambican hair care runs the Afro-textured triad — chemical relaxers/desfrisagem, braids/tranças as the dominant salon revenue line, and a smaller natural-hair and oils segment — with a brand map that splits by GEOGRAPHY OF SUPPLY, not consumer taste: the relaxer aisle is South-African-lineage (Dark & Lovely confirmed via Krolyc's listing; TCB and Motions distributed regionally), arriving down the Shoprite/SA corridor; the aspirational register is Brazilian (novela-driven since 1986, salon clients citing novela looks) — but the Brazilian BRANDS that own Angola's shelves show no confirmed Mozambique retail presence at all: no Skala, no Salon Line, no Novex listing anywhere surfaced krolyc.co.mz; regional distributor pages; absence-reads Jul 2026 [!] Mercado Central shop-by-shop check queued. The oils segment is tracked as its own category by panel research (Sagaci counts 986 hair SKUs across 245 brands in its Mozambique panel — the detail paywalled, the structure telling sagaciresearch.com [!] the panel pull is the category's one purchasable shortcut) and is served today by UNBRANDED decants: coconut and castor oil sold loose at Xipamanine and its 62 sibling markets. That is the third lane: culturally legitimated by the natural-hair current, commercially unowned by anyone with a label.

C2 · Demand architecture — the decant, the chair, and the wallet

Start where the macro decode ended: the palm of loose coconut oil decanted into the customer's own bottle. That transaction — pennies of product, zero packaging, vendor-trust as the only quality assurance — is the third lane's incumbent, and it teaches three things. The demand is REAL and habitual (oil already belongs to the braiding ritual: pre-braid prep, under-braid scalp weeks, loose-hair care). The competition is a PRICE FLOOR with a trust ceiling (a decant costs less than any branded SKU ever will; it also watered, adulterated, or rancid often enough that a sealed bottle with a name answers a felt anxiety). And the wallet is the constraint that shapes everything: at the agriculture wage floor (MZN 6,688/month), a 200ml branded oil at ~230 MZN is 3.4% of a month's income — a considered purchase, bought maybe monthly. The architecture therefore leads with the SMALL bottle (60–100 MZN, inside the daily-cash tolerance), sells the 200ml as the household step-up, and treats the trancista as the volume route: her per-client usage (15–20ml) turns one bottle into five services, making the chair the one place where the branded oil is cheaper PER USE than her decant habit once quality-loss is counted.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown

Dark & Lovely (SoftSheen-Carson/Godrej) — the reachable incumbent. Confirmed at Krolyc; supplied down the SA corridor; owns the relaxer register. Lesson: the chemical lane is served and defended — and it is the lane the programme refuses anyway. Its real teaching is logistical: SA-corridor supply WORKS here, which is exactly why the SA-repack duty study (macro M3) matters for Dabur's own landed costs krolyc.co.mz [!] price unretrieved.

TCB / Motions — the regional chemical second tier. Distributed via Lusophone-market distributors; no MZ prices surfaced. Same lesson at lower altitude.

The unbranded decant — the true incumbent. No brand, no pack, no price list — and most of the volume. Beaten only on trust: sealed cap, honest label, a name that returns tomorrow. The street SKU's entire design brief is this paragraph.

The Brazilian ghosts (Skala/Salon Line/Novex) — present in the culture, absent from the shelf. Their absence is the deck's biggest single strategic fact: it means the tropical-natural register is UNCLAIMED at retail. Dabur will not pretend to be Brazilian — but ingredient-forward naming, generous honest formats and warm PT copy (the code's load-bearing elements, decoded in Angola's T7b) are free to use because nobody on the shelf is using them. If a Brazilian brand lands later (the Angola infrastructure is one border away), Dabur's defence is incumbency in the lane plus the door's distribution — another argument for speed absence-reads [!] re-verify quarterly.

The salons (Tranças com Celly, Beleza Chic tier) — the conversion machine. Instagram/Fresha-visible at the upscale end; bairro braiders below them uncounted. The chair mechanics that worked in Lusaka (starter kit, sale-or-return, reorder-as-research) transfer with one adaptation: recruitment here starts from the door's wholesale customers (the shopkeepers know which braiders buy oil weekly) rather than from Instagram fresha.com listings; [!] bairro price truth = W0 ask.

Self-teardown — the wallet bet stated plainly. Everything above says the lane is open; nothing above says the lane is RICH. The entry's central uncertainty is whether a US$623 economy carries ANY branded premium in oils — the macro bet named "wallet" as one of its three failure modes and this deck owns that clause. Three mitigations, none decorative: the small bottle priced inside daily-cash range; the chair route where per-use economics beat the decant; and the door's wholesale layer, which strips retail margin stack-up in wave 1. The kill-test is cheap and early: two quarters of tray-and-chair data. If the answer is no, the programme learns that the batch's poorest wallet needs a different model (bulk-to-salon only, or wait-for-LNG) — knowledge Namibia/Botswana don't need but the B8-SUMMARY's ranking does.

C5 · Register architecture, hair edition

Three rules govern the words. (1) Warmth borrowed, transformation never promised: the novela register licenses warm, first-person Portuguese and ingredient romance; it does not license "hair like the screen" — no texture promises, no before/after of texture change, ever. The barred list transfers from the batch intact, opening with "cabelo ruim", and the panel extends it. (2) The languages are layered: pack and Maputo copy in Portuguese; the vendor and chair sentences rendered ALSO in Changana for the south (panel extension brief); Emakhuwa waits for the north wave. Nothing vernacular ships unpanelled — GAP RULE, absolute. (3) Three fences stand: curandeiro world (no strength-of-spirit claims — oil is cosmetic comfort, not healing), the dignity rule (the door sells the oil; the oil never sells the door), and the lightening lane (a hair deck seems far from it, but "glow" vocabulary creeps — the private law's audit covers hair copy too).

The consumer decode in one scene — the bottle she brings

Hold one image from the census design, because the whole category lives in it: at Xipamanine, the customer BRINGS HER OWN BOTTLE to the oil stall. That habit — refill culture, packaging as her property, the vendor's measure as the unit of trust — is what the third lane's branded entry must respect rather than override. It is why R1 is a small SEALED bottle rather than a sachet (the bottle is already the format; the seal is the innovation), why the honest-ml statement matters more than any claim (she knows exactly what a palm-measure is; the label competes with her eye), and why the vendor's margin is the launch's first media spend (the woman who filled her bottle for six years is the only endorsement that moves her to a sealed one). A launch that arrived with foil sachets and TV would be answering questions this market never asked. The deck's design answers the two it does ask: is it really full, and is it really pure.

The worked example — one kit in Chamanculo, eight weeks

Run the smallest unit end to end, Mozambican numbers. A trancista in Chamanculo takes kit #31 in May: two 200ml bottles, six minis, one card, nothing paid. Her braid sets run 400–600 MZN at bairro prices; over eighteen clients she uses ~300ml on prep and scalp finishing — a service upgrade that cost her nothing and let her say one new sentence, panel-written, now in her own Changana. She sells four minis at 90 MZN: 360 MZN of found revenue, roughly half margin, against a decant habit that cost her ~150 MZN over the same weeks and once arrived rancid. Week eight, the reorder: she pays ~500 MZN for kit two because the maths visibly worked and two clients asked for "o óleo da senhora" by name. Multiply by three hundred chairs: ~1,800 bottles a quarter through chairs alone, a reorder ledger that IS the wallet read-out, and six hundred paid-for recommendations a week. The failure branch, run just as honestly: she uses the free kit, sells one mini, never reorders, and her verbatim — "as clientes trazem o seu óleo" (clients bring their own) or "é caro" — routes into the M9 board verdict. Dabur's downside is the kit's landed cost across the non-reordering tail: at 40% reorder, roughly sixty wasted kits ≈ one-third of one container's margin. The information those sixty verbatims carry about the batch's poorest wallet is worth multiples of that — the kit programme is priced as research even when it fails as sales.

The third lane in programme perspective

Set beside its siblings, this deck completes a triptych the B8-SUMMARY should hang as one exhibit. Zambia's oils-first entered an OPEN market on a warm window — the model's easiest case. Angola's join-the-code entered a FULL market through a borrowed register — the model's cleverest case. Mozambique enters an EMPTY-but-poor market through a trading door — the model's hardest case, because nothing here tests strategy; everything tests the floor. If oils-first works at six hundred dollars per capita through wholesale ledgers and bairro chairs, the model has no remaining untested condition in Southern Africa and Namibia/Botswana inherit it as settled machinery pointed at richer wallets. If it fails here, the failure will be legible (wallet, trust or machine — the bet's three post-mortems) and the model survives with a floor annotation: "oils-first proven above US$1,000 per capita; below it, bulk-to-salon only." Either outcome is batch knowledge priced at one narrow launch — and the deck notes, for governance, that this is the third consecutive market where the hair deck carries the batch's heaviest experimental load while paying the thinnest rent; the B8-SUMMARY should say aloud that the hair wedge's value across this batch has been overwhelmingly informational, and budget its successors accordingly.

C16 · What does not transplant

Angola's JOIN-THE-CODE stays across the border: there the code owned the shelf and joining it was the entry; here the code owns only the screen, so the entry CLAIMS a lane instead of joining one — same register warmth, opposite strategic verb. Zambia's radio-led launch stays home a second time (the believable media here are the chair, the shopkeeper and TikTok — the batch's youngest media plan). The braider-kit mechanic transplants with its third adaptation (Instagram-recruited in Angola, door-recruited here). Mauritius's fairness machinery and SA's claim-back stay home. What this deck exports: the DOOR-RECRUITMENT variant of the kit (wholesale ledgers beat platforms wherever a trading community runs the supply chain — Kenya-adjacent files should note it), the decant-anchored pricing method (trust-premium over loose goods as a ladder anchor — reusable in every 95%-informal market), and the wallet read-out protocol, which Namibia and Botswana — richer wallets — will invert into a premium-tolerance test.

DEEP THINK · The first-mover question, argued honestly. The third lane is open, but open lanes in poor markets stay open for a reason: sometimes nobody shelved the story because the story doesn't pay at this wallet. The deck's answer is that THREE facts distinguish Mozambique's emptiness from unviability — the decant volume proves the habit (demand exists, unbranded); the trust gap is felt and priced (adulteration anxiety is a real willingness-to-pay the census will quantify); and the launch's cost base is the batch's lowest (existing shelves, existing services, existing catalogue — first-mover loneliness without first-mover infrastructure bills). If those three facts survive W0 contact, the lane is a business; if the census breaks any of them, the deck's kill-test fires before the second container ships. Either way the programme buys the answer cheaply — and the B8-SUMMARY should note that Mozambique, not Zambia, is the batch's true test of the oils-first model's floor.
DEEP STRAT · One fortnight, three categories — the shared-wave build. Hair's W0 fortnight is deliberately over-designed to serve the set: the Xipamanine census prices oils AND honey AND jelly floors in the same twenty stalls; the door interviews qualify the importer for all wave-1 categories at once; the Recheio listing meeting tables three catalogues in one sitting. This is the twin-toolkit logic applied INSIDE the country: pay the research and relationship cost once, amortise it across the set. The consequence for governance: hair's deck OWNS the fortnight and the door relationship; honey and jelly ride as passengers (their decks specify cargo, not vehicles); and the single-owner rule — flagged to the B8-SUMMARY twice already — gets its third instance here: one name owns the door.
