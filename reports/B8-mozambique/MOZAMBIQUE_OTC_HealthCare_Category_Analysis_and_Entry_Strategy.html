C2 · Demand architecture — recovery country, sweetness country

Health demand here is shaped by malaria first (the macro's numbers: ~15% national prevalence, ~40% childhood) — the household rhythm of fever-and-recovery gives tonics and strength foods cultural work everywhere in the country. The NCD curve adds the modern layer: diabetes DOUBLED between STEPS waves (2.9%→7.4%, 2005→2014/15) while awareness and treatment fell — a growing sweet-toothed urban population that does not yet manage its sugar PMC9701039 [!] third-wave check. Honey sits at the joint of both stories: a food of strength and recovery, already branded on shelf, already trusted in its unbranded roadside form (and already suspected when watered — the macro decode's trust gap). Chyawanprash arrives as a stranger — no Ayurvedic wellness presence exists — and the deck prices its education budget honestly rather than assuming the herbal register carries free. The sugar-free doctrine reaches its sixth market with its most pointed evidence yet: a diabetic population that stopped being treated is precisely the population a SUGAR-FREE strength jar serves without a single therapeutic claim.

The consumer decode in one scene — the two honeys

A Sunday in Zimpeto, northern Maputo. A grandmother buys honey twice a year: once from the roadside on the EN1 — a reused water bottle, amber and cloudy, from a seller whose face she knows, for the grandchildren's coughs and the morning porridge — and once, when the season's money allows, a SEALED jar from town, because the year her neighbour's roadside bottle turned out watered with sugar syrup, the child's cough got worse and the shame lasted longer than the cough. She does not distrust the roadside seller; she distrusts the road behind him — the chain of hands no one can vouch for. That split purchase is the entire honey strategy in one shopper: the roadside lane answers relationship-trust and will keep doing so (Dabur neither can nor should compete with a known face); the sealed-jar lane answers CHAIN-trust, and today it is served by an SA import brand whose own supply chain spans two continents. A jar that says tested, sealed, traceable — and prices itself honestly above the local brands it respects — is not taking her roadside purchase; it is upgrading her town purchase. The deck's every clause descends from keeping those two purchases distinct.

The worked example — one aisle, one afternoon, one verdict priced

The W0 honey read is the cheapest decisive fieldwork in the entire Mozambique set, so the deck scripts it. One Recheio aisle, one afternoon: photograph nothing, note five brands' prices and grammages, compute per-gram, and place three markers — the local-identity band (Good Trade, Afronita, Bom Dia), the import tier (Goldcrest ×2 sizes, San Francisco), and the gap between them. Three outcomes, three pre-written responses. If the import tier runs 20–40% above local per-gram (the expected shape), H1 lands at the tier's top with its credential clauses and the slot is confirmed. If the tiers overlap (imports priced AT local levels), the premium slot is narrower than structured and H1 enters smaller — 250g only, top of shelf, and the H2 step-up waits a further two quarters. If the import tier is absent from the shelf that day (stockouts, delistings), that is the loudest finding of all — either the tier failed (investigate before shipping) or supply broke (the door's opportunity). One afternoon, three branches, every one of them already in this deck — which is what authored-but-ready looks like when it works.

C16 · What does not transplant

Angola's WAIT-FOR-THE-PATH stays across the border, and the pair now form the programme's cleanest paired exhibit: same method, same portfolio, adjacent Lusophone markets, OPPOSITE verdicts — because the evidence differed on exactly the dimensions the method weighs (track, shelf, gate count). The honey taxonomy's prior verdicts all stay home (Zambia's source-don't-sell, Angola's census-then-decide) — Mozambique's shelf evidence produced the twelfth and the range is now complete. What this deck exports: the TWO-CLOCK grammar (food-track cargo + paper-track fast-follow — reusable wherever a portfolio straddles a regulatory split), and clause 4's sourcing-door discipline (procurement conversations routed away from marketing, third statement of the batch's rule).

DEEP THINK · The verdict defended against its own attractiveness. Premium-beside- the-cooperative is a pleasing sentence, and pleasing sentences are how transplants happen — so the deck stress- tests it. Is the premium slot REAL, or a desk construction? Three evidence points say real: the shelf already carries a five-brand spread with an import tier (someone pays above local prices today); the roadside trust gap is documented in the macro decode (watered honey is a known household anxiety a sealed credential jar answers); and the cooperative economy's own ceiling (~26t peak for MHC, ~500t national) means local supply physically cannot serve mass premium demand — the import tier exists BECAUSE the local lane is small, which is also why protecting it costs Dabur almost nothing commercially. Is the protection clause real, or reputation-washing? The test is auditable: zero price actions, no displacement asks, quarterly review — and the clause survives the cynical reading precisely because the commercial sacrifice is small: Dabur is not giving up the value tier out of virtue; it is declining a tier that was never its position anyway, and writing the declination down so it binds when a future quarter's numbers tempt someone. Doctrine that costs nothing today is only worth something if it is written where tomorrow can't unwrite it — that is what the trade-terms clause is for.
DEEP STRAT · The two-clock build as the set's economic keystone. Honey is the Mozambique set's margin engine (the ~10% rent against hair's 6% and oral's 9%) and its earliest revenue — cargo on a fortnight that was happening anyway. The strategic consequence: honey's early cash and shelf presence BUY TIME for the two slower stories (the supplement paper, the wallet read-out) without pressuring them. This is the inverse of Angola's set, where the categories queued behind gates and the macro bet had to defend patience; Mozambique's set self-finances its own patience. The B8-SUMMARY comparison writes itself: Angola = expensive door, cheap rooms; Mozambique = cheap door, thin rooms — and the honey jar is what makes the thin rooms affordable. One governance note: because honey trades first and best, the temptation to extend it (flavours, sizes, wellness claims) will arrive before the supplement paper does — the year-2 file (T9b) exists so extension happens by review, not by drift.
