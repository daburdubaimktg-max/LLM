THE ASK: Oral is Dabur's clearest local-make right-to-win in Ethiopia — the BET — because the incumbent it must displace is not Colgate; it is a free twig, and that twig is botanically Miswak. Ethiopia's default dentifrice is the mefakia (መፋቅያ) chewing stick, cut from Salvadora persica (arak) — the exact plant Dabur Meswak is made from — or neem/wild-olive, used with charcoal (ከሰል) and salt (ጨው). Only ~12% of Ethiopians brush with a modern brush; in the rural majority, toothpaste is a zero-penetration, first-purchase category. So this is a penetration-build (convert stick → tube), not a share-steal, and peer-reviewed Ethiopian studies find the mefakia's oral-hygiene outcomes comparable to modern brushing — which lets Dabur ratify, not insult, the stick and upgrade the same ritual: Dabur Miswak/Herb'l/Red = "the mefakia you trust, now in a tube." The lowest-friction entry narrative in the whole programme. Two structural keys: (1) the fluoride switch — a herbal, fluoride-free paste stays in the fast EFDA cosmetic notification lane (and is the exact SKU set Alfarag already sells), while a fluoride ≥1500 ppm / anti-caries claim tips into the heavy therapeutic lane — so enter herbal-light, add a fluoride bridge later; (2) local-make — the market leader Unilever already makes Signal locally at Dukem (2019), so locally-made Signal retails ~ETB 65 while an imported herbal tube lands at ~ETB 200–260; local/toll manufacture cuts landed cost ~50–60%, escapes the FX/tax wall, and Dabur already owns the cloneable Festac (Nigeria) herbal-toothpaste + school-CSR template. The digital-shelf gap is sharp: Dabur Herb'l is locally listed (DeliverAddis), but the halal hero Miswak and Ayurvedic Red are grey/cross-border only [!] confirm. Category verdict (macro T18): BET. Halal is a positive for the ~⅓ Muslim east; "natural/plant" is congruent with Orthodox fasting — a dual cue one herbal formula serves.

C1 — The ask (executive summary)

Macro anchor (§M4, §M7, T18): Oral is ranked BET — the mefakia-to-tube bridge is culturally exact, the category is penetration-led (~12% brushing), and Dabur owns a cloneable herbal-toothpaste plant template (Festac). Enter herbal via the light cosmetic lane through the deepened Alfarag beachhead; sequence to local-make; served from RAK/Turkey.

The play in one screen. Three truths make oral Dabur's sharpest local-make bet. First, the ritual is Miswak by another name: Ethiopians clean their teeth with a Salvadora persica stick — the literal plant of Dabur Meswak — so a herbal toothpaste is a continuity upgrade, not a foreign import, and peer-reviewed local evidence ("mefakia works") lets Dabur ratify the heritage and upgrade it rather than denigrate it. Second, this is a first-tube category: with only ~12% brushing and toothpaste effectively zero-penetration rurally, the prize is owning the first tube a household ever buys — a penetration build won on continuity, sachet trial and habit-formation (the Festac school-and-radio flywheel), not a share war with Colgate. Third, the regulatory lane and the consumer sweet-spot coincide: a fluoride-free herbal paste stays in the fast EFDA cosmetic notification lane and is exactly what Ethiopian consumers already trust — so Dabur enters herbal-light and layers a fluoride "anti-caries" bridge (heavier therapeutic lane) later, to give stick-users a functional reason to trade up. The move is an equity conversion + a local-make commitment: get the Miswak hero and Ayurvedic Red onto local priced shelves (today they are grey/cross-border only while Herb'l is listed); anchor the value-to-mid ETB ~350–600 band (undercutting imported Colgate/Sensodyne, out-storying the Chinese generics on heritage); win the pharmacy + kiosk + school-CSR engine; and convert from Alfarag import to local/toll manufacture — because the market leader already makes Signal at Dukem, and an import-only herbal tube is structurally priced 3–4× a locally-made one. Do not fight Colgate on the clinical-fluoride frame, do not insult the mefakia, and do not stay import-only. The LEAP lesson (C13): Dabur's "Nature's own toothbrush" idea is strong but invisible in Amharic — the win is Presence in Amharic + Afaan Oromoo. Reputation Bet (C18): by 31 Dec 2027, a locally-made or locally-packed Dabur herbal toothpaste (Miswak hero, ETB ≤600), launched on a "mefakia-you-trust-in-a-tube" continuity story with a Festac-style school/radio programme, out-converts first-time-paste buyers vs any import-only flanking SKU — or Dabur stays a grey/import herbal niche while local-made Signal owns the first tube.
