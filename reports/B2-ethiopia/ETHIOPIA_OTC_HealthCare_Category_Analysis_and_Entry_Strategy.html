THE ASK: OTC is a BUILD / Phase-2 — but the most pre-sold herbal-OTC market in the whole programme. Ethiopia uses traditional/herbal medicine first-line (WHO-cited ~80% population; 37–57% in cohort studies), and every core Dabur OTC concept has a living Amharic folk analogue already sold by healers and spice sellers: Honitus = damakase (ዳማከሴ) + tenadam (ጤናዳም, rue) + honey + ginger; Chyawanprash = the honey/black-seed tonic habit; Hajmola = the tenadam/besobila digestive. So belief is not the job — packaging, dosage, hygiene and EFDA classification are. But OTC is gated: the EFDA classification fork is the whole game. A juice can land at 0% duty as an "essential medicine," 5% as a food supplement, or eat the full ~60–115% cosmetic/luxury stack — a 50-point swing before a birr of trade margin — and the "traditional/herbal medicine" lane is a regulatory vacuum, a trap, not a shortcut. So Dabur runs two heroes on two clocks: Phase-1 (food/supplement lane, NOW) = Dabur Honey + Hajmola (as confectionery) + activated ORS (already Alfarag-listed) — fast to the ~thousands of suks/kiosks, building the herbal-Dabur house and a trust bank; Phase-2 (drug lane, QUEUED) = Honitus, the strongest cultural fit but the slowest to register, filed now to land into a pre-warmed distribution. The economics command local toll-manufacture (EPHARM/Cadila/Julphar are proven templates): ~40–55% cheaper shelf, EPSA's 25% local-preference, thinner local retail markups (imported pharmacy 18–186% vs local 4–30%), and FX cut to raw materials. The digital shelf is stark: Honitus/Hajmola/Chyawanprash are absent in-country (cross-border only) — clean whitespace [!] confirm. Category verdict (macro T18): BUILD. Dual cue: halal east + "natural/ plant" (Orthodox fasting) highlands.

C1 — The ask (executive summary)

Macro anchor (§M4, §M7, T18): OTC is ranked BUILD / Phase-2 — the 80%+ traditional-medicine base pre-sells the herbal concept, but it is gated behind the heavy EFDA drug/traditional-medicine dossier. Enter the fast food/supplement lane now via the deepened Alfarag beachhead; queue the Honitus drug dossier; sequence to local-make; served from RAK/Turkey.

The play in one screen. Four truths shape the OTC strategy. First, the belief is already there: Ethiopians treat honey, tenadam, ginger, garlic and eucalyptus as first-line cough/immunity/digestive remedies, so Dabur's herbal OTC is a legitimise-and-bottle play, not a category-creation one — the job is hygiene, dosage, consistency and trust-at-scale, which the healer/spice floor cannot offer. Second, the classification fork decides everything: register Honey/Chyawanprash/Hajmola in the light food/supplement lane (fast, 5%/food duty, sold through the ~thousands of suks and Merkato with no pharmacist gate) and never float a claim-bearing SKU in the herbal-medicine vacuum (unregulated, discretionary, a trap) — reserve the heavy drug lane only for Honitus, whose therapeutic cough claim buys pharmacy authority (and 0% "essential medicine" duty, possibly FX priority) at the price of a 12–24-month dossier. Third, reach lives in the food lane; authority lives in the drug lane — so run two heroes on two clocks: a Phase-1 commercial hero cohort (Honey + Hajmola + activated ORS) for cash, distribution and a trust bank, and a Phase-2 category hero (Honitus) filed now to land into that pre-warmed network. Fourth, the economics command local-make: an imported finished OTC on the full stack is priced out by grey imports and local-made substitutes within a year, while toll-manufacture (EPHARM/Cadila/Julphar templates) cuts the shelf ~40–55%, earns EPSA's 25% preference and thinner retail markups, and shrinks FX to raw materials. The move is a build: activate the Alfarag ORS listing and add Honey/Hajmola in the food lane; file the Honitus dossier; seed the "tenadam-honey herbal heritage, bottled and pharmacy-trusted" story in real Amharic + Afaan Oromoo (the LEAP Presence gap, C13); and commit a local-make path. Do not lead "India's No.1," do not trust the herbal-medicine lane, and do not stay import-only. Reputation Bet (C18): by 31 Dec 2027, Dabur has ≥3 food-lane OTC SKUs (Honey/Hajmola/ORS-class) on local shelf and reordering across Addis + ≥2 hubs, a Honitus EFDA drug dossier filed, and a committed local-make path — or the herbal-OTC whitespace stays owned by the healer floor and Vicks while Dabur's trusted trio remains a cross-border curiosity.

C2 — Category frame, size & the self-medication structure

Ethiopia's OTC pool is demand-deep but formally thin: high self-medication (46–49% pooled) meets a small licensed retail lattice (~3,468 formal outlets over 132–136M people), so the traditional-healer floor and the kiosk fill the gap. The tracked segments confirm the opportunity shape — analgesics lead, cough & cold is sized (~US$55m), and Statista's own narrative flags a "surge in demand for traditional herbal remedies."

C3 — Competition & brand-by-brand teardowns (Module 1)

Certified honey brands (Beza Mar) prove a branded-natural premium exists. Dabur (via Alfarag) lists an ORS/Healthcare line but the flagship OTC trio is absent.

C5 — Whitespace & the strategic bet (DEEP THINK)

Three tiers, and the middle is empty. Imported-OTC mainstream (Vicks, syrups) is FX-exposed, foreign, chemical. Traditional-healer floor (damakase, tenadam, honey) is trusted and near-free but un-dosed, un-hygienic, un-branded, quality-variable. The hole is the branded, hygienic, dosed, halal, value-to-mid, locally-made-eventually herbal OTC house — and Dabur's own trio (Honitus/Hajmola/Chyawanprash) is absent in-country, so the lane is uncontested.
