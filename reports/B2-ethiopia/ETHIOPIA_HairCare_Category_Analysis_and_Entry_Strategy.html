THE ASK: Hair is Dabur's single clearest right-to-win in Ethiopia — the LEAD/BET — because the country already performs Dabur's core benefit, daily. Ethiopian hair care is oil-and-butter-first, shampoo-light: near-universal use of qibe (ቅቤ) — spiced clarified butter on hair and scalp — plus seed oils (telba ተልባ flax, nug ኑግ, selit ሰሊጥ sesame, castor), all consumed inside the shuruba (ሹሩባ) braiding ritual, on predominantly type-4C textured hair where the Habesha long-hair ideal makes growth · length · anti-breakage the #1 benefit territory — the exact equity of Dabur Amla. Dabur does not need to create the oil habit; it needs to hygienise, fragrance, bottle and brand it. Hero = Dabur Amla Hair Oil = "the modern qibe" (no rancidity, no smell, consistent, trusted, halal-certified, fasting-congruent plant base). The comms lesson is decisive: Sunsilk (local factory, bespoke range) already owns "made for Ethiopian hair" (formula) and "real natural hair" (authenticity) — so Dabur must enter underneath the shampoo war, on the oil/ritual territory Sunsilk structurally cannot claim, then convert to a shampoo+conditioner trio. And the economics command it: on the 2024-floated birr and a compounding tax stack, local/toll manufacture lands ~56% cheaper at factory-gate than imported finished and escapes the excise trap — Dabur is already listed via Alfarag but present only passively (grey-resold on Jiji from ~ETB 360, no branded storefront) [!] confirm. The job is distribution visibility + priced availability + the oil-credential story, not ingredient awareness. Category verdict (macro T18): LEAD / BET.

C1 — The ask (executive summary)

Macro anchor (§M4, §M7, T18): Hair is ranked LEAD/BET — the least-friction cosmetic lane, the tightest ritual fit, and Dabur's most-proven African travelling brands (Vatika/Amla). It is the spearhead that proves the distribution-and-price model the rest of the portfolio rides. Enter via the deepened Alfarag beachhead; sequence to local-make; served from RAK/Turkey.

The play in one screen. Three truths make hair Dabur's best Ethiopian bet. First, the ritual is already Dabur's: Ethiopians oil their hair daily with qibe and seed oils for shine, growth and breakage control — a moisture/seal technology for 4C hair, not decoration — so a branded herbal hair oil is a ritual upgrade, not a new habit. Second, the benefit ideal is Amla's home turf: the Habesha aesthetic prizes long, thick, strong hair, and "growth · length · anti-breakage" is exactly what Amla (Indian gooseberry) has stood for across every market Dabur has entered. Third, the incumbent Dabur actually replaces is the artisanal butter tier — homemade/loose qibe and local butters (Kibbeh, Sheromeda, Mekab, Tenketem) that own authenticity and price but are inconsistent, rancid, unfragranced, unbranded and uncertified. Dabur is the branded, hygienic, fragranced, halal-certified version of what Ethiopia already does. The move is an equity conversion, not a launch: lead with Dabur Amla Hair Oil as "the modern qibe" in a sachet + 150ml pack ladder; hold the value-shampoo line with Hobby (Dabur's Turkish fighter brand) while Vatika carries the premium-of-value herbal oil; win the salon/braider (ሹሩባ) as the credibility and recommendation engine; seed via radio + coffee-ceremony word-of-mouth; and — the decisive economic move — convert from Alfarag import to local/toll manufacture before scaling, to beat the tax-and-FX wall Sunsilk already beat with its own factory. Do not enter shampoo-first (into Sunsilk's guns), do not price toward the diaspora tier (Cantu/Shea Moisture at ETB 2,000–6,500 reach <5%), and do not transplant Indian glam creative. The LEAP lesson (C13): Sunsilk owns the "bespoke formula" flag, so Dabur owns the open "modern qibe / oil-ritual" territory — in real Amharic and Afaan Oromoo. Reputation Bet (C18): by 31 Dec 2027, Dabur Amla + Vatika are priced, branded and reordering on shelf across Addis + ≥2 secondary hubs at LUP/sachet + 150ml, salon/braider-endorsed, on a committed local/toll-make path — or the oil lane stays owned by loose qibe and grey resale while Sunsilk locks the shampoo shelf.

C2 — Category frame, size & the oil-first structure

Ethiopia hair care is best read by penetration and ritual, not by its dollar tag — the birr float distorts every USD figure. Statista pegs the market at ~US$1.04bn by 2029 on low-single-digit real growth, but that number is inflation-distorted and folds in informal spend; the board should lead with penetration × population (132–136M) × a universal oil habit, not the headline. The convertible frontier is shampoo/conditioner penetration (low outside urban) — oil is already near-universal.

C3 — Competition & brand-by-brand teardowns (Module 1)

The field has four tiers: an MNC mass-shampoo layer led by Sunsilk (ሰንሲልክ) — which built a local factory + bespoke Ethiopian range (2023), dodging the import-cost wall before the float made it fatal — plus Dove/Garnier; a diaspora curly premium (Cantu, Shea Moisture, Mielle, TRESemmé at ETB 2,000–6,500) reaching <5%; Chinese imports (price + extensions); and the artisanal butter/oil tier (Kibbeh, Sheromeda, Mekab, Tenketem) that owns authenticity and price. Dabur's own Hobby (Turkish value shampoo) sits in the listing as a ready fighter brand.

C5 — Whitespace & the strategic bet (DEEP THINK)

The field has three occupied tiers and an empty centre. MNC shampoo (Sunsilk) owns lather + "made-for-Ethiopia formula" but has no oil-ritual authority. Diaspora premium owns 4C benefit language but is unaffordable to >90%. Artisanal butter owns authenticity + price but nothing on hygiene, consistency, fragrance, brand or halal. The hole is the branded, hygienic, plant-powered, halal, value-to-mid "modern qibe" — the authenticity of the butter tier with the trust/consistency/fragrance of a real brand.

DEEP THINK — why the centre, not the poles. Dabur cannot out-media Sunsilk on shampoo, out-price the loose-butter tier, or reach the diaspora's wallet. But none of the three can hold "branded + authentic + hygienic + halal + affordable oil" — Sunsilk has no oil credential, the butter tier has no brand or hygiene, the diaspora has no mass price. Strategist read (opinion): the durable win is ownership of the modern-qibe identity, compounded through the braider and the coffee ceremony and defended by Amla's growth equity — an annuity the poles' business models forbid them from contesting. Enter oil-first; convert to the trio inside households already won, rather than fighting for shampoo shelf you don't yet hold.
