C1 · Category frame — state machinery, disease burden, open lane

Three stacked facts make this the batch's most institutional wellness file. The machinery: the Ayurvedic and Other Traditional Medicines Act 1989; practitioner registration under a Traditional Medicine Board; SIX public Ayurvedic hospitals and seven clinics treating hair-fall, skin, digestive and metabolic complaints; the 2015 India–Mauritius traditional-medicine MoU renewed since; an AYUSH Chair; the first Jan Aushadhi Kendra outside India; and the AYUSH Centre of Excellence folded into the India-supported 500-bed SSRN hospital build [!] timeline. The burden: adult diabetes 20.1% (2024, WB/IDF — world #12; nearly one in two over-50s; 31.7% of diabetics poorly controlled), obesity 36.2%, NCDs consuming ~70% of the health budget. The lane: the CAM retail seam is loose (the 2016 census found 16 organised Ayurvedic outlets, half selling OTC without consultation); food/nutraceutical SKUs route through food-safety clearance, far lighter than the drug path; pharmacy retail runs through MedActiv (15), Sun, Pharmacie Nouvelle and independents with delivery apps (Bongéni, MedicarePharm) — and no organised Indian wellness rival exists: Himalaya sits at "Coming Soon" under a beverage distributor, Patanjali is a Facebook page. One warning tempers everything: pharmacy prices are a live political wound (Panadol +25% in 2024; ≥80% of medicines repriced since 2023) — the fair-price register from the hair deck applies to wellness with double force.

C2 · Demand architecture — wellness wallets on a diabetic island

Wallet one: the metabolic household. With one in five adults diagnosed and one in two over fifty, nearly every extended family manages sugar. The demand this creates is DAILY-PREVENTION spend — and the product that serves it must respect the condition structurally, not rhetorically: the sugar-free Chyawanprash leads this market's story even more decisively than it led Qatar's or South Africa's, with the classic serving the ritual cohort and the label doing no winking. Wallet two: the clinic-taught believer. Twenty-five years of public Ayurvedic services created something no marketing could: a population for whom "ayurvédique" is a word their own health ministry uses. These buyers need availability and price, not conversion — the hair deck's formalisation logic, transposed. Wallet three: the winter-and-festival household. The southern winter (June–August) drives immunity spending; Divali drives gifting — the hamper is the wellness range's announcement vehicle exactly as it is hair's. Wallet four: the honey kitchen. Honey here is an everyday import commodity with a terroir crown (Rodrigues) that mass brands should salute and never contest. The fence around all four: no diabetes claims, no cure whispers, no clinic co-branding — the state built the register's legitimacy; the brand's job is to be worthy of shelf adjacency, not to borrow the crest.

The consumer decode in one story

A winter Tuesday in Vacoas. A retired schoolteacher — diagnosed eleven years, controlled with metformin and discipline — waits at the Ayurvedic clinic her ministry runs, where the doctor she trusts speaks of diet and daily habit in the vocabulary her grandmother used. On the way home she stops at MedActiv for her husband's chronic script and passes the vitamin wall: British brands, French brands, nothing from the tradition her own public clinic just practised on her. At Winners she buys honey for the family's morning bread — an anonymous import; the Rodrigues jar is for gifts — and, in the tea aisle, remembers the Chyawanprash her Chennai cousins spoon each winter, unavailable here except through a grocer's occasional consolidation at a price that makes it a curiosity. Her island taught her the register at a public clinic, taxes her sugar-aware, sells her anonymous honey, and offers her nothing that connects the three.

One shopper, three touchpoints, three absences: the empty slot beside the vitamin wall (R3's home), the nameless honey lane (R4's), and the jar her family's tradition already trusts (R1/R2's). The deck's whole architecture is her Tuesday, completed.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown

3.0 Self-teardown: absent from the friendliest room

Chyawanprash could not be confirmed in any Mauritian retail channel — every search resolved to India or US listings [!] open gap, not proven absence — walk + grocer check. No Dabur Honey, no wellness line, no distributor. In the one jurisdiction on earth whose HEALTH MINISTRY operates the tradition Dabur industrialised, the company has no confirmed shelf. The oral deck's silence datum recurs here but reads differently: wellness products are heavier, pricier per basket, and better suited to diaspora-grocer consolidation than toothpaste — the grey trickle likely exists below web visibility, which is why the verdict is a launch (plug in), not a test. The teardown's sentence: two governments built the socket; the company never brought the plug.

3.1 Vitabiotics and the pharmacy vitamin wall — the incumbent register

Vitamins run through pharmacy with dedicated distribution (Vitabiotics named; Centrum/Jamieson via cross-border) [!] MUR ladder at walk. Their teardown: clinical-adjacent packaging, pharmacist adjacency, winter pushes — the standard playbook, competently run, with NO Ayurvedic occupant beside them. The lesson: the pharmacy wellness wall has a ready-made empty slot whose register the state already taught.

3.2 Adcock/Aspen-equivalents — the symptomatic shelf

Panadol and the cold-flu set own symptoms; their 2024 price rises own headlines. Not contested — but their price-politics wound defines this deck's opening: a wellness range that arrives with CECPA-documented fair pricing enters a conversation the island is already having, on the consumer's side.

3.3 The Ayurvedic outlets — sixteen doors of proof

The 2016 census's 16 outlets (3 pharmacies, 2 clinics, 5 shops, 6 centres) are small, fragmented — and the category's living evidence: practitioners prescribe, believers buy, half the doors sell OTC. They are the credibility channel (stock them early, honour their practitioners) and the market-intelligence channel (what they already import, at what prices, is the browse's wellness annex [!]). They are NOT the volume channel; supermarket+pharmacy is.

3.4 The honey shelf — a crown, a commodity, and an anonymous lane

Rodrigues honey wears the terroir crown at premium prices; anonymous imports (China, Australia, INDIA at #2) fill the everyday band at MUR 620–1,050/kg retail; local production shrinks. No adulteration scandal primes this market (unlike SA) — purity here is hygiene, not the whole strategy. The teardown lesson: the everyday band is an anonymous lane waiting for a trusted name, and India already ships it — unbranded.

Reading the slot against three benchmarks

The empty-slot claim deserves its triangulation, like hair's middle tier before it. Benchmark one: the pharmacy channel carries ~50% of comparable CAM turnover in structurally similar markets (the HPA of SA figure the batch carries), and Mauritian pharmacies demonstrably range imported wellness at premium tickets — the wall exists and pays rent. Benchmark two: the state's clinics process real patient volumes in exactly this register — demand with a waiting room. Benchmark three: the grey trickle and the sixteen outlets prove private purchase happens at friction prices. Three independent lights all point at the same shelf gap; what none of them proves is the gap's SIZE, which is why the KPI tree measures doors and mix before it dares measure share, and why the bet's commercial clauses lean on trajectory rather than totals. The slot is real; its width is the year-one finding.

C5 · Register architecture, wellness edition

Register one — the state-taught plainness (leads): «ayurvédique» spoken as the health ministry speaks it — factual, unexotic, French-first; the brand as the tradition's household FORM, the clinics as its medical form, and never a co-branding whisper between them. Register two — metabolic dignity: Qatar's strength-positive law verbatim («le bien-être qui respecte vos chiffres» as the working draft panel); the sugar-free variant marketed as addition, never reproach. Register three — festival and winter warmth: the hamper's language, the June chest-rub months, panel-built Creole life. Register four — honey honesty: origin stated (India), purity stated plainly, the Rodrigues crown saluted by never imitating terroir language. The refusals: no clinic co-branding or practitioner endorsements-for-hire; no diabetes claims in any language ever; no "miracle" vocabulary the outlet trade sometimes tolerates; and no pricing theatre — this island reads pharmacy price tags like political texts.

The classic-jar question, answered honestly

A deck this committed to the sugar-free lead owes the classic jar a fair hearing, because the classic is the heritage households' actual memory and the volume plan's actual base. The resolution is positional, not moral: the classic sells as what it is — a traditional winter food, sugar-honest on its label, priced fairly, marketed to the ritual that already wants it — while the sugar-free carries every gram of the brand's METABOLIC story. What the deck forbids is the blur: classic advertising that gestures at health outcomes in a 20.1%-prevalence country, or sugar-free framing that shames the classic's keepers. Two jars, two truths, one label discipline. The Phoenix nurse from the South African file has a Mauritian cousin, and this deck was built so that nothing on either jar would embarrass her at work.

The channel doctrine in one paragraph

Wellness inverts hair's channel order: pharmacy leads despite grocery's volume, because the category's story SKU (R3) needs the white coat's quiet legitimacy before the supermarket's reach — the staircase logic the programme built in Riyadh and rebuilt in Johannesburg, at island scale. The sixteen Ayurvedic outlets come early but small: they are the register's living rooms, stocked out of respect and read for intelligence, never leaned on for volume they cannot carry. C11 · The flag-moves

DEEP THINK — riding state legitimacy without stealing valour. The temptation this file must name: six public hospitals, a CoE, a Jan Aushadhi Kendra — the adjacency assets glitter, and the fastest-looking route is co-branding, practitioner endorsements, a CoE ribbon-cutting photo. Every step of that route is a trap. The state's Ayurvedic legitimacy is JOINTLY OWNED public property (two governments, one diaspora's institutional pride); a private brand caught borrowing it converts goodwill into scandal at diplomatic speed, in a market where everyone knows everyone. The correct ride is adjacency without contact: shelf presence in the pharmacies where clinic patients fill their households' baskets; the plain register the ministry itself taught; product quality that makes the tradition's OWN doctors privately respectful; and patience. Second-order: done right, the state's machinery keeps teaching the register forever, free, while competitors who arrive later and grab at the crest inoculate the market against themselves. Kill-test: if ANY commercial arrangement with state Ayurvedic infrastructure is ever proposed internally, it routes to G2G-level counsel first — the deck pre-commits the escalation so the temptation has a procedure instead of a shortcut.
DEEP STRAT — the ninth honey derivation: BRAND-THE-LANE. Eight verdicts exist (Kuwait ladder, Uganda no-go, Kenya study, Tanzania source-co-brand, Rwanda pass-footnote, Saudi format-entry, Qatar respect-and-routine, SA purity-proof-or-pass). Mauritius's facts force a ninth: India is ALREADY the island's #2 honey supplier — anonymously; local production cannot fill demand and shrinks; Rodrigues owns terroir at crown prices; no scandal primes distrust; the everyday band is a nameless import commodity. Verdict: BRAND-THE-LANE — put a trusted name on a flow that already exists, priced mid-band, origin stated plainly, purity declared as table-stakes hygiene (batch certificates available, not brandished — this is not SA), and the Rodrigues crown saluted explicitly in comms posture (never in imitation). Works if: the browse confirms the everyday band's anonymity and the CECPA food-line status holds. Fails if: a supermarket private label brands the lane first — the same one-conversation risk as everywhere, faster on an island. Kill-switch: if pre-empted, honey folds into the hamper-and-tonic system as a supporting SKU (the Rwanda-footnote shape) and the derivation's lesson — anonymous import lanes are first-mover prizes with short fuses — exports to Zambia and Mozambique, where the same shape recurs.

The World Diabetes Day silence, defended

The calendar's strangest entry deserves its argument. Every marketing instinct says a wellness brand in the world's #12 diabetes market should show up on November 14th — a booth, a screening sponsorship, a well-meaning post. The deck rules all of it out, for a reason stronger than caution: on that day, the island's attention belongs to its patients, its clinics and its public-health institutions, and any brand presence — however respectful — converts a health observance into a marketing surface. The silence is not absence; it is the fence made visible, and in a small society where such choices are noticed, a brand that demonstrably knows when NOT to speak earns a kind of trust no campaign can purchase. The pharmacists who stock R3 will notice. The clinic doctors will notice. That is the entire point.

C16 · What does not transplant

Three stay-homes, two arrivals, one culmination. South Africa's regulatory-split machinery (supplement-lane vs discipline-lane dossiers) stays home in its heavy form — Mauritius's light food lane and statutory Ayurveda make the split nearly trivial; the PRINCIPLE (launch on the lightest honest lane, file the deeper one in parallel) arrives intact. SA's purity-proof honey doctrine stays home — no scandal primes this market; certificates are hygiene, not strategy (the 9th derivation replaces the 8th, not extends it). The GCC's scholar-gate grandeur stays home (the community-respect discipline lives in the oral deck's activation rules). What arrives: the sugar-free-first sequencing in its culminating market — 20.1% prevalence, a state that fights NCDs with 70% of its health budget, and a register the health ministry itself taught; and the fair-price register from this island's own hair deck, doubled in force. The culmination: across nine markets the honey question has now produced nine different answers — the taxonomy is the programme's proof that it re-derives rather than transplants, and Mauritius's BRAND-THE-LANE closes the batch's Indian-Ocean chapter of it.

What this deck teaches the batch — and what the doctrine's trilogy now shows

Three markets have now carried the sugar-free-first doctrine to three different proofs: Qatar argued it from prevalence statistics against a premium-wallet backdrop; South Africa upgraded it to policy alignment (the state taxes sugar); Mauritius completes the trilogy with INSTITUTIONAL alignment — a state that treats metabolic disease as its central health emergency AND operates the very medical tradition the product speaks. If the mix clause lands here (≥35% sugar-free share), the doctrine graduates from sequencing tactic to portfolio law: in any market above ~15% adult prevalence, the sugar-free variant is not a flanker but the franchise's lead voice. The second export is the adjacency ledger (T4b) — every future market with state-run traditional medicine (Sri Lanka and Nepal loom in later batches) inherits a ready-made fence between riding legitimacy and stealing valour. And the ninth honey verdict completes a nine-answer taxonomy that is now the programme's single clearest exhibit of its founding rule: nothing transplants; everything re-derives.
