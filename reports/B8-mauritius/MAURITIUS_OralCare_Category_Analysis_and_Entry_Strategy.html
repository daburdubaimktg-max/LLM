C1 · Category frame — a category invisible to desk research

What is knowable: Mauritius brushes with the global mainstream — Colgate presumed dominant by regional pattern and marketplace category-page structure (Oral-B and Parodontax pages exist on Ubuy.mu), Sensodyne reaches the island via EU cross-border pharmacy sites, Signal is plausible-unconfirmed [!] all three — walk. What is documented ABSENT: Meswak, Dabur Red, and Himalaya toothpaste — no listing, grey or formal, anywhere; and the herbal-toothpaste category itself, which in India, the GCC and even South Africa's wellness niche carries an established story, has NO visible presence in Mauritian retail. What is structurally present: a ~17–18% Muslim population (2022 census) with the miswak's sunnah anchor alive in practice; a Hindu-majority mainstream that queues at six public Ayurvedic clinics and needs no persuading that herbs belong in healthcare; and a price-control regime that names pharmaceuticals and infant formula but — on current evidence — NOT toothpaste [!] gazette confirm before any architecture signs. No national DMFT/caries dataset surfaced source gap — MoH ask. The frame's honest summary: a category where the incumbents are unpriced, the challenger is untried, and the demand is inferred from adjacent behaviour — exactly the conditions where the programme's discipline says TEST, not launch.

C2 · Demand architecture — three hypotheses wearing the shape of demand

This deck's demand section is honestly hypothetical, and numbers each hypothesis for the test to answer. H1 — the sunnah anchor: practising Muslims (Plaine Verte, Port Louis trading families, island-wide) hold the miswak tradition; Meswak-brand toothpaste converts stick-practice warmth into daily-format purchase, as it does in the Gulf — IF the community's retail loyalty can be reached through its own shops with JUM certification visible. H2 — the herbal-mainstream crossover: the Hindu-majority mainstream that accepts state Ayurvedic clinics extends the same trust to an ayurvedic toothpaste (Dabur Red's register), positioning it as the family's second tube — the evening tube, the parents' tube — beside an incumbent Colgate habit too entrenched to attack head-on. H3 — the null: toothpaste is the one category where the island's Indian heritage does NOT travel — brushing habits formed under French-influenced retail and two generations of Colgate advertising leave no register space for herbal entrants at any price. The test is designed so any of the three outcomes is a clean, cheap, documented answer — and H3, if it wins, saves the programme from repeating the error at scale in Zambia or Mozambique.

The consumer decode in one story

Two bathrooms, one question. In Plaine Verte, a hardware trader in his sixties keeps a miswak stick by the basin — cut, as his father taught him, used after fajr — beside a tube of Colgate the household actually brushes with. The two do not compete; they occupy different registers of the same mouth's care, the way prayer and pragmatism share his day. Nobody has ever offered him the object that unites them: a paste carrying the stick's lineage with the tube's convenience, certified by the body his community actually trusts. He does not know he wants it, which is precisely what a test exists to find out. In Quatre Bornes, a schoolteacher whose bathroom shelf holds a state-clinic Ayurvedic liniment for her knee and Colgate for her family's teeth has simply never seen the two traditions meet in the toothpaste aisle — the clinic legitimised the register; no shelf ever stocked it. Her herbal trust is proven, paid-for behaviour; whether it crosses the aisle is the island's open question.

Read together: H1 is the trader's basin, H2 is the teacher's shelf, and the honest possibility that both say "no thank you, the Colgate is fine" is H3 — the null this deck refuses to fear. The test's design serves all three an answer.

One epistemological note completes the demand section. The three hypotheses are not equally weighted, and the deck says where its priors sit: H2 is the strongest (the state's own clinics have spent twenty-five years legitimising the register this SKU speaks; behaviour that crosses from clinic to pantry usually crosses to the bathroom eventually), H1 is genuinely uncertain (practice and purchase are different loyalties, and the stick may satisfy the practice entirely), and H3 is the respectable underdog whose victory would still pay for itself in exported caution. The priors matter operationally: if early signals force a choice of attention, H2's instruments get read first. But priors are not thresholds — the read-out honours the pre-registered numbers, not the narrative the team has grown fond of by month ten, which is precisely the failure mode the governance table exists to prevent.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown — thinner by necessity, honest about it

3.0 Self-teardown: the one Dabur line the grey market didn't bother with

Every other Dabur category reaches Mauritius informally — hair via three grey routes, wellness plausibly via diaspora grocers. Oral care reaches it not at all. That asymmetry is the deck's most interesting datum: grey channels are demand-following, so the absence of grey Meswak is weak evidence of weak pull — the reason this deck's verdict is a TEST rather than the confident whitespace-claim the South Africa file could make (there, Meswak had grey flow proving demand; here it has silence). The self-teardown's sentence: the brand owes this island an experiment, not a promise.

3.1 Colgate — the unpriced king

Presumed dominant on regional pattern, generations deep, likely priced in the MUR 40–90 band for 75–100ml by income analogy AUTHORED [!]. Its defence here is habit plus French-format retail relationships. Not attacked: the test's whole design routes AROUND the incumbent — second-tube positioning, community channels, herbal register — so that even total Colgate indifference (the likely response to a one-case test) costs the test nothing.

3.2 Sensodyne and the pharmacy tier

Cross-border listings prove the island's premium-sensitivity demand reaches for EU supply; MedActiv's 15 pharmacies are the likely local home [!] walk. Relevance to the test: the pharmacy tier demonstrates three-digit MUR toothpaste tickets clear — pricing headroom above the test SKUs if they graduate.

3.3 The absent herbal precedent — both warning and instrument

No Himalaya toothpaste, no Patanjali, no herbal private label: nobody has told the story. The South African corridor logic said an empty cell beside a weakening king is an invitation; the Mauritian version must add the humility that an empty cell on a small island may simply be an unprofitable cell. The test instrumentates exactly this: the herbal story gets told ONCE, well, cheaply (the hair wave's coattails), and the shelf answers.

Why the silence datum outweighs the census, for now

A reader could object: with ~215,000 Muslims and a live miswak tradition, why does the deck rate H1 "genuinely uncertain" rather than probable? Because the grey market has already voted, and its vote is informative. The same cross-border and diaspora channels that profitably carry Vatika hair oil to the island carry toothpaste at identical logistics cost — the marginal effort of adding Meswak to an Ubuy basket or a grocer's consolidation order is near zero. That no one has done so suggests the pull, at courier prices, rounds to nil. The counter-reading — that toothpaste's low ticket and high weight make it uniquely unsuited to courier economics, so grey silence measures channel physics rather than demand — is real, and it is exactly the ambiguity a fairly-priced shelf test resolves. The deck holds both readings, weights them, and refuses to let either one masquerade as a finding. This paragraph exists because the programme's worst historical temptation is hearing what it hopes in ambiguous data; writing the ambiguity down is the cheap vaccine.

C5 · Register architecture, oral edition

Register one — herbal-familiar (H2's voice): French copy in the plain register the state's own Ayurvedic services legitimised — «le dentifrice ayurvédique» needs no exoticism on this island; the second-tube frame keeps it beside, not against, the family's Colgate. Register two — community-respect (H1's voice): JUM certification visible on pack, Plaine Verte trade served through its own shopkeepers, the miswak lineage referenced with the same restraint the Gulf decks codified — and NO national devotional theatre for a 17–18% community. Register three — the hair coattail: the Divali wave and the fairness story carry the toothpaste case physically (same container, same shelf talkers' family) without a dedicated campaign. The refusals: no health claims on a data-free epidemiology; no anti-Colgate comparison; no spend that a one-case test cannot justify — the whole comms budget is shelf presence, pack design, and one community activation.

The community etiquette, stated before the register

Before any register work, one etiquette paragraph the activation must obey. The Mauritian Muslim community is not a market segment discovered by this deck — it is a 190-year-old trading community with its own institutions (JUM among them), its own commercial memory, and a long experience of being marketed AT during its own festivals. The difference between H1 succeeding and H1 offending is procedural: certification sought from JUM before launch, not after complaint; the activation voiced by the community's own shopkeepers with the brand as supplier, not narrator; Ramadan timing set by the community calendar's own authorities; and zero borrowing of religious language in any brand-authored copy — the pack carries the mark and the lineage, and the mark speaks for itself. This is the Gulf files' scholar-gate discipline, scaled to an island where the relevant scholars are a short drive from the distributor's office — which makes cutting the corner both easier and less forgivable.

The channel doctrine in one paragraph

The test's channels are chosen for signal purity, not reach — the inversion of every other deck's logic. Way plus a Winners subset gives H2 a clean mainstream read precisely BECAUSE the sample is small and observable: a dozen doors where the distributor's sell-out data and a quarterly manual census can attribute every tube. The community channel gives H1 its own uncontaminated instrument — if Meswak moves in Plaine Verte but not at Winners, that is a finding, not a failure, and the graduate path it implies (community-first depth, mainstream later) is different from H2's. The channels NOT used matter as much: no pharmacy tier (its premium halo would flatter the test), no e-commerce push (cross-border noise would blur origin of demand), no Intermart French-format placement (wrong register for both hypotheses). A test's channel map is its laboratory bench; contamination costs more than coverage gains.

C11 · The flag-moves

DEEP THINK — why a test deck is not a small campaign deck. The temptation is to write Mauritius oral as a miniature of South Africa's corridor thesis. The discipline says otherwise: SA had grey-flow demand proof, a priced ladder, and a duopoly with a visible empty cell — evidence enough to plan a LISTING. Mauritius oral has silence where grey demand should be, zero price data, and hypotheses instead of segments — evidence enough only to plan an EXPERIMENT. The difference cashes out in design: a campaign optimises for share; a test optimises for INFORMATION PER RUPEE — two SKUs not five, no promotions ever, channels chosen for signal cleanliness (community shops answer H1; the hair-adjacent shelf answers H2), and a pre-registered read-out date with all three outcomes written down before the container sails. Second-order: if H3 wins, the programme banks a rule ("heritage equity does not automatically cross category aisles") worth more than a small market's toothpaste revenue. Kill-test: if even the test's tiny economics fail the annex check (HS 3306 excluded from CECPA AND the stack goes negative), the test shrinks to the community channel alone — H1 answered for hundreds of dollars, H2 deferred.
DEEP STRAT — the read-out protocol. Test design in five commitments: (1) DURATION — four quarters, spanning one Divali and one Ramadan, because both hypotheses have seasonal pulses; (2) SIGNALS — repeat-purchase rate (the only metric that matters at test scale; trial is vanity), basket attachment to hair SKUs, community-shop reorder cadence; (3) THRESHOLDS — pre-registered: H1 confirmed at ≥30% repeat in community channel; H2 confirmed at ≥20% hair-basket attachment; both numbers AUTHORED and honest about it, to be sanity-checked against the distributor's category norms at appointment [!]; (4) THE MINUTE — at M12+30 days, a one-page verdict goes on the record: graduate (build the range), persist (one more year, one variable changed), or exit (shelf withdrawn, lesson exported); (5) NO MID-TEST RESCUES — no promotions, no range additions, no panic media; a rescued test is a corrupted test. The protocol's own export: this five-commitment shape is the programme's template for every future sub-scale category question.

Reading the playbook as one sentence

Compressed: put two honest tubes on the shelves the hair range already earned, certify the one the community deserves, tell the story once in each register, and let twelve months answer what no desk research on earth currently can. The graduate path, sketched so the test knows its own upside

A test without a pre-drawn success path wastes its own good news, so here is what GRADUATE means, sketched at the fidelity a minute can trigger. If H1 clears: the community channel deepens first — the twin-pack T3 activates, Meswak's facings extend through the island's Muslim-owned trade with JUM's mark doing the introductions, and the pharmacy tier opens as the premium rung the Sensodyne evidence says exists. If H2 clears: Red graduates to the full Winners network and the Intermart/Super U wave, the second-tube platform gets its first paid media (still modest — this island's whole media plan is a rounding error), and a third SKU (a kids' or gum-care variant) joins at the next container. If BOTH clear: Mauritius becomes the programme's only market where the oral range grew out of evidence rather than analogy — and the read-out minute becomes the template pitch for the category in Réunion and the Seychelles, where the same Franco-Indian-Ocean retail grammar applies. None of this is promised; all of it is pre-authorised, so that a clean positive read converts to action inside one quarter instead of waiting for a strategy cycle.

What this deck teaches the batch

The read-out protocol and the test-vs-campaign doctrine table are the exports, and their reach is wider than toothpaste. Batch 8's remaining files each contain at least one category where evidence quality collapses below campaign-grade — Zambia's skin file, Mozambique's oral file, Angola's everything — and until now the programme's only honest options were to overclaim or to punt. The Mauritian test deck supplies the third option: a fully specified experiment with pre-registered outcomes, coattail economics, and a governance table that makes corruption visible. The five-commitment shape (duration, signals, thresholds, minute, no-rescues) plus the T11b governance rules travel as a unit. One caution travels with them: the protocol works only where SOME infrastructure already carries the marginal cost — Mauritius oral rides hair's container and buyers; a naked test in a market with no sibling category would buy its information at campaign prices, and should usually just wait.

C16 · What does not transplant

Nearly everything, and the deck says so. South Africa's corridor thesis stays home (no priced ladder, no duopoly, no grey demand proof); the GCC's Ramadan platform stays home in its scholarly-gated grandeur (one community activation, shopkeeper-voiced, is this island's whole devotional register); Qatar's kids-and-caries angle stays home (no epidemiology to arm it); and even the batch's own fairness launch stays with hair (a test does not spend unrepeatable stories). What arrives: the read-out protocol itself — pre-registered thresholds, no mid-test rescues, the minute — which is this deck's contribution TO the programme rather than a borrowing from it. Mauritius oral is the file where the programme demonstrates it can want an answer more than it wants a launch.

The register's one landmine, defused in advance

Dabur Red's Indian marketing history includes strength-and-tradition tropes that translate poorly into a French-inflected island register — the panel's first task is not translation but TONALITY: the second-tube frame must sound like a pharmacist's suggestion, not a heritage sermon. The test's comms are deliberately under-written for exactly this reason; a pack, a talker and a shopkeeper's word leave less room for tonal error than a campaign would, and whatever voice the read-out's graduates earn will be built on twelve months of listening rather than a launch-week guess.

The bet's shape, for the board. This bet is unusual: it does not bet on the test SUCCEEDING — it bets on the test being RUN HONESTLY and read on time, because at this scale the programme's asset is its own epistemic discipline. A graduated Meswak line by 2029 would be pleasant; a clean, minuted null would be nearly as valuable, priced across every future market where the herbal-oral question arises. The one outcome the bet treats as failure is the familiar human one: the test that was never really run — promoted mid-way, rescued quietly, read late, or left unminuted.

A final word on scale and self-respect: it would be easy to read this deck as the programme's least ambitious — two tubes, one case, an island. The opposite reading is truer. Every other deck asks the market a question the file mostly already answered; this one asks a question nobody on earth currently knows the answer to, prices the asking at a rounding error, and binds itself in advance to believe whatever comes back. If the programme's forty-two files teach a method and not just forty-two markets, this small deck is the method at its barest: evidence graded honestly, claims sized to evidence, and the discipline to prefer a true "no" over a flattering "maybe".
