C1 · Executive summary — the one screen

So what. UAE hair care is a US$430–445M market growing 4–6% — but its luxury tier is growing 11.6% CAGR (US$255M → $550M by 2030), and the single most galling fact in this deck is who is monetising it: Gisou sells honey hair oil at AED 310/100ml and Fable & Mane sells Dabur's own champi ritual at ~AED 157 through Sephora — a 10–45× per-ml premium over the Dabur Amla bottle (AED 6–8/100ml) that taught the region the ritual. Dabur's fortress is real (claimed #1 in oils and hair creams, Hammam Zaith #1 in KSA/Kuwait and growing double-digit, coop-to- Carrefour distribution, RAK proximity economics) — and it is capped: the Vatika/Amla marque codes "trusted value", the shelf tops out at ~AED 25, and per-unit brand contribution is AED 1–2. The demand side is pre-sold for a climb: the "Dubai Hair" expat hair-fall folklore and the near-universal hard-water belief support an empty AED 40–150 rung between mass shampoo and the AED 500-filter/ PRP economy; Emirati women spend ~AED 2,550/yr on beauty products and already practice hair bakhoor; #hairoiling runs 170M+ TikTok posts with no brand owning the Arabic تزييت الشعر conversation. CHAMPI 2.0 therefore runs two plays on one chassis: (1) DEFEND — hold the fortress with price-pack hygiene, Hammam Zaith variant velocity, and quick-commerce hero-SKU capture; (2) CLIMB — launch a new-marque premium line (never "Vatika Premium") built on the six-move premiumisation pattern the teardowns prove: one hero ritual elixir (amla × sidr honey × black seed, oud-compatible fragrance), visitable provenance, paid in-salon champi/hammam-zaith service as the trial engine (Tips & Toes anchor), a Sephora-ME or Faces exclusivity window, price integrity (never below −20%), Arabic-first storytelling («أملج الذي ذكره ابن سينا»). Clocks: Diwali 2026 (diaspora premiumisation) → Ramadan 2027 (Khaleeji recode). Ask: marque workstream + salon program (AED 1.5–2.5M) + Tips & Toes negotiation mandate.

C4 · Consumer & culture — need-states, cohorts, occasions

So what: the UAE hair consumer is pre-sold on the problem (Dubai Hair, hard water) and pre-trained in the solution (oiling — by heritage for 62% of the market, by TikTok for the rest). What no brand has done is connect the two at the right altitude with the right fragrance codes: Emirati women will not adopt an oil that fights their bakhoor layering; South Asian professionals want the ritual without the "aunty bottle"; hijabi/sheila wearers want scalp comfort claims. All three are formulation-and-register problems CHAMPI 2.0's heroes are built to solve.
