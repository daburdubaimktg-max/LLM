M1 — Demography, geography & the scoping decision

The Democratic Republic of Congo is not a bigger Congo-Brazzaville; it is a different category of country. 112.8M people (2025), median age 16.9, 45.9% under fifteen — the youngest large population on earth — urbanising at 44.5% with Kinshasa at 17M+ and on trajectory to be Africa's largest megacity by 2030. The deck's first decision is therefore not whether to enter but WHERE the country is, commercially: and the honest answer is that for a new FMCG entrant in 2026, "DRC" means the western corridor — Kinshasa, Kongo Central (the Matadi/Banana port axis), and later Katanga's Lubumbashi — while the east (Goma and Bukavu, under M23 control since early 2025) is a separate, currently non-serviceable commercial geography with no road/rail linkage to the west. This deck states the scoping in print: the east is out of scope for the entry horizon, national statistics are read as blends of a functioning western spine and a conflict-degraded east, and no plan line assumes eastern distribution. What remains in scope is still enormous: Kinshasa alone is three times the population of Congo-B and Gabon combined, runs ~400 markets with ~1M traders, and shares a language, a music and a river with Brazzaville — the "Kinshasa doctrine" written in the Congo-B file (shared Lingala/rumba creative, never shared logistics) now cashes in the other direction: the small bank taught; the big one enrols (strategist read on agent evidence, 8 Jul 2026).

M2 — Economy & THE GIANT THAT PAYS IN COINS

The defining economic finding: the batch's giant is also its poorest consumer. GDP per capita runs $871 (2025) rising toward $1,122 (2026, IMF vintage); 69% of the population sits below the national poverty line (INS 2024) and 81.1% below the $3/day international line (World Bank 2025); the FinScope survey's bleakest datum — 71% of respondents sometimes forgo food, medical care or school fees for lack of money — is the affordability wall stated in consumers' own lives. Yet the aggregate is huge and growing: nominal GDP somewhere between $79–123bn (sources conflict — carried as a range), mining growing 10.1% (2025) with cobalt at ~70% of world supply and copper+cobalt at 90–95% of exports, non-mining growth ~3.1%, blended outlooks above 5% through 2028. And the price environment just turned favourable: inflation collapsed from 17.7% (2024) to 7.5% (2025), the central bank cut its policy rate from 25% to 17.5%, and the state stopped monetising the deficit. The consumer read for Dabur is the Congo-B sachet doctrine at 20× scale: the mass market is coin-denominated, sachet-shaped and enormous — Kinshasa's inequality is rising (Gini 32.4→40.2, 2012–2018), so a thin premium tier exists too, but the entry's centre of gravity is the 100–500-FCDF-class purchase repeated a hundred million times. The one growth caveat: mining royalties, not consumer income, drive the boom — the coin economy grows on urbanisation and population, not on wages (strategist read; GDP range carried, not resolved).

M3 — Currency, payments & THE DOLLAR'S EVICTION NOTICE

Two currency stories run at once, and both are strategy-grade. The first is the reversal: the CDF depreciated steadily to an all-time low near 2,900/USD (Aug 2025), then appreciated over 20% to ~2,280–2,313 by mid-2026 as inflation collapsed and confidence returned — an unusual print for DRC that makes 2026 a favourable pricing window but must never be anchored as a trend [!] re-check at every pricing decision. The second is the eviction notice: DRC retail is 60% dollarised at street level and ~90% in the banking system — and the state has decreed the end of that world: from April 2027, foreign-currency CASH transactions are banned, with the BCC becoming the sole authorised importer of physical dollars (decision of 9 Apr 2026). Whatever the enforcement reality proves to be, the planning consequence is absolute: every Dabur contract, price list and collections design carries a CDF contingency from day one — USD-linked distributor terms (today's default, and the structure whose absence helped kill Shoprite's DRC venture) are a regulatory risk inside the entry horizon. The rails that survive either currency world: mobile money (M-Pesa 51% / Airtel 31% / Orange 17%; 6.4M+ active M-Pesa users; Visa Pay interconnection launched 2026) and the cambiste street layer that already arbitrates every retail price. Banking reaches ~39% of adults (2022, from 3% in 2011) — better than the programme feared, worse than any plan should lean on. The prepaid-EUR discipline from the CEMAC pair translates here as prepaid-hard-currency at the import node + CDF-native architecture at retail (strategist read on agent evidence).

M4 — Politics, governance & the war's true reach

Three layers. The war: M23 took Goma and Bukavu in early 2025 and still holds them; the Washington track (DRC–Rwanda presidential signing, 4 Dec 2025) and the Doha track (DRC–AFC/M23 framework, 14 Nov 2025) both exist and both stall — Rwandan troops had not withdrawn as of the latest reviews, front lines remain active, and the sixth oversight meeting (London, 24 Jun 2026) expressed "serious concern." Its reach into the west is INDIRECT: no logistics linkage touches the Kinshasa–Matadi corridor, but the war consumes the presidency's bandwidth, strains the budget, and colours the national mood every campaign must respect (no militarism, no eastern imagery, no cheap unity messaging). The constitutional clock: Tshisekedi's implausible 73% (Dec 2023), the Senate's 89–0 referendum-enabling vote (15 Jun 2026) and his own warnings that the war could postpone the December 2028 elections mean the political calendar itself is a risk variable — watched quarterly, never speculated upon in print. The governance floor: CPI 20/100 (rank 163/182, unchanged), 88% of citizens perceiving official corruption, ~85% of firms operating informally, and a parafiscal customs stack that institutionalises friction — the operating posture is the programme's standing one, hardened: every payment documented, every clearance through named licensed brokers, zero informal facilitation, and the IGF-era tax-collection improvement noted as the one genuine positive trend (operating read on agent evidence).

M5 — Trade, customs & the two-port clock

The tariff architecture is lighter at the top than CEMAC's — a three-tier 5/10/20% structure with finished consumer goods at 20% (vs the CEMAC 30% band) — but the PARAFISCAL stack is heavier: OCC at 1.5% of CIF plus lab fees, FPI, FONER and OGEFREM levies layered at clearance, a cosmetics excise running 3–40% by product (with hydroquinone/mercury-bearing lines explicitly outside the standard category — the tax code itself encodes the clean/dirty cosmetics distinction), and mandatory BIVAC pre-shipment inspection for everything ≥$2,500 FOB — every Dabur container triggers it, so inspection lead-times and fees are COGS, not contingency. Logistics is a two-port clock: today, Matadi — a river port 365km from Kinshasa by rail/road, ~45–50 days ocean transit from Asia; from early 2027, the Banana deep-water port (DP World + Mota-Engil, $250M phase one signed Mar 2025; 600m quay, 18m draft, 450,000 TEU/yr; dredging phase one complete, completion slipped to early 2027) — the structural unlock the CEMAC pair never offered. The entry stages accordingly: pilot volumes via Matadi in H1 2027, scale volumes timed to Banana. The India lane is real but industrial (pharma, machinery — 4.6% of DRC imports, 2023; the SEZs reportedly Indian-dominated [!] ANAPI verification), with a 9,000-strong Indian community already running pharma warehousing — the connective tissue exists; the consumer lane is Dabur's to build (trade read; assumptions printed).

M6 — Regulation & THE HEAVIEST PAPER WALL IN THE BATCH

The registration story inverts Gabon's headline: the lane Gabon wrote into law, DRC routes through the drug agency. Under the 2015 AMM decree, cosmetics, medical devices AND food supplements all clear the same DPM pathway as pharmaceuticals — GMP-inspected sites, French-language dossiers, 90–180 days processing, five-year validity — and since 2020 a second authority (ACOREP, operational from Nov 2022, expanding provincially) holds an overlapping mandate over the same categories: which agency is today's actual gatekeeper is an unresolved architecture question, and answering it is the first regulatory task on the ground, before any dossier is drafted. Three consequences follow. First, the OTC food-lane doctrine does NOT transfer — supplements are AMM-class here, so the DRC OTC deck must re-derive its lanes from scratch (confectionery-class food SKUs may still route lighter — the G1 counsel question). Second, timing: 90–180-day statutory processing against an untested two-agency handoff means registration STARTS at G1, in parallel with everything else. Third, the 2006 lightening-products ban — programme lore since the Congo-B file — could not be verified as a citable decree this pass: the tax code's hydroquinone carve-out proves the state DISTINGUISHES, but the ban text, authority and enforcement record are a declared gap with the French query queued. Labelling: French, per the AMM decree's own requirement; no separate consumer-labelling statute found (gap) (regulatory read on agent evidence).

M7 — Retail, channels & the Shoprite lesson

Modern trade is genuinely contested here — new to the batch: Kin Marché leads (30+ stores, ~20% share, built from one outlet since 2004), Carrefour arrived July 2025 via a Hyper Psaro franchise (6,300+ SKUs in the first hypermarket, the rest of Psaro's stores converting), City Market fights on, and Shoprite is the cautionary tale — exited November 2022 after a decade, citing currency volatility, inflation, import duties and USD-denominated rents: the exact quartet this deck's architecture is designed against (CDF contingencies; prepaid import node; duty-priced ladders; no USD-fixed obligations). Below modern trade sits the real volume engine: ~400 markets, ~1M traders — Grand Marché (72.5ha), Marché de la Liberté, Gambela, Matete — with documented elite capture of market governance (a distributor-economics fact, not a moral footnote). The import houses are old and real: Beltexco (1922), Socimex (1998), Orgaman [!] scale, plus Marsavco distributing its own lines. Pharmacy runs through a certified-register system (the VillageReach répertoire) under Shalina's shadow. E-commerce is thin (kinmarche.com, CasaKonnect, likonzi.com; Jumia's DRC status itself unclear — gap); WhatsApp/Facebook social commerce is the dominant digital retail mode, which means the programme's printed-price catalogue discipline arrives pre-adapted (channel read on agent evidence).

M8 — Competition, incumbents & THE RAWJI PRECEDENT

The competitive map's most strategically loaded fact is a century old: Marsavco — the country's largest soap, detergent and margarine maker, ex-Unilever's first international manufacturing operation, now the Rawji Group's — is an Indian family house, founded on trading roots laid in 1910. The precedent cuts every way at once: it proves Indian enterprise can own Congolese FMCG at national scale, it offers a potential toll-manufacturing partner with real (if grid-constrained) capacity, and it means the "Indian brand" register in Kinshasa is already NORMAL — not exotic, not suspect, simply present. Shalina is at home here: #1 pharma, 28 branches (6 in Kinshasa, newest at Bibwa), 250+ SKUs, ~$309M group revenue [!] private figures — in Congo-B it was the shadow incumbent supplied informally; here it is the legitimate king of the affordable-OTC lane, and the DRC OTC deck must position AROUND it, not against it. Angel Cosmetics manufactures in Gombe (the Caro White/Caro Light ownership link remains unconfirmed — gap); Darling's Kinshasa presence is strong retail/brand with NO confirmable factory (the carried correction — manufacturing is Lagos-centred, SFMC is CI-registered); the multinationals (Colgate, Unilever, P&G) are asserted at category level but their DRC shelf reality is THE declared audit gap. Dabur's own absence is total — and uniquely here, the absence sits beside a hundred-year-old Indian commercial establishment that makes the introduction easier than anywhere else in the programme (structural read on agent evidence).

M9 — Culture & register (pre-read for the category decks)

Five registers, three of them inherited from the Congo-B file and now cashed at scale. The church is the trust network: >95% Christian, with the Kimbanguist church institutionally embedded (it helps nominate CENI's leadership) and the Catholic church the state's loudest critic — the programme's standing rule (the pulpit is never bought) matters more here than anywhere, because the temptation will be constant. La SAPE is home: Kinshasa is the other sapeur capital (Papa Wemba's city), so the Congo-B hair deck's «chez ta coiffeuse» ceiling-geometry RETURNS — accompany the sapeur, never costume him — after Gabon retired it; elegance-as-dignity is the native grooming register. The nappy movement has infrastructure: Josepha Cosmetics (2015), the Pullman meet-up (Oct 2016), live Nappy accounts — the texture-neutrality law arrives with a decade of local history to respect on both sides (the bifurcation is old here). The bleaching register runs to escalation: hydroquinone, mercury, household bleach and glutathione injections are all documented in Kinshasa practice — the tone-neutrality doctrine faces its largest-scale test, with «tshoko» carried as the (unvalidated) Lingala term and the whole lexical gate rebuilt for Kinshasa Lingala per the now-standing rule. And the median age is 16.9: every register above is being renegotiated by people younger than the brands themselves — the creative centre of gravity is a Lingala-speaking teenager in a city of seventeen million (register read; Lingala overlaps with the Congo-B base marked, native validation queued).

M10 — Risk board & the four clocks

Four clocks tick. The de-dollarisation clock (April 2027): the entry's contracts are built for it from day one — this is the risk the deck can fully engineer away, and the Shoprite autopsy shows the cost of not doing so. The constitutional clock (referendum push → Dec 2028): watched quarterly; the operating posture is total political non-adjacency. The port clock (Banana early 2027, already slipped once): pilot volumes never depend on it; scale volumes always do. The war clock: currently a western non-event operationally, but the risk board carries the tail scenario (escalation toward the fiscal base → FX shock → the 2023–25 depreciation era returns), which is why the two-column FX instrument runs in every DRC waterfall. The standing risks — counterfeit convergence (a corridor that EXPORTS counterfeits to its neighbours, per the batch's own seizure record), parafiscal creep, electricity (<17% access; daily load-shedding even in Kinshasa — no local-manufacturing assumption without audited backup power) — carry the programme's standard controls (risk read).
