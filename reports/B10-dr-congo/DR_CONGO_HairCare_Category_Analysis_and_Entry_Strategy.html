C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: BUILD — "sell the care under the kabelo." Three findings shape it. First, the spend is already enormous and already rhythmic: Kinshasa women buy 2–10 mèche packages every two weeks (CDF 10,000–16,000 biweekly), the install cycle runs 2–4 weeks, demand peaks June–September and December, and the human hair itself — «kabelo», the Kinshasa word for the trade — is imported from India, China and Vietnam: the wallet, the rhythm and even the India-as-hair-origin signal (the Gabon deck's exhibit, reproduced here at megacity scale) all pre-exist. Second, the care shelf under that installed hair is a vacuum: the scrape found a $5 shampoo floor, unbranded growth oils, a castor-oil actives tier at $5–35 where growth claims command 3× premiums, chébé arriving as a Chadian import, ROZINO beard cream at $5 per 20g proving premium-per-gram tolerance — and NO branded care system: Darling sells fibre with WhatsApp-gated prices, Zuri sells wig care at Carrefour, the rest of the Godrej bloc is invisible, ORS has no DRC distribution, Marsavco makes no hair products and Angel's hair line is an afterthought. Dabur builds the category the extensions economy forgot: scalp care under the install, braid-down care between installs, wash-day systems, and the actives-oil tier where Amla's story outguns anonymous castor — while refusing the mèche fibre itself (China-direct economics; the Gabon rule holds). Third, the texture politics are asymmetric here: the nappy pole is documented and STIGMATISED (a university disciplining an afro; banking dress codes; «being an artist, a crazy person or a drug addict»), while the relaxer-revival pole that shaped Brazzaville's dual-SKU law is unconfirmed on this bank — so ORS leads with its braid/scalp/leave-in lines through the salon network, its relaxer kits SEQUENCED BEHIND the audit rather than assumed, and the texture-neutrality law holds with Kinshasa-specific force: in a city that punishes natural hair professionally, a brand that serves every texture without comment is not neutral decoration — it is the safest strong position available (strategist read on agent evidence, 8 Jul 2026).

C2 — Market size & growth

Sizing honestly: no DRC hair-care market value exists publicly (the continental CFA-3,000bn extensions figure is a CFA-zone aggregate and is NOT used — flagged to prevent misuse); the deck sizes by wallet rhythm and channel structure. The wallet: 2–10 mèche packages biweekly at CDF 1,000–9,000 per package by district, braiding labour from CDF 10,000 (basic) through $10–50 (extension styles), salon tiers marking up 2–3× over street vendors, stylists earning $50–70 on weekdays and $100–120 on event Saturdays — a hair economy whose LABOUR layer alone out-earns most of the batch's category shelves. The district-arbitrage table is the structural map: the same mèche package at 1,800 (Lemba) / 2,500 (Bandal) / 5,000+ (Gombe) means district, not brand, is the current price-discrimination lever — a market organised by geography because no brand system has organised it by trust. The care layer's thinness is the growth story: a $5 shampoo floor, a 3× anti-chute premium, castor at $5–35, no sachet tier found (the macro's sachet-first architecture meets a category where the compression play is genuinely unbuilt — the sub-$5, sub-CDF-10,000 care entry is white space, not a fight). Growth logic: ride the install rhythm (care products purchased ON the braid cycle, at the braid-down and the re-install), brand the actives tier, and let the men's market's documented price deflation (haircuts CDF 5,000→3,000, Apr 2025) calibrate the coin economy's realism (strategist read).

C3 — Consumer & ritual decode

Three registers govern. First, the install ritual is the category's clock: the 2–4-week braid cycle structures when hair is touched, what it needs (scalp access under the install; repair at braid-down; prep at re-install) and when money moves — Dabur's care system is designed AS cycle stages, not as generic SKUs. Second, texture is political and the politics are punitive: Kinshasa's nappy movement has a decade of infrastructure and a live stigma record — a university called an afro «indecent», banks are described as dreadlock-hostile, and the phrase that survived into print («une artiste, une dingue ou une droguée») shows what natural hair still costs professionally. The Brazzaville dual-SKU law arrives here with an asymmetry: the relaxer revival that justified Brazzaville's other pole is UNCONFIRMED on this bank, so the law's Kinshasa form is serve every texture, advertise none against another, and never make the stigma worse by "celebrating courage" — patronising the nappy pole is as banned as ranking it. Third, the occasion grammar is triple: the church Sunday (weekly, low-ticket, «impeccable» — served commercially, never borrowed spiritually: the pulpit rule stands), the event Saturday (the stylist's $100–120 days), and the three-ceremony wedding (civil + coutumier + religieux, each a separately priced hair occasion at $70–80) — three recurring demand spikes no care brand currently programs against. The vernacular register is already written: «coiffure kitoko», the pride-in-craft hashtag language brands ride organically — Lingala warmth exists in the market's own voice, and the native-validation gate decides what Dabur may borrow of it (register read; kabelo confirmed, nsuki/suki variant flagged for the native pass).

C4 — Competitor teardown (Module 1)

The teardown finds a one-brand shelf and a vacuum. Darling is the only organised hair brand visibly working Kinshasa: two Facebook operations, an Instagram, TikTok co-branding with Brazzaville content (the shared-creative doctrine practised by the incumbent!), a physical shop on the 15ème rue — and NO published prices: even the market leader sells through ask-seller opacity, which means no brand in this market has yet spent the money to build price trust. Zuri holds a boutique beachhead (Carrefour shelf + a Luxury Hair Bar) at the top. The rest of the Godrej bloc returned nothing — absence or unindexed, the audit decides. ORS has no DRC distribution (a French cross-border e-tailer's shipping list is not a channel), Angel's hair line is a gel-and-shampoo afterthought beside its lightening-led skin business, Marsavco makes no hair products, and the naturalista tier's most interesting player — Mango Butterfull, with an actual Kinshasa production unit and the Dingaya salon — proves local manufacture of natural hair care is feasible at boutique scale. The structural conclusion: the extensions economy has brands; the care economy has listings — unbranded oils, bundles and claims. Dabur does not enter a fight; it enters a vacancy with the only purpose-built ethnic-care house (ORS) that the market's own supply chains never brought here (structural read on agent evidence).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

Five positions. «La mèche du jour» — Darling's and the kabelo trade's: refused. «Le luxe capillaire» — Zuri's Carrefour-and-bar niche: not contested. «Le naturel militant» — the nappy collectives' and Mango Butterfull's: accompanied with oils, co-narrated, never claimed, never patronised (the stigma makes cheerleading dangerous in both directions). «Le soin sérieux» (the serious care system — sealed, dosed, printed prices, cycle-staged) is EMPTY: Dabur's seat, taken by ORS's care lines with Vatika/Amla in the actives tier. And «le prix qui ne ment pas» (the price that doesn't lie) is this market's version of the honest register: in an economy where the same package costs three prices by district and the leader gates its prices behind WhatsApp, the brand that PRINTS its prices — on pack, in catalogue frames, at POS — buys a trust position no incumbent has paid for (strategist read).
