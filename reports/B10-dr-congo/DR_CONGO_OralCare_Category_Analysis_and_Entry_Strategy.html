C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: WEDGE — "the coin format Beltexco forgot, the clove the pharmacopeia already knows." Three findings force the shape. First, the shelf is one house holding both ends: Beltexco — the century-old distribution giant — runs Colgate Triple Action as the self-described "Congolese reference" AND Maxam, the Chinese value brand whose Black & White charcoal SKU already sits in the value-natural seat; Unilever exited in 2009, so the usual second multinational simply isn't there. The consequence is structural: the charcoal wedge that led three batch markets is OCCUPIED here by the incumbent's own value brand — so charcoal RIDES as a variant and the wedge re-derives. Second, the coin economy's capital has no coin format: no sachet or small-format toothpaste could be found in Kinshasa — while Nigeria's 5g sachets prove the format works in African mass markets and the affordability math explains why it's needed here: an annual supply costs only ~0.17 labour-days at the new formal SMIG (analyst estimate), but 69–81% of the population lives below the poverty line where the constraint is DAILY CASH-FLOW LUMPINESS — the 2,000-CDF tube is not expensive, it is indivisible. The sachet-first macro architecture finds its purest application: Dabur builds the coin rung the incumbent house never bothered with. Third — the deck's headline — Dabur Red takes its first lead in the programme. In every prior market Red waited wave 2 because clove had no local schema and education was too expensive. Kinshasa inverts both premises: toothpaste is on the national Essential Medicines List (fluoride AND non-fluoride, NEML 2020 — the state itself classifies the tube as health infrastructure), eugenol — clove's active — is a listed DRC pharmacopeia ingredient for dental analgesic use, care is pain-driven (30.1% of caries cases at a Kinshasa clinic presented with complications; root-canal treatment costs $120–200 against $2.4–3.1/day incomes — the toothache is managed at home because the alternative is unpayable), and the glossary already contains the need in three words: lino mpasi, the tooth hurts. The clove-therapeutic register needs no education budget here — the state wrote the schema; Dabur Red arrives speaking it (strategist read on agent evidence, 8 Jul 2026).

C2 — Market size & growth

Sizing honestly: no market value exists; the deck sizes by burden, band and void. The burden: 40.2% of Congolese carry some oral/dental disease (periodontal ~70% of those, caries ~50% — the PNSBD director's own 2023 numbers), children's caries runs 79–80% (directional), four provinces have zero dentistry services, and the Kinshasa clinical record shows what unmet care looks like: 30.1% of caries patients at the Boyambi clinic arrived with complications, two-thirds of those with apical periodontitis — the tooth-loss pathway — skewed toward the unemployed and the unpaid. The band: Colgate 140g prints at 1,929 CDF (the basic-needs basket) and 2,870 CDF (e-commerce with delivery), bracketing a mass band of ~1,900–2,870 (~$0.85–1.28); Maxam sits under it as the "unbeatable" value tube; Shalina's Flodent and GALAPharma's MALULOR PLUS hold the pharmacy channel; the premium tier (Total, Sensodyne-class) returned no Kinshasa price at all. The void: no sachet, no coin rung, no format under the 140g tube — in the batch market with the MOST coin-dependent consumer. Growth logic: the coin rung grows the category (new users the tube's indivisibility excludes), the Red wedge converts the pain register the health system cannot serve, and the school institution grows brushing frequency itself — the only multiplier that changes the 40.2% (strategist read).

C3 — Consumer & ritual decode

Three registers. First, the pain register is the category's true entry point: where root-canal care costs two months of median income, the toothache is a household event managed with what the boutique sells — and the language already holds it (lino mpasi). Clove-for-toothache is not an exotic story here; eugenol sits in the national pharmacopeia as a dental analgesic, meaning the association Dabur Red monetises is one the health system itself teaches. The creative rule that follows: Red sells RELIEF-ADJACENT DAILY CARE («la pâte au clou de girofle — the clove the dentists use»), never treatment claims — the EML's proximity makes over-claiming both illegal and unnecessary. Second, the chew register is local and named: kongo bololo — the bitter-leaf whose shoots Kongo-Central communities use to brush teeth — is the programme's first DOCUMENTED local chew-plant register (Libya's was unmeasurable, Congo-B's measured-absent, Gabon's undocumented): Miswak still parks (Salvadora has no root here), but the kongo bololo bridge enters the glossary and the PR file as the local truth that a bitter plant cleaning teeth is ancestral knowledge — saluted, never claimed on a pack. Third, the diastema note: nzela ya mino — the gap between teeth — is culturally prized; whitening-and-straightening perfection vocabulary imported from global oral care can misfire against a local aesthetic that values character over uniformity: the casting-and-lighting rule from the skin file extends to SMILES — real Kinshasa smiles, gaps and all, in every asset (register read; Lingala items dictionary-sourced, native pass queued).
