C1 · Category frame — one owner, two hair businesses, two questions

Dabur's US hair position is two businesses that must be analysed separately because they share nothing — not consumer, not channel, not risk profile, not even a name in public. Wing one: Namaste/ORS — a Chicago-manufactured, mass-distributed Black haircare franchise (ORS Olive Oil, HAIRepair, HAIRestore, Curls Unleashed — all confirmed live on orshaircare.com and Walmart) whose legacy core (no-lye relaxers) is in structural decline and named litigation. Its question is defensive: how does the franchise transition its equity from the relaxer era to the natural era while the MDL runs? Wing two: the Vatika/Amla import tail — diaspora-grocery and Amazon-sold Indian hair oils that turn out, on census inspection, to have quietly crossed over: the flagship Amla listings carry 14,364–14,670 ratings at 4.2–4.4★ (aggregator-derived, live re-pull queued) — volumes far beyond a diaspora niche. Its question is expansive: the US mainstream just adopted hair oiling as a ritual (#hairoiling: 785M+ TikTok views by Apr 2024, with US media explicitly crediting the practice's Ayurvedic roots), prestige brands monetise that wave at 10–20× Dabur's unit price — what is the value player's winning move inside a wave it did not start but has always owned the substance of?

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown — what the last decade of US textured + heritage hair actually taught

1 · Mielle Organics × P&G — the breakout and the boycott, one lesson each

The breakout: Rosemary Mint Scalp & Hair Strengthening Oil went from creator post (Alix Earle, 27 Dec 2022, 3M+ followers) to 42.7M cumulative TikTok views and a national stockout — Amazon third-party resellers repriced $9.99 → $29. Proof one hair-oil SKU can carry a brand through a platform wave. The boycott: P&G's acquisition (announced 11 Jan 2023, weeks after the viral peak) triggered a genuine "sellout" backlash in the Black beauty community — ownership identity is a live purchase criterion in this category, and corporate acquisition of a community brand carries a tax. Both lessons price directly into this deck: wing two's wave play is SKU-led (the Mielle pattern), and wing one's ownership profile (Indian-multinational-owned, neither Black- nor Korean-owned in a channel where ~10,000 beauty-supply doors are ~70% Korean-American-owned and fewer than 400 Black-owned) is handled with quiet operational excellence, never with borrowed community language.

2 · Carol's Daughter / L'Oréal — the divestment signal

L'Oréal acquired the founder-led brand in 2014 (net sales >$27M then) and in 2025 divested it back to founder Lisa Price — a major strategic exiting Black haircare after a ten-year hold, at the exact moment the relaxer litigation reprices the category's risk. For the estate this is the live comparable: strategics are actively re-deciding whether legacy Black-haircare positions still fit. The deck's read for Namaste (public data only): the franchise's transition assets (Curls Unleashed's natural positioning, Chicago manufacture, mass shelf) argue for transition over exit — but the Carol's Daughter precedent makes "hold by default" indefensible; the hold must be an argued decision, revisited after the mid-2027 bellwethers (macro option A/E boundary).

3 · Shea Moisture / Unilever — the scaled survivor

The 2017 Sundial acquisition (reported ~$1.6bn, never officially disclosed — flagged) remains in Unilever's portfolio and remains the mass textured-shelf's anchor brand. Its lesson is longevity mechanics: community founder story retained in packaging and voice, mass distribution executed hard, price held accessible. The counter-example to the boycott case — corporate ownership survives when the shelf price and the brand voice don't betray it.

4 · The prestige flank — Fable & Mane, Shaz & Kiks, Ranavat

Sephora's shelf now carries the exact ingredient story Dabur has bottled since 1940 — amla, coconut, bhringraj, neem, shikakai — at $34–60 per unit: Fable & Mane (Sephora's first Ayurvedic sibling-founded brand; HoliRoots/MahaMane franchises; 400% growth in India funding its US push), Shaz & Kiks ($60/8.45oz pre-wash built on twenty Ayurvedic botanicals), Ranavat (amla-hero serum). They monetise the oiling wave at 10–20× Vatika's per-ounce price — and they validated the exact claims-register (pre-wash ritual, scalp care, heritage-told-in-US-grammar) that wing two can use at mass price. The teardown boundary, learned from the macro's Indē Wild case: their entry key was the founder story; Dabur's key is authenticity-at-value — "the original, at the price the ritual was always meant to cost." The deck refuses a Dabur-masthead prestige launch (option D held); it takes the register downmarket instead.

5 · The Doux — the mass-set precedent

An indie textured brand drawing 60–70% of sales from mass retail (Sally, Target, Walmart — Glossy) proves the mass textured set is now an institution, not an experiment. Wing one's Curls Unleashed lives or dies on exactly this shelf; the precedent says the shelf rewards distinct positioning and consistent supply — both within Namaste's control regardless of the MDL's weather.

5b · Sally Beauty and the channel between — a note on the professional layer

One channel deserves a bridging note between the mass set and the beauty-supply wall: Sally Beauty Holdings (~$3.75bn estimated 2025 revenue, ~58% retail / 42% professional via Beauty Systems Group) is both a retailer expanding its textured assortment and a professional distributor reaching stylists — the same dual role Melcom played in Ghana and the pharmacy wholesalers played across Batch 5, in American form. ORS's specific Sally listing status is a named gap (T12 #2), which is itself notable: a 30-year textured-hair franchise whose presence in the category's specialty anchor cannot be confirmed from public search is under-merchandised in the digital layer whatever the physical truth. The stylist tier matters doubly here because the natural-hair era made stylists the category's teachers — the braider-specifier logic the programme built in Ghana applies to wing one intact: the stylist who recommends the curl cream is the wall's real gatekeeper, and stylist education programs are cheaper than shelf fees.

6 · SELF-teardown — the estate's honest mirror

Wing one: real assets (US manufacture, mass relationships, four live lines) and real drags — the legacy core is the named product in plaintiff-firm headlines ("Olive Oil Relaxer Lawsuit" pages); Curls Unleashed is under-reviewed and under-invested (a Walmart page with two ratings is not a transition, it's a placeholder); no public reformulation/response narrative exists (flagged gap — the silence itself is a finding: competitors' MDL postures are documented, Namaste's is not). Wing two: an unmanaged hero (Amla's 14k ratings), an official storefront most SKUs of which have uncaptured metrics, a scent complaint nobody has answered, and zero presence in the natural/prestige channels where its own story trades at multiples. Both wings under-operate their positions. That is the deck's actual finding: the US hair problem is not strategy scarcity — it is operating deficit.

The defend file's public-silence finding

One census absence deserves its own paragraph. L'Oréal's and Unilever's MDL postures are extensively documented in trade and legal press; Namaste's is not — no reformulation statement, no public response narrative, no disclosed reserve beyond the parent's rating-agency note surfaced in this pass. Two readings: low US media attention to the India-owned defendant (plausible), or a genuinely quiet posture (also plausible, and possibly deliberate counsel advice). This deck does not second-guess counsel. What it flags is the STRATEGIC cost of silence as the bellwethers approach: when verdicts make headlines in mid-2027, every defendant's public record will be re-read at once, and the brand with no affirmative story on file (ingredient policy, product evolution, the Curls Unleashed transition told plainly) will have its story written by plaintiff-firm landing pages — several of which already run "Olive Oil Relaxer Lawsuit" URLs. The defend agenda's move 1 and 2 are therefore also communications assets in waiting: a visibly transitioned portfolio is the only MDL-era narrative that needs no adjectives. Preparing it is strategy; deploying it is counsel's call.

The scene that anchors wing one's stakes: a beauty-supply wall in Chicago's south side, where ORS built its name — the relaxer kits still sell to a loyal, shrinking cohort; two shelves over, the natural-care wall grows annually; the store owner restocks whichever moves. Nothing about the MDL changes what she orders — supply reliability, shopper pull and margin do. Wing one's defence is fought on that wall, in those three variables, years before any courtroom resolves anything.

The prestige question, sized once and parked

Before the comms decode, the deck disposes of its most tempting distraction with numbers. The prestige flank's economics are real: $34–60 unit prices, Sephora's traffic, the wave's desire layer. But the entry arithmetic is unforgiving for a masthead FMCG: prestige retail demands exclusive assortment (new SKUs, new packaging, new cost structure), marketing at prestige intensity (creator programs, sampling, retail support commonly consuming 30–40%+ of net sales in launch years — industry norm, flagged as such), and a story the gate's buyers can merchandise — which, for every Ayurveda-coded success on that shelf, has been a founder's face. Dabur's US prestige option therefore prices as: new-brand-build economics with no founder key, against incumbents who arrived first with better keys. Meanwhile the value lane compounds at ~21% operated contribution on subscription packs with zero brand-build spend. The deck parks option D not from timidity but from arithmetic — and notes the one configuration that would reprice it: a partnership or acquisition (macro option E) that brings the founder key with it. That decision belongs to the macro's H2 2027 review, informed by this deck's operated-franchise reads.

DEEP THINK — is the original-at-value position defensible, or just early?

The steelman against the extend play: value positions in waves get commoditized — private label and Amazon-native brands can bottle amla oil tomorrow at $7.99, and the prestige flank owns the desire layer; the original gets squeezed from both ends. The deck's answer, argued: first, the census shows the squeeze has NOT happened in the years the wave has run — the 14k-rating listings faced no private-label displacement visible in this pass, because trust in ingestibles-adjacent tradition products concentrates on provenance (the same force that powers the prestige flank works FOR the 85-year original at $9.99); second, the defensible asset is not the price point but the heritage-plus-badge combination — an official storefront selling the brand US media already credits as the ritual's source, a claim no private label can copy and no prestige brand can undercut; third, the position's true risk is operational neglect (the current state), not competitive attack — which is why every move in T5 is an operating move. If the thesis is wrong, the kill-switches are cheap and the diaspora floor (move 4) never depended on it. Early and defensible are, here, the same thing — provided the estate finally operates what it owns.
