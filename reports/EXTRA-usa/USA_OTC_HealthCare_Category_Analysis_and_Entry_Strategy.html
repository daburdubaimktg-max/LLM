C1 · Category frame — a $13bn market that already buys the herb but not yet the system

The US herbal-supplement market reached $13.23bn in 2024 (+5.4%, second consecutive growth year — ABC HerbalGram #144), and its structure tells Dabur exactly where its door is. Ashwagandha — a Dabur heritage ingredient — is a top-tier mainstream herb at $144.5M (+13.8%) in the mainstream channel alone (~$165.7M with the natural channel), sold at retail pharmacy (46.95% of the global category's channel mix), Kroger (Himalaya's 761 doors), Amazon and TikTok Shop (supplements = the platform's #1 H&B subcategory at $784M). Turmeric leads the natural channel. The ingredients crossed. What has not crossed is the SYSTEM: "Ayurvedic" as a label carries the heavy-metals media file, classical multi-herb formats (the Chyawanprash jar) have no mainstream shelf grammar, and the delivery-format wall is real — the US supplement buyer increasingly expects gummies (a >$4.2bn format) or capsules, not spoonfuls from jars. The deck's design problem, stated once: sell the herbs America already buys, in the formats America already swallows, with paper that outruns the file — while keeping the jar's ritual alive where rituals actually sell.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown

A structural note frames all seven teardowns: the US wellness shelf is unusual in this programme for having no dominant brand at all — a $13bn market whose largest single herb line is a few hundred million dollars, fragmented across thousands of brands, private label, and platform natives. The programme's usual teardown question (how did the winner build its moat?) becomes plural here: each case below proves one LANE viable rather than one brand dominant. That fragmentation is the entrant's friend — no fortress to refuse — and its warning: nothing here compounds without distinctiveness, because the shelf forgives nobody's sameness.

1 · Himalaya Wellness USA — the playbook incumbent, decoded to parts

The Sugar Land, TX operation is the proven Ayurvedic-multinational route into US mainstream wellness, and its parts list is precise: single-herb SKUs (ashwagandha, not multi-herb classicals), USDA-Organic certification as the lead trust badge, "clinically studied" language anchored to specific extracts, caplet format (US-native delivery), and channel patience that culminated in 761 Kroger doors for its organic ashwagandha. What it deliberately is NOT: Ayurveda-forward (the word appears as heritage garnish, never as claim), classical-format (no jars, no pastes), or price-premium (mainstream supplement pricing). The teardown lesson is the macro's register rule made operational: the tradition travels ingredient-first, certification-badged, in local formats. What Himalaya left open: the value-conscious mass tier below its organic pricing, the diaspora's classical formats (it abandoned them for the mainstream — Dabur need not choose), and the creator-commerce lane (its playbook predates TikTok Shop's rise).

2 · Goli — the format winner

Goli built one of the category's fastest franchises on ashwagandha gummies — the herb's crossover moment captured in the format Americans prefer. Its lesson is double-edged: the gummy wall is real (the format converts light users the capsule never reaches), and the gummy shelf is now crowded, private-labelled and margin-compressed — entering it in 2026 means fighting on price against Goli's scale and Kirkland's private label. The deck's format derivation (C6) takes the lesson without the lemming move.

3 · Organic India — the traditional-format survivor

Tulsi tea built a durable US natural-channel business WITHOUT converting to gummies — because tea is a ritual format the US already practices. The precedent matters for Chyawanprash: traditional formats win when they map onto an EXISTING US ritual (tea, matcha, golden-milk lattes), and fail when they demand a new one (a spoonful of herbal jam from a jar has no US ritual slot). C6 builds directly on this line.

4 · Banyan Botanicals — the practitioner depth play

The practitioner channel (Ayurvedic professionals, yoga-teacher networks) sustains a small, loyal, full-classical-format business — proof the deep-tradition register has a US home, and proof of its ceiling: the channel is tiny. The deck notes it as Chyawanprash's natural first mainstream-adjacent shelf (practitioner + yoga-studio retail) without pretending it scales.

5 · Moon Juice and the adaptogen-prestige tier

Adaptogen blends at prestige prices ($38–58 jars) sold through Sephora-adjacent wellness retail prove the ceiling ashwagandha can command when wrapped in design and creator language. Like hair's prestige flank: the register is validated, the founder-story key is the gate, and Dabur's play is the value-credibility tier beneath it, not the tier itself.

6 · Patanjali — the null control, again

India's largest Ayurvedic house has no formal US retail arm (five grey importers, no shelf). The macro filed it; the OTC deck repeats it once because it is the category's cleanest natural experiment: heritage scale at home converts to zero abroad without structure — the counter-argument to anyone in the room who believes the diaspora shelf builds itself.

7 · SELF-teardown

Strengths: the herbs the market already buys, with formulation history no US startup can claim; the official storefront and DSHEA-literate listings; the Honitus paper; the diaspora floor generating real velocity data; group-scale manufacturing. Weaknesses: the W0 paper unresolved (NDI status, 99-42 roster, Prop 65 search — the entry is GATED and this deck honours the gate); no US-format supplement line exists today (the door is unbuilt, not unmanaged); no third-party testing badge on any US-facing SKU found this pass — the single most important absence in the whole census given the file; the Silvassa DWPE shadow over any India-sourced supplement supply chain until W0-1 clears; and the master-brand question (DEEP THINK inherits the macro's three-degrees answer: ingredient-forward, Dabur as back-of-pack heritage line).

C4 · The file, outpapered — the trust architecture that gates everything

The heavy-metals file is the category's permanent weather and this deck's first budget line. The record, stated without flinching: JAMA 2004 found ~20% of US-sold Ayurvedic products with detectable lead/mercury/arsenic; the 2008 follow-up found 20.7%; CDC case reports keep the narrative periodically alive; FDA's Import Alert 99-42 (revised Apr 2025) holds ten named Ayurvedic products under automatic detention; Prop 65's plaintiff economy files hundreds of notices monthly with lead/cadmium leading. None of this is answered with indignation or with "our products are different" — it is answered with paper that precedes the question: third-party testing (USP-class verification or equivalent) on every US wellness SKU, batch-level certificates one QR away, Prop 65 compliance engineered at formulation (botanical selection and sourcing against cadmium/lead thresholds — turmeric's naturally-occurring load is a known trap), and the NDI/grandfather question resolved per ingredient BEFORE launch (macro W0-5). The claims side mirrors it: structure/function language with disclaimers, substantiation files per claim, zero disease words. The Ghana doctrine's US form, third appearance in the set: the FDA's enforcement is the honest brand's unpaid moat — 98 seizures in Accra, ten named products on 99-42, same logic — the registered, tested, boring entrant gains share every time the tide is policed.

C6 · The format derivation — the jar, the capsule, and the ritual slot

The programme's format discipline (Ghana derived sachets from the bitters ladder; six markets refused jar-reflexes) runs its US form on three facts: the gummy wall exists (>$4.2bn), capsules remain the seriousness format the supplement mainstream trusts, and traditional formats succeed in the US exactly when they occupy an EXISTING ritual slot (tea → Organic India; matcha → the whisk ritual imported whole; golden-milk lattes → turmeric's crossover vehicle). The derivation: actives travel in capsules; rituals travel as rituals — and Chyawanprash's US ritual slot is the warm-milk spoon, adjacent to golden milk, not the breakfast jar. So the line splits: Dabur Botanicals capsules carry the mainstream volume thesis; the Chyawanprash jar is operated as a ritual product (creator content teaching the warm-milk spoon; sachets for trial; winter/immune-season timing in structure/function-safe language) with honest expectations — a niche-builder, not a volume line. The refused alternative (chyawanprash-flavoured gummies) dies on both edges: it abandons the authenticity that is the jar's only US asset and enters the format war where Goli's scale wins. If the golden-spoon ritual catches (the matcha precedent says rituals DO cross when creators can perform them), the jar earns its shelf; if not, it remains what it already is — the diaspora's winter classic, sold well where it is loved.
