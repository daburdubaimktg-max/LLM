M1 · Engagement frame — the census instrument pointed at ourselves

Thirty-six markets taught this programme to open with the census: presence × price × badge. The United States is the first market where the instrument returns an estate, not a tail — and the estate has three distinct wings that share almost nothing but an ultimate parent:

Wing one — Namaste Laboratories, LLC (Chicago). Acquired via Dermoviva Skin Essentials Inc. for US$100M cash (announced 16 Nov 2010, closed 3 Jan 2011), with ~12% of the US ethnic-haircare market at acquisition. It manufactures the ORS/Organic Root Stimulator franchise (ORS Olive Oil, HAIRepair, Curls Unleashed) at Chicago/Blue Island facilities and sells through Walmart, Target and Albertsons/Vons. It grew +6.2% in FY2025-26 (Dabur results commentary) — the slowest of Dabur's named international units (Sub-Saharan Africa +20%, Bangladesh +22%, UK/EU +10%). And it is a live named defendant in MDL No. 3060 — the hair-relaxer cancer litigation before Judge Mary Rowland (N.D. Illinois): ~5,400 cases named Namaste/Dermoviva/DINTL as of the ICRA note (20 Mar 2025) within an MDL that reached 11,723 total active cases by 1 June 2026 (two different denominators — this deck never conflates them). Dermoviva and Dabur International were dismissed for lack of personal jurisdiction; Namaste remains on the hook, with first bellwether trials expected mid-2027, ₹105 crore of litigation cost incurred FY2024 (₹70–80 crore expected FY2025), insurance expected to cover outcomes, and relaxers contributing <1% of group turnover — financially contained, reputationally live.

Wing two — the diaspora FMCG tail, already semi-formalized. Unlike every grey tail the programme has graded, the US tail carries badges: an official "Dabur USA" Amazon storefront (Chyawanprash, Meswak, Red — with DSHEA disclaimer boilerplate in place), a direct D2C site, dedicated Dabur pages at Patel Brothers (52 stores across 20 states), and — the census's most surprising find — Dabur Honitus cough drops registered in DailyMed, the FDA's own drug-listing system, in four flavour SKUs. The tail is thin but it is PAPERED — a materially better starting position than assumed.

Wing three — the empty lanes. Three proven routes to the US mainstream sit unoccupied by Dabur: Himalaya Wellness (Sugar Land, TX) put organic ashwagandha into 761 Kroger doors; Indē Wild (Ayurveda-inspired, Unilever Ventures-backed) launched into 178 Sephora doors in March 2026 targeting $20M ARR; Patanjali proved the null case — no formal US arm, five grey importers, no shelf. Dabur currently occupies none of these lanes coherently.

So what. The macro mode is DEFEND-AND-EXTEND: defend wing one (litigation discipline, portfolio transition away from relaxers), formalize and scale wing two (the diaspora tail's paper is better than its distribution), and decide wing three deliberately (M10's option set) rather than by default. Every instrument the programme built for reading other people's estates — takeover species, badge grading, price anchors — runs here on Dabur's own shelf, which is precisely why this report can be unusually concrete.

M2 · Market & demography — the two markets inside the market

The US opportunity is two nested markets. The diaspora core: ~5.2M identifying as Indian (2023 ACS; ~5.8M by broad ancestry), the highest-median-household-income ethnic group in the country, grown +78% since 2010, concentrated in retail corridors (Edison/Iselin NJ, Bay Area, Devon Ave Chicago, Hillcroft Houston, Dallas, Atlanta — corridor-level density flagged legacy-knowledge, re-run queued). This core already knows every Dabur brand from home and shops a growing ethnic-grocery infrastructure (Patel Brothers adding stores; the broader Asian-grocery channel investing — H Mart at 89 stores with a ~100,000 sq ft Bay Area flagship planned). The mainstream shell: 335–340M consumers inside a US$13.23bn herbal-supplement market (2024, +5.4% — ABC HerbalGram #144) where ashwagandha — an ingredient Dabur has sold for decades — is now a top-5 mainstream herb at US$144.5M (+13.8%), hair-oiling is a TikTok-mainstreamed ritual, and Ayurveda-branded prestige just reached Sephora. The strategic geometry: the diaspora core is Dabur's by heritage and under-monetised; the mainstream shell is growing toward Dabur's portfolio and being harvested by others.

Tariffs — the moving floor

The landed-cost math is deliberately NOT settled in this report, because the ground is moving weekly: the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA reciprocal tariffs (20 Feb 2026); a flat 10% Section 122 surcharge took their place and is reported to expire around 24 July 2026 — seventeen days after this report's date; and the 6 Feb 2026 US–India joint statement frames an interim agreement with an 18% reciprocal tariff on select Indian goods whose cosmetics/food coverage is unconfirmed. The discipline this forces is the programme's currency-before-price clause in tariff form: every category deck's landed-cost line carries a date and an HTS-code query (HTS 3304 cosmetics, 3306 toothpaste, 0409 honey, 2106 supplements — T14 #6), and no US price architecture is board-final until the July expiry resolves. Namaste's US manufacturing is the natural hedge: wing one makes its products in Illinois, immune to the whole question — an argument (M10) for weighting US-made lines in any expansion.

M6 · Demand engine — where the waves actually form

The US wellness demand engine in 2026 is platform-native and creator-led: TikTok Shop's supplement subcategory ($784M, its largest H&B line), the hair-oiling and ashwagandha waves that made Dabur-adjacent rituals mainstream without Dabur's participation, and retail media as the paid layer (Amazon 79.7% share; Walmart Connect's H&B vertical growing fastest). Diaspora media exists (ZEE5 US since 2021) but its US-specific reach data is a gap — and the sharper truth is that the diaspora core is reached more efficiently through the ethnic-grocery shelf and platform targeting than through TV buys. The one demand case study this deck elevates: Indē Wild — an Ayurveda-literate creator (Diipa Büller-Khosla) converted audience into a Unilever-Ventures-funded brand, Sephora's 178 doors, and a $20M ARR target, in under five years. The lesson is not "copy her" (her right-to-win is a founder story Dabur cannot manufacture); the lesson is that US mainstream retail now buys Ayurveda-coded propositions when they arrive in US-native packaging, pricing and creator language — the gate is open; what walks through it is a brand-architecture decision (M10, DEEP THINK).

M7 · The wellness substrate — demand growing toward the portfolio, trust file working against it

Two opposing currents define Ayurveda's US substrate. The demand current: herbal supplements at $13.23bn (+5.4%, second straight growth year); ashwagandha top-5 mainstream at $144.5M (+13.8%) with North America holding >37% of the global ashwagandha market; turmeric leading the natural channel; hair-oiling and "Ayurvedistry" normalised by creators. The ingredients won. The trust current: the category's standing heavy-metals file — JAMA 2004/2008 (~20% of tested Ayurvedic products with detectable lead/mercury/arsenic), CDC cluster reports, Import Alert 99-42's ten named products, Prop 65's enforcement volume — means "Ayurvedic" as a LABEL carries a media liability the INGREDIENTS have escaped. Himalaya's US playbook shows the resolution: single-herb, USDA-Organic, "clinically studied" caplets — the tradition sold in the shell's own trust language. The category decks inherit this as their central register question: ingredient-forward (ashwagandha, amla) travels; system-forward ("Ayurvedic medicine") invites the file.

A regulatory-cultural current the oral deck inherits — the fluoride bifurcation

One 2025–26 development belongs in the macro because it reshapes a whole category's premise: Utah and Florida banned community water fluoridation in 2025, and HHS leadership has pushed CDC to revisit the national recommendation. Whatever one's public-health view, the commercial fact is a bifurcating market: fluoride remains the ADA-endorsed clinical standard AND a growing consumer segment now actively seeks fluoride-free alternatives — the exact lane where Dabur's Meswak and Red (correctly positioned as fluoride-free cosmetics) already sit, and where Himalaya's Botanique line already holds Whole Foods shelf from $7.49 with a hydroxyapatite SKU. The oral deck derives the positioning; the macro's contribution is the framing rule: ride the segment, never the politics — the brand sells botanical oral care to people who want it, and stays out of the fluoride argument entirely (the Ghana anti-evangelism refusal, US form).

DEEP THINK — the brand-architecture question the whole engagement turns on

The estate's deepest tension is not regulatory or legal — it is architectural. Three names live in this market: ORS (known by millions of Black American consumers, litigation-shadowed, never marketed as Dabur), Dabur (known by the diaspora as home, unknown to the mainstream, and — if scaled carelessly — one JAMA-citing headline from carrying the Ayurveda file), and whatever wing three would need (the Sephora gate opened for a creator-founder story, not an FMCG masthead). The honest reading: the three wings want three different degrees of Dabur visibility — Namaste should stay un-Dabur'd while the MDL runs (contagion runs both directions); the diaspora tail should be MORE Dabur, not less (the badge is the value); and the mainstream options work best ingredient-forward, where the Dabur name is a heritage footnote on the back of pack, not the promise on the front. The programme has never before recommended that a company be three different amounts of itself in one country. The US estate's structure — assembled by acquisition, regulation and migration rather than by design — is exactly why it is the right answer here, and why it must be a documented decision rather than the current drift.

DEEP STRAT — what the inversion teaches the programme

Thirty-six macros asked "what did the demand already build?" and graded strangers' estates. The 37th grades our own, and the instruments hold: the badge axis found the official storefront; the two-denominator discipline kept 5,400 and 11,723 from blurring into one scary number; the supply-before-demand law became operate-the-paper-before-the-plan; and the honest denominator caught the data-broker's implausible $15M Namaste revenue estimate before it could anchor anything.
