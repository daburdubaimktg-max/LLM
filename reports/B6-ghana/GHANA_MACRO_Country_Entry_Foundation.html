M1 · Engagement frame — what kind of market is this?

Ghana is the programme's 36th market build and its first in ECOWAS. The census instrument — the standing first page since it was born in Batch 5 — returns a reading here that no Batch 5 market produced: a genuine near-white space. In Azerbaijan the instrument found 24 grey SKUs; in Armenia a platform had built a Vatika category node unasked; in Georgia the reading split. In Ghana, the sweep found no official Dabur entity, no FDA product registration, no Melcom or pharmacy-chain listing, and no mainstream Jumia storefront — only a faint informal tail: Dabur Amla and Vatika oils on the Jiji classifieds rail (from GH₵85, INDEX-DERIVED, 7 Jul 2026), one Ghana-facing e-grocer listing a single Vatika SKU (ieatghana.com, price 403-blocked), and the cross-border reseller Ubuy quoting Vatika coconut oil shipped from foreign stock. The demand signal exists; the supply structure does not. Ghana is unbuilt, not unwanted.

What makes the page unusual is the door next to it. Ghana's dominant modern retailer — Melcom, 72 stores and 12 cash-and-carry depots (Melcom Group Fast Facts, 2025) — was founded in 1989 by Bhagwan Khubchandani, a Sindhi trader whose community has run Ghanaian consumer commerce since the late 1940s; India is Ghana's third-largest trade partner (~US$3.3bn, 2023, High Commission of India Accra); Indian firms hold >US$2bn across 700+ projects; and July 2025 saw an Indian PM in Accra pledging to double bilateral trade in five years (Forbes Africa, 3 Jul 2025). The programme has met kinship registers before — but never a market where the retail infrastructure itself is diaspora-built. The entry question Batch 6's brief poses (import vs distributor vs local manufacture vs JV) therefore opens differently here: the first conversation is not with a stranger.

So what. The macro mode is FOUND-AND-BUILD: nothing to take over (no torn bands, no monopolist resellers — the Batch 5 takeover species do not apply), everything to establish — registration, distribution, price identity — with one warm institutional door (diaspora retail) and one unresolved structural fork (the duty question, M3) that between them decide the entry's economics before any marketing decision is taken.

M2 · Macro economy & demographics — the cedi cycle is the planning constant

Ghana enters 2026 in the best macro shape of its post-crisis cycle — and the plan must treat that shape as cyclical, not structural. The cedi lost 45.1% in 2022, slid to an all-time low near GHS 16.4/US$1 in November 2024, then — under the IMF's US$3bn ECF (Fifth Review completed 17 Dec 2025) — became the world's best-performing currency of 2025, trading at GHS 11.38/US$1 on 7 July 2026 (Trading Economics). Inflation fell from a 54.1% peak (Dec 2022) to a record-low 3.8% (Jan 2026) — and has since re-accelerated to 5.3% (Jun 2026), the first turn of the new cycle. A landed-cost model built on crisis-era rates overstates cost by a third; a price card built on today's rate and never stress-tested is a landmine. The doctrine from seven Batch 5 markets applies verbatim: set the price identity deliberately, defend it absolutely — and pre-write the FX trigger (here: a move through GHS 13/US$1 sustained one quarter re-opens the card).

Demographics that shape the four categories

Median age 21.6; ~38% of the population is 15–35 (GSS 2021). Three of five Ghanaians live in urban areas, led by Greater Accra (5.46M, 87.4% urban — GSS 2021 PHC). Kumasi is the second city and the commercial capital of the Ashanti interior — home of Kejetia, the largest single market in West Africa at >8,000 stalls (Wikipedia, headline stat) — with Sekondi-Takoradi (west coast, oil) and Tamale (northern hub, Muslim-majority) the next tiers; exact city populations were not captured this pass and sit in T18 [!] re-validate. The religious geography matters commercially: the Muslim fifth of the country concentrates in the three northern regions and in zongo quarters — Hausa-speaking trading neighbourhoods embedded in every southern city, including Accra and Kumasi. Halal-relevant framing therefore reaches into "Christian" cities through their zongo commerce, not only across the northern savannah (M8).

M3 · Trade & regulatory — one cascade, one fork, one ad gate

Ghana's import regime is legible and single-windowed — and it contains the entry's biggest unresolved number. The Food and Drugs Authority (FDA), under the Public Health Act 2012 (Act 851), regulates cosmetics, herbal medicinal products, food supplements and household chemicals through one registry. Cosmetics registration runs on sample submission plus manufacturing/ingredient review, takes 1–2 months and holds for 3 years (Firmus Advisory summary of FDA guidance, INDEX-DERIVED — the FDA's own fee schedule returned 403 and is T18's first row). Import permits price at 0.5% of value for cosmetics, 0.8% food, 1.8% drugs, processed through the ICUMS single window (trade.gov / ghanacitizenship.com, cross-confirmed). By Batch 5's friction spectrum this is mid-band paper: harder than Georgia's letter, far easier than Uzbekistan's build or Russia's EAC stack — closest to the Kenya/Tanzania class the B7 decks costed.

The origin fork — the entry's W0 #1

Batch 5 taught the programme that a single tariff answer can swing an entry's economics more than any marketing decision (Azerbaijan's PTA fork: six margin points). Ghana presents the same class of fork. Dabur International acquired manufacturing in Nigeria (the Discaria deal; the Lagos-area plant was historically framed as "the hub of Dabur in Africa"), and Nigeria and Ghana share the ECOWAS trade area — if that plant is running relevant SKUs and if its output qualifies under the ECOWAS Trade Liberalisation Scheme's rules of origin, the 20% CET band in T3 falls toward zero and the landed multiple drops from ~1.47× to ~1.22×. But the sourced record describes the Nigeria/Egypt plants as serving "North and East African markets" — West African routing is UNCONFIRMED, and this deck refuses to build a P&L on it. The fork is stated, priced in both branches, and assigned to internal verification (it concerns Dabur's own manufacturing scope — exactly the class of fact this programme never sources externally).

The advertising gate

Ghana's FDA does something few programme markets do: it vets the advertisement itself, separately from the product. Herbal medicines, supplements and pre-packaged foods cannot legally be advertised without product registration plus ad approval (FDA guideline FDA/DRI/DMS/GL-ADV/2023/01). Enforcement is uneven — a Media Foundation for West Africa test found only 2 of 8 major media houses demanded FDA approval before airing a fake herbal ad — but the programme's standing rule (write for the skeptic's desk) makes the gate a feature, not a burden: every Dabur herbal claim ships pre-vetted while the incumbent radio-herbal economy runs half-checked. The compliance asymmetry is a positioning asset in a market whose FDA publicly fights adulterated cosmetics and unapproved herbal claims (M9). Budget the ad-vetting cycle into every OTC and oral launch plan as a sequential gate after registration.

M4 · Retail landscape — the market square, the diaspora chain, and a shelf vacuum

Ghanaian FMCG volume still runs through the open market and the table-top store: the traditional share is estimated between ~60% (Euromonitor 2023, cited secondhand — UNCONFIRMED) and the historic ~80%+ (GSS 1998 baseline); the truth is somewhere inside, it dominates either way, and the exact split is a T18 row. Makola (Accra) and Kejetia (Kumasi, >8,000 stalls) anchor a national web of market-women-led distribution whose named wholesale layer (Kwatsons, Forewin, Distro, Lesfam, Berhill, Matcom — brandsenvoy.com, INDEX-DERIVED, each to be vetted) moves stock into thousands of container shops. Modern trade is concentrated and consolidating: Melcom (72 stores + 12 depots) towers over Palace (Spintex flagship), MaxMart (East Legon), Koala (Osu/Cantonments) and China Mall — and Shoprite is exiting Ghana (binding offer for its 7 stores + 1 warehouse, June 2025, citing currency volatility, duties and dollar rents). A middle-class shelf vacuum is being carved open exactly as Dabur would arrive.

M6 · Media & demand engine — radio in Twi, WhatsApp in everything

The B6 brief's demand-engine hypothesis (traditional trade + distributor + activation + radio + WhatsApp; test digital, don't assume live-commerce) survives contact with the evidence — with sharpened proportions. Radio remains the mass medium: Peace FM broadcasts ~90% in Twi; Adom FM (Multimedia Group) is its Twi rival; both out-reach English-only stations among mass shoppers. Digital is deep but message-shaped: 24.3M internet users (69.9% penetration), and among internet users 16–64, WhatsApp ~92%, Facebook ~77%, TikTok ~65% (DataReportal 2025). WhatsApp is not a "channel" here so much as the connective tissue of commerce — reseller catalogues, distributor order books, salon bookings. Live-commerce in the East Asian sense: no evidence, correctly refused by the brief. The seasonal spine: Detty December (up to 40% of annual revenue for many retail/hospitality businesses — News Ghana), Christmas/Easter, the two Eids (zongo + northern commerce), and September back-to-school [!] re-validate.

M7 · Local substrates — what Ghana already believes

Four substrates shape all four category decks; each is Ghana's own, none is transplanted:

Shea (the sovereign). Northern Ghana is a global shea heartland: US$92.6M butter exports (38,792t, 2022); >900,000 northern women earn from the shea chain (UNDP-consistent, three sources). Shea is "Africa's gold," a cultural equity as strong as any ingredient story the programme has met — the skin and hair decks treat raw market shea as the free incumbent (the analogue of Georgia's grandmother's rose water or the Caucasus vaseline floor) and treat a shea-inclusive gesture as near-mandatory for credibility. An entrant who appears to extract from the shea narrative without sourcing or benefit-sharing hands the market an own-goal story.

Alata samina (black soap). The Twi name literally remembers trade ("the pepper trader's soap"); plantain-ash and shea-bark soap is a living, daily-use tradition for hair and skin. Domestic GHS pricing uncaptured (diaspora-export listings run US$6–15) — a T18 row, and a hair-deck census item.

The institutional herbal system (the bridge). Ghana is one of the few programme markets with state-institutionalised herbal medicine: the Centre for Plant Medicine Research (Mampong, est. 1976) sees >16,000 outpatients monthly and has developed ~30 registered herbal medicines; KNUST grants a herbal-medicine BSc; public hospitals run herbal units; the FDA maintains a herbal registration class. The heritage grammar's Ghana state is therefore a genuine open question for the OTC gate — the substrate resembles kinship (a living, institutional herbal tradition that Ayurveda can stand beside as a peer from elsewhere), and the bitters culture below is its commercial expression. The OTC deck runs the full gate; the macro only names the candidate state.

The bitters economy (the incumbent format). Ghana's dominant herbal-wellness format is the bitters bottle: Adutwumwaa Bitters (AddPharma — piles, constipation, menstrual pain, rheumatism indications, on-label), Alomo Bitters (Kasapreko — alcoholic, health-positioned), and a radio-advertised long tail. Any Ayurvedic tonic entry must respect the format's grammar — dosed, indication-led, radio-sold — rather than import a jar habit unexamined; honey verdict #22 and the Chyawanprash format question are the OTC deck's to derive.

M8 · The halal file — positive and quiet, zongo-aware

Per the B6 brief and the census of religious geography: halal framing in Ghana is positive in the Muslim north and the zongo quarters, neutral elsewhere — the "positive-and-quiet" posture the programme ran in Muslim-Turkic Central Asia, not the mandatory-and-loud posture of Indonesia or the GCC. Practical form: halal-suitable formulation facts available on-pack and in trade conversations (zongo wholesalers, Tamale distributors, Eid trade), never a lead message in national creative. Hausa is the zongo lingua franca — the Hausa phrase file is a T18 commission, and Eid is a real trade moment in the north's calendar. DEEP THINK — is the clean page an asset or a warning?

The census's white page admits two readings, and the deck holds both. Reading one (asset): Ghana is unbuilt because no one has built it — Dabur's Africa attention historically ran east and north (Egypt, Kenya via the Discaria footprint), Nigeria absorbed the West Africa effort, and Ghana fell between; the demand fundamentals (young, urbanising, naturals-literate through shea/alata samina/bitters, Indian-trading-diaspora retail) were never the problem. Reading two (warning): the page is clean because the economics repel — a 1.47× landed multiple into a sachet-priced market where the free incumbent (raw shea) sets the value benchmark and the 2022 trauma hardened price sensitivity. The deck's honest weighing: reading two explains the absence of premium imports generally — yet Cantu, Shea Moisture and Aunt Jackie's hold dedicated Jumia storefronts at diaspora-US prices, and Godrej's Darling built a category-leading extensions business on Indian ownership and local relevance. Premium-adjacent personal care demonstrably clears the landed math when the story fits. The page is clean because nobody brought the right story at the right price, not because the market cannot pay. That is a buildable gap — and it is also the entry's falsifier: if the hair deck's price architecture cannot land Vatika coconut oil under the Jiji informal anchor (GH₵85) with full margin, reading two wins and the entry re-scopes to a narrower premium-diaspora play.

DEEP STRAT — the batch opens on a different first move

Batch 5 ended with an instrument set built for occupied ground: takeover species, grey-price anchors, heritage gates run against incumbent registers. Ghana's first move is older and simpler — establish, don't inherit: paper first (registration in the strong-cedi window), one distributor signed on a vetted national footprint, the Melcom table taken with all four categories on one sheet, the walk photographed before any price card is final, and the ad-vetting gate filed with the dossier so the first radio flight airs legal. The Batch 5 instruments do not retire here; they wait. The census re-runs behind the 403 walls may yet find a fuller informal tail (the asymmetry amendment: a negative read re-tests, only positive reads interpolate), and if it does, the takeover vocabulary re-enters. Until then, Ghana is the programme's first FOUND-AND-BUILD since the census instrument existed — the cleanest test yet of whether the method can start a market from nothing but paper, walks and doctrine.
