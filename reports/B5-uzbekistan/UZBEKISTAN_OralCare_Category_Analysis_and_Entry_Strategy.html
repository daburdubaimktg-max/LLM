The 60g's dignity, written before the marketing plan can forget it

One paragraph the deck insists on carrying in full: the decision to HOLD the 60g at the floor is the file's most important conduct choice, and its reasoning deserves permanence. When the growth reviews come (and they will) proposing to "harmonise" the rungs upward, this paragraph is the file's standing answer, and moving it requires the same board-level override the protocol demands — because some pricing decisions are conduct decisions wearing spreadsheets.

The price test on the ground — two pharmacies, one question

T9b staged concretely. Two dorixonas in matched districts — one in Tashkent's Chilonzor, one in Namangan — each already stocking the 170g at whatever the grey chain priced it. In cluster A nothing changes but measurement. In cluster B, three things change together on the same Monday: the price moves to 21,000, the shelf-strip appears with the heritage card's triple-gated line in Uzbek-first tri-script, and the pharmacist gets a one-paragraph brief (what miswak is — which she knows better than the brief does — and what the paste's provenance is, so her counter answer is confident). Twelve weeks of till data; the read is UNITS AT THE NEW PRICE versus cluster A's baseline drift, and the honest metric is week-8-to-12 repeat, because a heritage register that converts once and doesn't re-purchase is a souvenir, not a position. The design's discipline: the price and the register move TOGETHER (the mid rung is priced-heritage or it is nothing — testing the price bare would answer a question nobody is asking), and the fallback is pre-written into the file, so a failed read produces a decision in a week, not a debate in a quarter. Cost: two shelf-strips, two briefs, and the analyst hours — the batch's cheapest empirical answer to its most interesting pricing question.
