The gulob word — the frame's quiet linguistic gift

One finding the glossary carries that deserves its strategic sentence: the Persian-heritage Uzbek word for rose water is gulob/гулоб — and Dabur's brand is GULABARI, from the same Persian root (gul, rose) through its Indian journey. The name itself IS the kinship claim: an Uzbek shopper sounding out "Гулабари" hears her own word for the product inside the brand — no copywriter could manufacture a truer bridge, and none needs to. The design consequence: the pack's Uzbek panel introduces the product AS gulob ("аtirgul suvi — gulob"), letting the etymology do silently what the film does explicitly. The tone gate still governs (a name is not a licence), but the deck records the gift: sometimes the localisation was done eight centuries ago by the traders who carried the word both ways, and the brand's job is only not to waste it.

The winter floor's two seasons — the climate note

Uzbekistan's continental sharpness cuts BOTH ways, and the barrier lane should plan for it: the cold winter drives the classic chap-and-crack demand (the Astana protocol's territory, one market south), while the HOT DRY SUMMER — dust, sun, irrigation-country wind — drives a second dryness season the Slavic markets never had. If Herbolene's check opens its lane, the deck scopes a two-season read in year 1 (winter barrier + summer after-sun/dryness use) before committing the seasonal lock's single-window logic — the third run of the play may need a two-window variant here, and cheap diary data decides it rather than assumption. The seasonal lock's DESIGN travels; its calendar re-derives per climate, which is exactly what a lock that fails safe is for.
