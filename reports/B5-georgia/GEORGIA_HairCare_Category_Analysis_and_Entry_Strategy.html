C1–C2 · Category frame — proven demand, absent name, fictional list prices

Georgia's hair census produced the batch's strangest inheritance: Vatika is PRESENT — genuinely, multi-platform, deep-range (43 SKUs on one storefront) — and Dabur is ABSENT: no Georgian shopper searches «დაბური», no storefront badges the parent, no marketing has ever run [category pass, 7 Jul 2026 [!] every figure index-derived under 403s]. Four storefronts carrying one SKU family strongly implies a single parallel-import wholesale source — classic bulk arbitrage, likely feeding from Russia, Turkey or UAE re-export, living in the secondary marketplace layer and never once appearing in the big-3 pharmacy catalogues. The deck's pricing language works in transaction prices throughout, with list architecture designed as the promotional headroom the culture expects.

The inheritance, graded precisely. Azerbaijan's grey band was reseller-run on one marketplace with the brand named; Armenia's was a legal platform corridor; Georgia's is a THIRD configuration — deep-range, multi-storefront, and NAMELESS. What the arbitrage proves: the product sells at 3.50–13.30 ₾ transaction prices with zero marketing. What it never built: a customer who could ask for the brand again. The niche is seeding ground, never the volume plan.

The folk register — castor, not burdock. The batch's most-travelled value rung (репейное масло) is absent from Georgian shelves [!] — the first market where it doesn't reach — and its cultural seat is held instead by CASTOR oil (აბუსალათინის ზეთი), framed by health-advice media as the grandmother's remedy for weak hair, sold as pharmacy generic rather than branded cosmetic. This is Vatika's local echo: the natural-oil-for-hair-strength idea already lives in Georgian folk memory — in the medicine cabinet, waiting to be moved to the shelf. No Georgian traditional hair canon beyond it surfaced (a flagged source gap, not an absence — the native-script queue carries it).

Self-teardown — the nameless line. Strengths: proven transaction prices across a deep range; the argan SKU already in the category's dominant story at the value slot; the batch's lightest paper and fastest lane behind any re-supply. Weaknesses, owned plainly: no name to build on (the badge starts at zero); the grey supply's freshness and labelling are unaudited [!]; the current storefront layer (secondary marketplaces) is not where hair volume lives (grocery is — unscraped); and the discount culture will read any un-designed list price as an invitation to churn. The two-job structure answers each weakness by name.

The price architecture — designed for the discount culture

Every prior founded-price doctrine defended a single held identity; Georgia's retail culture (40–62% promotional depth as standing practice) would read a flat held price as either arrogance or an error. The adaptation, stated once: the architecture sets BOTH tiers deliberately — a designed list (the promotional headroom the culture expects, ~1.7–2× the transaction target) and a defended TRANSACTION corridor (the real identity: Vatika shampoos at ~4–6 ₾/200ml and ~8–10 ₾/400ml transaction; the argan oil at ~12–14 ₾; Amla introduced at ~10–12 ₾/200ml [!] ALL AUTHORED, G1-re-based) — with the discipline moved one level up: what is never breached is the transaction corridor's floor, and promotions are calendar-planned depth within designed headroom, never reactive cuts below it. The doctrine's Georgian form: set the fiction deliberately, defend the truth absolutely.

The discount culture, read one level deeper. The 40–62% promo depth is not irrationality; it is a NEGOTIATION RITUAL retail inherited from the bazaar — the list price plays the opening ask, the sale tag plays the settled deal, and the shopper's satisfaction comes from the visible distance between them. The dual-tier architecture works because it plays the ritual honestly instead of refusing it: the designed list is a real opening ask (premium-tier neighbours price there), the transaction corridor is a real settlement (the margins survive there by design), and the calendar's promo rhythm gives the ritual its tempo. What breaks the ritual — and the brand — is the third state the culture also knows well: the desperate cut below the settlement, which reads not as a bargain but as distress. The floor is sacred because the ritual only respects a seller who knows his bottom.
