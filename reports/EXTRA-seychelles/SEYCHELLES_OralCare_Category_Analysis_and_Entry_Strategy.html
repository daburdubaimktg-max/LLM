THE FORTY-SECOND CORRIDOR FINDING — FLANKER-RACED. The Seychelles oral file contains the queue's friendliest spectrum reading — real premium room for the first time since Italy, parcel-level proof of herbal demand, a neutral trade gate — and one fact that reorganises the whole entry: Colgate Swarna Vedshakti, the incumbent's own Ayurvedic toothpaste, is already listed for courier delivery to Victoria (desertcart.sc — INDEX-DERIVED). The incumbent has seen the same herbal signal Dabur sees, and has a flanker built for it. The strategic consequence is precise: where the incumbent fields its own version of the challenger's story, the story stops being a moat — any «herbal toothpaste» claim Dabur makes, Colgate can match on the next container, with the STC-anchored distribution muscle Dabur doesn't have. What the incumbent cannot match is system depth: one flanker SKU against Dabur's entire herbal oral house — Meswak's single-tree story, Dabur Red's multi-herb tradition, Herb'l's active-led variants — merchandised as a BLOCK that makes «herbal oral care» a category Dabur owns rather than a claim two brands trade. And what neither brand can buy later is first shelf: in a 122k-person market where the first price is a public statement and the first shelf is the category's founding memory, speed is not a virtue of this plan — it is the plan. The finding's instruction: when your differentiator is flanker-contestable, race the shelf with the house, not the hero — three SKUs in the first carton, the block placement negotiated as one facing, and the clock set at two quarters from the W0 gates to the STC shelf.
