## 1. The batch in one paragraph

Batch 8 asked one question seven ways: how does a 140-year Indian herbal house enter Southern Africa —
a region with no Ayurvedic shelf tradition, three currencies' relationships to the rand, two Lusophone
markets, the world's two highest Gini coefficients, and retail structures running from Luanda's candonga
to Gaborone's own listed champions? The seven answers share one spine (value-first, fences absolute,
every number sourced or authored-and-labelled) and differ everywhere the evidence differed: Zambia opened
through a warm window, Angola through an expensive forex gate, Mozambique through a century-old trading
door, South Africa through a contested heritage claim, Mauritius through a regulated classroom, Namibia
through a supply chain that already crossed the border, and Botswana through its own local champions.
**No number, code, register or playbook was transplanted between countries — and by the batch's end, its
instruments were making decisions, not just reports.**

wait, Mozambique's fast-follow, Namibia's foodstuff lane, Botswana's light paper) was set by the
regulatory lane's shape and the trust environment's depth — never by demand, which was real everywhere

others. Companion instrument: **the inherited-read protocol** (Botswana deciding its tonic from Namibia's
