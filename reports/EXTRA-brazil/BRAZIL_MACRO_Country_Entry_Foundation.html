M1 · THE THESIS. Brazil presents the estate-species taxonomy's eighth entry — and its first NULL: SEED-ABSENT. Every prior market's estate grew from a diaspora seed (a grocery shelf, a believer network, an unaccompanied crossing); Brazil has no seed — the Indian-origin population is ~3,000–25,600 people against 213 million (0.001–0.012%), there is no confirmed Dabur SKU in legitimate retail, no ANVISA registration of record, no distributor, and the ORS-LatAm premise in the brief is CORRECTED: ORS's own footprint statement (US, Africa, Middle East, Europe, Caribbean) omits Latin America entirely. The custody gradient ends at zero here: nothing to watch, shape, paper, prove, mandate, connect, or accompany. The mode is therefore FOUND-ON-FIT — enter only where Brazilian culture has ALREADY built the seat Dabur's product fits: the cronograma capilar (a national hair-treatment ritual with a named oil step), the honey-as-medicine mental model (Sebrae-documented), the água de rosas heritage category (Granado, since 1870), the natural-toothpaste niche (Boni Natural's proven path). Three structural walls shape everything: ANVISA requires a Brazilian AFE-holder before any legal sale (a foreign company cannot register directly); ashwagandha is effectively banned (absent from RDC 26/2014 and RDC 243/2018 — Chyawanprash as formulated is not legal: the flagship's posture is WALLED, the grammar's sixth cell); and the import tax stack can exceed 50% of CIF (directional estimate — exact NCM rates queued). One inversion crowns the file: Brazil is a honey/propolis EXPORTER (US$85.2M apicultural exports 2023; 140–150t of premium green propolis to Japan yearly) — the honey question here is not "sell into" but "source from" (verdict #30, ruled in the OTC deck). And the whitening fence meets its hardest trial: clareador products are LEGAL and normalised in Brazil — the fence's 20th market holds on ethics where no law forces it.
