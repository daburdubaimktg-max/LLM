C1 · Executive summary

The play. Oman hair care is an estimated US$45–55M pool (10–11% of the country's US$472.7M beauty market — the direct Statista page is the named re-run [!]), and Dabur enters it as the incumbent, not the challenger: Amla trades in four-plus formats at LuLu (100ml at 0.640; an aggressive 300+100ml band at 1.470), Vatika runs oils, shampoos, serums and the Arabic-named Hammam Zaith hot-oil treatment, and the portfolio is already multi-channel (Talabat Mart, Sharaf DG, noon). What the shelf audit exposes is not a distribution job but four unclaimed stories sitting on top of an owned ladder. First, the khareef window: Salalah runs 75–90% humidity from June to September with 1.03M seasonal visitors, and no brand has ever run khareef-coded anti-frizz communications — a seasonal category moment as legible as "monsoon hair care" in India, which happens to be Dabur's home genre. Second, the desalination anxiety: "hair fall since moving to Oman" is a live consumer worry that clinics monetise and no shampoo brand owns — and Vatika Cactus Hair-Fall Control already sits on shelf at 1.115 waiting for the story. Third, the premium-naturals gap: oils ladder from Parachute's 0.23/100ml floor through Amla's mid to Indulekha's ~2.7/100ml premium with nothing between 1.2–2.5 — and H&S Oud, P&G's pan-GCC proof that scent codes carry 2× premiums, has no Oman listing yet [!]. Fourth, the luban option: a Vatika Omani-frankincense variant (Dhofar-sourced, Hammam-Zaith-adjacent) is the most localisable NPD in the batch. The ask: repair the ladder's gaps (missing prices, the OOS 700ml hero), run the two-story platform (khareef seasonal + desal-protection evergreen) through licensed Omani creators, pilot Vatika Oud/Luban at OMR 1.9–2.4 into the empty rung, and let the CEPA lane carry value packs India-direct. Y3 band: US$2.5–4.5M incremental on the base (C6). Kill-switch: story-led velocity +20% on oils in two quarters or revert to distribution maintenance.

C4 · Consumer & culture — two engines, two climates, two covered heads

Demand runs on two engines the macro named: the ~507k-strong Indian bloc (weekly oiling as maintained home ritual — Dabur's base) and Omani women, whose practices are distinct but adjacent — oil as pre-wash treatment, and the bukhoor/luban tradition of scenting hair over fragrant smoke, which makes hair a fragrance carrier exactly as in Bahrain, but with a national ingredient (Dhofari luban) attached. Two climate zones split the functional brief: the north's hot-dry year (dehydration, sun, hard desalinated water — the "hair fall since I moved here" anxiety clinics monetise) and Dhofar's monsoon quarter (75–90% humidity, frizz, 1.03M visitors). And an Oman-unique observation the category has never used: both genders cover their hair daily — women's hijab/lihaf and men's kuma cap or massar turban — making scalp-freshness and oil-balance a legitimately dual-gender platform (flagged as hypothesis for local qualitative work; no survey data exists [!]).

MENA-CONSUMER-INSIGHT read: Oman hands Dabur something no other market in this batch does — a national ingredient (luban) whose ritual grammar already includes hair, plus a monsoon season that makes India's own category playbook (monsoon-proof hair) native rather than imported. DEEP THINK on W1. Inversion: how does a story platform fail on an owned shelf? (1) Habit inertia — oil buyers already buy; the stories must recruit new users (Omani women trading from unbranded practice) and new occasions (pre-wash ritual frequency), so G1 measures segment velocity, not just Dabur share of it. (2) Season mis-execution — a khareef campaign with July stock arrival is a wasted year; the macro's T13b clock (stock lands May) is the binding constraint, and the first khareef (2027) should be deliberately modest — one serum, one travel pack, Salalah LuLu + pharmacies — a rehearsal that earns the bigger 2028 window. (3) Register mismatch — Dubai-style glam creative fails here; the licensed-Omani-creator bench and soft-humour lane are not preferences but requirements (macro M4/M5). Second-order: success invites Marico to story-code coconut (their CEPA costs drop too) — pre-empt by owning the rituals (حمام زيت by name, khareef by season) rather than generic "protection", which a coconut brand can copy. Base-rate: Bahrain's HUNGRY WATER validated desal-protection framing for GCC audiences; India's monsoon category proves khareef-class seasonality monetises; H&S Oud proves Gulf scent premiums. Kill-switch: +20% oil-segment velocity in two quarters or the platform reverts to maintenance; W2/W3 are independently killable.
