Central African Republic — Hair Care: Category Analysis & Entry Strategy

The seed is the survey: in a market with no data, no audit and no marketplace, the smallest honest shipment — instrumented — is the only market study that will ever exist, and Amla is the SKU built to run it
Prepared for the Dabur International board · 7 July 2026 · Public-web evidence only · Routing: africa-consumer-insight-expert · Research base: CF_FULL_NOTE.md; macro #187 standing orders (WATCH-AND-SEED) · PROXY DECLARATION: no CAR hair-care data of any kind exists publicly — Cameroon-proxy cells are marked as such throughout · Litigation fence: ORS/Namaste SEALED

THE THIRTY-SEVENTH CORRIDOR FINDING — SEED-AS-SURVEY. Every prior hair market gave the programme something to read before entering: a scrapeable shelf, a market study, an estate, a data panel. The CAR gives nothing — no size, no shares, no pricing survey, no retail audit; the category record is a blank (CF_FULL_NOTE.md, stated plainly) — and the honest response is not to guess harder but to invert the sequence: where research cannot precede entry, the entry must BE the research. The finding names the machine: a carton-scale seed of Amla small formats, shipped inside the Cameroon distributor's own Douala flow, with sensors attached at zero marginal cost — the PK5 stockists' reorder pattern (the wallet's telemetry), WhatsApp price reports from the wholesale row, and direct messages to the five named, Facebook-visible Bangui salons (L'Olympe, Sapphire Beauty, Sarah Fashion, Ma Reine Beauty, CHIC-CHIC — CAPTURED, names only). Two reorder cycles of silence and the clause goes dormant for the cost of a pallet; steady reorders and the re-review date arrives with real data no desk could have bought. The SKU was chosen for signal value as much as sales value: Dabur Amla already retails one border away in both directions (Miassar Cameroon, Douala delivery; Kinshasa listings ~10,000 CDF — CAPTURED), the oil format suits a low-water environment, and the anti-casse claim maps to braiding stress without a word of consumer education. The deck that follows designs the seed, prices it against the duty wall, and pre-writes both of its futures.

The braid economy map carries one strategic asymmetry worth a paragraph. In Kinshasa the extensions trade had an industrial champion (Darling's local factory) whose marketing built the category's media culture; Bangui has no equivalent — the mèche trade is pure import wholesale, unbranded at the culture level. That means Amla enters a hair economy with NO dominant brand voice teaching anything: the stall and the braider are the entire media ecosystem. This cuts both ways. No incumbent voice means no incumbent to displace and no category vocabulary to rent; it also means every unit of brand recognition must be carried up the corridor in the product's own name. The seed's bet is that a franchise with sixty years of diaspora-carried recognition — already leaking into both neighbouring markets — is the only kind of hair brand that can enter such a voiceless category at declared-import economics.

One instrument note before the teardown. The corridor finding's name — SEED-AS-SURVEY — is deliberately not «test market». A test market presumes a scaling decision waits behind it and budgets accordingly; the seed presumes NOTHING waits behind it except a dated re-review, and budgets at option-value scale. The distinction disciplines every design choice: a test market would ship three SKUs to segment demand (the seed ships one franchise in two formats, because segmentation data is worthless until existence is proven); a test market would fund awareness to give the product «a fair chance» (the seed funds nothing, because the question is precisely whether the product moves WITHOUT support); a test market's failure embarrasses a plan (the seed's failure completes one). The teardown below reads every competitor through the same lens: not «how do we win share» but «what does their presence teach the sensors to look for».

C4–C6 · Teardown, shopper, scenes

A word on the teardown's most important absentee. In every prior hair market the deck tore down a LOCAL winner whose entry mechanics taught the plan something — Tío Nacho's marketing fluency, Darling's factory, Skala's merit crossing. Bangui offers no such subject: no hair brand has ever, on the public record, ENTERED this market as a strategy — everything on the shelf arrived as freight. That absence is itself the finding's proof. There is no entry playbook to study because no one has written one, which means the seed's telemetry will constitute the first documented hair-care entry data in the country's history — a small archive, but a real one, and the re-review's decision will rest on evidence no competitor possesses.

The shopper table's most important row is the language one, and it deserves expansion. Bangui's shopper hears Sango, reads French (if she reads the pack at all), and buys by the stall's spoken word. The pack therefore works as a visual object first — the Amla bottle's green-and-gold is its own recognition system for anyone who has seen it in Douala or in a relative's luggage — and as a French text second. This is why the seed phase ships existing Francophone-export artwork unchanged: a CAR-specific pack would cost money the option-value budget doesn't have, for a literacy context that rewards consistency with the corridor's existing visual memory more than local adaptation. The one adaptation that WOULD matter — a Sango radio voice — is exactly the asset the graduation decision holds hostage until the sensors earn it.

Before channels: the scenes above fix the deck's two operating truths. The inbox scene says the influence economy is, for once, FREE to reach — a fact so unusual in this programme that it deserves its own sentence: in Paris, the corridor's teachers charged real money; in Kinshasa, the gate cost a kit programme; in Bangui, the gate has a public inbox and answers politely. Small markets sometimes pay their entrants in access what they cannot pay in volume.

C7–C9 · Channels, margins, the waterfall

The channel section's brevity mirrors the market's: there are exactly two decisions to make (which wholesale house, and whether the supermarket courtesy listing is worth one meeting), and the macro's screening rules govern both. What deserves emphasis is what the channel plan does NOT contain — no distributor exclusivity (the seed is too small to be worth fencing), no listing fees anywhere (the option-value budget's hard zero), and no direct salon supply (the DM gate listens; it does not sell — a salon that wants stock is routed to the PK5 wholesaler, which keeps the channel honest and the relationship clean). The one standing courtesy: RAYAN or SOCIMCO get the first supermarket conversation because the Lebanese retail tier's goodwill costs nothing to bank and may matter enormously at the re-review.

The waterfall's 11% deserves its honest defence. It is the thinnest funded margin in the Africa queue so far, and the deck ships it anyway because the unit economics are not the unit's job — the carton's P&L rounds to zero at corporate scale, while the information it buys does not. The pricing three-step still governs (the local reference is the audit's to set; the earned gap is the named active's premium over refill oils; the margin stays honest rather than aspirational), and the pre-authorized kill condition (W0 #3 breaking the waterfall) keeps the thin margin from becoming a habit. What the deck refuses is the false alternative: pricing the bottle at 2,000 XAF to make the spreadsheet smile, in a market where the elite ceiling sits at 1,500 and the target shopper counts coins. A seed priced out of its own market surveys nothing.

C13–C18 · Sensitivity, anti-transplant, calendar, glossary, queue

Second-order board before the sensitivity table. Incumbent response: none expected at carton scale — Biopharma will not see the seed, and the refill-oil trade has no brand apparatus to respond with; the first reaction that could matter is a PK5 wholesaler copying the import himself if reorders run hot, which is not a threat but Future A arriving late — the gift-rule terms answer it. Trade reaction: the stockist's real question is margin-per-carton against goods he can clear informally; the seed's terms concede a visibly better stall margin (25% vs the 20% norm assumption) precisely to buy the shelf trial — a term, priced in the waterfall, not a war. Second-order on the salons: if one salon posts about a sample, the others will know within a week — Bangui's beauty economy is small enough that the DM gate's five conversations are, socially, one conversation; conduct accordingly. Kill-switch: pre-authorized twice over — W0 #3 breaks the waterfall, or two silent cycles; both end in the same one-page dormancy memo.
