Central African Republic — Country-Entry Foundation (Macro)

A city at the end of a 1,400-kilometre road: Bangui is the market, the Douala corridor is the country's aorta, and the honest verdict is WATCH-AND-SEED — zero fixed investment, one distributor clause, two SKUs into PK5, and a dated re-review
Prepared for the Dabur International board · 7 July 2026 · Public-web evidence only · Routing: africa-consumer-insight-expert · Research base: CF_FULL_NOTE.md (no Euromonitor/Nielsen/retail audit exists for CAR — every category claim graded; the gaps table is a first-class deliverable) · Sequence note: CAR was the user's first-named Central Africa market; DR Congo ran first because its research banked first — the order evens out within the wave

M1 · THE THESIS. The Central African Republic is the smallest, thinnest, hardest market in the twelve-country Africa queue, and the macro's job is to say so with precision rather than dress it up. The addressable market is Bangui (~889k city, likely >1M metro — France MFA, CAPTURED) plus a handful of corridor towns; national GDP per capita is US$390–516 — bottom-five on earth — with 65.7% below US$2.15/day (World Bank Poverty Assessment 2023, CAPTURED) and per-capita income still shrinking (growth 2.1–2.9% vs population +3.1%). Everything the market consumes arrives up one road: the Douala–Bangui corridor carries ~80% of all external trade, 1,400–1,500 km, 2–14 days transit, US$0.35–0.40 per ton-km — more than double Southern-Africa costs — and an August 2025 haulers' strike closed it entirely for three weeks (World Bank corridor project P513683; Ecofin — CAPTURED). No Dabur presence exists (negative finding, CAPTURED); no Indian trading community could be found (negative, flagged for French re-check); the formal economy is a handful of Lebanese houses and the PK5 Muslim-trader wholesale engine. Yet the corridor already carries Dabur-adjacent goods: Dabur Amla Gold retails today in Cameroon (Miassar, Douala delivery) and in Kinshasa (~10,000 CDF) — the supply beachhead exists one border away (CAPTURED). The verdict this macro will defend: WATCH-AND-SEED — no entity, no warehouse, no media; a CAR-rights clause in a Douala distributor agreement, Amla small formats plus one herbal balm SKU seeded into PK5 wholesale, honey and Chyawanprash skipped, the lightening segment never touched, and a re-review dated to corridor rehabilitation plus one year of post-election calm. Bangui rewards the patient wholesaler, not the brand-builder — for now.

The city-market logic deserves one paragraph of arithmetic. Bangui holds roughly one sixth of the national population but — given displacement-driven urbanisation, the corridor's terminus economics and the collapse of interior commerce — plausibly a large majority of national FMCG cash spend. The four corridor towns add reachable secondary nodes only insofar as PK5 wholesalers already serve them on their own trucks; the plan never serves them directly. This is the queue's purest expression of a rule the DR Congo macro introduced: the addressable market is not the census population, it is the population a working supply chain can reach at a price it can pay — and here that is one city and whatever rides out of it on a trader's vehicle.

The working-capital consequence of T2 belongs in the money section's header: a 2–14 day transit variance on a 1,400-km single road, behind a port (Douala) with its own congestion history, means the cash-conversion cycle for a Bangui shipment runs three to six months end-to-end. For a seed of two SKUs this is a rounding cost; for any scaled entry it would be the P&L's silent killer — one more reason the WATCH-AND-SEED architecture caps exposure at carton scale until the corridor project changes the arithmetic.

M3 · Money and the duty wall

The currency is the queue's most stable: the CFA franc (XAF) in the CEMAC/BEAC zone, pegged to the euro at 655.957 — no FX risk against the euro, convertibility managed by BEAC (CAPTURED). The price problem is not the currency; it is the duty wall: the CEMAC common external tariff puts cosmetics in Category IV at 30%, CAR adds a 25% excise on perfumes/cosmetics (WTO TPR, CAPTURED), and the widely cited 19% VAT (UNCONFIRMED this pass — Code Général des Impôts check queued) would stack the as-written landed burden toward +85–95% over CIF — which is precisely why much of the real trade clears informally or under-declared via the corridor (INDEX-DERIVED, flagged). A new CEEAC-CEMAC common external tariff entered force on 1 January 2026; its chapter-33 detail is queued (CAPTURED that it exists; UNCONFIRMED at line level). The macro's consequence is blunt: Dabur prices to the street reality it will find in PK5, ships fully declared itself, and accepts that the informal competitor's landed cost is structurally lower — the seed SKUs must survive that asymmetry or not ship.

Reading T3 as a strategist rather than an accountant: the duty wall's true function in this market is SELECTION. It filters out every product whose only argument is price, because the informal trade will always undercut a declared importer on price; what passes the filter is product whose name, format or claim does work that a no-name equivalent cannot. That is the analytical reason the seed carries Amla — a franchise with a named active and corridor recognition — rather than a generic oil that would arrive pre-beaten. The wall is not an obstacle to be lamented; it is the market telling entrants which game is winnable here.

M4 · People: Sango, the two communities that matter, and the religion rule

Two social facts shape every commercial choice. First, Sango: co-official with French since 1991 and spoken by ~90% of the population — one of Africa's rare cases where a single indigenous language is genuinely national (CAPTURED). Advertising here can be national in ONE local language: radio-first (Radio Ndeke Luka and peers), Sango-voiced, market-crier and salon word-of-mouth beneath it; Facebook/TikTok reach only the connected ~10% — but that sliver includes every trader who decides what gets stocked (INDEX-DERIVED, flagged). Second, the religion structure and its hard rule: ~61% Protestant, ~28% Catholic, ~9% Muslim (Pew 2019; Oxfam up to 15%), with Muslims concentrated in Bangui's PK5 and the trade economy (CAPTURED). The commercial consequence inverts the usual halal logic: halal is a trade-gate factor, not a consumer claim — the Muslim minority CONTROLS the wholesale channel, so halal-friendly formulations smooth the stockist conversation even though the consumer majority is Christian. And the 2013–14 sectarian trauma writes the rule in stone: religion-coded marketing of any kind is a hard no — no faith symbols, no community targeting, nothing that picks a side in a city that bled over sides (INDEX-DERIVED judgement, flagged as such, held as binding).

One structural observation completes the retail picture, and it is the note's quiet masterstroke: the Marché Central is documented (Jeune Afrique) as the place where Muslim and anti-balaka traders re-found commercial common ground after the 2013–14 catastrophe — commerce functioning as the city's reconciliation infrastructure. A foreign brand entering these markets inherits a duty of care toward that fragile equilibrium, which is why the religion rule (M4) is written as absolute rather than prudent: in Bangui, neutral commerce is not just safe positioning, it is a small act of civic maintenance, and the brand that understands this will be remembered by the traders who decide everything else.

M6 · The honey puzzle — verdict #33, the quartet's strangest input

The research pass's most surprising CAPTURED finding: CAR is the world's #1 per-capita honey consumer (~2.8–3.19 kg/person/yr), producing ~16,200–16,800 tonnes (FAOSTAT 2021 — exact tonnage FAOSTAT-imputed, [!] re-validate), with a forest-honey culture documented back to pre-colonial Aka (BaAka) honey-gathering in the Sangha-Oubangui interfluve (IRD scholarship) and an EU-funded beekeeping project in Lobaye since 2006 (CAPTURED). Honey here is an everyday food and remedy, abundant, trusted, raw and cheap. Run the quartet's checklist from #185: production exists — YES, massively; production serves its own market to standard — YES, by the market's own standard: the consumer's reference is raw forest honey whose provenance she trusts more than any seal, the exact inverse of Kinshasa's adulteration anxiety. The checklist therefore lands in the taxonomy's oldest cell, and HONEY VERDICT #33 is ABSTAIN-JAR, FULL STOP — no imported Dabur Honey beyond, at most, a token elite-supermarket listing that this macro declines even to recommend. The reverse curiosity (CAR forest honey as a global sourcing story) is noted in one honest line: it rhymes with the sourcing mandate, and it fails today on logistics, standards and conflict overlay — parked, dated to the same re-review as everything else (G10 carries the Dzanga-Sangha apiculture check).

M8–M10 · Entry architecture, risks, the ninety days, and the instruments

The options table below is short because the market's arithmetic eliminated most of the space before strategy began. What survives examination is a single live question — does the free clause plus a carton-scale seed beat doing literally nothing? — and the answer turns on option value: the seed's total at-risk cost (a few thousand dollars of stock and the W0 gates' ~US$500 of fieldwork) buys real market sensors in a country where NO desk research can ever substitute for a stall's reorder pattern, plus first-mover position in the trade's memory if the corridor project delivers. Against that, the pure pass saves a rounding error and buys a blind spot. The table records the reasoning; the verdict was never close.

DEEP THINK · Three objections, answered before the board asks them

"Why produce five full reports for a market the macro itself calls watch-list?" Because the queue's discipline is the product. The user's directive names CAR, and the programme's worth rests on treating a US$15–40M city with the same evidentiary honesty as a US$40B economy — the same graded facts, the same refusals to invent, the same instruments run to their honest outputs. A watch-list verdict reached rigorously is a deliverable; a market skipped casually is a hole in the atlas. And the seed architecture means the reports are not academic: they specify a real, near-free option with sensors attached, which is exactly what a board should hold for a market like this. The four category decks that follow will be honest about proxy-dependence in every table — and the PK5 audit they all queue costs less than this paragraph did to research.
