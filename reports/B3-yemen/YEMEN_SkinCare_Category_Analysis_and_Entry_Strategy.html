C2 — Category frame, size & the climate structure

Yemen skin is a climate-driven, ritual-layered, three-tier market. The climate splits the need-states: Tihama's heat and humidity (prickly heat; the wars yellow face-paint doubling as folk sun protection) versus the highlands' cold, dry, water-scarce winters (cracked heels and hands — تشقق — the jelly's home ground). The Aden shelf prices a real three-tier structure: a qat-note tier ($1.9–2.9: Vaseline, Thai YC, Veet), a mid tier ($3.7–6: Himalaya, St. Ives), and a surprisingly present derm-premium tier ($11–25: Cetaphil, CeraVe, La Roche-Posay) serving the remittance/returnee segment. Above soap (HSA's YCGSI plant, since 1982), nothing is locally manufactured — every cream, jelly and lotion is imported.

C4 — Demand engine: the ritual layer & the forty-night clock (Module — consumer truth)

The wars ritual: golden powder mixed with rosewater or milk into the bridal face paste — and worn daily as the Tihama's yellow face-paint (beautification + sun protection at once). The bridal protocol: كريم العروس, nightly for five days before the event — a named, packaged, regionally exported tradition. The nurah heritage: the lime depilatory paste of the Arabian bath, sanctioned by fatwa, first used — tradition says — by Bilqis herself. And the clock: the fitrah hadith's forty-night limit, which turns hair removal from discretionary grooming into scheduled religious hygiene for both sexes.
