YEMEN — COUNTRY ENTRY FOUNDATION

Dabur India · Batch 3 (Levant / Iraq) · Country-Macro deck (M1–M11 + O1–O6) — the archetype: "commerce-through-war" (GO-SELECTIVE)

Prepared 7 July 2026 · Public sources only · arabic-expert + mena-consumer-insight lens (bespoke — no GAP RULE)

THE ASK: Yemen is NOT a Sudan-style WATCH — it is "commerce-through-war," a GO-SELECTIVE with known, priceable costs. The evidence: consumer imports never stopped (6M MT even in 2025's worst year; 70–80% of food via Hodeidah); Unilever and Nestlé held distributor coverage in both zones all war; HSA Group has manufactured and distributed nationally without interruption since 1938; remittances at 38% of GDP (world's 3rd most remittance-dependent; ~61% from Saudi) fund a consumption floor Sudan never had; and the frontlines have been frozen since the April 2022 truce. The war economy is institutionalised, not chaotic — predictable extraction, priceable friction. The operating system is the two-zone reality: Houthi north (Sana'a — ~⅔ of the ~36–40M population, old rial force-pegged ~535/USD) vs IRG south (Aden — new rial, violently volatile: 2,900→1,618/USD within months of the July 2025 CBY crackdown), two central banks, two customs regimes, and a 100% double-customs levy on goods crossing Aden→north — so the only economic design is two-port, two-distributor: Hodeidah + a Sana'a wholesaler for the northern two-thirds (old rials; Houthi permits — subject to FTO legal review, the deck's one non-research gate), Aden + a southern distributor for the IRG zone. Three structural gifts sharpen the entry: (1) Dabur is already trading — Vatika (10+ SKUs incl. 900ml family sizes), Amla and Herb'l toothpaste live on Bazzarry at YER prices, Fem holds a dormant equity (Yemen named at its 2008-09 acquisition), and Yemen was once cited in Dabur's own MD&A for 49% growth; (2) the boycott flip — Houthi authorities banned American goods from northern shelves (Apr 2025): P&G and Colgate are exposed, Indian-origin Dabur is neutral-to-positive in the larger zone; (3) the ritual map is native — Yemen is the miswak heartland (the arak tree is indigenous; Meswak's name IS the daily ritual), the sidr tree gives both the world's most prestigious honey AND the traditional women's hair-wash leaf (a Vatika-Sidr bridge no rival owns), and Prophetic medicine is natively practiced (the Jordan OTC playbook travels here, unlike Lebanon). Two disciplines above all: price under the qat threshold (qat takes ~10–28% of household spend — every rial competes with the afternoon bundle) and never out-honey a Yemeni (Do'ani Sidr at $100–500/kg is sacred national turf — Dabur Honey plays the everyday tier or stays an ingredient). Category board: Hair LEAD · Oral WEDGE (Meswak heartland) · OTC BUILD-narrow (Honitus) · Skin FLANK (Fem revival + value jelly).

M1 — Demography

Yemen is the programme's youngest large market — a median age of ~18.5–20.5 in a population of ~36–40M (no census since 2004; sources span 34–42M — the spread itself a finding [!]) — and its consumer mass sits in the north: the Houthi zone controls ~⅓ of territory but two-thirds or more of the population.

So what: three demographic facts set the commercial frame — the northern consumer mass (any strategy that only serves Aden forfeits two-thirds of the market), the youth bulge (habit-forming brands compound for decades), and the Saudi diaspora bridge (2M+ Yemenis shop Saudi shelves where Dabur Amla holds 34.2% of hair oil — the brand arrives pre-sold through the remittance channel).

The India connection is demography, not just trade. The Hadhrami merchant diaspora threaded Yemen to Hyderabad, Gujarat and Malabar for centuries; India supplies ~10–12% of Yemen's imports today ($843M in 2024) and Indian brands — hair oils above all — have traded in Yemeni souks for generations. For a programme that spends much of its energy managing India-origin perception, Yemen is the rare market where "Indian" reads as familiar heritage rather than foreign novelty, and where the Gulf-returnee consumer has already met Dabur on the Saudi shelf at full sophistication.

M2 — Economy (the defining head)

The economy is a war economy with a remittance floor. Real GDP per capita is down 58% since 2015 [IMF Art IV, Apr 2026]; oil exports have been zero since Houthi strikes on the terminals (Oct 2022); ~17M people are food-insecure. And yet consumption has not collapsed to Sudan levels — because remittances run at 38% of GDP (3rd highest dependence in the world; $3.77B+ formal, up to ~$10B with hawala) and consumer imports kept flowing through every phase of the war.

So what: one SKU in Yemen carries two price points, two currencies of settlement and two customs regimes — distributor contracts must specify zone and banknote type. The Aden rial's 44% appreciation in one quarter (Jul→Dec 2025) is the warning label: southern pricing must be re-validated quarterly and hedged in USD terms; the north's force-pegged old rial is, perversely, the more stable pricing environment. The remittance channel — 61% Saudi — is why the value-mid tier exists at all: the FMCG wallet is a remittance wallet.

The qat wallet — Yemen's unique competitor. No Yemen consumer model works without qat: ~72% of men and ~33% of women chew; qat takes ~10% of household budget across all income groups, up to 28% among vulnerable households [ACAPS 2022], and qat spend is protected even when meals are cut. The afternoon تخزينة is the daily cash event every discretionary purchase competes against — and also the retail rhythm: qat souks are daily, all-cash, neighbourhood-level touchpoints. The pricing law this sets for every category deck: hero packs must land under the cheapest daily qat bundle (a shampoo sachet, a 50g Meswak, a styling-cream 70ml at ~$1) so the purchase never triggers the qat-guilt comparison — and the distribution lesson is to treat the qat-adjacent dukkan web as the sachet lane.

Payments and the cash-hawala rails. The financial plumbing matters more in Yemen than anywhere else in the batch. The formal banking system is split between two central banks and strained by the FTO designation; the real rails are cash (in two rival banknote regimes) and hawala, with a licensed e-wallet layer (Floosak, Jawali, ONE Cash, Jeeb, SabaCash) growing from a tiny base. For the commercial model this means: trade terms are cash-against-delivery or short-cycle cash credit; distributor working capital is the scarce commodity (and the reason established houses like HSA and Thabet command their position); and any consumer-promotion mechanics that assume digital payment are years early. The remittance system doubles as a marketing channel of sorts — the Saudi wage-earner who sends money home often also specifies the shopping list, which is one more reason the Saudi shelf functions as Yemen's aspiration shelf and why pack recognition built in KSA transfers directly into Yemeni demand.

M3 — Politics & stability

The political read inverts the intuitive one: the north is extractive but stable; the south is internationally recognised but convulsive. Frontlines have been frozen since the April 2022 truce (lapsed Oct 2022, holding de facto). The north's Houthi administration is hierarchical, taxing and increasingly institutionalised (a 20% khums levy enacted Apr 2025; a merchants' strike against levies in Nov 2025). The south delivered the programme's starkest recent warning: in December 2025–January 2026 the UAE-backed STC seized the oil provinces, was reversed within days by a Saudi-backed PLC counter-offensive, its ministers were expelled and its leader fled — Aden's politics convulsed overnight while Sana'a's did not.

So what: the uncomfortable, load-bearing conclusion is that commerce can operate in both zones and must plan for friction in both — but the risk profiles differ in kind: the north's risk is legal-financial (FTO exposure in the payment chain; rising levies), the south's is political-kinetic (overnight convulsions, currency spirals). The design answer is the two-distributor architecture with independent kill-switches per zone, not a single national bet.

Why "commerce-through-war" and not WATCH — the Sudan comparison made explicit. This programme assessed Sudan as WATCH because its war destroyed the commercial system itself: the banking network fractured beyond dual-zone workarounds, Khartoum's wholesale hub was physically razed, corridors changed hands mid-shipment, and no national distributor kept operating on both sides. Yemen's war, for all its horror, produced the opposite commercial topology: the frontlines froze early and have barely moved in four years; the extraction (permits, levies, double customs) is administered rather than anarchic — published rates, known checkpoints, receipts; the two currency regimes are stable enough to plan against (one force-pegged, one volatile but tradable); and the country's biggest company never stopped manufacturing soap, ghee and biscuits in four cities across both zones. The practical test is the shelf itself: eleven years into the war, a shopper in Sana'a can buy Vatika shampoo in six variants and a shopper in Aden can buy Vaseline in five formats. A market where that is true is not a WATCH — it is a market with a known tax on doing business, and the strategist's job is to price that tax honestly, structure around its legal edges (the FTO gate), and compete where the incumbents' exposure (American origin, north) has just handed a challenger the cleanest opening in a decade.

M4 — Regulation

Regulation is duplicated across the divide: YSMO (the national standards body — GSO/SMIIC/Codex member, headquartered in Sana'a and thus under Houthi administration) vs parallel IRG ministry functions in Aden; Houthi import permits and levies apply to anything crossing north regardless of entry port.

M5 — Trade & tariffs (the entry economics)

Yemen imports ~90% of its food and most consumer goods; it is a GAFTA member with LDC preferences, so RAK-origin Dabur product should enter duty-light — but wartime customs practice ≠ treaty text: both zones apply their own schedules and fees, and the decisive cost fact is the double-customs trap.

The two-zone commercial design, stated as an operating model. Because the two-port rule governs everything downstream, it is worth writing the target operating model explicitly rather than leaving it implicit in the tables. Each zone runs as a self-contained P&L cell: its own port of entry, its own distributor, its own currency of settlement, its own price list, its own regulatory file, and its own kill-switch — coordinated only at the level of brand, pack and supply planning out of RAK. This is not an exotic structure; it is precisely how the incumbents that never left (Unilever's distributors, Nestlé's, HSA's own divisions) have operated through eleven years of war, and it is why their products never disappeared from Yemeni shelves. The discipline it demands of Dabur is mostly negative: never let commercial pressure tempt a cross-zone shipment "just this once" (the 100% double-levy plus checkpoint risk destroys the margin and can strand the consignment), and never let one zone's crisis contaminate the other's operations.
