C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: FLANK-VIA-TOLL — "the jelly nobody makes and the cream nobody can afford." Two structural absences define the entry. (1) The jelly gap: the research identified no dedicated Algerian petroleum-jelly manufacturer — the shelf is served by intermittent Unilever Vaseline imports (400ml lait at 590, kératine 200ml at 1,000; out-of-stock at capture — the import-supply stutter visible live), lookalike repacks (Dermasoft "vaseline"), and liquid-paraffin adjacents (Nehil) [!] high-value verification: confirm no at-scale local jelly production. A tolled Herbolene — jelly base via RAK as intermediate, filled locally — would be the first locally-made branded jelly in the market, in a value segment the FX architecture protects. (2) The depilatory absurdity: Veet's DAPS-and-FX-inflated spread runs 2,700–4,500 DZD per 100ml (with 400-DZD grey-tube outliers proving the parallel chaos) — in a country whose OWN manufacturers (Venus's cires et crèmes dépilatoires; Sabrinel's depilatory line since 1982) already hold the licences the gated category requires. A tolled Fem at 700–900 sells the same ten minutes at a QUARTER of the incumbent's price, with the bridal-hammam occasion (hammam laaroussa; Tlemcen's UNESCO-listed Chedda; the Dec-2024 henna inscription Algeria co-holds) as its story home. The glow line holds absolutely: the formal channel has already legitimised CLINICAL brightening (Bioderma Pigmentbio éclaircissant at 6,400) while whitening creams sit inside the same prior-authorization gate as everything else — Dabur speaks occasion and heritage, never fairness. Gulabari waits: Algeria's rose water is a 350–560-DZD hydrolat shelf (Genilux), not Morocco's heritage economy — a probe, not a pillar.

The set's closing logic lands here as it did in Morocco: skin is the fourth family on rails the other decks pay for — the same toll architecture (Venus-class primary or the ECI/Industrie-Blanche pure-OEM route with no competing brand to protect), the same importer, the same wholesale spine, the same made-here creative licence. And the research's subtlest finding gives the flank its voice: Nivea's Algerian resellers market "100% authentique, origine européenne" — authenticity anxiety IS the import tier's own advertising. A verifiably made-in-Algeria Dabur line doesn't fight that anxiety; it renders it irrelevant (strategist read).

What the −88% import collapse actually means for skin. The fall from ~$500M to $58M of cosmetics imports is usually read as a trade statistic; read as a CONSUMER statistic it says something starker — an entire tier of branded skin care simply left the Algerian shelf within three years, and the demand it served did not vanish, it went three ways: down to local-functional brands that gained forced share (Venus's Viderm expansion), sideways into the grey/parallel layer (the 400-DZD Veet tubes; the suitcase corridor the macro maps), and into abstinence (the shopper who now uses nothing where she used Nivea). Every one of those three flows is addressable by a made-here brand at honest prices: forced share is loyal to nothing, the grey layer is being policed out, and abstinence is latent demand waiting for a price it can respect. This is why the deck reads the import collapse not as a shrunken market but as a displaced one — roughly $440M of annual spend (estimate: the import delta) that changed lanes and is still looking for a legitimate home (strategist read).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

The space maps as the set's familiar barbell with two skin-specific twists. Occupied: local-functional (Venus/LINATOL at 450–650, clinical register), import-trust (Nivea at 500–1,700 selling authenticity), dermo-clinical (3,500–6,400), and the informal layer trading everything at parallel prices. Twist one: the value-staple cell (jelly, body basics) has no local owner at all — not merely no story, no MANUFACTURER; Herbolene's entry is category creation with a made-here certificate. Twist two: the depilatory cell is price-orphaned — the licensed locals make unbranded/basic lines while the branded incumbent floats at 2,700+; between 650 and 2,700 sits a void exactly one tolled brand wide. The open position, stated once: the trusted staple, made here, at the price the DAPS wall stole — with the bridal-hammam occasion as the emotional architecture the functional locals never built (strategist read).

Why the occasion architecture is skin's differentiator and not a decoration: Venus and the local bench compete on function (hydration, SPF, anti-acné) in a register that treats every purchase as solitary; the bridal-hammam reality is the opposite — a PROVISIONED occasion where a household assembles a basket under time pressure and social scrutiny, where completeness beats comparison and a trusted assembler earns the whole basket. No brand currently owns that assembly. The kit is therefore not a promotion mechanic but a channel invention: it converts three mid-priced SKUs into one occasion purchase at 1,500–1,800, sells through bridal networks and editorial partnerships rather than shelf-adjacency, and builds the brand's meaning (the household's reliable provisioner) in the single highest-emotion moment Algerian skin care touches. Morocco's negafa economy suggested this; Algeria's UNESCO-anchored bridal culture (Chedda, Constantine, henna) gives it three named regional stages to play on (strategist read, opinion).
