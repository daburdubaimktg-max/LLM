C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: BUILD-VIA-TOLL — "be the second Sunsilk, not the first import." Three findings set the play. (1) The clean slate is confirmed, and cleaner than the macro hypothesised: an exhaustive digital sweep found zero Dabur/Vatika presence inside Algeria — no Ouedkniss listing, no e-shop, no distributor trace — and the suitcase hypothesis actually INVERTED: the documented diaspora flow runs Algeria→France (Venus and El Mordjene in returning suitcases), not Vatika inbound. Dabur enters as a true unknown; awareness must be built, not harvested. (2) The tolling lane is doubly proven in hair specifically: Unilever's Oran plant already manufactures Sunsilk and Dove locally ("100% of portfolio manufactured locally" — VP El Watan interview; 10,000 t/yr, 1.35bn DZD unit) and prices them at 350–380 DZD — competitively beside Venus (economy ≤300) rather than at import premiums; Genilux has run a British-license-to-local- manufacturer model since 2012. Global-brand-locally-made is a working formula on this exact shelf. (3) The premium ceiling is higher than the economy floor suggests: Katsaros, a founder-led curl brand, sells gel at 1,200 DZD — 4–10× the mass tier — proving Algerian women pay real money for a story that respects them (the Décomplexés de la Boucle movement). The play: a tolled, "fabriqué en Algérie" Vatika line at 330–450 DZD anchored on the henna-UNESCO story (inscribed Dec 2024 — Algeria a co-filer) and the zit-zaytoun oil culture, plus a curl sub-line at 500–700 under Katsaros's umbrella — all gated on the macro's G2 partner LOI, which precedes every dirham of this deck.

The anti-transplant discipline set by the macro governs throughout: Morocco's ritual architecture is a hypothesis library here, and this deck built its consumer spine from Algeria-native evidence only — the henna-UNESCO co-filing, the Kabyle matrilineal henna transmission, the hammam argan-finish (eastern Algeria/Aurès), prickly-pear seed oil as a DOMESTIC specialty, and a named, dated curl movement with a named commercial expression. Where Morocco's hair deck governed an existing mess, Algeria's builds on a blank page — different risk, different playbook, same herbal codes (strategist read).

How to read a market whose growth is price-led. Euromonitor's observation that Algerian hair-care growth comes from unit-price inflation rather than volume is not a discouragement — it is a map of where value migrates. In a volume-flat, price-rising market, the winners are whoever justifies the next price rung with something the shopper can believe: Venus justifies via the POY badge and familiarity; Sunsilk via global-brand trust; Swalis via ingredient claims; Katsaros via movement authenticity at 4×. The tier that CANNOT justify its prices — imported masstige, whose rises come from FX and DAPS rather than any consumer benefit — is where share leaks from, and the leak has nowhere local-plus to land: the 450–1,000 DZD band is nearly empty (T14's barbell). A tolled Vatika at 380–700 enters as the only global-heritage brand whose price rungs are justified by story AND protected from FX — collecting the masstige leak that Venus is too economy-coded and Sunsilk too story-thin to fully capture (strategist read).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

Map the shelf on two axes: price tier (economy ≤300 / local-plus 330–450 / import-masstige squeezed / luxury 20,000+) and story depth (price-value ↔ heritage-science). The occupied cells: Venus owns economy-value; Swalis holds local-plus on ingredient claims alone; Sunsilk holds local-plus on global-brand familiarity; Katsaros owns heritage-science at luxury-niche. The open cell is local-plus × heritage-science: a brand at 330–450 DZD that carries a REAL story — henna's UNESCO-celebrated, matrilineally-transmitted depth, the zit culture's oils, amla as the Indian grandmother's answer to the Algerian grandmother's olive — with lab-grade proof language and "fabriqué en Algérie" on the front. Nobody holds it: Venus has price without story-depth, Sunsilk has brand without story, the naturals tail has stories without scale, Katsaros has story at 4× the price. That cell is Vatika's natural shape, and the tolling lane is what makes its price point possible (strategist read).

The single-brand discipline, argued. Morocco's decks could deploy Amla, Vatika, Miswak and Fem simultaneously because each carried existing shelf equity; Algeria's cold start cannot fund four brand builds, and splitting a launch budget across two hair marques (Vatika + Amla) would leave both below the awareness threshold in a Facebook-first market where reach is bought in Darja content, not GRPs. The deck therefore recommends a single Vatika masterbrand with Amla as a hero VARIANT ("Vatika Amla — shine") — preserving the amla story (told through the zit register) while concentrating every dinar of media on one name. The exception clause: if the Darja concept test shows Amla name-recognition among diaspora-connected households exceeding ~20%, the dual architecture re-opens as a wave-2 question (opinion, testable).

Why the playbook's centre of gravity is moves 1 and 3, not the media plan. In Morocco the strategy's hinge was governance; in Algeria it is the LOI and the made-here claim — everything else is execution detail that any competent team adjusts in-market. Move 1 determines whether the entry exists; move 3 determines what the brand MEANS on first contact, and first contact is unrepeatable: a blank-page market grants exactly one introduction, and "the global herbal house that chose to make its products in Algeria, celebrating the henna Algeria gave the world" is an introduction no incumbent can match and no import strategy could honestly deliver. The deliberate omissions mirror the market's shape: no 1L economy assault (Venus's fortress), no import-premium tail (the FX trap), no mega-influencer spend (the micro/nano model is both cheaper and more trusted here), and no sachet architecture yet — the épicerie single-use norm is a flagged gap the Darja pass must resolve before tooling money moves (strategist read).

The waterfall is in C11 (T13): tolled economics at ~21% contribution on the 380 corridor — leaner than an import model's paper margin but real where the import's is fictional (the DAPS alternative needs a 900+ shelf). Three value-chain specifics. First, the toll fee is the negotiation: the ~150 DZD conversion+materials line swings ±20% on partner terms, and every point is a point of contribution — the two-track approach (macro sequence) exists to create that tension. Second, the royalty pipe is capped by compliance: the service-import authorization regime (Jul 2025 rule) gates what the brand can extract; conservative transfer pricing with a reinvestment clause beats aggressive extraction that invites scrutiny (macro G4). Third, scale economics run through the export rider: Algeria's 48M plus the partner's existing Tunisia/Libya/Niger dossiers make the tolled plant a REGIONAL asset — at which point the toll fee amortises across three markets and the contribution profile approaches import-free Morocco levels. The value chain's endgame is the Unilever-Oran template with Dabur economics (strategist read).

The A&P math a 21% contribution supports: at plausible year-two volumes (estimate: 2–3M units across the 6-SKU wave at national épicerie distribution), contribution of ~67 DZD/unit funds a media plan that goes far in Algeria's cost environment — micro-influencer seeding, Darja content production and Facebook reach cost an order of magnitude less than the GRP-led plans corridor brands run in Morocco, and the made-here film is a one-time asset with years of shelf life. The discipline is sequencing: no media dinar moves until the toll line runs clean (move 3's claim must be literally true), and the first two quarters' budget weights trade (wholesale terms, épicerie visibility) over consumer media — in a fragmented, 95%-private retail landscape, availability IS the first advertisement (strategist read, opinion).
