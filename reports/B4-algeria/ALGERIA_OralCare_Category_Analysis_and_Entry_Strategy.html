C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: WEDGE-AS-RIDER — "the word is taken; the brand is not — ride the hair line." Three findings reshape the Morocco playbook for Algerian conditions. (1) A footprint correction: the hair deck's zero-footprint finding does NOT extend to oral — Dabur Miswak is already sold in Algeria (hiind.net at 280 DZD/170g; a dedicated brand page at kadisse.com [!] 520-error re-fetch) as passive parallel-import stock, priced as the cheapest oral SKU found in the entire market scrape — a bargain-bin anchor that must be absorbed and repositioned, not ignored. (2) The word is crowded: miswak is already a cross-brand modifier — Signal Herbal Miswak, Colgate Miswak, Venus Buccowhite Siwak and NaturaPro Miswak (two LOCAL manufacturers) all field siwak variants. Morocco's brand-vs-variant wedge survives, but narrower: Dabur's claim compresses to being the ONLY house where miswak is the brand itself, with the 170g grams story and the heritage register nobody local can match. (3) The fluoride question is clinical, not cosmetic: a published randomized comparison (PMC) found Signal's fluoride formula outperforms Dabur Miswak on remineralization ion-release — in a high-caries market (29% orthodontic need; school-caries alarms) where pharmacists steer, the herbal-only position is a flank, not a fortress; the two-line architecture (herbal hero + fluoride variant) moves from Morocco's option to Algeria's requirement. The play: oral rides the hair deck's toll partner and CACQE authorization at marginal cost — the second SKU family on the same line, launched one wave behind hair, repositioned from 280-bargain to 330–380 heritage-value.

The rider logic is the deck's spine: Algeria's oral category on its own merits would struggle to justify a standalone entry (volume-flat, inflation-led growth per Euromonitor; a crowded siwak field; modern retail at ~3%) — but as the SECOND family through a toll line whose fixed costs hair already carries, the marginal economics are compelling and the portfolio logic (one partner, one CACQE gate, one wholesale spine, two categories) mirrors what Venus and NaturaPro themselves do. The deliberate humility: this deck launches nothing until the hair LOI lands, and its bet is dated accordingly (strategist read).

C5 — Positioning space & the open territory

The Algerian oral shelf maps onto three occupied bands and two open seams. Occupied: the value floor (Polycos/Colgate-TA at 275–370, where Dabur currently sits by accident), the local-mass middle (Signal's made-in-Oran range, Venus/NaturaPro), and the pharmacy therapeutic tier (Sensodyne at 3–5× mass). Open seam one: heritage-value — a brand whose siwak claim is identity, not flavour, at 330–380 with made-here economics; four rivals use the word but none can say "this is who we are" — Signal's Miswak is one variant among thirty, Venus's is an ingredient pairing, NaturaPro's one SKU in a functional range. Open seam two: locally-made therapeutic-adjacent — the specialty-capacity gap plus Sensodyne's import pricing leave a sensitive/gum rung (500–700) unserved by local economics; a wave-2 option once the line runs. The wedge enters seam one at launch and holds seam two as the expansion path — both riding the same toll line (strategist read).

The crowded-word economics, stated precisely. When five players share a word, the word's value migrates to whoever owns its MEANING — and meaning is built from three assets the variants lack. Asset one: time — «من خمسين عام» is a verifiable claim (Dabur's miswak-toothpaste history spans five decades); Signal's variant dates from the 2010s, the locals' from later still. Asset two: totality — for every rival the siwak SKU is a sliver of the range; for Dabur Miswak it is 100% of the brand, so every dinar of brand media compounds the word (Morocco's Signal asymmetry, now generalised against four rivals at once). Asset three: the stick's own register — the religious-practical content culture (permitted-while-fasting explainers) organically reinforces the WORD, and the brand named after the word free-rides that reinforcement in a way no herbal variant can. The counter-asset the locals hold is made-here economics — which is precisely what the toll neutralises. Post-toll, the wedge's arithmetic is simple: their price, plus our meaning (strategist read).

C14 — Lead-brand cost-to-profit value chain (Module 7)

The waterfall is in C11 (T13): tolled rider economics at ~22% contribution on the 355 hero — materially identical to hair's 21% because the categories share every fixed cost except formulation. The rider's three economic properties: first, marginal-cost entry — oral adds a product family to a paid-for partner relationship, wholesale spine, media presence and authentication programme; its true incremental cost is tooling, registration and formulation; second, portfolio insurance — two categories on one line halve the partner-concentration risk per category (a hair-line quality issue doesn't strand the whole relationship); third, the reposition arithmetic — moving from the 280 import anchor to a 355 tolled hero is +27% price with a −60% landed-cost change (estimate: parallel-import landed vs toll), meaning the reposition is margin-accretive even if volumes halve during transition — which they should not, because the 100g rung holds the floor. The wave-2 sensitive seam is the value chain's upside option: local economics against Sensodyne's 1,250–1,450 import pricing is the single largest per-unit margin pool visible in this category (strategist read).

What the fluoride line does to the P&L beyond its own sales. The Miswak F rung at 380–420 earns ordinary rider margins, but its portfolio effects are outsized: it neutralises the PMC-study attack vector before a rival weaponises it (defensive value), it converts the pharmacist from a Sensodyne-defaulting skeptic into a stockist with a mid-priced answer for the caries-worried mass shopper (channel value), and it pre-builds the regulatory and formulation muscle (NA 8283 compliance, NaF handling at the toll plant) that the wave-2 sensitive seam requires (option value). Priced against these three externalities, the F-line's development cost is cheap insurance — and the two-forms choice architecture (concept ②) converts what could read as herbal-brand weakness into projected confidence. The discipline it demands in return: absolute claim hygiene, because one therapeutic over-claim on the F-tube risks the CACQE standing of every SKU on the shared authorization (strategist read).
