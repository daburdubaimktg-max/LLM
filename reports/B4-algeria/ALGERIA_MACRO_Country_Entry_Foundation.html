M1 — Demography

So what: the Maghreb's biggest population (~48M), three-quarters urban, median age 28.8 — a larger consumer base than Morocco with LESS purchasing power per head at street level, because the dinar's two prices (official vs parallel, ~80% apart) tax every imported product twice. The demography argues for entry; the currency architecture dictates HOW.

M2 — Economy (the two-price economy)

So what: Algeria runs two exchange rates ~80% apart — official ~133 DZD/USD, parallel ("square"/Port-Saïd) ~237–240 (Jul 2026) — and that single fact does more strategic work than any other number in this deck: it taxes imports, distorts repatriation, prices the informal shelf, and makes locally-manufactured goods structurally unbeatable on cost. The macro is hydrocarbon-funded and stable at headline level (inflation ~3.7–4%), but street-level affordability stress is real enough to produce Ramadan-2026 marketplace unrest.

Two planning consequences. First, every landed-cost model runs twice: once at 133 (formal import economics, repatriation) and once at 237 (what the shopper's dinar is actually worth, and what the suitcase-trader's cost base is). A formally imported SKU pays duties on the official rate's valuation and then competes against informal stock priced off the parallel rate — a structural squeeze no pricing cleverness escapes. Second, local manufacture collapses the problem: DZD costs in, DZD revenues out, no domiciliation queue, no DAPS, no FX valuation gap — which is precisely why the state engineered the incentives this way, and why 70% of the cosmetics market is already locally made (M5/T5). The economy's message to an entrant is not subtle (strategist read).

M3 — Politics & stability

So what: a hydrocarbon-funded security state in managed transition — Tebboune re-elected Sept 2024 on disputed turnout, parliamentary elections 2 Jul 2026 (outcome pending at research date [!]), real power widely read as residing with the military leadership. For an FMCG entrant the operative politics are ECONOMIC: import-substitution is state doctrine, enforcement is active (the suitcase-trade crackdown), and social-stability sensitivity around prices makes affordability a political variable, not just a commercial one.

The political read an entrant actually needs: Algeria's headline politics (disputed turnouts, the military's role, the July 2026 parliamentary results pending [!]) matter less to an FMCG entry than three quieter facts. First, economic nationalism is the one policy with total elite consensus — whoever holds which office, import substitution and local-employment optics survive every reshuffle, which makes the make-to-enter lane politically durable in a way no import licence ever is. Second, price stability is treated as a security matter — the Ramadan-2026 marketplace unrest was met with enforcement, not just subsidy, and a foreign brand seen raising prices into a stressed street is a headline risk no marketing budget repairs; the affordability posture is brand-safety policy here. Third, the crackdown state cuts both ways — the same apparatus that raids suitcase stock will, for a compliant local producer, function as free brand protection against counterfeits and grey re-imports; Dabur's أصلي authentication programme finds in Algeria an enforcement partner the other B4 markets lack (strategist read).

M4 — Regulation

So what: three gates stack for personal care: (1) the import-forecast programme (general), (2) the cosmetics technical authorization with toxicology review (category), (3) domiciliation (financial). Medicines add PharmNet price control — Morocco's PPV jail with an Algerian accent. Halal is a hygiene-factor expectation; no dedicated Algerian halal-cosmetics certifier was identified (source gap) [!]. The regulatory conclusion mirrors the economic one: every gate except product registration disappears if the product is made in Algeria.

Read as a decade, the pattern is perfectly coherent: every gate that closes faces importers; every door that opens faces producers. An entrant who reads the 2026 rulebook literally will be planning against next semester's text; an entrant who reads the direction plans once, correctly: manufacture locally, employ locally, export regionally if possible (the Unilever-Oran template), and become the constituency the policy is FOR rather than the leakage it is against (strategist read).

M5 — Trade, tariffs & the entry lane (the defining head)

So what: there is no clean import lane into Algeria in 2025-26 — and unlike Morocco, no treaty arithmetic rescues it: DAPS safeguard duties (30–200%) apply regardless of preferential origin; Algeria unilaterally suspended GAFTA tariff preferences in Feb 2016 [!] current status; Algeria is NOT an Agadir member; no India FTA exists. RAK's certificate, Egypt's Agadir card and India's cost base are all worthless at Algerian customs. The viable lanes are local: tolling or JV with an Algerian manufacturer — legally simplified since the 51/49 abolition — in a market where local production already holds ~70% of a $903.6M cosmetics market and imports have collapsed from ~$500M to ~$58M [!] years.

The post-Jumia digital reality deserves emphasis, because it breaks a tool the programme relies on elsewhere. In Morocco, Jumia served as the public price board — the place where governance was measured, dispersion tracked, official stores planted. Algeria now has no such board: Jumia's February 2026 exit (Algeria was ~2% of group GMV; Temu/Shein pressure cited in the FY2025 disclosure) leaves Ouedkniss classifieds, Facebook groups and TikTok sellers as the visible digital shelf — none of which supports official brand storefronts, structured reviews or price observability in the same way. Consequences: (1) price governance must run through the WHOLESALE tier (El Eulma, Semmar price lists) rather than a marketplace dashboard; (2) the review-economy playbook from Morocco's skin deck has no venue — trust here is built through Facebook-group word-of-mouth and the partner's existing trade reputation; (3) counterfeit and grey-stock monitoring loses its easiest observation post, raising the weight of physical market visits in the re-validation queue; and (4) Temu/Shein's rise is itself a consumer signal — Algerian shoppers will buy cross-border direct when the formal shelf disappoints, which both proves latent demand for affordable variety and warns that a locally-made Dabur range must beat parcel-import prices, not just local rivals (strategist read).

The honest limits of this consumer picture — and the rule they impose. Algeria's public consumer record is markedly thinner than Morocco's: no equivalent of the HCP's statistical depth in beauty-relevant domains, no Jumia review corpus, no rich blog/forum layer surfaced in French-language search (the Darja layer lives on Facebook, largely invisible to outside scraping). This deck therefore carries FEWER verified ritual claims and MORE flagged gaps than its Morocco twin — deliberately. The rule it imposes on the category decks: every consumer insight must be validated through the Darja/Facebook-native pass or the partner's own trade knowledge before creative money moves; the Morocco set's rich ritual architecture (hammam occasion math, swak-beldi gender split, akker-fassi vocabulary) is a HYPOTHESIS LIBRARY for Algeria, not a fact base. Where the programme's anti-transplant rule usually guards against China conclusions, here it guards against the nearest neighbour — the subtler and therefore more dangerous transplant. What CAN be asserted from Algeria-native evidence: henna's depth, halal's universality, Facebook's primacy, the value-corridor's price points, Ramadan's dual character, and the diaspora's brand familiarity — enough to design the entry, not yet enough to write its advertising (strategist read).

The affordability picture in one paragraph: at SNMG 24,000 DZD and a parallel-rate street wallet near $100/month, Algeria's mass corridor (300–400 DZD shampoo, ~350 toothpaste) already prices personal care at roughly the same share-of-wage burden as Morocco's — but with a harder floor under it, because the imported alternative costs 2–7× more rather than 20–40% more. Why the clean slate is an asset, not only a deficit. The deficit is real too — zero reviews, zero shelf memory, no diaspora springboard beyond suitcase familiarity — but the programme's repeated lesson (Lebanon's gray anchor poisoning oral; Morocco's anarchy taxing every deck) is that starting clean at the right price beats inheriting demand at the wrong one (strategist read).

DEEP THINK — second-order moves, scenarios & risk board

The Venus fork (strategist read): Laboratoires Venus is simultaneously the best partner (category-exact plant, national distribution, 40-year trade trust) and the incumbent with least to gain from empowering a global herbal brand. Approaching Venus first risks educating the champion and being declined; approaching second-tier tollers (Nedjma, Dermal, LINATOL) first trades capability for negotiating balance and keeps Venus as the escalation option. The sequencing recommendation: parallel discreet feasibility with two second-tier tollers while a banker-led approach sizes Venus's appetite — the LOI that matters is signed with whoever concedes brand control, quality audit rights and an exit ladder. Second-order: (1) a spurned Venus becomes a motivated fast-follower — file trademarks in Algeria BEFORE any approach (Vatika/Amla/Miswak/Fem classes) [!] INAPI status check; (2) the state's localisation doctrine can flip to advantage — a Dabur JV announcement is exactly the "foreign investment in non-hydrocarbon industry" story Algiers wants to tell, worth ministerial goodwill in programme approvals; (3) the royalty pipe (service-import authorization, Jul 2025 rule) is the structure's weak joint — price transfer conservatively and validate repatriation precedent with counsel before signing [!].

Why conditional-GO rather than WATCH — the honest case both ways. The WATCH case is respectable: institutional churn (ALGEX dissolution mid-transition), FX opacity, repatriation uncertainty, election-cycle politics, and a partner-dependent path with no partner yet named. Sudan earned a WATCH on thinner grounds. But three facts tip Algeria to conditional-GO. First, the risk is front-loaded into negotiation, not capital — until an LOI is signed, the spend is feasibility studies and trademark filings; the WATCH-vs-GO distinction costs almost nothing until G2, so optionality is nearly free. Second, the policy direction is FOR this entry shape — unlike Sudan (war) or Yemen (fragmentation), Algeria's obstacles are designed incentives pointing at exactly the structure Dabur would build; the state is a difficult partner, not an adversary. Third, the window is competitive — the same suitcase crackdown squeezing informal Vatika flow squeezes every foreign beauty brand equally, and the first global herbal house to sign a credible local toll converts the whole enforcement apparatus into its moat. Waiting cedes that first-mover conversion to whichever competitor reads the direction next (strategist read, opinion).
