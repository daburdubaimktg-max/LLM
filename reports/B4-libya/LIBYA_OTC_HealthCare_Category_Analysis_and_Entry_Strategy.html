C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: BUILD-NARROW — "the shelf is the health system now; arrive sealed." The category's demand engine is documented state failure: three-quarters of Libya's primary-health-care facilities are not functioning (NRC 2021), the 2022 WHO facility assessment found acute shortages of antibiotics, insulin and blood-pressure drugs across 116 sites, over 80% of nursing staff left in 2014, and those who can afford it fly abroad to be treated — so the household treats itself, and the pharmacy (3,089 of them, 85% independents) is the clinic that stayed open. The digital proof that the shelf is ready: DSM.ly — the country's structured online pharmacy — already runs Digestive Health, Immune Boosters, Diabetic Supplement and Cough Cold & Flu as live categories with ZERO Indian or Ayurvedic brands in any of them; entry is listing, not education. The lane is the food/FDCC gate: Libya's supplement space is a documented regulatory vacuum («poor guidance and lack of regulations» — the KAP study's words), while the medicine gate (NCDCR registration + the new GS1 DataMatrix serialisation mandate) guards syrups — so Hajmola enters as a digestive confection and Honitus as a lozenge, with the syrup dossier parked, exactly the Tunisia/Algeria conclusion re-derived from Libyan machinery. And two Tunisia plays are REVERSED by Libyan evidence: honey demotes to wave 2 — the prestige tier here belongs to Jabal Akhdar sidr terroir no import can claim, the adulteration anxiety Tunisia monetised is unconfirmed in Libya, and an ~18–19% adult diabetes rate cuts both ways — and Chyawanprash defers entirely, because Libya has no bsissa: no fortifying-paste tradition was found to bridge it. Same portfolio, different order — the evidence chose (strategist read on agent evidence, 7 Jul 2026).
