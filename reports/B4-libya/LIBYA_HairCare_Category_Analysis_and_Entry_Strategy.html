C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: BUILD — "the oil lane is already Indian; brand it." Three facts organise the category. First, the demand proof Dabur didn't have to buy: a dedicated Facebook storefront sells «زيت شعر الباراشوت الهندي الأصلي في ليبيا» — original Indian Parachute hair oil — into Tripoli, and a Benghazi social-commerce seller moves «زيت المهرة» hair-growth oil at 50 LYD by WhatsApp with delivery to all Libyan cities. Indian hair oil is not a concept to introduce here; it is a grey trade to formalise — the Tunisia absorption logic transposed to a market where the grey belongs to a RIVAL, which makes official entry both an absorption and a counter-move. Second, the price map has a hole where Vatika lives: the captured ladder runs baby-shampoo 12.5 LYD (pharmacy Facebook post) → mid-tier Dermacol 39 LYD (Zumerrde, the one hard mass-tier anchor) → social-commerce oil 50 LYD → a 245–623 LYD salon tier (Kérastase/Olaplex/Redken at VogaCloset) — nothing branded, natural-story-led and officially distributed sits in the 25–60 LYD band the working target derives. Third, authenticity is the category's live crisis: the FDCC-linked raids of Feb 2025 (counterfeit factory in Tripoli using expired raw materials; lead/mercury injuries documented by name; seizures in Tripoli, Benghazi and Sabratha; a January stockpile destruction) plus a counterfeit-marketing economy paying influencers up to 15,000 LYD means the macro's serialisation doctrine is not insurance here — it is the selling line. Dabur enters as the first hair brand whose Libyan presence is OFFICIAL by design: signed agent, sealed batches, one price list, verified creators only (strategist read on agent evidence, 7 Jul 2026).

First, the distributor's 15% is the strategy priced in: three points above the B4-Maghreb standard buys a partner who prepays hard currency, carries dinar collection, owns FDCC clearance and polices the counterfeit sweep — in a market whose whole risk profile is operational, the extra margin is cheaper than any alternative structure and the macro's zero-receivables doctrine survives intact. Second, the regime is B4's kindest IF the open number breaks right: no VAT found, no classic tariff since 2005, GAFTA at 0% and a 4% port tax — but the 25–50% consumption-tax list remains unpulled for HS 3305, and the waterfall's honesty depends on it: at 25% the oils band shifts to 38–45 and survives; at 50% the mid-band thesis needs the gift-set mix to carry it — which is why the tax pull is gate 1, before any pricing is printed. Third, serialisation is COGS here, not marketing (the Tunisia certificate doctrine, fourth application): in the only B4 market with a documented counterfeit-cosmetics factory raid, the seal is the product's first feature and its cost (~3–4% of landed estimate) buys the pricing power the whole ladder rests on (estimates all).
