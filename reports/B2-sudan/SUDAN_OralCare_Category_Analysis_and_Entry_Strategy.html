Halal, mosque authority & the alcohol-free rule. Halal is total and effectively mandatory (~97% Muslim) — table stakes, not a differentiator; the differentiator is Sunnah-alignment, which Meswak inherits by botanical + nominal identity. Sudan is heavily Sufi-order (turuq) shaped, so mosque-adjacent, imam-endorsed distribution is a legitimate high-trust channel (endorsement-adjacency, not fake fatwa). The two hooks Meswak uniquely bridges. What makes Meswak more than a me-too miswak paste is that it answers two Sudanese truths at once that no rival product resolves together. The ritual hook is the Sunnah siwak — revered, native, spiritually superior, and the reason a Sudanese consumer reaches for a chewing stick before prayer. The public-health hook is caries — a real and worsening crisis (Khartoum preschool caries rose from 5.5% to 65.5%; Sudan's pooled prevalence ~57.8%) that the raw stick does not fully solve, because it delivers no fluoride. Colgate solves the second hook but not the first (a cavity brand with a miswak variant bolted on); the siwak sellers embody the first but not the second (Sunnah authenticity, no fluoride). Meswak is the only product that credibly claims both — the Sunnah plant and name, plus modern fluoride fortification — which is the honest strategic case for "continuity, not substitution": Dabur is not asking the consumer to abandon the stick, it is offering the same botanical and the same word, upgraded to protect against the decay the stick alone lets through. Strategist read (opinion): that dual-hook honesty is what an imam can endorse without contradiction, and it is why the faith channel is credible rather than opportunistic.

Payment & settlement (the war-economy money stack). How trade and consumers pay dictates the commercial terms. On the trade side, letters of credit are essentially unavailable — imports are financed by importers' own USD bought on the parallel market, settled via hawala with prepayment norms, so Dabur prices and invoices in USD/AED and expects prepaid, hawala-settled orders (never SDG credit terms). On the consumer side, physical SDG cash is scarce and the digital rail is Bankak (Bank of Khartoum, ~7M+ users, ~SDG 3M daily limit), while where mobile signal fails hawala withdrawal fees run 10–50% of value. The practical consequence, as in hair, is that a fixed SDG shelf price is impossible: price in USD internally, expose small SDG sachet tickets, and expect frequent re-pricing as the parallel rate slides. The distinctive oral-care twist is the importer/wholesaler concentration — in a scarcity economy the tier that rations FX and shelf access is disproportionately powerful, which is why the entry strategy is to win 2–3 Port Sudan importers, not to build retail distribution: control the FX-holding gatekeepers and the sachet flows through the dukkan network behind them.
