The war is the dominant entry variable, not a footnote — it dictates geography (only the SAF-held east/centre is addressable), price (a downtraded, dollarised sachet economy), supply (import via Port Sudan; local manufacture is rubble), and timing. Treat Sudan as an option on post-war reconstruction, not a live FMCG market.

Sudan is ~50.4M people (2024 projection; the war has scrambled actual distribution — no reliable current count exists [!] re-validate), the third-largest country in Africa, with the Nile as its demographic spine and the Red Sea coast (Port Sudan) as its lifeline to the world. The war has de facto partitioned it into two zones that must be understood before any commercial thought.

M2 — Macroeconomy & the two-currency split

The economy has collapsed and fractured. Real GDP contracted ~13.5% in 2024 (on top of ~-18–20% cumulative in 2023); AfDB shows a fragile +1.2% 2025 rebound, the IMF is more bearish — treat 2025 as stagflationary stabilisation at a devastated floor. Poverty exploded to ~71% (from ~36% pre-war) — ~23M people now poor — hollowing out the addressable middle class. FX reserves are ~$1.17bn (~1.1 months of imports).

The two-currency split (critical). The official rate (~600 SDG/USD) is a fiction; the parallel market sets the real economy: SDG fell ~600 (early 2023) → ~2,500 (May 2024) → ~4,400–5,000+ (2025), with quotes near ~9,900 SDG/USD in RSF-affected zones (Feb 2026). In January 2025 the SAF central bank issued new banknotes (incl. a new SDG 1,000 note) to modernise and to strand cash held in RSF areas; RSF administrations banned the new notes (possession punishable by prison). The result is two monetary zones: new SDG in the east/centre, old notes + heavy USD in the west.

How money moves. The Central Bank and much of the commercial banking system were physically destroyed/looted; cash withdrawals are largely impossible. The dominant digital rail is Bankak (Bank of Khartoum's app; +85% activations since war start), throttled by power/connectivity; hawala networks + traders carry remittances, credit and even humanitarian cash; barter is returning where cash has failed. Remittances (historically ~$3bn/yr, now largely informal) + gold are the two dollar taps keeping the consumer economy alive. Gold is also the war's financier — the UAE reported ~$1.97bn of Sudanese gold imports in 2024 (~29t), with RSF-smuggled gold routing via Chad/Libya to the UAE.

So what: never model on the official rate. Price in hard currency, expect a dollarised top and barter/informal bottom, build a 10–50% informal-fee / FX-slippage leakage into any model, and assume redenomination risk within the planning horizon. This is a currency you survive, not one you plan around.

M3 — Consumer & culture

Sudan is ~97% Sunni Muslim (predominantly Maliki), with exceptionally strong Sufi orders (turuq) — Khatmiyya, Ansar, Qadiriyya, Tijaniyya — that shape ritual, community and scent/incense culture. Halal is total and non-negotiable — the exact opposite of the China stance. Arabic is official (Sudanese colloquial the lingua franca; plus Beja/To Bedawi in the eastern Red Sea/Kassala corridor — the realistic beachhead — and Nubian in the north). Pack copy and comms must be in Sudanese-Arabic register, not Gulf or Egyptian MSA [!] re-validate.

The consumer has been financially destroyed (70–90% purchasing-power loss; poverty 71%; salaries covering days, not weeks), forcing a hard sachet / lowest-unit-price economy — the addressable spend is survival-adjacent hygiene and essential grooming, plus a thin diaspora-funded premium pocket in Port Sudan. But the beauty-and-grooming ritual is deep, female-led and remarkably war-resilient — tied to marriage and identity, it persists even as budgets shrink and rituals move home-based. This is Dabur's cultural handhold.

The physical lane is clear; the reputational lane is the real gate. The regulatory & compliance clearance (feasible, but sequence it now). Two regulators gate Dabur's portfolio, and the dossier work can and should be done during the WATCH window so a recovery launch is turnkey. SSMO (the Sudanese Standards & Metrology Organization, with a Port Sudan branch) runs a mandatory Pre-shipment Verification of Conformity (PVoC) — a Certificate of Inspection issued at origin (India/UAE/Turkey) before shipment, so it is built into the export SOP, not cleared on arrival. NMPB (the National Medicines & Poisons Board, under the Medicines & Poisons Act 2009) regulates medicines and cosmetics: cosmetics require plant registration, while anything therapeutic/OTC (Hajmola, Pudin Hara, medicated oils, chyawanprash-type) faces fuller, slower medicine registration — and an Ayurvedic/herbal claim can be pushed onto the heavier medicine track, so classify early. Sudan is an OIC/SMIIC member, so OIC/SMIIC halal standards (1 for food, 4 for cosmetics) are the reference and a SMIIC-recognised halal certificate is the route. Arabic labelling is de-facto mandatory in a ~97% Arabic-reading market.

M5 — Origin stance

India-origin is a pragmatic asset on affordability + herbal credibility — but the framing must flip from "Ayurveda" to "natural·herbal·halal." India is a top-tier, historically embedded trade partner (exports to Sudan ~$545m in 2024; the #1 pharma-import origin), and Indian hair oils, textiles and films carry deep cultural pre-familiarity across Sudan. In a currency-collapsed economy, India's value-for-money reputation is a structural advantage. Two guardrails: (1) a documented Africa-wide "cheap-Indian-import" quality skepticism (and a specific 1999 contaminated-IV-fluid scar) means Dabur must lead with visible quality cues — sealed packaging, Arabic + halal marks, brand heritage; (2) do NOT lead with "Ayurveda" — the word carries a Hindu/Indic religious connotation off-strategy in a ~97% Muslim, Arab-identifying market.

The winning origin story: not "ancient Indian science" but "natural, herbal, halal — the plants you already trust." India origin stays as a value + herbal-heritage signal; the cultural framing is Islamic-natural, Arabic-first. Meswak (siwak) is the near-perfect origin-neutral, faith-positive hero — siwak is Sudanese/Islamic vocabulary, not Indian. RAK/Dubai-made provenance is reassuring on quality (Gulf QC halo) — but keep the UAE supply lane out of the war-economy narrative (M4).

Dabur's portfolio maps onto this with unusual precision.

Why the ritual survives the war — and why that matters commercially. The single most important consumer insight for a recovery-window entrant is that Sudanese beauty ritual is counter-cyclical. Because dukhan, dilka, khumra and karkar are home-made, communal, low-cash and tied to marriage and female identity, they do not collapse when incomes do — they move informal and shrink in budget, but the behaviour and the ingredient literacy persist. A displaced woman in Kassala still knows what talh smoke, turmeric-honey dilka and sesame karkar do for skin and hair; the demand is dormant, not destroyed. That is categorically different from a discretionary category that a recession simply switches off. For Dabur it means the recovery-window task is not demand-creation but demand-capture: offering the branded, convenient, consistent-quality, halal version of rituals the consumer already performs — Vatika for the karkar oiling, a turmeric-honey DermoViva for the dilka scrub, Gulabari for the khumra scent-layer, Meswak for the siwak. The Sufi orders reinforce this: scent and incense are devotional as well as cosmetic, so a fragrance-forward, natural, halal positioning is culturally load-bearing in a way it is nowhere else in the programme.
