C1 · Category frame — a priced shelf and an absent category

Namibian OTC wellness splits into a mapped half and an empty half. The mapped half: the chains' vitamin wall (the full SA supplement assortment through Clicks/Dis-Chem — the region's most developed pharmacy retail, with Dis-Chem's first-ever non-SA store opened in Windhoek in 2014), and a honey shelf that is a NET-IMPORT market (~US$1.23M/yr, 2023, +82% y/y) whose top source is Zambia ($657k/156t — the batch's own sourcing thesis sitting in the customs data), whose branded ceiling is Goldcrest at ≈N$155/kg under the peg, and whose local production is artisanal and capacity-constrained (no national brand found) UN Comtrade 2023; shoprite.co.za Goldcrest pricing; Beekeeping Association of Namibia [!] 2025 series + in-store NAD confirm. The empty half: no Ayurvedic wellness tonic is merchandised in mainstream retail anywhere in the SACU/CMA bloc — Chyawanprash exists in the region only through diaspora e-commerce (Ubuy, Desertcart, IndiaBazaar) — so the entry here is category creation on the region's smallest, cheapest, best-instrumented stage ubuy.co.za/desertcart absence-reads Jul 2026 [!] one Dis-Chem aisle browse for any Himalaya/Patanjali tonic that may have opened the slot. The regulatory door is genuinely ambiguous and the deck says so: NMRC registers complementary medicines AND runs a Schedule 0 general-sale class AND is reportedly still finalising CM guidelines — whether a food-based herbal jam is foodstuff (Ordinance 18/1979), Schedule-0 general-sale, or a registrable CM is EXACTLY the letter's question, with all three doors pre-planned nmrc.gov.na; MHSS docs; macro M4 [!] the classification letter = task #1.

C2 · Demand architecture — the affluent patient and the honest niche

The demand map is unusually precise because the epidemiology is: diabetes prevalence runs ~5.1% nationally but 8.5% in the richest quintile against 2.4% in the poorest — an inverse-of-typical gradient that puts the NCD-aware, wellness-spending consumer EXACTLY where the chains already are (Windhoek and the coast), with a ~9.3% urban directional figure reinforcing it PubMed 30058263; BMC PH 2024 [!] STEPS current. That is the Chyawanprash shopper: urban, salaried, supplement-literate, walking a Dis-Chem vitamin wall that already sells her omega-3s and multivitamins at SA prices — what the wall does not offer is a food-format herbal tonic, and the entry's demand question is whether the format translates outside its diaspora. Honey's demand needs no such question: the shelf exists, imports grew 82% in a year, and the buyer already pays the Goldcrest ceiling for pure-honey positioning — Dabur Honey's ask is one facing beside it at a credential premium, sized to a market the deck refuses to inflate: ~US$1.2M of annual import value makes honey a NICHE here, and the 13th verdict's whole content is entering at that size without anchor ambitions.

Lesson: Chyawanprash's pack, claims and price must read as this aisle's peer — pharmacy-plain, certificate-forward — while its FORMAT (a food, a spoonful, a family jar) is precisely its differentiation from everything on the wall.

The diaspora e-commerce tier — the demand signal and the wrong lane. Ubuy/Desertcart/IndiaBazaar carry Chyawanprash into the region at import-parcel prices: proof of diaspora demand, and a lane the entry deliberately does not build on — the category question is whether NON-diaspora shoppers adopt the format, and only the mainstream aisle answers it.

Self-teardown — the pilot argument, stated and stress-tested. The deck's boldest claim: Namibia is the RIGHT place to answer the CMA's Ayurvedic-tonic question — smallest stage, same chains, cleanest instrumentation (one wall, chain EPOS, the seam trial's machinery reused), lowest cost of a negative. The stress-test: a category pioneered on 3M people may under-read demand that exists at SA's scale and diversity (a Johannesburg diaspora-adjacency effect Namibia cannot show). Answer: the pilot's read is designed as a FLOOR, not a ceiling — adoption here (no diaspora tailwind, small stage) implies stronger adoption at scale; rejection here bounds the claim-free food-format proposition specifically, and Dabur SA can still test claim-led or diaspora-led routes at home. The pilot risks a false negative on one specific proposition, and prices that risk at two facings.

The consumer decode in one scene — the trolley with room in it

Saturday, The Grove Mall. A mining-company accountant — the richest-quintile profile the epidemiology describes, pre-diabetic since last year's check-up — walks Dis-Chem's wellness wall with actual discretionary money, a habit of reading labels, and a quiet, specific anxiety about sugar. She already buys a multivitamin and an omega-3 at SA prices without blinking. What the wall cannot sell her is anything that feels like FOOD — everything is capsules, clinical and joyless — and what the honey shelf sells her is sweetness she is trying to ration. A sugar-free herbal jam with a spoon-a-day ritual and a certificate-forward label lands precisely in the gap between her wall and her worry — IF the format reads as wellness rather than as someone else's grocery. That "if" is the entire pilot, and this shopper — not a persona, a prevalence statistic with a postcode — is why the pilot runs HERE: she exists in measurable density, she shops at exactly two chains, and her till data will answer the CMA's question without a single survey. Her counterpart in Ondangwa is not in this deck's year-one map, and the deck says so to her credit rather than its own convenience.

C5 · The letter with three doors — and the pilot's design

The classification question, filed via counsel to NMRC in Q1: is a Chyawanprash-composition food-based herbal jam (a) a foodstuff under Ordinance 18/1979, (b) a Schedule-0 general-sale substance, or (c) a complementary medicine requiring registration — and if (c), under which guideline given the CM framework's in-progress status? Door A (foodstuff): the fastest lane — label compliance, claims held to food-safe language, on shelf within quarters. Door B (Schedule 0): general-sale with NMRC visibility — modest dossier, similar timeline. Door C (CM registration): the long lane — and the decision point for the ZaZiBoNa option, where Zambia's dossier work and the founding-member machinery finally earn their keep IF the category's read justifies the cost. The deck's tempo rule: the honey slide never waits for the letter (food, unambiguous); the tonic never ships before it. And the PILOT'S DESIGN, fixed pre-launch like oral's: two facings on the wellness wall, the food-format proposition claim-free, chain EPOS tracked against the wall's comparable new-listing benchmark, read at two quarters — the CMA category question answered for the price of a shelf-talker.

The two-clock note, third use

Mozambique invented the two-clock grammar (food now, paper later); Angola inverted it (all clocks gated); Namibia runs its lightest edition — and adds the grammar's missing rule: WHAT SETS THE CLOCK-SPEED. Honey's clock runs at review-calendar speed because nothing about it is novel here (an import brand joining an import shelf). The tonic's clock runs at letter-plus-pilot speed because BOTH its door and its demand are unknowns — and the deck refuses to let honey's easy tempo pull the tonic forward ("the two clocks never blur", now a three-market law). The rule, stated for the batch: a product's clock is set by its largest unknown, not by its container's departure date — the sentence Angola's OTC deck built a whole file to defend, now cheap enough to state in passing because the programme paid for it once.

C16 · What does not transplant

Angola's wait has no work here (the shadow is light, the paper legible); Mozambique's two-clock grammar TRANSPLANTS in shape (honey now, tonic on paper) but not in stakes — there the letter decided a mass entry, here it schedules a pilot. The Mozambican protection clause finds no counterpart industry and travels as posture only — the deck refuses to manufacture a protectee for symmetry's sake. Zambia's dossier machinery arrives as a DORMANT module for the first time (opened only if door C meets a positive read — the paper relay learns to idle). What this deck exports: the PILOT-AS-REGIONAL-INSTRUMENT design (small-stage category creation with a floor-not-ceiling read, priced at two facings — the programme's cheapest way to answer any regional category question), and the honey taxonomy's completed RANGE-WITH-SIZES: sell-anchor (nowhere yet), sell-beside (Mozambique), sell-niche (here), source (Zambia), defer (Angola), abstain (the early files) — six postures, each with a market that argued it.

DEEP THINK · Category creation, argued against its own romance. Creating a category is the most flattering story a deck can tell itself, so this one interrogates the impulse. Why does the CMA lack an Ayurvedic-tonic category — absence of supply, or absence of demand? The supply answer is documented: no mainstream retailer ranges the format, and the diaspora lane's friction (parcel prices, 24% landed fees) suppresses whatever demand exists. The demand answer is genuinely unknown — which is precisely what makes a two-facing pilot the right-sized response and anything larger a vanity spend. The deck's protection against romance is arithmetic: the pilot's fully-loaded cost (talkers, a tasting cycle, trial terms) is smaller than the cost of ONE mis-sized container of any sibling category — so even a clean negative is bought at the programme's lowest-ever price for regional knowledge. And the floor-not-ceiling caveat cuts the other way against despair: Namibia's 3M-person, diaspora-thin stage is the format's HARDEST fair test in the CMA; survival here travels, failure here bounds one proposition only. The deck creates a category the way the method demands: cheaply, instrumented, and ready to be wrong.
DEEP STRAT · The set's economics, closed by this deck. With the wellness slides priced, the Namibia set's full arithmetic is visible: hair pays the fieldwork (the fortnight, the audits, the panel), oral and skin ride at slide cost, and OTC delivers the set's margin peak (14%) plus its only regional deliverable (the CMA memo). Total set launch cost indexes below ANY single category launch elsewhere in the batch — the extension market's whole thesis in one line. The strategic consequence for the B8-SUMMARY: Namibia should be ranked not by its own modest revenue ceiling but by its INSTRUMENT value — the drift number, the seam read, the category pilot, the intra-group relay curve — four numbers the programme reuses across every CMA and middle-income file to come. Small markets earn their place in a 42-country programme by answering questions cheaply; this set is designed to answer four.
