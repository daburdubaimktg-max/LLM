The consumer decode in one scene — the basket that isn't rationed

Month-end, Checkers Windhoek. A teacher loads her trolley and the toothpaste decision takes four seconds: the family 140g Colgate, same as always, because nothing about the category asks for more of her attention — it works, it's there, it costs what it costs. This is what a solved category looks like from inside, and it is why every access-era instinct the batch built must stand down south of the Red Line: she is not rationing, not switching on price, not waiting for distribution to reach her. The ONLY doors into her basket are curiosity and claim — a shelf-talker that says "plants beside your paste" at a price two rand above her default, low enough to try without deciding anything. Her equivalent in Ondangwa shops the batch's usual way — the smallest tube, the vendor's stall, the wage floor's arithmetic — and for her the doors are the old ones: price, presence, and a seal she can trust. The deck's entire architecture is these two shoppers refusing to be averaged: a seam for the teacher, a tube for the north, and no sentence in this file that pretends they are one person.

C16 · What does not transplant

The batch's access-first oral grammar — Zambia's, Angola's, Mozambique's — does not transplant SOUTH of the Red Line: this is the programme's first oral market where the mass floor is already affordable to its shoppers, so the southern entry is a PREFERENCE play at a seam, a posture no prior file needed. Angola's parity-minus-one rule inverts (the seam is parity-PLUS to mass — argued, not assumed); Mozambique's Meswak machinery has no counterpart (no Muslim-minority geography); the fluoride commandment arrives at its lightest (a standards-body email, not a public-health gate). What this deck exports: the SEAM ENTRY as a posture for solved categories in richer markets (the programme's remaining middle-income files will need it), and the pre-written two-posture slide — the cheapest contingency design in the batch.

DEEP THINK · The seam defended against both simplicities. Simplicity one says: skip the south — it is solved, saturated and small; put everything into the north's access play where the batch's craft applies. But the south is where the margin is (12% vs single digits), where the entry is nearly free (a slide on an existing agenda), and where the herbal register's Namibian question gets answered with real velocity data that the north's play then inherits — skipping it buys nothing and learns nothing. Simplicity two says: the south IS the market — its numbers will look complete within two quarters; declare victory and let the north wait indefinitely. That is the two-speed failure the hair deck named, and this deck holds the same line: the north's tube is a judged clause, not a roadmap decoration. The seam and the tube are one category's two honest plays, and the deck's value is refusing to let either masquerade as the other.
DEEP STRAT · The trial as the batch's cleanest experiment. Every prior oral file measured access effects — doors, rationing, usage growth — in markets where the category itself was the variable. Namibia's seam trial is the batch's first CLEAN preference experiment: a solved category, a stable ladder, chain-tracked velocity, an SA benchmark for comparable new listings, and a single new variable (the herbal story at the seam). Whatever it returns is unusually interpretable: acceptance quantifies the herbal register's draw in an SA-typical retail environment — a number Dabur SA itself can use one border south; rejection cleanly bounds it. The deck therefore treats the trial's DESIGN as a deliverable: the benchmark defined before launch, the read date fixed, both outcomes' next moves pre-written. Two quarters, one variable, either answer valuable — the cheapest good experiment the programme has run, on the batch's smallest stage.
