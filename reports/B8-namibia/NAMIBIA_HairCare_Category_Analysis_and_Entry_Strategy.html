NAMIBIA · HAIR CARE — "THE LISTING WALKS": EXTEND-THE-LISTING

The programme's first extension entry: Dabur Amla is a live Dis-Chem SA catalogue SKU, and Dis-Chem's shelves continue into Windhoek — so this deck begins with a confirmation, proceeds as account management, and becomes strategy only where the north begins · All N$ numbers at NAD=ZAR parity + measured drift unless flagged · Copy panel rules per macro T5b · 6 July 2026
FOCUS · One line: get Dabur Amla (and Vatika behind it) onto Namibian shelves by the shortest route that exists anywhere in this programme — walking an existing South African listing across a zero-tariff border into the same retailer's own stores — then earn the rest of the country in the batch's usual way: a quantified salon economy at bairro prices, a value tier owned by local chains, and a northern half that speaks Oshiwambo and shops by the wage floor.

C1 · Category frame — a market that is mostly an aisle, and partly a country

Namibian hair care in the salaried band IS South African hair care: the same Dark & Lovely, ORS, TRESemmé, Motions and Sofn'Free assortments, the same Clicks/Dis-Chem planograms, the same catalogue prices give or take an unmeasured 5–15% drift chain pages; SA catalogues under the peg [!] the drift walk. What the aisle does not describe: a quantified salon economy (the batch's cleanest — Windhoek relaxing from N$150, relaxer service N$300, braiding N$200–450, cuts N$210–530 via Fresha listings, with the bairro and Oshakati tiers below it unmeasured fresha.com Jul 2026 [!] northern ladder = a named gap), an informal north where decanted oils and single-unit cash buys rule, and a storied-oils column that shows no confirmed occupant — no Vatika, no Amla, no Himalaya CONFIRMED on a Namibian shelf, though the supplying retailer's SA catalogue carries Dabur Amla 100ml today dischem.co.za live SKU; Namibian aisle UNCHECKED — the deck's first fact and first task. The frame, honestly drawn: this is the programme's smallest hair market, its cheapest possible entry, and its most instructive test of when reuse is right.

C2 · Demand architecture — the aisle shopper and the wage-floor shopper

Run the macro's two scenes through hair. The Katutura nurse at Clicks evaluates a storied oil the way her Johannesburg counterpart does — against TRESemmé's treatments and D&L's after-care, on claim, at SA-parity price; for her, Dabur Amla is a NEW OPTION on a familiar shelf, and the chain's credential does the introducing. The Oshakati trader's customer has never seen the aisle: her oil is decanted, her price reference is the wage floor, and a sealed 90ml bottle at N$25–35 would be her category's first branded object — the batch's standard value-market build, wage-anchored, trader-routed, Oshiwambo-registered. The demand architecture therefore runs two engines at two speeds: the SOUTH is won at the listing review (facings, adjacency, the drift-checked price), the NORTH is won the slow way the batch knows (wholesale margin, the trader's repeat experience, radio later). Between them sits the salon economy — braiders whose service prices are quantified and whose product habits are not [!] the 10-salon product-use probe, W0 — the kit mechanic's fourth market, wholesale-recruited, priced to bairro reality rather than Fresha's tier.

C3 · Brand-by-brand teardown

Dark & Lovely / the SA relaxer complex — the incumbent system. Present through every chain; the relaxer-vs-natural tension runs here as it does across the continent (D&L itself now sells a Natural range). Lesson: the chemical lane is served, defended, and refused by programme law — its value to this deck is the AFTER-CARE shelf it creates, where a storied oil sits legitimately beside the SA brands Ubuy Namibia listings; SA catalogues [!] Namibian facings check.

TRESemmé / the treatment mid — the price architecture. SA catalogue prices proxy the mid band; Amla's SA price (a live number on the Dis-Chem site) already POSITIONS the brand there — the rare teardown where the entrant's own price exists before the entry dischem.co.za [!] pull the current figure.

The decant economy — the north's incumbent. Same job description as three prior markets: beaten on seal, honesty and a name that returns; never raced to its floor. The northern SKU's design brief is inherited whole, re-priced to the N$ wage floor.

Himalaya — the column's other question. In SA Clicks/Dis-Chem today; Namibian presence unconfirmed. If it crossed, the herbal column has a neighbour and Dabur differentiates within it; if not, Dabur's extension may carry the column across alone. One aisle answers both hair and oral's versions of this question — the shared W0 walk's efficiency himalayawellness.co.za; [!] the crossing check.

The salons — the quantified machine. Windhoek's ladder is measured; its product economics are not. The kit mechanic arrives in its fourth market with its cheapest recruitment yet — Wutow's wholesale ledger and the chains' own beauty-counter staff know the salons — and its usual rule: field-verified arithmetic or the kit doesn't ship.

Self-teardown — the extension states its own risk. An entry this cheap has one failure mode the batch has never priced: NEGLECT. A listing that walks across a border with no owner, no facings plan, no drift check and no velocity floor is not an entry — it is an accident that will be quietly delisted at the first range review. The deck's answer is the macro's ownership clause made operational: one named Dabur SA key-account manager, a facings-and-adjacency plan agreed at the extension meeting, the drift number on the price file, and a velocity floor benchmarked to the SKU's own SA per-door numbers — the extension managed as seriously as a launch precisely because it costs too little to demand anyone's seriousness by itself.

The consumer decode in one scene — the shelf she already trusts

Saturday morning, Maerua Mall Clicks. A civil servant in her thirties — relaxed hair, a standing six-week salon appointment, a phone full of SA natural-hair TikTok she half-intends to act on — walks the hair aisle she has walked for a decade. She is not waiting for Dabur; she has never heard of it. But she has bought unfamiliar brands from this exact shelf before, because the shelf itself vouches: if Clicks ranges it, it is real, returnable and priced like everything beside it. When an Amla bottle appears between the D&L treatment and the TRESemmé mask at a price that reads normal for the set, her adoption question is not "can I trust this company?" (the chain answered it) but "what does it do that my current one doesn't?" — a CLAIM question, answerable by a shelf tag and one TikTok. Six hundred kilometres north, her cousin's adoption question is entirely different — "is it really full, is it really pure, and can I afford it this week?" — and no chain exists to answer any of it. One brand, two questions, two decks' worth of work inside one deck: the extension answers the first question by arriving; everything else in this file is for the second.

C5 · Register architecture, hair edition

Three rules. (1) The south speaks South African — the one legitimate creative transplant in the batch: Dabur SA's existing English trade materials and pack faces serve the chains' aisle as-is, AUDITED once against the Namibian fences (otjize, healers, the lightening law) before reuse — an audit that costs an afternoon and prevents the programme's worst headline. (2) The north speaks Oshiwambo, later, properly — the new panel's copy, the trader's one-liner, radio only behind filled distribution; nothing northern ships in English because a shelf in Oshakati with the wrong-language label is just a more expensive decant. (3) The fences are three and absolute: otjize (never referenced, never "inspired by"), the healer world (oil is care, not cure), and the lightening law (the private-law audit covers hair copy's glow-adjacent drift — pending the gazette's classification, binding regardless).

The worked example — one range review, four categories

Run branch B's meeting end to end, because it is the intra-group relay's whole mechanism. Dabur SA's key-account manager sits in a scheduled Dis-Chem range review in Johannesburg — a meeting that exists anyway. Agenda item: Namibian doors. The case is three of the chain's own numbers (Amla's SA velocity, the Windhoek stores' hair-category sales, the after-care set's facings) plus one new number (the measured drift). The ask: range Amla 100ml in the Namibian stores at the SA price + drift, one facing beside the after-care set, reviewed quarterly against the ×0.7 floor. Then the relay clause: the SAME slide tables Herb'l and Herbolene for the same doors — the pharmacy aisle's herbal column and the jelly set — so oral's and skin's Namibian entries are agenda ITEMS, not separate campaigns. Total incremental cost of adding three categories to the meeting: three slides. If the batch's relay index measured 45–55% for categories sharing a route, the intra-group version measures in single digits — and THAT number, documented at this one meeting, is the limit-case data point the B8-SUMMARY's relay section needs to complete its curve.

C11 · The flag-moves — refusals, said once

Five refusals. No chemical-lane entry — final statement, fifth market. No otjize adjacency in any creative, including reused SA assets — the audit is the enforcement. No silent drift-pocketing — the premium over SA price is measured and displayed, never absorbed into margin by inattention. No English-labelled northern push — the wrong-language shelf is a more expensive decant. And no strategy theatre in the south — the deck refuses to dress a listing extension as a conquest; the honest name for wave 1 is account management, and the programme's credibility is better served by saying so.

The extension in programme perspective

Six hair decks in, the programme has now entered by wedge (Zambia), by code (Angola), by claim (Mozambique) and by extension (here) — a complete grammar of entry modes, each derived from its market's structure rather than from the last market's success. The B8-SUMMARY should print the four side by side as the batch's method exhibit: the same category, the same portfolio, four different first moves, and in each case the deciding fact was structural (a window, a code, an empty shelf, a border that isn't one). That exhibit is worth more to the programme's next forty markets than any single country's revenue — and Namibia's contribution to it is the cheapest lesson of the four.

The two-speed governance note

One paragraph on the deck's structural tension, because it will surface in every quarterly review. The south will produce numbers within a quarter; the north will produce milestones for a year before it produces numbers — and boards read numbers. The predictable failure: wave 3 quietly starved because wave 1's dashboard looks complete. The deck's protections: the north's milestones (panel commissioned, board built, wholesale terms drafted) sit ON the same dashboard as the south's velocities, owned by the same named owner, reviewed in the same meeting; and the bet's clause 5 makes the north's honest entry-or-deferral a judged outcome, not a footnote. A country that is two countries needs one dashboard with two kinds of progress on it — the batch's two-wallet grammar, promoted from pricing to governance.

C16 · What does not transplant

Nearly everything the batch built for hair LAUNCHES stays home, because this is not a launch: Zambia's radio skeleton, Angola's code-joining, Mozambique's door-recruitment all answer questions the southern aisle never asks. What transplants is the batch's VALUE-MARKET half, aimed at the north: the two-anchor pricing method, the kit mechanic (4th adaptation: chain-and-wholesaler recruitment), the sealed-bottle-vs-decant design brief, the panel-as-launch-team law. And one transplant is licensed that the programme has refused everywhere else: Dabur SA's creative crosses the border as-is, because the border is commercially internal — with the fence audit as the price of the licence. What this deck exports: the ANTI-NEGLECT PACKAGE (owner, plan, floor, drift — the governance kit for any future extension entry), and the finding that an extension market's hardest work is its NON-extension half — Botswana should expect the same shape.

DEEP THINK · The cheapest entry, stress-tested. An entry that costs one meeting invites two failures the batch has never faced. First, NEGLECT (argued in C3): cheap things go unowned; the anti-neglect package is the deck's actual product. Second, FALSE CONFIDENCE: if Amla trades well in three Windhoek Dis-Chems, the numbers will look like national success while describing one salaried suburb — the two-board discipline exists so the dashboard never mistakes Katutura's mall for the country. The stress-test's honest conclusion runs the other way too: if the extension UNDERPERFORMS its SA benchmark, that is a finding about the drift, the facings or the catchment — not about Namibia, and not about the brand; the velocity floor is set at ×0.7 precisely so small-catchment noise doesn't read as strategic failure. The extension is cheap; reading it correctly is the expensive skill.
DEEP STRAT · The worked fortnight — how W0 decides a country. Day 1–2: the Windhoek walk (Dis-Chem Wernhill + Grove, Clicks ×3) — rows 1–4 of the matrix, the branch, the Himalaya verdict, photographs of the after-care set for the facings plan. Day 3: the drift basket at shop.pnp.na plus physical tags — one spreadsheet, the number every ladder cites. Day 4–5: the fence audit of Dabur SA's creative library. Week 2: Katutura + Oshakati — the decant census (row 6), the salon probe (row 7), the Oshiwambo panel's commissioning brief, the Wutow introduction. Ten working days, two people, and every open question in this deck has an answer or an owner — the batch has never bought a country's design this cheaply, and the discipline is spending the savings on the north rather than banking them.
