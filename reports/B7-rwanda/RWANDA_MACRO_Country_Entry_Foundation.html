Language reality — and a warning this report writes into policy. Kinyarwanda is the near-universal mother tongue (~99%); English is the official school medium since the 2008 pivot; French persists among older elites; Swahili became the fourth official language in 2017 but remains legislative rather than street-level. Brands and ads run Kinyarwanda + English mixes. Two consequences: first, this is the programme's first Kinyarwanda market — no bespoke language expert exists (gap rule applies), and the research pass has already caught one dangerous false-friend: uruhu, a plausible-looking gloss for "skin," in fact means foreskin in dictionary sources [!] re-derive ALL Kinyarwanda glossary entries via the Iriza dictionary + native panel before any copy ships. Every Kinyarwanda term in these five reports is therefore panel-gated at a stricter standard than the programme's Swahili work, where regional exposure runs deeper. Second, unlike Tanzania's single-language radio nation, Rwanda's registers stratify by cohort — mass radio speaks Kinyarwanda, urban aspiration mixes English — so copy architecture needs two registers from day one.

SO WHAT: the demographic read is a small, young, single-language, tightly-administered nation — the opposite of Tanzania's sprawling plurality. Smallness is the strategic fact: Rwanda cannot carry a stand-alone entry P&L at Dabur's usual depth, but as the fourth seat on infrastructure the batch already pays for (the Dar corridor, the EAC registrations, the radio playbook), it is nearly free optionality on the region's fastest-growing economy.

SO WHAT: three wallet facts rewrite the Batch-7 template. First, Rwanda is materially richer per household than Tanzania (27.4% national poverty vs 71% below $3.65) — the LUP law softens; the ladder can start one rung higher. Second, the May 2026 inflation print (12.3%) against a stabilised franc says price-point durability, not currency, is the near-term risk — design price points with headroom. Third, 76%-of-adults mobile money plus the eKash interop rail make Rwanda the batch's only market where a digital promo mechanic (cashback-to-wallet) can reach the mass consumer at launch rather than in year three.

The wallet read beneath the averages

The M2 numbers deserve one paragraph of interpretation because Rwanda's wallet is easy to misread in both directions. The optimistic error: 9.4% growth and a 27.4% poverty rate suggest a consumer boom — but the base is small, the growth is construction-and-services-led, and 38.5% still live under $3/day; this is a market of careful shillings, not discretionary ones. The pessimistic error: treating Rwanda as Tanzania-poor — it is not; the household at the median has meaningfully more room, urban Kigali runs a visible aspirational tier (Sensodyne at 5,500 sells; knotless braids at 30,000 sell), and the mobile-money rail means promotional value lands in wallets instantly rather than leaking through trade. The correct read sits between: a two-tier market — a Kigali tier that buys mainstream-premium on brand trust, and a national tier that buys the smallest honest pack of a name radio has made familiar. The decks build both tiers from day one because the distribution (modern trade + Kimironko wholesale) reaches both from the same Kigali depot — another smallness dividend.

SO WHAT: Rwanda's regulatory bar is the highest in the batch — and that is the moat. Every rail that raises entry cost (per-SKU registration fees, S-Mark, Track-and-Trace, the plastic ban, the lightening regime) also raises the cost of competing informally, in the one market whose state actually enforces. The formal entrant's premium is protected here as nowhere else in East Africa. The binding design change is packaging: no sachets — the trial rung must be engineered as the smallest honest rigid pack.

The regulatory read completes with a sequencing instruction the decks inherit: in Rwanda, paper precedes freight by a full quarter. The per-SKU fee structure punishes speculative breadth (register eight SKUs, not thirty); the import-visa requirement means an unregistered container is a stranded container; and the S-Mark plus Track-and-Trace enrolment each carry their own clock. W0 in the wave plan is therefore four months long not out of caution but out of arithmetic — and the July launch window (post-Kwibuka) conveniently absorbs exactly that runway if filings begin in the new year.

Dabur's actual footprint — the correction that reframes the entry

The first research pass returned "zero footprint"; the category sweep corrected it: Dabur Herb'l toothpaste (Gel Mint&Lemon 140g @ RWF 3,500, + 75g) and Dabur Herbolene petroleum jelly (Argan and Aloe 225ml) are live on the Isokko marketplace, sold by Sawa Citi — the supermarket group that opened Rwanda's first SPAR. No Vatika, no Amla oil, no Meswak, no Red. The likely route is Nairobi wholesale re-export (a Nairobi wholesaler carries Meswak six-packs) [!] confirm Sawa Citi's source — authorized vs parallel. Note the price signal: Herb'l at 3,500 sits ABOVE Colgate Herbal at 2,500 — the grey route has accidentally positioned Dabur as a premium herbal brand. That accident is information: someone at Sawa Citi believed the brand could carry a 40% premium over Colgate, and stocked it.

SO WHAT: the competitive synthesis in three sentences. The champion (Sulfo) is a partner-shaped incumbent, not a CCIL-shaped rival. The price umbrellas Dabur needs — herbal oils above mass (Hemani at 10,000), herbal paste above Colgate (its own grey listing at 3,500) — already exist, built by others. And the entry's first commercial act is not a launch but a phone call: to Sawa Citi, to formalise a relationship that already trades.

The authenticity thesis, fourth market

The programme's East-African through-line — in counterfeit-taxed markets, genuineness is the scarcest attribute a brand can own — arrives in Rwanda inverted. Tanzania and Kenya are high-counterfeit environments where Dabur must BUILD authenticity infrastructure; Rwanda is a comparatively low-counterfeit environment (antibiotic falsification 8.2%, the region's low) where the STATE has already built it — S-Mark, Track-and- Trace, an enforcement police that publishes seizure values. The brand's job flips from constructing trust rails to being conspicuously ON them: registered, marked, traceable, and visibly compliant with the plastic law that half the region's FMCG formats would violate. In the governed market, compliance IS the marketing — the entrant that files everything first can say the one thing that matters here: we are exactly what the system says we are. That claim, in this country, does work that a thousand GRPs cannot.

What the price board teaches before a single deck is written

Three instructions carry from M9 into all four category decks. First, the smallest legal pack IS the trial rung: with sachets banned, Colgate's 70g tube at RWF 1,500–1,600 defines Rwanda's entry price point, and every Dabur trial format must be engineered as a small rigid pack that lands at or near that money — the sachet mathematics of Tanzania translate into tube-and-mini-jar mathematics here. Second, the herbal premium is already priced: Hemani's amla oil at 10,000 and the grey Herb'l listing at 3,500 (40% over Colgate) prove Rwandan urban consumers pay for herbal stories — the decks price INTO those umbrellas deliberately rather than discovering them by accident. Third, the generic floor is ruthless where the state serves the need: branded Panadol at 800 against a 550 hundred-tablet generic pack shows what happens to brand premiums in categories Mutuelle and the CHW system already cover — the OTC deck reads that signal as a boundary, not a challenge.

DEEP THINK — is Rwanda worth a seat at all? Invert the entry: the honest bear case is that a ~US$240M total FMCG market, growing fast off a small base, with per-SKU registration at US$1,250, a banned sachet format, a state that absorbs episodic health demand, and an April-dark calendar, cannot repay a full Dabur country programme — and the bear case is RIGHT, which is why this report never proposes one. The bull case rests on four asymmetries. (1) Marginal cost: supply rides Tanzania's container, registration rides the EAC dossier family, the radio playbook is one station, and the first distribution conversation is with a retailer already selling the brand — the true incremental cost of Rwanda is a fraction of a standalone entry. (2) Option value on governance: Rwanda is the region's laboratory state — eKash, Track-and-Trace, the plastic ban — and patterns proven here (no-sachet LUP packs, wallet cashback promos, formal-rails authenticity) become templates as the rest of the EAC's regulation matures in the same direction. (3) The grey-flow arbitrage: Dabur stock WILL reach Kigali via Kariakoo whether or not Dabur acts; formalising converts leakage into margin and protects pricing before the grey route sets it permanently (it has already set Herb'l at 3,500 — a gift this time; next time it might be a discount). (4) Strategic geography: Rubavu and Rusizi sit on the DRC border — the same posts Sulfo built branches on — and eastern DRC is a future programme question that a Rwanda base answers cheaply. Second-order: the kill condition is corridor friction — if the Dar consolidation gain dies in clearance costs despite the 2025-26 pacts, Rwanda reverts to a Nairobi-served maintenance market and the bet is surrendered gracefully. The falsifiable terms in M11 price exactly that.

M11 — Synthesis: the decision board

The board-level read in five sentences

Rwanda is the batch's smallest market and its clearest system: one language, one dominant radio station, one champion who would rather carry Dabur than fight it, one women-first platform that reaches feature phones, and one government whose rails reward exactly the formal, registered, traceable entry Dabur runs everywhere anyway. A calibration note on this report's evidence base

Honesty about sources, stated once for all five Rwanda reports: most Rwandan e-commerce and government domains block automated retrieval, so every RWF shelf price in this foundation is search-snippet-derived and individually flagged; the Rwanda FDA fee schedule beyond the US$1,250 headline, the licensed-pharmacy census, and the REMA exemption text are all direct-fetch items on the appendix queue; and the two most load-bearing inferences — that sachets are effectively absent from formal retail, and that Sawa Citi's Dabur stock is parallel-routed — are labelled as inferences with the exact re-run that would confirm or kill each. Nothing strategic hinges on an unflagged number: the verdicts rest on documented structures (the plastic law, the registration regime, the Mutuelle system, the corridor pacts, the on-shelf listings) that survive any single price being wrong. The re-validation queue is sized at roughly two analyst-weeks in-country — the cheapest diligence bill in the batch, fitting the smallest market.
