The gapped ladder: Spain keeps a living two-tier rose-water tradition with a named retail shelf — and the middle rung is empty

THE BANNER. Spain's skin census produced the directive's starkest single finding: a named brand, a real fit, and a zero footprint. Dabur Gulabari — rose water, the exact product class Spain still keeps as a living tradition — has no Spanish retail evidence at all; Dermoviva likewise (the DOUBLE ZERO, both pending direct confirmation, queue #1). Meanwhile the tradition itself thrives at two price poles: Alqvimia at luxury (Bulgarian Rosa Damascena hydrolat, ~450 flowers per litre, El Corte Inglés, Luxury Spain member) and Instituto Español at mass/traditional — and Druni maintains a dedicated "Tónicos > Agua de Rosas" category page: the shelf is named, live, and waiting. The corridor diagnostic returns its twentieth finding: LADDER-GAPPED — a tradition surviving at two poles with a vacant middle rung; the entry is ON the ladder, AT the gap (~€8.99-class [AUTHORED] between Instituto Español's mass price and Alqvimia's luxury), speaking the tradition's own vocabulary — agua de rosas, never "gulab jal" as lead. This is Europe's fourth rose-water geometry (category → tiers → icon → ladder), derived not transplanted. Two disciplines fence the deck: ISDIN's summit is never contested (9.95% pharmacy dermocosmetics, the national champion — a benchmark, not a target), and the whitening fence holds its 18th market with its first PROACTIVE application — AEMPS has withdrawn hydroquinone creams while admitting informal channels stay imperfectly policed, so Dabur's task is not merely abstention but ACTIVE GOVERNANCE: ensuring its own India-range fairness SKUs never ride Spain's informal import routes. The brief's Sensilis/"Cabaña" rose-water names were not found and are corrected, not repeated. This deck closes the Spain set.
