M1 — Demography, geography & the single-hub gift

Gabon compresses even further than Congo-Brazzaville did. The country holds ~2.57–2.65M people (DataReportal Jan 2025; Worldometer mid-2026) at 83.8–87% urbanisation — the most urbanised country in Central Africa, roughly double the continental average — and its capital does triple duty: Libreville is the political capital, the commercial capital AND the container gateway (Owendo port sits in the metro), holding ~899,000–915,600 people = ~45% of the urban population and ~39% of the entire nation (Macrotrends 2025–26). Port-Gentil, the oil city, is the secondary hub but consumer-goods flow is Owendo-dominated. Where Congo-B offered a two-city gift joined by 510km of rail, Gabon offers something simpler and sharper: one metro where the container lands, the regulator sits, the modern trade clusters and two of every five Gabonese live. The entry map is therefore not a network problem at all — it is a depth problem: win Libreville street by street (Carrefour's eight stores, Ceca-Gadis's banners, Mont-Bouët's 1,400 registered enterprises), treat Port-Gentil as the first extension, and refuse the interior for waves 1–2 (rural poverty at 59.5% is a different market and a different decade). The single hub also concentrates risk — one port strike, one municipal ruling, one flood affects everything — which the risk board prices (strategist read on agent evidence, 8 Jul 2026).

M2 — Economy, FX & THE TWICE-LYING HEADLINE

The defining macro finding: the headline lies twice. The first lie is upward: GDP/capita of ~$9,380 (2025 consensus of World Bank/IMF vintages; PPP figures of ~$18–20k are REJECTED for all pricing work) reads as 5.6× Congo-Brazzaville and "upper-middle-income Africa" — but 34.6% of Gabonese live below the $6.85/day line (World Bank 2024, and RISING since 2022), rural poverty runs 59.5%, and a Gini of 38 in an oil-rent economy means the mean is a poor guide to the median. What the headline actually buys Dabur is not a 5.6× market — it is a genuinely large, Libreville-concentrated premium tier (civil servants, oil-sector formal employment, expats, the Lebanese/West African trading community) sitting on a poverty base not dramatically better than Congo-B's. The second lie is downward-hidden: the calm EUR peg (identical CEMAC/BEAC architecture — 655.957, French Treasury convertibility, zero FX risk on EUR invoicing) sits in front of a state under real fiscal strain — oil is >35% of GDP and ~70% of export receipts and declining; growth is decelerating (AfDB: 2.3% 2025, 2.1% 2026); the deficit runs >5% of GDP through 2027; debt is a carried CONFLICT (World Bank 80.2% of GDP 2025 rising to 82.6% vs official ~FCFA 7,000bn = 63%); and domestic + external arrears hit USD 792M by end-Oct 2025 with FCFA 1,050bn of the 2026 budget consumed by debt service. Congo-B's lesson (financing beats tariffs; the state's liquidity problems become your receivables problem) transfers with full force: prepaid EUR discipline holds in Gabon, even though the payments plumbing is friendlier — banking penetration is materially higher (30% bancarisation vs 56.8% Findex account access — the second carried conflict), and mobile money reaches ~68% of adults (~1.6M accounts; Airtel Money 58% / Moov 37% / BGFIMobile 5%; FCFA 4,087bn transited 2024). The same CEMAC exchange regulation applies (domiciliation above FCFA 5M; 150-day repatriation; 10% fine), so the zero-XAF doctrine is not needed in Congo-B's extreme form — but the receivables rule is absolute: no credit terms to any state-adjacent buyer, ever (strategist read; conflicts carried per programme discipline).

M3 — Politics, institutions & the operating reality

The political cycle that began with the 30 Aug 2023 coup (Gen. Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema ending the 56-year Bongo dynasty hours after a disputed 64% re-election) is now formally closed: Oligui won the 12 Apr 2025 presidential election with 94.85% (Constitutional Court final, 25 Apr 2025) on 70.11% turnout, and legislative/local elections in Sept–Oct 2025 seated a new National Assembly. The operating read has three layers. First, surface stability is real: a seven-year mandate, a restored France relationship (Macron in Libreville 24 Nov 2025; the defence pact renewed for two years, refocused on environmental security), and no active conflict. Second, the legitimacy is thinner than the score: legislative turnout of ~34% against the presidential 70% reads as institutional distrust, and the late-Feb-2026 nationality-code revision (tightened criteria, political restrictions, opposition objections) is a live tension point. Third — and this is the layer that touches commerce directly — the state has demonstrated it will switch off platforms: in Feb 2026 Gabon cut Facebook and TikTok citing threats to stability, and the 26 Feb 2026 ordinances now require platforms to appoint an in-country/CEMAC legal representative and reclassify influencers as regulated «animateurs» under HAC/ARCEP oversight. For a consumer brand this is not background noise — it is a design constraint on the entire media architecture (M7). The slow-burn risk is fiscal, not putschist: weak oil prices against debt-financed public spending is the scenario to watch, because its first commercial symptom is exactly what the Casino/Mbolo situation already shows — unpaid suppliers (operating read on agent evidence).

M4 — Regulation, registration & THE DOCTRINE FLIP

The regulatory map has one brand-new institution, one familiar gap, and one finding that flips a Congo-B posture on its head. The new institution: ANMAPS (Agence Nationale du Médicament et des Autres Produits de Santé), created by Ordinance 0004 of 24 Oct 2023 and ratified 26 Jan 2024, replacing the old pharmacy directorate — it governs all medicines and health products with a documented dossier list (GMP certificate, origin-country marketing authorisation, price attestation, SPC) but untested throughput: a two-year-old agency has no track record to price a registration timeline against, so the OTC lane's food-vs-medicine question matters even more here than in Congo-B — especially since the 2026 Finance Law removed pharma's VAT exemption (imported health products now carry 18% VAT — a dated, material cost shock to any medicine-lane plan). The familiar gap: AGANOR (the standards agency, est. 2014) documents certification for locally-manufactured goods but no clear cosmetics-IMPORT registration procedure — the practical gate appears to be the customs-side AMM (issued by the Competition and Consumer Affairs directorate) plus health certificates for specified foods; the exact scope by HS line is G1 work. The flip: on skin-lightening, Gabon is the anti-Congo. Congo-B has no hydroquinone ban at all; Gabon banned topical hydroquinone in March 2000, adopted new anti-mercury texts in Nov 2024, and is one of three global pilot countries (with Jamaica and Sri Lanka) in UNEP's $14M 2023–2026 mercury-elimination programme — hosting the regional workshop in Libreville 20–24 Jan 2025. Dabur's whitening ban, which in Congo-B was a voluntary standard held against a silent state, is in Gabon alignment with a national showcase policy — a genuine tailwind for the naturals/Ayurvedic file, with the sharp edge that ambiguous «brightening» claims will face HEIGHTENED scrutiny here: the lexical gate built for Congo-B travels intact and gets stricter. Halal, the other recurring gate, is a niche here, not a mandate: Muslims are ~12–15% and 80–90% foreign-resident — a community-trust signal for the West African/Lebanese trading tier, nothing more (regulatory read on agent evidence).

M5 — Trade regime & the landed-cost wall

Gabon's landed stack is heavy but — unlike Congo-B's — mostly knowable. The CEMAC CET applies in its four bands (5/10/20/30%; finished consumer goods at 30%) plus a 1% Community Integration Tax [!] reconcile 1% vs the ~1.5% combined CEEAC/CEMAC levies in the Congo-B file; VAT is 18%; and the customs IT fee — Congo-B's 20–60%-of-CIF wildcard — resolves in Gabon to the RUSID at ~1.5% of customs value [!] broker confirmation at Owendo queued: the Congo file's wildcard does NOT transfer, and the two-column waterfall the batch built for that conflict is therefore not needed here in its extreme form. What Gabon adds instead is TIMING: the 2026 Finance Law introduced a 25% ad-valorem excise on imported cosmetics and perfumes (framed as a luxury-import tax; ~FCFA 60bn of 2025 vehicle+beauty imports in scope; ~FCFA 20bn revenue target) and stripped pharma's VAT exemption — the tax door swung most of the way shut in the very year of entry, which reshapes the skin/personal-care ladder before a single container moves. Logistics are the compensating gift: Owendo runs a single consolidated customs office at the terminal (300m quay, 10m draft, 130,000 TEU/yr, new RTG cranes), the market is co-located with the port, and there is no inland corridor at all. India–Gabon trade shows institutional warmth (>50 Indian companies at the Nkok SEZ; the India–Gabon Business Council launched Aug 2022) but no FMCG lane whatsoever ($72.75M of Indian exports, led by meat, machinery and vehicles) — Dabur would be building the consumer lane, not joining one. The pre-shipment-inspection question (Congo-B: COTECNA/ECTN) is a declared Gabon gap: no equivalent regime confirmed, and G1 asks the broker directly (trade read; assumptions printed).

M6 — Channels & the distributor bench

Gabon's retail bench is the strongest in the batch — and it comes with one red flag. The incumbent is Ceca-Gadis: ~90 years old, >104 stores under a full ladder of banners (Gaboprix, Cecado, Intergros, Supergros, the Ckdo family), the fourth-largest private group in the country — with the governance caveat that the state (via the Caisse des Dépôts) took 35% of its shares in Feb 2024 in a nationalisation-adjacent move that any partner due-diligence must price. The challenger is the region's best modern-trade story: Carrefour via Prix Import — Bernard Azzi's Lebanese-Gabonese group (est. 1992, ~10 stores) converted to Carrefour banners from 16 Dec 2021 and opened eight stores in four months including a 6,000m² hypermarket by Apr 2022. Prix Import is this market's warm-handshake analog to Congo-B's Daswani/Regal call — a family trading group with modern-trade scale and import capability. The red flag is Casino/Sodigab (Géant Mbolo + two Scores): the French parent's restructuring (control to FRH, Nov 2024 «Renouveau 2028» plan) shows up locally as blocked deliveries over an unpaid supplier balance reported at FCFA 300M and staff strikes — a live counterparty-risk case study; Dabur lists there LAST, on prepaid-or-COD terms only. Below modern trade sits Mont-Bouët — the country's largest market, 1,400 registered enterprises of which 487 operate in "absolute informality," now fighting a contested 17:30 municipal closure rule — the volume engine for any value tier. Pharma wholesale runs through UbiPharm Gabon (in-market since 1996; the group's pharmacist-shareholder model, >15,000 SKUs); Laborex/Eurapharma presence is UNCONFIRMED (gap — do not assume the Congo-B pattern). E-commerce is thinner than the income level suggests: no Jumia marketplace operates in Gabon (jumia.ga is a classifieds shell), Jiji does not operate here (a correction to the batch brief), and the working scrape platforms are CoinAfrique, Facebook Marketplace and pharmacy e-shops (Pharmacie Sainte Marie). WhatsApp commerce patterns are assumed Congo-like but UNMEASURED here — the 60–70% figure is NOT carried over; the category decks must capture Gabon's own number (channel read on agent evidence).

M7 — Media, digital & THE SWITCH-OFF DOCTRINE

The media architecture starts from a fact no other market in this programme has forced: in February 2026 the state cut Facebook and TikTok nationwide, and the ordinances that followed (26 Feb 2026) built a standing legal apparatus for platform control — mandatory in-country/CEMAC platform representatives, influencers reclassified as regulated «animateurs» under HAC/ARCEP. The reach numbers are otherwise attractive (internet 71.9% penetration, 1.84M users; Facebook ~1.01M = 40.7% of population by Sept 2025; TikTok rising fast), but attractiveness is not resilience: the doctrine this market writes is "build reach on rails the state cannot switch off." Concretely: radio and TV carry the trust layer — with the sharp local twist that Gabonese TV viewing (95% daily, 4h10/day) flows 91% to INTERNATIONAL channels via Canal+ bouquets and only 9% to national channels (the lowest local-channel share in Sub-Saharan Africa), so the TV buy is pan-African bouquet inventory, not RTG; WhatsApp catalogues and SMS carry the commerce layer (unnamed in the Feb cut, though nothing is guaranteed); POS carries the proof layer (seal demos, printed prices — the batch's standing discipline); and the social layer is built deliberately REDUNDANT — content designed to live on whichever of FB/TikTok/IG is up, with the boutique WhatsApp network as the fallback distribution spine. Influencer work faces a double gate: the programme's fabrication rule (direct platform verification only — no aggregator lists, and NO verified Gabonese roster exists yet: a declared gap) plus the new «animateur» compliance regime, which makes formal contracts with registered creators the only defensible shape (media read on agent evidence).

M9 — Culture & register (pre-read for the category decks)

Four register findings set the category decks' guardrails. First, la SAPE does not transplant: the codified sapeur institution is Congo-B/Kinshasa; Bongo-era Gabon had its own elite dress culture but no equivalent documented institution — the Congo-B hair deck's «chez ta coiffeuse» geometry must be re-derived, not copied, and any sapologie hook is banned from Gabon copy pending local verification. DEEP THINK — GO argued both ways

The case against (steelman): Gabon is a 2.6M-person market whose state is running out of money in real time — arrears compounding, new luxury-import taxes landing exactly on Dabur's categories, a demonstrated willingness to switch off the internet's commercial layer, and a retail bench where one of three modern-trade groups is already failing to pay suppliers. The premium tier that justifies entry is narrow (a third of the country is poor; the interior is poorer), the e-commerce infrastructure is weaker than the income level implies, and the excise regime could tighten again with every lean oil year. A rational allocator waits two years and watches the fiscal clock. The case for (answer): every one of those risks is visible, dated and priceable — which is precisely what makes this a good entry: the stack is knowable (RUSID 1.5%, not 20–60%), the hub is single, the modern trade is the batch's best and HUNGRY (Carrefour's rollout wants brands), the greenfield is the cleanest in the programme, and the two worst risks (platform cuts, counterparty failure) are design constraints Dabur can engineer around from day one rather than discover later. The fiscal squeeze cuts both ways: a revenue-hungry state taxing imports is ALSO a state whose showcase health policies (mercury elimination) welcome a compliant, certificate-led entrant. Waiting buys no information this deck doesn't already carry — it only cedes the Carrefour shelf to whoever moves first. The synthesis: GO, single-hub, two-speed, prepaid, switch-off-proof — with the G1 per-HS excise ruling as the one number that could still shrink the skin ladder to a premium-only play, and the honest admission printed that this market's upside is depth-per-capita, never breadth (deep think, 8 Jul 2026).
