C3 — Consumer & ritual decode

Three registers organise Gabonese hair culture, and none of them is the Congolese SAPE. The first is the braid-and-rite register: Gabon's documented tradition encodes clan, age and marital status in braiding (Fang scarification-and-style history; Punu and Myene styles), and its living commercial peak is the mariage coutumier — the customary marriage that outranks the civil one socially, binds four clans, and commands dedicated bridal styling (live TikTok bridal-prep content confirms the occasion's spend). The named traditional styles that surfaced (bakimbis, bipunu, mindassa, maboude, the Mitsogho ponytail) are QUARANTINED — image-only sources, unverified names — and nothing ships on them until a native pass confirms; the bridal OCCASION, however, is bankable now. The second register is the texture debate, sharper-edged here than in Congo because it has a face: L'Hair Afro's founder publicly frames natural hair against BOTH relaxing and skin-bleaching («plus belle au naturel sans défrisage ni dépigmentation») — which means a relaxer house cannot court the movement without hypocrisy. The two-house architecture resolves this cleanly: oils are texture-neutral (every texture oils), so Vatika/ Amla may accompany White Afro Day; ORS never does — its lane is the salon network and the relaxed-and-braided majority, served without apology and without texture-ranking creative (the law from Congo-B, held). The third register is elegance without sapologie: Gabonese sources explicitly distinguish their beauty culture from Congo's SAPE (present at the urban margins, not the register) — so the Congo deck's «chez ta coiffeuse» ceiling-geometry is retired here and replaced with the occasion geometry: the wedding, the Sunday service, the office — Libreville's own elegance clock (register read; quarantines explicit).
