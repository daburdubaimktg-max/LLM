C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: FLANK-NARROW — "beside the artisans, below the French, compliant by architecture." Three findings shape the flank. First, the showcase-street gap is the market's defining fact: Gabon banned hydroquinone in 2000, decreed against mercury cosmetics in 2023, hosts UNEP's global pilot and signed the «Engagement de Libreville» (Jan 2025, ~200 delegates, the First Lady as patron) — and yet the WHO-Gabon 2025 report found mercury-positive products ON SALE, the national awareness campaign reached 750 people, no seizures or prosecutions surface anywhere, and the «Kwandza» trade runs openly through Mont-Bouët, TikTok storefronts and Mobile-Money home delivery, its grey frontier already migrated from mercury to glutathione/vitamin-C «brightening» (the DISAAR range live on Facebook Marketplace). Dabur's answer is not to join the enforcement debate but to be its counter-example: compliant by architecture — registered, sealed, batch-coded, zero tone claims — the brand that behaves as if the law were enforced, which in a showcase state is both the safe position and, quietly, the institutionally-favoured one. Second, the tone-neutral shelf has a structure the Maghreb and Congo never showed: a genuinely rich LOCAL naturals ecosystem (Moabi butter as the signature ingredient; Ndossy, Magningo, Myalo, Cosmos-certified Ivindo Bio; the Okani concept store curating ~15 Gabonese brands) sitting at 2,500–11,000 FCFA, a French dermo-pharmacy tier at 8,000–30,500, a luxury mall tier (Beauty Gabon: Dior, Clarins), and a DARK mass floor (no NIVEA/Vaseline/soap price findable — the oral deck's pattern repeating). The flank enters the dark mass with a sealed naturals soap ladder and Herbolene, prices against the imports and never under the artisans (the co-narration rule made pricing policy), and reserves the premium seat for a curated test BESIDE the local brands at Okani rather than against them. Third, the entry mode has a proven template: Yves Rocher entered Libreville in December 2020 through Ceca-Gadis — the beauty-specialist door and the grocery door are the same landlord, and Dabur's G2 conversation inherits that fact. Gulabari, for the second deck running, finds real local schema (Jorhel's rose water at 3,500 FCFA proves the register) and enters at parity beside it; Fem and OxyLife remain struck from every wave, restated here so no future team rediscovers them (strategist read on agent evidence, 8 Jul 2026).

C2 — Market size & growth

Sizing honestly: no market value exists for Gabon skin care; the deck sizes by the four-level shelf the capture mapped. Level one, the informal/classifieds mass: coconut oil 1,500, face masks 3,000, gommage and savon noir 5,000 — the price floor where most of the 34.6%-poverty population actually shops. Level two, the local-naturals middle: Moabi-butter pots 7,500, massage oils 6,500, Jorhel's blue clay 2,500 and rose water 3,500, Ikwia kits 11,000, Ivindo Bio's certified range at ~9,000–14,000 FCFA equivalents — a genuinely vibrant Made-in-Gabon tier with its own curated retail (Okani) and airport placement (Destination Détente). Level three, the French dermo tier: Avène micellar waters at 8,275–14,350, Bioderma Hydrabio at 26,100, La Roche-Posay Créaline DS+ at 30,500, a fifteen-SKU sun-care wall at 15,183–36,500 — pharmacy-gated, peg-priced, colonial-inherited. Level four, luxury (Beauty Gabon's Dior/Clarins/Estée Lauder wall, prices uncaptured). And beneath it all, the DARK mass floor: not one Gabon price for NIVEA, Vaseline, Dove or a mass soap bar could be verified — the audit lights it before anything prices. The bleaching economy runs through all levels informally (the ~2013 LCDP profile: 70% female / 30% male users, 65% aged 16–40 — old numbers, flagged; the 2025 survey's 70% possibly recycled), with its commerce now discreet (TikTok, WhatsApp, Mobile Money, home delivery). Growth logic for the flank: brand the unsealed mass (the classifieds tier has no seal, no dose, no registration number), price below the French tier's absurdity with equal dignity (the OTC deck's umbrella logic transposed), and let the showcase state's own policy narrative make compliance the story money can't buy (strategist read).

C3 — Consumer & ritual decode

The decode corrects a transplant risk the Congo file could have caused: Gabon's bleaching vocabulary is its own. The practice is «Kwandza» (six independent press sources, 2021–2025), possibly coexisting with the older genericized «Ambi» [!] relationship unresolved; the aspirational tones have local names («teint jaune banane», «teint jaune poussin»); and Congo's «maquillage» false-friend does NOT govern here — proving, for the second country running, that the lexical gate must be rebuilt per market, never carried. Three more decode facts matter. First, the male share is demand-side as much as user-side: 30% of practitioners are men (LCDP, ~2013), but the 2021 reporting documents men as ENCOURAGERS — demanding lighter-skinned partners — which makes any men's grooming line a tone-economy adjacency this doctrine cannot manage; the men's lane is refused, as in Congo, now for a Gabon-specific reason. Second, the natural register is identity-positive and commercially organised: «100% naturel / bio / Made in Gabon» is one positioning cluster, Moabi butter is its signature molecule, and Ndossy's tone-EVENING (not lightening) framing shows local brands already walking the compliant line — Dabur joins that conversation as a peer with an Ayurvedic accent, never as a corrector. Third, the clinical counter-voice is personal, not institutional: Dr Stéphanie Ntsame's dermatological caravans (Oct 2025) carry the anti-Kwandza message where the state's 750-person campaign did not — if Dabur ever supports that discourse it does so through the PR firewall (unbranded, clinical-led), never in ads, and never lecturing the practising majority (register read; Myènè capture queued — Okani itself is a Myènè word: the language is already on the shelf).

C4 — Competitor teardown (Module 1)

The tone-neutral shelf: the French dermo bloc (Avène/Bioderma/LRP/Vichy + eight more confirmed at one pharmacy) owns legitimacy at 8,000–30,500; Yves Rocher owns the mass-premium specialist seat via Ceca-Gadis; Beauty Gabon holds luxury; the local naturals hold identity; Mustela holds maternity (10,170 for the vergetures oil — priced this time); and the mass floor (NIVEA/Vaseline/Dove/mass soap) is present-but-dark. Nobody — not one player — holds sealed, registered, honestly-priced naturals at the mass rung: the artisans are unsealed and small-batch, the French are priced out of the mass, and the informal tier has no architecture at all (structural read on agent evidence).
