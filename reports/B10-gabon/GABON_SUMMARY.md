## What Gabon taught the programme (doctrine bank additions)

1. **The switch-off doctrine** — platform shutdowns are a live state tool (Feb 2026); reach is built on rails the state
   cannot switch off (radio, Canal+ bouquets, WhatsApp/SMS, POS), with social as amplification only and no launch ever
   platform-dependent.
2. **The per-country lexical gate is doctrine** — Congo says «maquillage», Gabon says «Kwandza»: the banned-vocabulary list
   is a rebuilt per-market artefact, never a carried one. The «éclat» gate now has hard evidence behind it (the grey market's
   migration to glutathione «brightening»).
3. **The doctrine flip** — the same whitening ban reads differently by state posture: voluntary standard in Congo (no local
   ban), national-policy ALIGNMENT in Gabon (UNEP showcase) — with the sharper scrutiny that alignment attracts.
4. **The showcase-street gap** — a state can be an international policy showcase and a zero-enforcement street market at
   once; the compliant brand's play is counter-example, never commentary.
5. **Evidence-led portfolio promotions** — three programme "defaults" reversed on local evidence in one country: oils to
   wave 1 (hair), Chyawanprash's first slot (OTC), Gulabari to wave 1 (skin). The anti-transplant method now works in both
   directions: it blocks false transfers AND unlocks earned ones.
6. **The never-under-the-artisans pricing law** — where a local naturals scene is organised (Moabi-butter brands, Okani),
   co-narration becomes arithmetic: price against imports always, parity beside locals, auditable in the bet.
7. **The institution-founding move** — where a category's institution has never existed (no school oral-health programme in
   national history), founding it costs a fraction of an ad campaign and owns the category's civic layer («L'École du
   Sourire»; cost-capped in print; unbranded-publication firewall).
8. **The entry-mode template** — Yves Rocher entered Libreville through Ceca-Gadis (Dec 2020): the grocery landlord IS the
   beauty-specialist door. G2 conversations inherit precedents, not just term sheets.
9. **The dark-shelf discipline** — when the mass floor is publicly invisible (oral AND skin), the ladder ships as a RANGE
   and the bet self-voids if priced before the audit. Honesty as a structural feature of the deck, not a caveat.
10. **The audit trail as governance product** — the Ndendé seizure record was corrected (Gabon macro), then honestly
    REOPENED when new evidence contested the correction (Gabon OTC): the programme corrects its own corrections in print.

## Attractiveness × right-to-win (country view)

- **Attractiveness: MEDIUM-HIGH (the batch's highest so far).** Only ~2.6M people, but: the richest per-capita market in the
  batch, one metro holding 39% of the nation with the port inside it, the batch's most organised modern trade (Carrefour ×9,
  Ceca-Gadis ×104), a knowable customs stack (RUSID 1.5%), codified registration lanes, and premium price umbrellas
  (French phyto at 11,000–22,700; dermo at 8,275–30,500) that leave the widest margin headroom yet. Against that: the 2026
  tax package (25% cosmetics excise, pharma VAT), rising poverty (34.6%), state arrears ($792M), platform risk, and the
  fraud-association hazard around the supplement category.
- **Right to win: HIGH on the chosen flanks.** The cleanest greenfield in the programme (zero Dabur signals, zero Indian OTC
  at retail, Shalina absent), no multinational lock on any door Dabur wants (sealed mass naturals, the honey import shelf,
  the tonic umbrella, the school institution), and two state-adjacent legitimacy partners no other market offered (IPHAMETRA;
  the UNEP-orbit clean-file story). The binding constraints are fiscal-timing (the excise's per-HS scope) and channel
  legality (the e-commerce landmine) — both G1-resolvable questions, not structural walls.
- **Batch view:** Gabon ranks ABOVE Congo-Brazzaville on entry economics (single hub, knowable stack, richer premium tier,
  codified lanes) and below it on mass-volume depth (2.6M vs 5.7M people; a thinner coin economy). The two are complements,
  not rivals: same CEMAC paperwork family, same EUR peg, one distributor conversation (Lebanese/Indian trading families both
  sides), and the Owendo/Pointe-Noire corridors never compete.
