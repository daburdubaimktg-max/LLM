C1 — Category verdict & thesis

Verdict: WEDGE — "price the visible gap, claim the empty school." Three findings shape it. First, the shelf is two islands and a dark strait: the only confirmed Gabon prices are a premium pharmacy tier (Sensodyne at 4,415–4,535, Hextril at 6,750, a 19,440 specialty mouthwash — a fully-stocked 24-SKU pharmacy e-shelf) and a natural/charcoal tier priced like a racket (Longrich MLM at 3,600 for 200g; an unbranded charcoal tube at 7,000), while the mass fluoride shelf that must exist at Carrefour and Gaboprix is publicly INVISIBLE — no price, no assortment, no promotion indexed anywhere. Dabur wedges into the strait: Dabur Herbal Charcoal at 2,200–2,800 FCFA — sealed, dosed, honestly priced against an MLM tier that charges 3,600–7,000 for the same register — with the full Herbal range laddering beneath it once the audit lights up the mass floor. Charcoal leads for the third time in the batch on a third distinct argument: in Libya it rode the counterfeit-crisis trust gap, in Congo it was the measured local natural register, and in Gabon it is the modern natural-whitening signal — proven not by tradition but by demand traces (Longrich's charcoal range selling at MLM prices; Gabonese TikTok search demand for Body Accel whitening paste) — and tooth whitening is a PERMITTED claim lane in this programme (the ban is skin tone, never dental blancheur; the lexical gate still reviews every asset). Second, the burden is enormous and institutionally orphaned: 46 dentists for 2.3M people, caries in 75% of Libreville six-year-olds and 81.4% of twelve-year-olds (the only study ever done — 2009), no national oral-health policy, no SSB tax, and — the deck's cleanest whitespace — no school oral-health programme has ever been documented in Gabon, by any brand, ministry or NGO. The first mover doesn't join that institution; it FOUNDS it. Third, the affordability math is bimodal like everything else here (an annual supply costs ~1.2 labour-days at the 80,000-FCFA SMIG but under one day at the 150,000-FCFA RMM — analyst estimate, WHO's field is blank), which is why the wedge carries a 50ml value rung rather than assuming Congo's sachet doctrine transfers (no sachet evidence exists in Gabon — the tier may genuinely be thinner) (strategist read on agent evidence, 8 Jul 2026).

C2 — Market size & growth

Sizing honestly: no market value exists for Gabon oral care; the deck sizes by burden and by the two visible price islands. The burden spine (WHO 2022): untreated caries in 36.6% of children 1–9 and 28.1% of everyone over five; severe periodontal disease 27%; 46 dentists nationally (0.2 per 10,000 — roughly one per 50,000 people, analyst arithmetic); total dental spend $7.7M ($3.7/capita — nearly four times Congo-B's $1.0, the income difference made visible); productivity losses $46M/year; sugar availability 47g/day and no SSB tax; oral care nominally 100% covered de jure in the state financing scheme — a paper entitlement the 20.7% clinic-attendance figure (93% of it pain-driven) exposes as fiction in practice. The Libreville school study is the demand map: 75% caries at age six rising to 81.4% at twelve with severity ABOVE the high threshold (CAO 4.9) — prevention isn't competing with treatment here; there is no treatment tier to compete with. The market structure: a premium pharmacy island (fully e-commerced — Sainte-Marie's 24 dentifrice SKUs with WhatsApp ordering — a channel sophistication Congo never showed), the gouged natural tier, and the dark mass floor. Growth logic: the wedge converts natural-register demand from the MLM tier at half the price, the value rung converts the SMIG households the affordability math excludes, and the school programme grows the category itself — brushing frequency is the multiplier no incumbent is working (strategist read).

C3 — Consumer & ritual decode

The decode differs from Congo's in three registers. First, charcoal reads modern here, not ancestral: Gabon's charcoal signal arrives through an MLM catalogue (Longrich), an imported whitening paste's search demand (Body Accel), and a Facebook consumer group's product chatter — globalised wellness-whitening aesthetics, not a rooted ethnobotanical practice. Creative that plays charcoal as "grandmother's remedy" would misfire; creative that plays it as "the modern natural, honestly priced and sealed" matches the register the market itself built. Second, no chew-stick tradition exists in the record at all — no miswak analog, no documented stick species, nothing tied to Fang, Myene or Punu hygiene practice; the one historical dental ethnography (tooth FILING in the Haute-N'Gounié basin, colonial-era) is a rite-of-passage modification record, not hygiene, and is explicitly NOT conflated with it in this deck. Miswak therefore parks in a third epistemic state new to the programme: not measured-and-absent (Congo), not unmeasurable (Libya), but undocumented absence — parked pending one ethnographic query, with no register test owed. Third, the pain threshold IS the category's ritual: 93% of the minority who ever reach a dentist go because it hurts — care is reactive, prevention is unsold, and the de-jure "100% coverage" line in the state scheme makes the gap institutional rather than informational. The comms register that fits is not fear (the pain is already known) but competence offered early: the school programme, the brushing-calendar mechanics, the value rung that makes twice-daily arithmetic work at SMIG incomes (register read; Fang/Omyènè lexicon queued — one confirmed word: ogwana, "mouth," Galwa).
