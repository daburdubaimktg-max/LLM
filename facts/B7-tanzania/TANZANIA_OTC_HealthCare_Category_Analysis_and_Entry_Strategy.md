**THE PLAY:** DEEP THINK — the sourcing inversion, stress-tested. The deck's signature move — buy Tanzanian honey instead of shipping Indian honey — deserves its own inversion because it is the first time in this programme the supply arrow points backward. What would make it wrong? (1) Quality risk : Dabur's honey reputation elsewhere rests on controlled supply; Tanzanian co-op honey is genuinely good (second place in a South-African quality contest, Balkans export acceptance) but variable — the mitigation is that Dabur is not buying honey, it is buying honey THROUGH an audit-and-certification machine it builds, which is precisely the capability it already runs for Indian apiaries. (2) Brand-architecture risk : does "Dabur" plus "Tanzania Honey" confuse the global brand? No — it strengthens the local one; the Kuwait deck sells Indian provenance to a market that prizes imports, this market prizes its own soil, and the same house selling both is exactly what a multinational's brand architecture is for. (3) Political risk, inverted into asset : a foreign FMCG buying from Tabora co-ops at audited prices, paying for traceability, and putting the state's own trademark on packs is the single most goodwill-generating supply chain Dabur could run in Tanzania — it converts the foreign-trader anxiety (the Kariakoo probe politics) into a farmer-income story a minister can stand next to. Second-order: if it works, the model exports — Ethiopia and Zambia (later batches) have comparable honey economies, and "source local, certify with Dabur discipline, co-brand with the national mark" becomes a repeatable African OTC entry template invented here. Kill-switch remains T19's: the co-op cost line is the honest arbiter.

**THE PLAY:** DEEP STRAT — the ADDO dispenser programme as the category's operating system. The oral deck borrows ADDO as a prevention-card channel; this deck needs the full build, so the design lives here. The asset: 9,000+ accredited shops, 19,000+ dispensers with formal training, a gazetted formulary, and a wholesale tier that already moves product to all of them — rural Tanzania's actual health infrastructure, underused by every FMCG brand in the market. The programme: (i) legality first — supplement classification puts Dabur SKUs on the stockable side of the formulary line; counsel confirms per SKU before any sell-in; (ii) the dispenser as professional, not shelf — a Swahili dose-safety training module (delivered through ADDO wholesalers' existing visit cycle) that improves the dispenser's own standing with customers, with Dabur as sponsor rather than subject; (iii) margin architecture — dispenser margin set a point above the equivalent import line, funded by the domestic honey line's 10% contribution, so stocking Dabur is the rational choice without a single push incentive; (iv) the feedback loop — dispensers are the country's best early-warning network for counterfeits, adverse events and demand shifts; a simple WhatsApp/USSD reporting channel with airtime micro-rewards turns 4,000 shops into a live market-intelligence panel no competitor possesses. Works if: wholesaler cooperation is secured (they gate the visit cycle) and the Pharmacy Council reads the training module as professional development, not marketing. Fails if: formulary interpretation excludes supplements in practice — in which case the programme narrows to the ~1,500 pharmacy-adjacent ADDOs in peri-urban zones and the model survives at reduced scale. Regulatory posture: everything co-designed with Pharmacy Council liaison from week 8, because a programme the regulator co-owns cannot be un-gazetted by a rival's lobbying.
