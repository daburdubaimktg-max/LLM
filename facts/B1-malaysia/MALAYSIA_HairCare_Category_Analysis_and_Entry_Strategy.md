## C1 · Executive summary — the one screen

> SO WHAT: So what. Malaysian hair care is a US$396M (RM~1.85bn) retail market growing a modest ~3.7%/yr (IMARC 2025) — but Dabur should not enter the market average; it should enter the two pockets growing fastest and defended weakest. The shampoo shelf (45% of value) is a fortress: Unilever (Sunsilk Hijab, Clear) and P&G hold ~50–60% combined L , and Safi Shayla (Wipro Unza) has owned the "syampu bertudung" (hijab shampoo) category it invented since 2013 — assault it head-on and die by promo. The open ground is the treatment layer : oils, serums, "vitamin rambut" capsules and scalp mists, where the market leader is a capsule (Ellips), the folk benchmark is RM50–99 virgin coconut oil sold by agent networks, the argan long tail is unbranded, TikTok live-selling is won by local halal viral brands — and amla, the single best-evidenced hair-oiling ingredient in South Asia, is unclaimed at scale . Dabur Amla is already in Malaysia — RM14.50 at LuLu, ghost-listed across Shopee 3P sellers, praised in Malay on Lemon8 ("RM7 for 100ml — very worthwhile") — with no official store, no halal mark on shelf, and no brand telling its story. The play, "PETUA 2.0": formalise the ghost demand through the Indian-cohort door at Deepavali 2026 (8 Nov — the oil-bath festival is literally a hair-oil occasion, macro §M4 T10), then cross to the Malay mainstream by Raya 2027 as a halal-certified amla treatment system — pre-wash oil, hair-vitamin serum, cooling scalp mist "sejuk" for the tudung day — priced masstige (RM12–35), distributed Himalaya-style (master distributor + pharmacy chain) with Grafen sequencing (chain feature first, TikTok Shop second). Ask: Deepavali GTM budget (RM1.2–2.0M band), chain-exclusive negotiation mandate, and the halal-certification filing already moving under macro T25 Phase-0.
