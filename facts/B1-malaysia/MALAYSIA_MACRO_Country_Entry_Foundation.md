## M1 · Demographics — a middle-affluent, 78%-urban, tri-ethnic market with a built-in Dabur cohort

> SO WHAT: So what: Malaysia is the opposite demographic problem from most of the 42-country list: not "find the affordable middle class" but "navigate three consumer nations in one country". The 58%-Malay core is halal-first and sets the mass-market rules; the 22%-Chinese cohort sets the premium beauty codes; and the 6.5% Indian cohort (~2M, 85% Tamil) is a ready-made Dabur beachhead that already knows Amla and Ayurveda — a launch asset Vietnam entirely lacked. One conurbation (Greater KL, ~9M, 1 in 4 Malaysians) concentrates the modern-trade and e-commerce value; East Malaysia (~20% of population) is a separate distribution economics problem to defer to Wave 3.

## M3 · Politics, geopolitics, trade & legal — a warm India door and a four-lane duty chessboard

> SO WHAT: So what: Malaysia is politically stable (Anwar's Madani government holding into 2026), explicitly warm to India (Comprehensive Strategic Partnership since Aug 2024, record US$19.5bn trade, Modi in KL in Feb 2026), and — unlike Vietnam — offers Dabur a bilateral India FTA (MICECA, in force since 2011) on top of AIFTA, a UAE CEPA in force since 1 Oct 2025, a Turkey FTA since 2015, and mostly-zero MFN on cosmetics anyway. Duty is therefore NOT the strategic variable it was in Vietnam; the border variables that matter are the 5% cosmetics SST, the 10% LVG tax on small parcels, and the WRT licence for foreign-owned distribution. The 2023–24 BDS boycott wave is the other lesson: origin politics in Malaysia punishes Israel-linked brands, not Indian ones — but it proves how fast Malay-Muslim consumer sentiment can mobilise, which makes halal credibility and cultural respect (see KK Mart case, §M6) existential hygiene.

## M4 · History, religion, values, cohorts & occasions — three medicine cabinets, one halal gate, two launch clocks

> SO WHAT: So what: Malaysia runs three living traditional-medicine systems in parallel — Malay jamu/ubat kampung (+ the Islamic sunnah -products layer), Chinese TCM halls, and Indian-Malaysian Ayurveda — and one pan-ethnic health idiom ("heaty/cooling") that all three share. Dabur doesn't need to teach herbal credibility here; it needs to pass the halal gate and speak each cohort's ritual language . The calendar hands Dabur a two-clock launch: the Deepavali oil-bath (8 Nov 2026) — a live Tamil ritual of head-to-toe oil anointing that is literally a hair-oil occasion — for the Indian-cohort beachhead, then the CNY(6 Feb)–Ramadan(8 Feb)–Raya(10–11 Mar 2027) triple window for the mainstream halal wave. The sharpest white space found: the hijabi hair-care paradox — up to 10 hours/day under the tudung driving scalp itch, oiliness and hair fall — a named, under-served segment tailor-made for a cooling herbal scalp-oil/serum.

## M5 · Media, digital, news & social — three cohorts, three funnels, one hard ad gate

> SO WHAT: So what: Malaysia is near-saturated digital (98% internet, 8h17m/day online) but the demand engine splits by cohort: the Malay mass market discovers on TikTok + Shopee + WhatsApp groups ; Chinese-Malaysian women use Xiaohongshu as their beauty search engine (~3–3.5M MAU); the Indian cohort runs on Facebook/YouTube + THR Raaga radio (1.48M weekly) + Astro Vaanavil. Radio overall is remarkably alive (93% weekly reach) — a cheap mass layer Vietnam didn't offer. The hard gate: any ad for a registered health product (Dabur's THMS/OTC SKUs) needs prior Medicine Advertisements Board approval with a KKLIU number on every asset — including influencer posts and livestreams . And from 1 Jun 2026 under-16s are banned from social media — teen sampling moves offline. Net: a compliance-literate, KOC-seeded, tri-funnel plan, with drop-style commerce (Khairul Aming's RM1.2M-in-30-seconds benchmark) rather than Vietnam-style marathon livestreams.
