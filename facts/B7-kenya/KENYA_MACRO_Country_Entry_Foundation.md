## M3 — Tariff & tax: the wall, with a door being cut

### T4 — Landed-cost stack (and the CEPA watch)
| Layer | Rate | Note |
|---|---|---|
| EAC CET | 25% finished goods / 35% band lines (unchanged since Jul 2022) | Same wall as Uganda |
| VAT | 16% | Two points kinder than Uganda's 18% |
| IDF + RDL | 2.5% + 2.0% of CIF | Cosmetics/oral NOT exempt from RDL [!] [!] IDF rate current status |
| Excise stamps (EGMS) | Mandatory on HS 3303/3304/3305/3307 — toothpaste, hair prep, oral hygiene | Stamp-fee hike (KES 0.6→2.5) proposed 2023, postponed [!] [!] status — direct hit on Dabur SKUs |
| India origin | Full CET — no preference | Unchanged |
| UAE (RAK) origin | Full CET today — but UAE–Kenya CEPA concluded Feb 2024, ratification pending (Parl. memo Mar 2025) | THE LIVE ITEM: if ratified with FMCG lines, the RAK hub gains a preference India lacks — reversing the Uganda duty-neutral premise [!] [!] check at every review |
| Procedure | KRA + KEBS PVoC / Import Standardization Mark (track-and-trace sticker) | The ISM doubles as an authenticity asset (M10) |

> SO WHAT: So what: Kenya's stack lands lighter than Uganda's (~40–48% of CIF vs 45–55%) and may soon split by origin: a ratified CEPA covering HS 33 lines would make RAK the preferred hub for Kenya while India remains full-CET — a supply-architecture decision that should be pre-modelled now so the switch is executed the quarter ratification lands, not studied afterwards. Meanwhile the Haleon exhibit reframes the manufacture question: its Likoni Road plant exports to seven EA markets plus South Africa, proving Kenya can be the G5 study's regional hub answer , not merely a market. The excise-stamp regime cuts both ways — a compliance cost on every tube and bottle, and a state-run track-and-trace layer that a genuineness-led brand can wear as proof.
