**THE PLAY:** FOCUS. One line: Dabur owns Saudi Arabia's hair-oil ground — Vatika's Gulf-built variants, a claimed #1 in hot-oil treatments, Amla's expat franchise — but the category's future consumer (the Saudi woman, newly salaried, Snapchat-native, Nice One-shopping) is forming her habits in channels where Dabur is absent and hearing heritage stories in a register (amla, the Indian oiling ritual) that is not hers; this deck deepens the franchise on three moves — re-narrate (Gulf-botanical register: black seed, argan-adjacent, henna-respectful), re-channel (Nice One relaunch, Nahdi beauty wall, quick-commerce replenishment), and claim the hijab-care whitespace no oil brand has named despite owning the functionally perfect product.

**THE PLAY:** DEEP THINK — the re-narration wager, inverted. The deck's thesis is that story, not product, is the binding constraint — invert it. (1) Maybe the Saudi consumer simply doesn't want oils — she is salon-and-serum native and reads oil jars as her mother's category. The evidence cuts against: the serum wave's fastest-growing formats are OIL-BASED treatments in modern dress; Hammam Zaith's claimed leadership shows the ritual sells NOW; and the hijab-care need profile (scalp comfort, breakage) is an oil brief. The risk is real but it is a FORMAT risk (jars vs droppers vs sprays), answered by R5's modern formats, not a category rejection. (2) Maybe the register doesn't matter — she buys Pantene without needing a Saudi story. True for the wash aisle, where function suffices; false for heritage-herbal, where the entire premium IS the story — a heritage brand speaking someone else's heritage collects neither the function premium nor the heritage one. (3) Maybe two registers are unaffordable at KSA media prices. Answer: the expat register already exists and pays for itself; the Saudi register is creator-led (Mawthooq-licensed micro/mid tiers, not TVC), and the Nice One relaunch doubles as its media. Second-order: the re-narration's success creates the copy risk — a major's black-seed line with Khaleeji creators could out-spend the story within a year of it proving out. The moat is layered first-mover depth: the hijab-care CLAIM (a named system, not an ingredient), the Hammam Zaith ritual franchise, and halal-plus- heritage credibility a Unilever line can imitate but not embody. Kill-test: the two-Ramadan brand-lift benchmark in the bet — if the story doesn't move the Saudi cohort in two cycles, it isn't the story, and the franchise reverts to a defended-base model without shame.

**THE PLAY:** DEEP STRAT — claiming hijab-care without a misstep. The whitespace is real (named needs, no owner, functionally perfect portfolio) and the risk is real (a modesty-adjacent claim executed clumsily by a foreign brand could read as exploitative). The design: (i) female-led end to end — Saudi female creative leads, female Khaleeji panelists, female creators; the brand's role is SERVING a need women already discuss among themselves (the content landscape audit maps that discourse first); (ii) comfort language, never garment language — the system talks scalp comfort, breathability, breakage protection; it never comments on the hijab itself, positively or otherwise — the garment is the context, not the subject; (iii) product truth first — lightweight non-greasy textures are the make-or- break spec (coverage + oil = the failure mode she already knows); the NPD brief leads with sensorials; (iv) soft-launch sequencing — Nice One exclusive first (the 73%-female channel as the safest test bed), listen, adjust, then mass; (v) the moat — first-mover NAMING: if «راحة تحت الحجاب» becomes the segment's reference phrase, followers market Dabur's category. Works if: the discourse audit confirms the need is discussed and unserved. Fails if: panel or soft-launch reads discomfort with ANY brand entering the space — then the claim retreats to ingredient-function language (lightweight scalp comfort) without the system framing, losing the naming moat but keeping the sales. Either way the products ship; only the claim's altitude adjusts.
