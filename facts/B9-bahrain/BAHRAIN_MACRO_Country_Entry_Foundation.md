> SO WHAT: So what. Bahrain is the anti-Dubai: a confirmed multi-year cost-of-living squeeze (VAT doubling + austerity + fees) landing on both nationals and a blue-collar remitter class that sends home a billion dinars a year against low wages. The demand consequence is precise: value packs, BHD 0.100–0.500 price points, and trusted-brand trade-down — shoppers stepping from P&G/Unilever premium into brands that deliver heritage credibility at lower cost. That is Dabur's natural slope. Every premiumisation instinct from the UAE deck gets inverted here; the Gulabari/sidr-tier prestige plays stay in Dubai. ## M3 · Trade, legal & regulatory lanes

### T3 · Tariff chessboard
| Lane | Duty | Mechanics | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| RAK-UAE plant → Bahrain | 0% | ≥40% local value-add = UAE/GCC-origin → free circulation under the GCC customs union | PRIMARY LANE — supply everything possible ex-RAK [!] [!] SKU-level origin qualification with RAK compliance |
| India → Bahrain direct | 5% CET on CIF | paid once at first GCC entry point, then free circulation; no India–Bahrain CEPA; India–UAE CEPA does NOT extend, and transshipment loses CEPA status | ethnic long-tail SKUs only |
| India → UAE (CEPA 0%) → Bahrain | CEPA lost on re-export | transshipped goods explicitly lose preferential status | no arbitrage — don't try |

### T4 · Legal & registration spine
| Item | Rule | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Agency law (Decree 10/1992, am. 1998/2002) | registered agent must be Bahraini national / ≥51% Bahraini entity; unregistered agencies unenforceable; since 2003: multiple NON-EXCLUSIVE distributors permitted; mandatory agent commission abolished | materially more principal-friendly than Kuwait/Qatar — negotiate non-exclusive, category-split distribution from day one; avoid the classic Gulf agent lock-in |
| Foreign ownership | 100% allowed in most sectors (ICLG 2026) | own trading entity possible later; not needed for entry |
| NHRA — cosmetics lane | notification aligned to GSO/EU (GSO 1943:2024); Arabic+English labels, INCI; ~3–6 months | hair oils, shampoo, toothpaste-cosmetic, skin care — the fast door |
| NHRA — health-products lane | separate food-supplement/health registration | Chyawanprash, honey-with-claims, Honitus-class herbal [!] [!] per-SKU classification before claims are written |
| NHRA — drugs | full pharma registration + import permits | avoid — keep claims below the drug line |
| Halal | GSO 2055-4:2021 (halal cosmetics) — positive claim, not blanket mandate | the standard MENA flip: lead with halal/alcohol-free as a shelf advantage, especially oral care + hair oils |

> SO WHAT: So what. The 2003 non-exclusivity amendment is the quiet gift of Bahraini law: Dabur can split FMCG (LuLu-facing) and pharmacy (Nasser/Boots-facing) between two specialist distributors without an exclusivity hostage situation — the single most common Gulf-entry mistake pre-empted by statute. And the tariff table means the UAE deck's CEPA arithmetic is replaced by something simpler: RAK-origin is the passport; the customs union is the corridor.
