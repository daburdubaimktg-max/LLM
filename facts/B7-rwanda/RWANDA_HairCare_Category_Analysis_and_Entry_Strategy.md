_source file: reports/B7-rwanda/RWANDA_HairCare_Category_Analysis_and_Entry_Strategy.html_

**THE PLAY:** FOCUS. One line: in Tanzania Dabur must CREATE the branded herbal-oil category; in Rwanda a Pakistani herbal house (Hemani) has already created it — own storefront, amla oil at RWF 10,000 per 200ml — and left the middle open. Vatika enters as the bigger, better-credentialed brand at a fairer price (RWF 6,000–8,000), in small rigid bottles the plastic law permits, through the women-first channel (Kasha) no competitor has organised. This is a CLAIM, not a bet: lower variance, faster proof, smaller ceiling — correctly sized to the batch's smallest market.

**THE PLAY:** DEEP THINK — why did nobody claim this already? The inversion that stress-tests the verdict: if the space under Hemani's umbrella is so obviously valuable, why is it empty? Candidate explanations. (1) The market is too small to notice — most multinationals run East Africa from Nairobi and sort countries by TAM; Rwanda's ~$240M FMCG pool doesn't clear their attention threshold. True, and irrelevant to Dabur, whose Tanzania infrastructure makes Rwanda's marginal cost a rounding error. (2) The sachet ban deterred the LUP players — the entrants who would normally storm this tier (Movit-class, Kenyan value brands) run sachet-first playbooks that are illegal here; the ban functions as an accidental moat protecting the bottle-native entrant. (3) Hemani's tier looked occupied from a distance — a wholesaler scanning shelf photos sees "herbal oils: taken" and moves on; only a category reading distinguishes an umbrella (price ceiling with nothing beneath) from a wall (defended tier). The grey trade made exactly this error, importing Dabur toothpaste but not Vatika. All three explanations favour entry; none suggests hidden rot. Second-order: the entrant most likely to follow Vatika in is Hemani itself, DOWNWARD — a mid-price Hemani line launched to defend. That fight is winnable on brand depth and distribution, but it argues for speed: the claim's value decays with every quarter the umbrella stands unchallenged. Kill-switch: if the July window is missed and Hemani or Movit moves first, re-derive rather than force — a contested claim is a different, worse deck.

**THE PLAY:** DEEP STRAT — the Kasha anchor deal, engineered. Kasha is the one channel in the batch that reaches the feature-phone rural woman directly — USSD ordering, mobile-money payment, discreet delivery, 600k+ customers, 61% female — and it has no anchor personal-care brand. The design: (i) Vatika as the platform's flagship herbal-hair line with category-anchor terms — preferential placement and a joint launch campaign in exchange for platform-exclusive pricing on one SKU (the R1 coconut bottle, making trial the platform's own offer); (ii) the reseller layer — Kasha serves resellers, and a Vatika reseller pack (6 × R1 at trade price) turns rural women into micro-distributors, echoing the stylist-as-distributor logic at feature-phone scale; (iii) the data covenant — monthly SKU-level sell-through by district, the market's only rural demand telemetry, contractually shared; (iv) the MoMo bridge — Kasha's payment rail and the cashback mechanic are the same infrastructure, so the promo runs natively on-platform first. Works if: the platform's economics can carry FMCG price points (their history is health/hygiene — adjacent, proven). Fails if: delivery costs swamp small-basket oil orders — then the reseller pack IS the model (aggregate baskets), and the direct-to-consumer layer waits for density. Cost: modest exclusivity concessions on one SKU. Value: a channel no competitor can copy without two years and a Series B.
