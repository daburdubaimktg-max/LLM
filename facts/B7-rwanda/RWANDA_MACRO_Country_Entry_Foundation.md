**THE PLAY:** FOCUS. One line: Rwanda is the smallest market in Batch 7 (~US$240M total FMCG [!] re-validate: derived from "largest manufacturer ~$10M = <5% of FMCG market" — triangulate with Statista B&PC US$667M figure, which measures a broader basket ) but the best-governed: 91% health-insured, 76% of adults on mobile money, a functioning standards-and-traceability state, and a president who personally polices the skin-lightening economy. The entry thesis is NOT a launch — Dabur products already sit on Kigali shelves (Herb'l toothpaste, Herbolene jelly, via the Sawa Citi supermarket group's marketplace store) at grey-route prices. The thesis is a formalisation : convert an accidental toehold into a registered, corridor-efficient, Kasha-partnered position — and treat Rwanda as the low-cost fourth seat on the Tanzania container.

**THE PLAY:** DEEP STRAT — why Sulfo is not CCIL. Tanzania's champion plays Dabur's game (herbal, toothpaste, local plant) — partnership there teaches a rival the playbook. **THE PLAY:** DEEP THINK — is Rwanda worth a seat at all? Invert the entry: the honest bear case is that a ~US$240M total FMCG market, growing fast off a small base, with per-SKU registration at US$1,250, a banned sachet format, a state that absorbs episodic health demand, and an April-dark calendar, cannot repay a full Dabur country programme — and the bear case is RIGHT, which is why this report never proposes one. The bull case rests on four asymmetries. (1) Marginal cost: supply rides Tanzania's container, registration rides the EAC dossier family, the radio playbook is one station, and the first distribution conversation is with a retailer already selling the brand — the true incremental cost of Rwanda is a fraction of a standalone entry. (2) Option value on governance: Rwanda is the region's laboratory state — eKash, Track-and-Trace, the plastic ban — and patterns proven here (no-sachet LUP packs, wallet cashback promos, formal-rails authenticity) become templates as the rest of the EAC's regulation matures in the same direction. (3) The grey-flow arbitrage: Dabur stock WILL reach Kigali via Kariakoo whether or not Dabur acts; formalising converts leakage into margin and protects pricing before the grey route sets it permanently (it has already set Herb'l at 3,500 — a gift this time; next time it might be a discount). (4) Strategic geography: Rubavu and Rusizi sit on the DRC border — the same posts Sulfo built branches on — and eastern DRC is a future programme question that a Rwanda base answers cheaply. Second-order: the kill condition is corridor friction — if the Dar consolidation gain dies in clearance costs despite the 2025-26 pacts, Rwanda reverts to a Nairobi-served maintenance market and the bet is surrendered gracefully. The falsifiable terms in M11 price exactly that.
