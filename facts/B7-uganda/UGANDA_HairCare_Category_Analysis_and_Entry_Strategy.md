## C1 — The ask

> SO WHAT: The decision this deck asks for: approve AMAFUTA — (1) relaunch Vatika + Amla as a managed oils franchise : unified national price ladder, sachet/LUP formats (90ml and below), UNBS Q-mark + batch-QR authenticity dress; (2) build the moisture-and-scalp story for Afro-textured hair in Luganda-first radio and TikTok — oiling is already the practice, Dabur arrives as its standardised, genuine, affordable version; (3) hold a strict no-go line : no relaxer launch, no extensions, no salon-chemical war with Movit and Darling. Budget shape: trade margin generous, media cheap (the world's lowest data costs), zero listing-fee theatre — the duka stocks what gets asked for.

## C3 — How this shelf was built

### T4 — Category history & imagery timeline
| Era | What happened | Residue today |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-1990s | Home care: shea (Nilotica in the north), castor, home mixes; braiding as social institution | Oiling and braiding are default practices, not trends |
| 1990s–2000s | Movit founded (1997) — local manufacture of jellies then hair; relaxer era builds salon culture; Darling factory (2002) | Local-made mass price points; chemical + added-hair segments owned locally |
| 2000s–2010s | Sachet economy matures; Indian oils arrive via Kikuubo importers; L'Oréal buys into EA mass (Interconsumer 2013) | Dabur's accidental beachhead; multinationals buy local rather than build |
| 2015–2022 | Natural-hair movement reaches EA social media; protective styles + moisture talk grow; TikTok arrives | Care-and-maintenance segment gains voice; relaxer growth flattens |
| 2022–26 | EAC 35% band (2022); counterfeit crisis mainstream (UNBS >50%); Jumia goes upcountry (>50% of orders); data cheapest in world | Authenticity anxiety + digital reach = the entry conditions this deck exploits |
