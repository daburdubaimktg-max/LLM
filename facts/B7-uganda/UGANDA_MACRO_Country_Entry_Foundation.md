### T2 — The Indian thread (origin stance raw material)
| Era | Fact | Residue for Dabur |
|---|---|---|
| 1890s–1972 | ~80,000 South Asians by 1972; railway origins, then trade dominance; Madhvani & Mehta agro-industrial empires | Indian enterprise is woven into Uganda's commercial founding story |

| Trade | India-Uganda trade ~US$1.7bn; Uganda imports from India US$1.27–1.3bn [!] [!] series mismatch | Indian goods are an everyday fact of the shelf, not an exotic import |

> SO WHAT: So what: the batch brief's origin flip holds, with a nuance. India-origin is an asset in the trade (Indian distributors, wholesalers and retail networks will receive Dabur as a known quantity) and neutral on the shelf (consumers buy Indian products daily without ceremony). It is NOT a consumer-facing badge to lead with — Ugandan shoppers reward "works, affordable, genuine" over provenance, and the political memory of 1972 argues for confident normality, not flag-waving. Lead with herbal-natural efficacy; let origin work quietly through the trade.

## M3 — Tariff & tax: the anti-Gulf

### T4 — Landed-cost stack (the inverse of Batch 9's corridor)
| Layer | Rate | Note |
|---|---|---|
| EAC Common External Tariff | 25% finished goods; new 35% band (Jul 2022) cited for cosmetics-adjacent lines [!] [!] confirm per HS code | EAC members 0%; COMESA FTA 0% |
| India origin | Full CET — no preference | RAK vs India is duty-NEUTRAL. Neither hub earns a corridor; the choice is freight cost, consolidation and speed only |
| UAE (RAK) origin | Full CET — no preference |
| VAT | 18% on (CIF + duty + excise) | Standard |
| Infrastructure levy | 1.5% | Railway development fund |
| Import WHT | 15% cited in some guides [!] [!] verify scope — materially changes landed cost | Priority tax-adviser question |
| Procedure | TIN + licensed clearing agent; UNBS PVoC CoC via Electronic Single Window | TÜV/SGS/Intertek pre-export verification |

> SO WHAT: So what: every Batch-9 deck ran on a 0%-duty, 0%-VAT corridor whose spread funded programmes. Uganda is the exact inverse: ~45–55% of CIF lands as tax before a single trade margin. Three consequences: (1) price architecture must be engineered backwards from the UGX 2,500–15,000 shelf reality, which means small formats, local-fit gramages and ruthless COGS work; (2) local manufacture or repack deserves a real study — Kampala Pharmaceutical Industries and QCIL both offer contract capacity, and Movit proves local personal-care manufacture wins at scale; (3) the EAC lane cuts both ways — goods made (or substantially transformed) inside the EAC move duty-free across Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda and into the DRC/South Sudan re-export trade that already flows through Kikuubo.

| Domain | Regime | Read for Dabur |
