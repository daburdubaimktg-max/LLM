## 1. The written standard (settled fact)

**Standard Written Chinese (SWC)** — 现代标准书面语 / 白话文 (*báihuàwén*) / 书面语 (*shūmiànyǔ*) — is the shared written form used in national media, education, signage, books, and formal writing.

- Its **grammar and vocabulary are based on spoken Mandarin (Putonghua, 普通话)**, standardized from the 1920s (May Fourth vernacular movement).
- **Every literate Chinese person reads it**, whatever they speak at home. A Cantonese, Shanghainese, or Hokkien speaker reads the *same characters*, pronouncing each in their own variety.
- It is therefore the **common denominator** that both Mandarin and Cantonese speakers understand in national media — exactly the shared script the team asked about.

**The subtlety that trips people up:** SWC reflects how *Mandarin* speakers talk. A Cantonese speaker *can* read SWC aloud in Cantonese, but it does not match colloquial spoken Cantonese grammar or vocabulary — it sounds "written/formal" to them. This is diglossia: they speak Cantonese, they write (and read formally) in SWC.

**Chengyu note:** SWC of all registers is threaded with 成语 (*chéngyǔ*, four-character classical idioms). Even casual writing uses them. They compress a whole story or moral into four characters and are a marker of fluency and craft — reach for them when composing, and gloss them when decoding.

## 2. Simplified vs Traditional — which market

| Market | Script | Written standard |
|---|---|---|
| Mainland China | **Simplified (简体字)** | SWC (Putonghua-based) |
| Singapore | **Simplified** | SWC |
| Hong Kong | **Traditional (繁體字)** | SWC formally; written Cantonese informally |
| Macau | **Traditional** | SWC formally; written Cantonese informally |
| Taiwan | **Traditional** | SWC with Taiwan-specific vocabulary (Guoyu 國語) |

Wrong script is an instant "you don't know us" signal. Never serve Simplified to Taiwan or Traditional to the Mainland unless deliberately targeting a diaspora reader who expects it.

**Taiwan vocabulary caution:** Traditional script alone isn't enough for Taiwan — word choice differs from the Mainland (e.g., software, potato, network terms, everyday nouns diverge). Flag when a Mainland-worded line is being aimed at Taiwan.

## 3. Written Vernacular Cantonese (書面粵語) — when it's right

Hong Kong (and Macau) colloquial writing has its own character set that captures spoken Cantonese verbatim: 嘅 (possessive), 啲 (plural/some), 喺 (at/in), 唔 (not), 係 (is), 瞓 (sleep), 咗 (completed aspect), 嘢 (thing), 冇 (don't have).

- **Use it** for informal, youth, social-native HK content — it reads warm, local, in-group. It appears in ads, magazines, comics, chat, social media.
- **Do NOT use it** for formal/national content, or for any Mainland audience — a Mandarin reader finds it odd-to-unintelligible.
- **Decision:** HK informal & you're sure the audience is Cantonese → written Cantonese is a warmth lever. Anything formal, cross-border, or Mainland → Standard Written Chinese.

## 4. The regional languages / topolects (方言 fāngyán) map

These are frequently *languages* (mutually unintelligible when spoken), not accents. In writing they mostly default to SWC; in audio/video they carry strong regional signal. Name the topolect precisely — it tells you audience, tone, and cultural code.

- **Mandarin / Putonghua (普通话)** — the national spoken standard and default. Sub-accents matter: Beijing (儿化 *érhuà* retroflex, "capital" authority/banter), Northern broadcast-neutral, Southern-accented Mandarin (softer, drops retroflex).
- **Cantonese / Yue (粤语/廣東話)** — Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong, Macau, huge global diaspora. Signal: prestige, Hong Kong cinema/Cantopop heritage, cross-border commerce, cosmopolitan cool. Nine tones; rich colloquial written form (above).
- **Wu (吴语) / Shanghainese (上海话)** — Yangtze delta (Shanghai, Suzhou, Hangzhou). Signal: metropolitan sophistication, old-money delta refinement, softness ("吴侬软语").
- **Min (闽语) — Hokkien / Minnan (闽南语) & Teochew (潮州话)** — Fujian, Taiwan (Taiwanese Hokkien), and the SE-Asian diaspora (Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines, Indonesia). Signal: Fujian/Taiwan roots, diaspora warmth, business networks, folk-religion heritage.
- **Hakka (客家话)** — dispersed across southern China, Taiwan, diaspora. Signal: migratory resilience, tight community, tradition.
- **Sichuanese / Southwestern Mandarin (四川话)** — Sichuan, Chongqing, Southwest. Signal: enormous comedic and warm-earthy charm; a dominant flavor in short-video comedy and food content. Very "alive" and likeable online.
- **Dongbei / Northeastern (东北话)** — the three Northeastern provinces. Signal: blunt, funny, big-hearted; the heartland of 小品 (*xiǎopǐn*, comic sketch) and a lot of livestream/short-video humor. A Dongbei accent reads as instantly comedic-warm.
- **Others to recognize:** Xiang (湘, Hunan), Gan (赣, Jiangxi), Jin (晋, Shanxi). Name them if they appear; don't force detail you can't verify.

When audio/video carries a topolect, ask *why*: authenticity, humor, regional pride, or a targeting choice. A topolect used for a product from elsewhere can read as borrowed or off.

## 5. The register ladder within Mandarin

Name the register before decoding or composing — same words in the wrong register misfire.

1. **News-anchor / official formal (书面/官方)** — CCTV cadence, government, luxury VO. Signal: authority, gravity. Risk: stiff, distant, "预制" (pre-made/canned).
2. **Everyday colloquial (口语)** — how people actually talk. The default for warmth and relatability.
3. **Youth / meme / fandom (饭圈 fànquān, 网络 wǎngluò)** — internet-native, fast, in-group. Number codes, pinyin abbreviations, memes. Signal: belonging, currency, "one of us." Risk: dates fast; misuse reads as a parent trying to be cool.
4. **Literary / 文言-flavored (文雅/古风)** — classical allusion, chengyu-dense, 古风 (*gǔfēng*, ancient-style) aesthetics. Signal: refinement, heritage, national-tide 国潮 pride. Risk: pretension if unearned.
5. **E-commerce livestream patter (直播带货)** — the rapid, high-energy sales cadence: "321，上链接！" (*sān-èr-yī, shàng liànjiē* — "3-2-1, link's up!"), "家人们" (*jiārénmen*, "fam"), urgency and scarcity calls. Signal: conversion, hustle, mass-market. Wrong anywhere else.

## 6. Script & numeral signals

- **Arabic numerals vs Chinese (一二三 / 壹贰叁)** — Chinese numerals signal heritage/formality; the formal banking forms (壹贰叁) signal contracts/gravitas.
- **Full vs half-width punctuation**, and whether emoji/kaomoji are present, signal platform and register.
- **Pinyin in the text** (e.g., "yyds") signals internet-native youth register — see the living lexicon.
- **Traditional characters in a Mainland context** can be a deliberate heritage/premium or Hong-Kong-nostalgia cue — read it as a choice, not an error, then verify.
