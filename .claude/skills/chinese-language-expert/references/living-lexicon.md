## 1. Number codes
Chinese numerals sound like words, so numbers carry meaning. Common, fairly stable:
- **520 / 521** — 我爱你 (*wǒ ài nǐ*, "I love you"). 520 is an unofficial online romance day (May 20).
- **1314** — 一生一世 (*yìshēng yíshì*, "forever / a whole life"). Often paired: **5201314** = "I love you forever." Red-envelope amounts ¥5.20 or ¥13.14 carry romance.
- **666** — 溜溜溜 (*liù*, "smooth/skilled") — "awesome, killing it, well played." From gaming; now universal praise.
- **88 / 886** — "bye-bye" (8 = *bā* ≈ "bye").
- **233** — "hahaha" (from a laughing emoticon numbered 233 on an old forum).
- **555** — sobbing (*wǔ* ≈ the sound of crying, "boohoo").
- **995** — "help / SOS" (救救我, *jiùjiù wǒ*).
- **748** — "go die" (去死吧) — rude; flag it.
- **250 (二百五)** — "idiot/half-wit." Never use innocently; it's an insult.

## 2. Pinyin / letter abbreviations
First letters of a phrase's pinyin. Signal: youth, internet-native, in-group.
- **yyds** — 永远的神 (*yǒngyuǎn de shén*, "eternal god") = GOAT, ultimate praise.
- **xswl** — 笑死我了 (*xiào sǐ wǒ le*, "dying of laughter") = LMAO.
- **zqsg** — 真情实感 (*zhēn qíng shí gǎn*, "genuine feelings") — earnest, heartfelt, esp. in fandom.
- **nsdd** — 你说得对 (*nǐ shuō de duì*, "you're right") — sometimes sincere, often mildly placating.
- **awsl** — 啊我死了 (*à wǒ sǐ le*, "ahh I'm dead") = "I can't, too cute/overwhelming."
- **dbq** — 对不起 (*duìbuqǐ*, "sorry").
- **bdjw** — 不懂就问 (*bù dǒng jiù wèn*, "asking because I don't get it").
- **u1s1** — 有一说一 (*yǒu yī shuō yī*, "to be fair / real talk").
- **xtl** — 心态了 / "my composure's gone."
- **emo** — borrowed English "emo" = feeling low/moody; verbed in Chinese ("我emo了", "I'm in my feelings").
- **plmm / xdd** — pretty girl / little brother(s), fandom/livestream address.

## 3. Current buzzwords (snapshot — verify before use)
From 2025 year-end lists and 2026 platform culture. **Confirm live.**
- **活人感 (*huórén gǎn*)** — "alive-person vibe": authentic, unpolished, real; the top compliment in an era tired of perfect personas. (Contrast 预制.)
- **预制 (*yùzhì*)** — "pre-made/canned" (from pre-made meals 预制菜): anything generic, formulaic, soulless, copy-paste. A cutting critique of content or writing.
- **松弛感 (*sōngchí gǎn*)** — "effortless ease / relaxed poise"; unbothered, graceful, low-stress aesthetic.
- **氛围感 (*fēnwéi gǎn*)** — "atmosphere / aesthetic mood"; a curated vibe (lighting, styling). RED/Xiaohongshu-native.
- **情绪价值 (*qíngxù jiàzhí*)** — "emotional value": what a person/product gives you emotionally; a key modern relationship & marketing frame.
- **破防 (*pòfáng*)** — "defenses broken": emotionally hit hard, moved to tears or overwhelmed (can be positive or negative).
- **班味 (*bānwèi*)** — "the smell of the office/shift": the worn-down look of someone ground by work.
- **显眼包 (*xiǎnyǎn bāo*)** — "attention-grabber": the goofy show-off of the group; now affectionate.
- **city不city (*city bù city*)** — bilingual TikTok/RED-refugee meme (~2024–25): "is it urban/cool or not?"; emblem of the bilingual comment era.
- **邪修 (*xiéxiū*)** — "black-magic method": unorthodox-but-effective hacks (e.g., 邪修做饭, "black-magic cooking").
- **那咋了 (*nà zǎ le*) / 如何呢又能怎** — "so what / whatever, chill" — a shrug-off-the-stress attitude.
- **泰裤辣 (*tài kù la*)** — homophone for "太酷啦" ("so cool!"), playful respelling.
- **YYDS, 绝绝子 (*jué jué zi*)** — "the absolute best / amazing" (绝绝子 can now read as slightly dated/cringe to some — verify).
- **哈基米 (*hājīmǐ*)** — a phonetic meme-sound from a Japanese anime song, remixed on Bilibili/Douyin; loosely attached to cute cats (esp. the viral orange cat) but **deliberately near-meaningless** — playful chaos, a cute-noise. Its power is that it *doesn't* insist on a meaning. Under-read it; over-reading is the error. Went viral mid-2024, still live 2026.
- **红温 (*hóngwēn*)** — "gone red-hot [in the face]": worked up, flustered, or embarrassed to the point of flushing — from excitement, cringe, or anger. 2026.
- **显眼包 (*xiǎnyǎn bāo*)** — verified warm-flip: the lovable attention-hog / scene-stealer ("you're a riot, never change"). Originally a dialect insult, netizens flipped it affectionate.
- **栓Q (*shuān Q*)** — homophone of "thank you" from a viral villager's broken-English clip; **swings between sincere thanks and sarcastic exasperation** ("gee, thanks a lot"). Context decides — flag, don't assume.
- **智商税 (*zhìshāng shuì*)** — "IQ tax": a scam/overpriced thing you pay for being gullible. A cutting skeptic's word — watch for it in comments on your own category.
- **剁手 (*duòshǒu*)** — "chop my hands off": shopaholic-splurge idiom (Double-11 lineage). 又要剁手 = "gonna overspend again."
- **真香 (*zhēn xiāng*)** — "smells good / so good": the U-turn meme — said when someone loudly swore off something then ends up loving it. Can be genuine or self-mocking.

## 4. Cultural-tension slang (slower-moving, socially loaded)
These name real generational tensions — durable enough to explain, but check nuance:
- **内卷 (*nèijuǎn*, "involution")** — self-defeating over-competition; everyone works harder for the same result.
- **躺平 (*tǎngpíng*, "lying flat")** — opting out of the rat race; passive resistance to 内卷.
- **摸鱼 (*mōyú*, "touching fish")** — slacking on the job, quietly.
- **打工人 (*dǎgōngrén*, "laborer/wage-worker")** — wry self-label of the ordinary employee; solidarity in the grind.
- **精神内耗 (*jīngshén nèihào*)** — "mental self-friction": exhausting internal overthinking.
- **社死 (*shèsǐ*)** — "social death": mortifying public embarrassment.
- **凡尔赛 (*fán'ěrsài*, "Versailles")** — humble-bragging.
- **下沉市场 (*xiàchén shìchǎng*, "sinking market")** — lower-tier / county markets (a real commercial term, not just slang).

## 5. Homophone & censorship-evasion play
A structural feature: when a word is sensitive or blocked, users swap in homophones or near-characters. The classic lineage is the 河蟹 (*héxiè*, "river crab") ≈ 和谐 ("harmonize/censor") play and the 草泥马 (*cǎonímǎ*, "grass-mud horse") homophone genre. **Mechanism to recognize:** odd character choices that *sound like* something else usually encode a blocked or edgy meaning. Decode by ear (pinyin), not by the literal characters. Verify current examples live — this genre regenerates constantly.

## 6. Fandom (饭圈 fànquān) vocabulary
A dense in-group system around celebrities/idols. Recognize: **爱豆** (*àidòu*, "idol"), **哥哥/姐姐** (bias), **CP** (couple/ship), **粉** (*fěn*, fan) and **黑** (*hēi*, hater/anti), **打call** (cheer/support), **battle**, **awsl/zqsg** (above), **yyds**. Fandom language is its own register — earnest, hyperbolic, fast. Flag it when present; it changes the tone of everything.

## 7. Emoji & sticker subtext — the decode table
**The rule: never read a Chinese emoji at Western face value. Check subtext AND generation.** The WeChat (微信) native emoji set carries meanings that diverge sharply from iOS/global. Generational split is real — the same emoji reads one way to a 50-year-old, another to Gen Z.

| Emoji / sticker | Western read | China / WeChat read (esp. younger users) |
|---|---|---|
| **🙂 / [微笑] "smile"** | friendly, happy | **Loaded and cold.** Passive-aggressive, contemptuous, or "this conversation is over" — an emotionally flat door quietly closing. Reserved for your *boss*, not friends. **The #1 trap.** |
| **👋 "wave"** | hi / bye | "We're done here / you said something dumb." A *plastic* goodbye, often dismissive — not a warm farewell. |
| **😁 [grin/龇牙]** | big happy grin | Among youth: awkward/forced smile, cringe, "I don't know how to respond," secondhand embarrassment. |
| **🙏 [合十]** | prayer / thanks / high-five | Thanks or "please" — but **generational weight differs**: breezy "cheers, mate" for the young; heavy, sincere "I truly need this" for older users. Mismatch misfires. |
| **微笑哭泣 / 破涕为笑 (laugh-cry)** | LOL | Youth: genuine "haha" OR a polite non-answer. Older: bittersweet, laughing-through-tears. |
| **[裂开] "cracked/broken" 我裂开了** | — (WeChat-specific) | "I'm falling apart / mind blown / can't cope" — comic breakdown. From streamer 我裂开了 ("I've cracked"). |
| **[旺柴/dog-with-smile], 狗头 (dog head)** | — | "just kidding / don't take this literally" — the irony/sarcasm shield. Attaching 🐶 flips a statement to non-serious. Very important: it changes literal meaning to joking. |
| **[骷髅/skull], 6 sticker, 打工人 stickers** | — | Context-heavy in-jokes; read the sticker's *text*, not just the image. |

**表情包 (*biǎoqíng bāo*, "sticker packs / meme images")** — beyond the built-in emoji, users trade custom meme images that carry whole attitudes. When one appears, decode the *text on it* and the *reference* behind it, not the picture alone.

**Textual tone-markers (not emoji, same subtext layer).** Some plain-text sounds carry the same hidden edge as the 🙂 trap:
- **呵呵 (*hēhē*)** — the textual twin of the passive-aggressive smile: cold, dismissive, contemptuous, "I'm not even engaging." Almost never warm.
- **哈哈 / 哈哈哈哈 (*hāhā…*)** — genuine laughter; the more 哈, the more real the laugh. (A single 哈 can read as flat/awkward.)
- **嗯 (*ǹg*)** — a bare "mm/ok" can read as curt or displeased, like a one-word text in English.
Read these the way you read the 🙂: check whether the surface "agreement/laughter" is actually a door closing.

## 8. Memes (梗 gěng) — how they work
A **梗** is an in-joke/meme; **玩梗** is "to riff on a meme." Origins: a homophone, a viral clip, a movie/game line, a celebrity slip, a news event. Life-cycle: born as a 梗 → spreads as a 网络热词 (hot phrase) → a few graduate to 流行语 (buzzword) marking a real cultural shift. **They date fast and are context-locked** — a meme means nothing (or the wrong thing) outside its moment and community. When decoding: name the origin if you can verify it, say what attitude it now carries, and flag if it may already be stale. When *producing*: don't reach for a meme unless you've confirmed it's current and fits — a misused meme is worse than none.

**Recent-wave context (2025–26, verify):** the RedNote/Xiaohongshu "TikTok-refugee" bilingual moment (Jan 2025) produced English-Chinese code-switched memes (city不city, Chinamaxxing, "Southern Little Potato"); a 2026 wave of AI-mockery memes weaponizes corporate/AI jargon ("align," "landing," "synergy") ironically. Confirm what's live before leaning on any of it.
