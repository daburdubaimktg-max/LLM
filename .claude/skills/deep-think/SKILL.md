# DEEP THINK

A 7-lens pressure-test for a single idea, hypothesis, belief, or concept — before it hardens into a plan.

## When to trigger

Fire this skill whenever the user includes the phrase **DEEP THINK** (any casing) anywhere in their message. The phrase is binding — do not skip the framework or deliver a shorter version even if the idea seems obvious or trivial. When the user reaches for DEEP THINK, they are explicitly asking for the full seven-lens treatment.

**Use DEEP THINK when:** the user is stress-testing a single idea, belief, hypothesis, or concept (a brand positioning, a career move, a strategic thesis, a personal conviction, a creative direction, an investment hunch, a spiritual insight, a life philosophy).

**Use DEEP STRAT instead when:** the user is choosing between multiple defined options, ranking alternatives, or running a full commercial decision with P&L implications.

If the idea itself isn't clear from the user's message, ask **one** clarifying question to pin it down. Then proceed.

## Operating principles

- **Voice:** thoughtful senior thinking partner. Warm, direct, intellectually honest. No hedging for comfort.
- **Format:** clear lens-by-lens headers, bulleted content within each lens, minimal prose walls.
- **Honesty over flattery:** if the idea weakens under a lens, say so plainly. The point is pressure, not validation.
- **Specificity beats abstraction:** name people, categories, numbers, examples. "The competitor" is weak; "P&G's Head & Shoulders holds ~28% SoM in KSA and has launched a ₹99-parity anti-dandruff SKU" is strong.
- **Stay inside the idea being tested.** Don't wander into adjacent ideas or propose alternatives mid-lens — that belongs in the rebuild step of Lens 4 or the refined restatement in the closing synthesis.

## What this skill produces

Nine parts, in this order:

0. **Pre-flight** — the idea in one sentence
1. **Lens 1** — What am I NOT considering?
2. **Lens 2** — What's the RIGHT question?
3. **Lens 3** — Three-perspective stress test
4. **Lens 4** — Destroy → Rebuild → Succeed-Scenario
5. **Lens 5** — Compression test
6. **Lens 6** — Assumption audit with weakest-link rewrite
7. **Lens 7** — Three time horizons
8. **Closing synthesis** — status, refined idea, Reputation Bet, 48-hour next move

---

## 0. Pre-flight — State the idea in one sentence

Before any lens, force clarity. Write the idea being tested in **one sentence**, maximum 25 words, no conjunctions like "and also," "but if," or "which could." If the idea can't fit in one clean sentence, that's the first signal it needs sharpening — flag it, write your best one-sentence version, and proceed.

If the user's original phrasing is already crisp, quote it verbatim. If not, offer a tighter restatement and use that for the rest of the analysis.

---

## 1. Lens 1 — What am I NOT considering?

The blind-spot check. Name the **three things most likely being ignored** about this idea — usually the unglamorous ones: execution friction, regulatory nuance, channel economics, cultural context, the boring middle of the implementation. For each, state *why* it's being overlooked (it's tedious, it's someone else's problem, it contradicts the excitement, it's invisible from the user's vantage point).

---

## 2. Lens 2 — What's the RIGHT question?

Reframe. The user asked about X; is X actually the right question, or is it a proxy for a better one? Offer **two sharper reframes**, each in question form, and state briefly why each may be the more productive question to answer. The goal is to surface whether we're solving the right problem, not whether we have the right solution.

---

## 3. Lens 3 — Three-perspective stress test

Run the idea through three independent viewpoints. Do not blend them — give each a clean hearing.

### The Skeptical investor
The professional capital allocator evaluating this idea. What are their **three strongest objections**? Focus on magnitude, defensibility, payback, and comparable precedents that failed.

### The Excited customer
The end user or consumer who would most benefit. What are their **three strongest reasons to love this**? What problem does it actually solve for them — not in marketing language, but in lived experience?

### The Competitor
The most credible rival. What do they do in response? And critically: **who else has this idea, and why haven't they won?** If nobody else is doing it, name three honest reasons why. If many are doing it, name what makes this version different — or admit it isn't.

---

## 4. Lens 4 — Destroy → Rebuild → Succeed-Scenario

Three sub-steps. Keep them distinct.

### Destroy
Enumerate **every plausible way this idea fails.** Aim for at least six. Include the boring failures (execution drag, budget erosion, team turnover) not just the dramatic ones.

### Rebuild stronger
With all those failure modes now visible, state the **stronger version** of the idea. What changes? What gets dropped, added, or sequenced differently? The output here is a sharpened restatement, not a new idea.

### Succeed-Scenario
Imagine it's 12 months out and this idea **succeeded beyond expectation**. Write three sentences describing what happened. Then work backwards: what had to be true in the first 90 days for that outcome to land? This surfaces the critical path the forward-view misses.

---

## 5. Lens 5 — Compression test

Clarity stress-test. Express the idea at three resolutions.

- **In 5 sentences:** the full version, with context and rationale.
- **In 1 sentence:** maximum 20 words, no conjunctions.
- **In 3 words:** brutal distillation. If it collapses into generic language ("drive growth sustainably," "transform brand equity"), the idea isn't sharp enough yet — say so.

The 3-word version is diagnostic. If it survives compression with specificity, the idea has a hard edge. If it doesn't, the idea is still foggy.

---

## 6. Lens 6 — Assumption audit with weakest-link rewrite

- **List every critical assumption** this idea rests on. Aim for at least six. Write each as a falsifiable statement ("Consumers in KSA will pay a 15% premium for naturals positioning"), not a vibe ("naturals is big").
- **Identify the single weakest assumption** — the one most likely to be wrong, or the one whose failure would collapse the idea fastest.
- **Rewrite the idea assuming that assumption is false.** What's left? Sometimes the idea survives with modification; sometimes it doesn't, and that's the finding.

---

## 7. Lens 7 — Three time horizons

Temporal triangulation. View the idea from three vantage points.

### 2020 reaction
How would a thoughtful version of the user from 2020 react to this idea? What's changed in the world since — technology, consumer behaviour, category structure, cultural permission — that makes this idea possible, necessary, or relevant *now* when it wasn't six years ago? If nothing has changed, the idea may be recycled rather than timely.

### 2026 obvious
If this idea is supposedly obvious and correct today, **why isn't everyone already doing it?** Name the non-obvious part. Either there's a real barrier (capability, conviction, capital, timing) or the "obvious" framing is wrong.

### 2050 historian
A historian in 2050 writes one paragraph about this decade's CPG / business / cultural moment. How does this idea get remembered — as prescient, as obvious-in-hindsight, as a dead end, as a fad, or as unremarkable? Be honest. Most ideas don't make the paragraph.

---

## 8. Closing synthesis

Close every DEEP THINK output with four lines. Keep it tight.

### Idea status after 7 lenses
One word: **STRONGER** (the idea held up and sharpened) / **WEAKER** (pressure exposed fragility) / **TRANSFORMED** (the idea changed shape meaningfully during testing). No fence-sitting.

### Refined idea
The best version of the idea, post-testing, in one sentence. This may be the original, a sharpened version, or — if status is TRANSFORMED — a materially different formulation that emerged.

### Reputation Bet
One sentence, first-person, stating the single claim the user would stake their credibility on. Format: **"If I'm wrong about [specific claim], I'll [specific consequence]."** If this feels uncomfortable to write, the testing isn't done yet.

### Next move (48 hours)
One concrete action the user will take in the next 48 hours — a conversation, a read, a test, a draft, a decision, or an explicit "park this for 30 days with X trigger to revisit." No action = the whole exercise is intellectual tourism.

---

## Scope note

Use DEEP THINK for single-idea pressure testing. For multi-option decisions with P&L, brand, and channel implications, use DEEP STRAT. For small tactical questions (copy tweaks, meeting prep, quick reads), don't run the full seven lenses — the trigger is the user's deliberate signal that they want the full treatment.
