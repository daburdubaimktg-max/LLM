# Information Heads — the complete research spine

This is the checklist of everything a country-category entry deep-dive must cover. **Macro heads M1–M11**
build the shared `COUNTRY_MACRO` report. **Category heads C1–C18** build each of the four category decks.
The **Own-Brand Audit (O1–O6)** runs both at portfolio level (in the macro report) and per category.

Do not treat these as boxes to fill mechanically — each head lists the *questions it must answer* and the
*decision it feeds*. If a head is genuinely N/A for a country, say so and why. If a head can't be sourced,
mark it `Source gap` and add it to the re-validation register (see `data-governance-validation.md`).

# MACRO HEADS (→ COUNTRY_MACRO report)

## M1 — Demographics
Population; age pyramid & median age; ageing vs youth bulge; urbanization & the city-tier structure
(name the tiers and the anchor cities); household size & formation; birth/marriage trends; migration;
regional/ethno-linguistic split. **Income distribution & the size of the addressable middle/affluent
class** — this, not total population, is the demand base. *Feeds:* cohort sizing, tier-city entry
sequence, which categories have runway.

## M2 — Macroeconomics & affordability
GDP, GDP/capita (nominal + PPP), real growth, inflation, disposable income & savings rate, consumer-
confidence trend, unemployment, currency regime & FX volatility vs the entrant's cost currencies.
Build a **PPP affordability index**: what does a comparable SKU cost as a share of local monthly income?
Derive the **price-and-pack architecture from local affordability, never from another market's bands** —
in low-income / high-inflation markets the entry unit is often the **sachet / small pack / low-unit-price
(LUP) format**, not the premium tube. Test whether premium/masstige has any runway at all before
assuming it. Note inflation & FX pass-through risk to price stability. *Feeds:* price architecture (C6/C7),
whether premium is even viable, pack strategy, FX-insulation of supply.

> **China-transplant guard:** do not import premium price bands (e.g. RMB 39–129) into a mass-income
> market. Anchor on the local affordability index and the prevailing entry pack format.

## M3 — Politics, geopolitics, trade & legal
Political stability & regime type; government stance toward foreign brands and toward the **entrant's
origin country specifically** (bilateral relations, tensions, boycott/nationalism history); tariffs,
import duties, FDI rules, cross-border-e-commerce policy & any signalled tightening; profit repatriation
& tax (VAT/GST, corporate); **IP regime** — trademark-squatting risk (register the brand/transliteration
early), enforcement reality; ease of doing business; sanctions/compliance flags. *Feeds:* entry-mode
choice (C17), the origin-headwind (M8), the risk register.

## M4 — History, religion, values, cohorts & occasion calendar
Deep-culture roots relevant to the categories (heritage ingredients, rituals, apothecary/traditional-
medicine lineage); religion & observance and what it forbids/enables (dietary, modesty, cosmetic norms);
**color & number semiotics, taboos, superstitions**; the **gifting & occasion calendar** (festivals,
new year, gifting seasons — when the category spikes); consumer values & live social trends (national
pride/heritage-revival, premiumization, health-consciousness, clean/green, singles/silver/she-economy,
Gen-Z codes). *Feeds:* positioning territory, claim framing, launch-timing, the "never" list.

## M5 — Media, digital, news & social ecosystem  *(deepened per user request)*
Map the *actual* media the target consumer uses, with names and URLs, not a generic list:
- **Digital penetration** — internet/smartphone %, time-spent, search-engine share (name it — Google?
  Baidu? Naver? Yandex?), and how people *discover* and *research* products.
- **Leading news & magazine outlets (name + website).** List the highest-traffic national newspapers,
  business press, and lifestyle/beauty/health magazines with their sites (e.g. for reach, ad context and
  earned-PR routes). These are where credibility and earned coverage live — capture the top 5–8.
- **Social platforms — deep map.** For each platform the category lives on: monthly actives, dominant
  content format, the shopping mechanic (does it sell in-app?), the discovery vs conversion role, and the
  KOL/KOC/influencer economy structure (tiers, typical rate cards as public ranges, seeding norms).
- **TV/OTT/radio/OOH** where still material. **Ad regulation** — claim restrictions, category bans,
  influencer-disclosure rules. **Payment & social-commerce infrastructure** (the wallets, the live-
  commerce norm; note mobile-money where it dominates, e.g. Telebirr/M-Pesa). *Feeds:* the channel
  engine, the comms plan, earned-PR strategy, C13 ad decode.

> **China-transplant guard:** do not assume the demand engine is KOL live-commerce / social-commerce.
> Test digital & smartphone penetration first. In low-penetration markets the growth engine is
> **traditional trade + distributor push + on-ground activation + radio/TV + messaging apps
> (Facebook/Telegram/WhatsApp/TikTok)**, and the platforms named will be local (not RED/Douyin). Let the
> penetration data — not the China playbook — pick the engine.

## M6 — Retail & e-commerce platform map  *(deepened per user request)*
- **Modern trade, general trade, pharmacy, specialty/beauty retail** — structure, concentration, the
  named chains, and their health (growing/declining). Quick-commerce & convenience.
- **E-commerce platforms — named, with URLs, ranked by relevance to these categories.** For each: gross
  role (marketplace vs B2C vs cross-border), the **cross-border lane** (the "free door" — does it bypass
  local registration/labelling?) vs the general-trade lane, seller model, and typical take-rate/margin
  architecture (front + back) as public ranges. Capture the top e-com destinations a new entrant would
  actually list on first.
- **Social-commerce & live-commerce** as a distinct channel. *Feeds:* the "Two Doors" regulatory
  sequence (M7), channel saliency & economics (C11), the e-com scrape (C12).

## M7 — Health-system, pharmacy, regulatory spine & the ENTRY-LANE map
First, the category regulator(s) and what can even be sold (the ingredient-legality gate, → Claim Ladder
C2). Then map the country's **actual viable entry lanes** — do NOT assume a free door exists. Enumerate
which of these are open, blocked, or mandatory in THIS country: (a) **cross-border e-commerce** (the
China "free door" — often absent or immaterial in low-e-com markets); (b) **direct import + local
distributor** (gated by tariffs, product registration, and — critically — **foreign-exchange
availability**: some countries ration FX / require Letters of Credit / have parallel FX markets that make
importing hard or impossible); (c) **franco-valuta / self-financed import**; (d) **local contract-
manufacturing**; (e) **JV / local partner**; (f) **licensing**. Sequence by *feasibility*, not by a
China-style default. In FX-constrained or import-substitution regimes, **local manufacturing or a JV may
be the only Phase-1 lane** — which overrides the organic-import default. Plus, where relevant: **pharmacy
structure**, traditional-vs-western medicine & self-medication culture (in many markets traditional
medicine dominates and pharma access is thin), supplement/"health-food" registration, derma/aesthetic
ecosystem, retail modernization. *Feeds:* entry-mode (C17), sequencing, the risk register (C18).

> **China-transplant guard:** the original "Two Doors — CBEC-first" framing is China-specific. Re-derive
> the lane map from scratch for each country. A free e-commerce door is the exception, not the rule.

## M8 — Origin-country stance & the localization doctrine
Diagnose the **specific stance** of THIS country toward the entrant's origin — it can be a **headwind, a
neutral, OR an asset**, and the strategy flips accordingly. Do not assume a headwind (that was the China×
India case). Examples of the range: India→China = headwind (counterfeit/quality meme, near-zero category
credibility) → localise hard, origin behind; India→East Africa (e.g. Ethiopia, Kenya) = often **neutral-
to-positive** (long Indian trade/education/investment presence, existing Dabur equity in Amla oil) →
origin can lead or co-lead. Assess: quality/counterfeit memes, category-credibility, geopolitical
baggage, diaspora presence, existing brand equity (tie to the Own-Brand Audit O1/O5).
- **If headwind:** apply the localization doctrine — lead with a **locally-legible, locally-legal hero
  ingredient/benefit**, origin **behind/endorsing**, label leads with the trusted manufacturing origin;
  build the trust-moat (published third-party testing + origin transparency).
- **If neutral/asset:** the origin (or an existing sub-brand) can front the play; still pressure-test with
  the Own-Brand sentiment read (O4) before assuming goodwill.
*Feeds:* brand architecture, naming, claim framing — the spine of every category strategy.

## M9 — Language & brand-name legality
Working language(s), scripts, dialects. Invoke the country-matched LANGUAGE expert. **Brand-name check:**
transliteration options, phonetic/semantic landmines, whether the name (and its local rendering) is
registrable and not already squatted, meme risk. Build the seed of the local-script glossary the category
decks extend. *Feeds:* the go/expensive-fix naming decision, IP filing (M3), every glossary.

## M10 — Source-confidence & re-validation register
A running table of every non-trivial number: value, source, date, confidence (High/Med/Low), and — for
anything from a login/geo-walled in-country platform — the exact `⚠ re-validate` query. Where a language
barrier caps western-source depth, attach the local-language deep-research prompt (see governance ref).
*Feeds:* board-grade honesty; the queue the user runs from an in-country workstation.

## M11 — Portfolio-sequencing synthesis (Phase 3)
Do the four categories share **one operating chassis** (one regulatory route, one channel engine, one org
spine, one ingredient/equity thread, one FX posture)? Rank them by role using the **4-cell typology**
(Momentum Engine / Breakout / Turnaround / White-space). Which category leads the chassis; how funding
tranches release; kill-switches. Close with a **portfolio-level Reputation Bet**.

# OWN-BRAND AUDIT (O1–O6)  *(added per user request — run for the entrant's own brands)*

Purpose: before recommending where to play, establish what the entrant's brands (e.g. Dabur's) *already*
mean and do in this country — awareness, availability, purchase, sentiment, positioning — then map the
open space. Run O1–O5 per category (fold into C3/C4/C12) and consolidate O6 per category + at portfolio
level in the macro report. **Everything here is public-signal-based; label proxies as proxies.**

## O1 — Awareness
Aided/unaided proxies: local **search-volume** for the brand & its hero ingredients (name the search
engine), **social-mention volume & trend**, presence in "best of / top X" local listicles, Wikipedia/
local-encyclopedia presence, any tracked brand-awareness studies (cite). State the honest read: unknown,
niche-diaspora-known, or mainstream. *Feeds:* whether the play is "build awareness from zero" vs "convert
latent awareness."

## O2 — Availability audit (local retail + e-com + social)  *(per user request)*
Where can a consumer actually buy the entrant's brands **today**:
- **Local physical retail** — modern trade, pharmacy, ethnic/import stores (name them).
- **E-commerce** — search each named platform from M6; capture live listings (seller, official-store vs
  grey-market/parallel-import, pack, price, in-stock).
- **Social-commerce** — any brand/store presence on the M5 social platforms; live-commerce appearances.
Distinguish **official presence** from **grey-market/parallel imports** — the latter is a signal of latent
demand AND a pricing/quality-control risk. *Feeds:* the "is there a beachhead already?" read, channel plan.

## O3 — Purchase signals
Public proxies for actual offtake: e-com **ratings count & best-seller/category rank**, review velocity,
"X sold" counters where shown, repeat-purchase signals, marketplace stock-outs. Convert to a directional
read (dormant / trickle / real pull) with `⚠ re-validate` flags on any GMV-style number. *Feeds:* sizing
sanity-check (C6), whether a category has a live vs cold start.

## O4 — Sentiment / feedback
Mine reviews, social comments, Q&A, forum threads for the entrant's brands & closest analogues:
**love-points, complaints, doubts (quality/counterfeit/efficacy), and the exact language consumers use**
(quote it, with translation). Separate what to amplify from what to fix. *Feeds:* claim framing, the
trust-moat design (M8), creative territory.

## O5 — Current positioning
How the brand *currently* shows up in this country (if at all) vs incumbents — the implied price tier,
benefit, and image. Contrast with how it should show up under the localization doctrine (M8). *Feeds:*
the reposition/relaunch decision, brand architecture.

## O6 — White-space & positioning-space map  *(per user request)*
Two explicit maps, per category and consolidated:
1. **Demand white-space map** — unmet/under-served need-states × occasions × cohorts where a legal
   Dabur SKU can win, scored by size × growth × door × incumbent intensity × Dabur right-to-win.
2. **Positioning-space (perceptual) map** — plot incumbents on the two axes that matter in this category
   (e.g. price × claim, tradition × science, mass × premium). Mark the **open, ownable cells** for Dabur,
   with the "works if / fails if" for each. This is the visual that answers "where should the new brand
   stand." *Feeds:* the recommended strategy (C7), creative territory (C8).

# RESEARCH-DEPTH REQUIREMENT (feed the deep-dive modules)

A summary-level fact brief is NOT enough to produce a China-standard deck. The category research pass must
go and gather the *granular* inputs the mandatory modules need — otherwise the writer has nothing to build
depth from and the report collapses into one-liners (the first-Vietnam-run failure). Explicitly collect:
- **Brand-by-brand entry sequences** for 4–6 winners: how each actually entered and scaled (route, timing,
  the one move that worked), not just their market share.
- **Real ad-copy lines** — actual taglines / slogans / hook phrases competitors use, in the local language,
  with source, so they can be decoded (literal + essence). Zero real ad copy = a failed C13.
- **Channel economics** — % contribution by sub-channel, growth trajectory numbers, and front/back margin
  ranges.
- **A price × pack × rating matrix** — real listings for ≥5 lead brands/SKUs (from the named e-com platforms).
- **A hero-SKU cost stack** — enough to build a value-chain waterfall (landed COGS, duty, platform take,
  trade margin) as public-range estimates.
- **≥25 glossary-worthy local terms** encountered during research.
Where the local language blocks western search (M10), emit the local-language research prompt to fill these
specific gaps — name them in the prompt, don't ask for a generic overview.

# CATEGORY HEADS (C1–C18) — run per category, referencing the macro report

The category deck must **cite the macro report** for country context, not repeat it. C1–C16 are the
proven China-deck spine; C17–C18 are additions.

- **C1 — Executive summary.** The one screen: the play, the size, the ask.
- **C2 — Market & sub-segment map + Claim Ladder.** Category size/growth; sub-segment grid (size ×
  growth × door × incumbent × entrant verdict); the **Claim Ladder** — the safe claims vs the
  special-registration/banned claims for this category in this country (the ingredient-legality gate).
- **C3 — Competition.** Share structure; the fortress incumbent (and why you don't assault it head-on);
  named challengers & format-natives; **positioning map (price × claim) with the open cells** (ties to
  O6). Use COMPANY-RESEARCH for teardowns.
- **C4 — Consumer & culture.** Need-states (the real, clinical/functional reality), the cohort × occasion
  × need map, the origin-problem honest read. Invoke the region CONSUMER-INSIGHT expert + MARKETING-
  PSYCHOLOGY.
- **C5 — Whitespace hypotheses — pressure-tested.** The hero bet(s); run **DEEP THINK** on the lead one.
- **C6 — Sizing the prize.** TAM/SAM/SOM built transparently with an assumptions ledger; Y1 / envelope /
  Y3 bands. Ambition set by the math, not by hope. Use HBR-DATA discipline.
- **C7 — Recommended strategy.** Brand roles (4-cell typology); phasing, gates & kill-switches;
  Door-1-first sequence; the brand architecture under the localization doctrine.
- **C8 — Creative territories.** LEAP logic via AD STRATEGIST + region ad expert; the demand engine.
- **C9 — DEEP STRAT stress test (condensed).** Three personas; danger-rated assumption audit; blind-spot
  scan. Invoke **DEEP STRAT**.
- **C10 — Validation checklist.** What must be true before the board treats numbers as facts.
- **C11 — Channel architecture & economics.** Saliency (contribution to category sales) + front/back
  margins by channel, as public ranges with `⚠ re-validate`.
- **C12 — E-commerce scrape snapshot.** Lead-brand price × pack matrix, share-of-vo
