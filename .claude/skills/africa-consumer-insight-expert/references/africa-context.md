# Africa Research Context — Reference

Grounding for the AFRICA Consumer Insight Expert. Use it to (a) judge whether a study's method was realistic for the market it ran in, (b) know where representativeness usually breaks, and (c) know which external sources are credible when adding context. **This file is orientation, not data. Never quote a number "from Africa context" — if you cite a figure, it must come from the supplied report or a live, dated external source you actually retrieved.**

## 1. Why "Africa" is not one market
- 54 countries, thousands of languages, radically different income, urbanisation, religion, media, and retail structures. A single "African consumer" claim is almost always a red flag.
- Useful working clusters (loose, not official): North Africa (Maghreb + Egypt), West Africa (Nigeria, Ghana, Francophone West), East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Ethiopia), Central Africa, Southern Africa (South Africa the outlier in retail maturity), plus the Indian-Ocean and Francophone/Anglophone/Lusophone splits.
- Language, dialect and code-switching matter enormously for questionnaire translation and qual moderation. When translation fidelity or dialect is in question, hand off to the relevant language skill (e.g., ARABIC EXPERT for MENA/North Africa).

## 2. Fieldwork realities that shape data quality
- **Face-to-face (CAPI) still dominant** in many markets; online is growing but urban-skewed.
- **Mobile-first**: phone (CATI), SMS, USSD, WhatsApp and mobile-app surveys reach further than web, but skew to the connected and literate; multiple-SIM ownership distorts phone frames.
- **Incentives, back-translation, and interviewer effects** are material; social-desirability bias is strong on income, hygiene, alcohol, sexual health, religion.
- **Seasonality & calendar**: Ramadan, harvest cycles, school terms, pay cycles, and rainy seasons move both behaviour and fieldwork feasibility.
- **Currency & inflation**: high-inflation markets (e.g., recent Nigeria, Egypt, Ghana, Zimbabwe) make price and value data perishable — always check field dates.
- **Sample sourcing**: quota + panel is the norm; probability samples are rarer and costlier. Ask how respondents were recruited.

## 3. Where representativeness usually breaks
- Rural vs urban (rural is under-sampled and harder/costlier to reach).
- Informal-sector and low-income segments (often excluded by online/phone frames).
- Language minorities and low-literacy respondents.
- Women's private behaviour in some contexts (moderator gender, setting).
- Youth over-representation online vs an older true population.
- "National" claims built on 2–4 cities.

## 4. Trade structure & retail measurement
- **Traditional / informal trade** (kiosks, dukas, table-top, open markets, spaza shops) is the majority of CPG volume in most markets outside South Africa. Retail audits historically under-cover it — always ask about informal-trade coverage before trusting share/distribution numbers.
- **Modern trade** concentrated in cities and in South Africa.
- **E-commerce / quick-commerce / social commerce** growing fast but off a small, urban base.
- **Distributor and wholesaler dynamics** shape availability more than consumer pull in many categories.

## 5. Regional / country nuances to hold in mind
- **Nigeria** — huge, young, price-sensitive, informal-trade dominated, high inflation; Lagos ≠ the North; multilingual (English, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo + pidgin).
- **Kenya / East Africa** — mobile-money-native (M-Pesa), strong urban modern trade in Nairobi, Swahili + English.
- **Egypt / North Africa** — large, Arabic-language (dialects matter), MENA-linked; hand off Arabic nuance to ARABIC EXPERT.
- **South Africa** — most retail-mature and best-measured; LSM/SEM segmentation history; multiple official languages; not representative of the rest of the continent.
- **Francophone West & Central Africa** — French-language fieldwork, different agency footprint.
- **Ethiopia, DRC, Angola, Lusophone markets** — thinner syndicated data; more primary work needed.
Treat every one of these as a hypothesis to confirm against the actual report, not a fact to assert.

## 6. Credible external sources for context (retrieve live; cite with date)
- **Syndicated / industry**: NielsenIQ, Kantar (Worldpanel, BrandZ), Ipsos, GfK, Euromonitor, GlobalData, Nielsen retail.
- **Macro / demographic**: World Bank, IMF, UN (DESA/Population), national statistics offices & census bureaus, Afrobarometer, DHS surveys.
- **Connectivity / media**: GSMA Intelligence, DataReportal (We Are Social / Meltwater "Digital" reports), ITU, GeoPoll.
- **Development / consumer**: McKinsey (Africa consumer/"Lions on the Move"), Deloitte, BCG, Boston-type consumer reports, Brookings AGI.
- **Category-specific trade bodies, regulators, and company annual reports.**
When you use any of these, they are **[EXTERNAL]** context — dated, cited, and firewalled from the report's own **[FACT]s**. Prefer original sources over aggregators; note when a figure is modelled/estimated rather than measured.

## 7. Recurring interpretation traps
- Generalising a 2-city online sample to a nation or the continent.
- Ignoring informal-trade coverage gaps in share data.
- Treating claimed purchase intent as demand.
- Comparing waves across a currency devaluation without flagging it.
- Reading a Western scale-use pattern onto African respondents (acquiescence/extreme-response styles vary by culture).
- Assuming urban modern-trade shopper behaviour reflects the mass market.
- Letting a vivid qual verbatim override a robust quant base (or vice-versa).
