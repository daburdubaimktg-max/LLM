# Methods, Statistics, Models & Metrics — Reference

A working reference for the MENA Consumer Insight Expert. Use it to (a) recognise and correctly name what a report actually did, (b) judge whether the method was fit for the stated objective, and (c) speak the client's and agency's language precisely. **Naming a technique correctly is not the same as vouching for it — always check it was executed and reported properly before you rely on a number.**

## 1. Quantitative methods
- **U&A (Usage & Attitude)** — foundational category study: who uses what, how often, why, attitudes, occasions. Watch claimed vs actual behaviour gap.
- **Brand Health Tracker** — continuous/wave-based measurement of awareness, consideration, usage, imagery, equity. Value is in the *trend*, not a single wave.
- **Concept / Product test** — monadic (one concept per respondent), sequential monadic, or comparative. Monadic is cleaner for absolutes; comparative for relative preference.
- **Home Use Test (HUT) / CLT (Central Location Test)** — product experience in-home vs controlled venue. CLT controls conditions; HUT is more realistic.
- **Conjoint / DCM (Discrete Choice Modelling)** — trades off attributes to derive utilities, importance, price sensitivity, share-of-preference simulation. Powerful but only as good as the attribute list.
- **Price research** — Van Westendorp (PSM), Gabor-Granger, BPTO. Van Westendorp gives an acceptable range, not the profit-maximising price.
- **MaxDiff (Best-Worst Scaling)** — forces discrimination across many items; better than rating scales for priorities.
- **Pack / shelf / eye-tracking quant, virtual shelf tests.**
- **Ad pre-test / post-test** — norms-based scoring (recall, branding, persuasion, likeability). Ask which norm database and whether it is category- and market-relevant to the MENA market in question.
- **CATI / CAPI / CAWI** — telephone / face-to-face / web administration. In the GCC, online/CAWI and panels are mature; in Egypt, the Levant and the Maghreb, CAPI and CATI still carry much of the load. Online panels skew urban, younger, higher-income, and (in the Gulf) can over- or under-represent nationals vs expatriates.

## 2. Qualitative methods
- **FGD (Focus Group Discussion)** — 6–8 respondents, group dynamic. Good for language, spontaneous associations; weak for sensitive or private behaviour.
- **IDI (In-Depth Interview) / Duo / Triad** — depth over breadth; better for sensitive topics.
- **Ethnography / immersion / accompanied shops / home visits** — observed reality vs stated behaviour.
- **Semiotics / cultural analysis** — codes, meaning, category conventions.
- **Online qual** — communities (MROC), bulletin boards, WhatsApp diaries, video diaries. Access-skewed (urban/affluent) outside the GCC; dialect- and Arabizi-aware moderation and coding needed.
- **Projective techniques** — collage, personification, laddering, sentence completion; surface latent motivation. Interpretation is inference, not fact.
- **Co-creation / workshops.**

## 3. Behavioural & observational methods
- **Retail measurement / retail audit** (e.g., Nielsen/Kantar Worldpanel-type) — sales, distribution, share, price. Coverage of traditional trade (baqala/groceries, souqs, kiosks) is the key question outside the GCC; modern trade dominates and is well-measured in the Gulf.
- **Consumer / household panels** — purchase behaviour over time; penetration, frequency, loyalty, repertoire.
- **Digital behavioural** — search, social listening, first-party CRM/loyalty, e-commerce clickstream. Representativeness = internet-active population only.
- **Implicit / reaction-time, facial coding, biometrics, eye-tracking** — measure response the respondent cannot or will not articulate. Treat as directional signal, not hard truth.
- **A/B and field experiments** — the gold standard for causal claims when done properly.

## 4. Sampling, weighting & representativeness
- **Probability vs non-probability** (quota, convenience, river, panel). Most commercial work is quota — margins of error are indicative, not textbook-exact.
- **Sample frame** — who could possibly be selected? In MENA, frame gaps (rural/interior, national vs expatriate mix in the Gulf, gender access, low-connectivity markets, multiple SIMs) drive most bias.
- **Weighting / rim / RIM weighting** — corrects sample to known population marks. Ask what it was weighted to and whether the census/benchmark is current.
- **Base size** — always check the *unweighted* base behind every sub-group %. Bases under ~30 are anecdote; 30–50 are directional; flag anything reported on a low base.
- **Booster samples** for small but important groups.

## 5. Statistics you will meet (and how to sanity-check them)
- **Mean / median / mode; top-2-box (T2B) / bottom-2-box** — T2B is standard for "% agree/like". Check the scale (5-pt vs 7-pt vs 10-pt) before comparing across studies.
- **Significance testing** — usually 90% or 95% confidence. "Significant" ≠ "large" ≠ "important". Ask the confidence level and whether multiple-comparison inflation was handled.
- **Margin of error / confidence interval** — for probability samples. Overlapping intervals mean "not proven different".
- **Correlation vs causation** — correlation in a tracker is not proof a lever works.
- **Regression (linear, logistic)** — drivers of an outcome; watch collinearity and R².
- **Key Driver Analysis** — Shapley, Kruskal, Johnson's relative weights, penalty-reward (from importance/performance). All are model-dependent estimates, not facts.
- **Factor / PCA** — reduces attributes to underlying dimensions.
- **Cluster / latent class** — builds segments; solution is a *choice*, not a discovery.
- **CHAID / decision trees; TURF** (reach/assortment optimisation).
- **Index numbers** — index of 120 means 20% above the reference base; always ask "index against what base?".
- **Effect size (not just p-value)** — the thing that actually matters commercially.

## 6. Standard CPG / brand-health metrics
- **Funnel**: Awareness (spontaneous/TOM/aided) → Consideration → Trial → Usage (P4W/P7D) → Loyalty/Repertoire.
- **Penetration × Frequency × Weight** (the growth arithmetic). Penetration usually the bigger prize (cf. Ehrenberg-Bass / "How Brands Grow").
- **Mental & physical availability; distinctive brand assets.**
- **Equity measures**: differentiation, relevance, esteem, knowledge; salience.
- **NPS** — one number, heavily caveated; useful as trend, weak as absolute, culturally variable in scale use across MENA markets (acquiescence and extreme-response styles differ by market).
- **Repertoire / SOR (share of requirements), SoW (share of wallet).**
- **Occasions / need-states (demand spaces).**
- **Price / value perception, willingness to pay.**
- **Media**: reach, frequency, GRP/TRP, VTR, attention metrics.

## 7. Analytical models & frameworks
- **JTBD (Jobs To Be Done); need-states / demand-space mapping.**
- **How Brands Grow (Ehrenberg-Bass): penetration-led growth, mental & physical availability, distinctive assets, light-buyer importance, Double Jeopardy, Duplication of Purchase Law.**
- **Fundamental laws**: Double Jeopardy, NBD-Dirichlet (predicts brand metrics from penetration).
- **Path to Purchase / shopper decision journey; first & second moment of truth.**
- **Category Entry Points (CEPs).**
- **Kano model** (delighters vs must-haves); **Means-End / laddering**.
- **Behavioural science lenses** (defer to the marketing-psychology skill for depth): salience, availability heuristic, social proof, loss aversion, anchoring, defaults, friction/EAST.

## 8. Segmentation & driver analysis
- Segmentation types: needs-based, attitudinal, behavioural, occasion-based, value-based, demographic. Best commercial segmentations are behaviour- or need-anchored and *addressable*.
- Test any segmentation on: distinctiveness, size, reachability, stability over time, and whether it changes what the business would actually do.
- Driver analysis outputs (importance, derived importance, penalty-reward) are **model estimates** — report them as such, never as measured fact.

## 9. Common buzzwords — plain-English translations
- **"Directionally"** = not statistically proven; treat as a hypothesis.
- **"Nudge / uplift"** = observed increase; check base and significance.
- **"Sentiment"** = coded polarity of text/social; method-dependent, sarcasm-blind.
- **"Deep-dive / drill-down"** = disaggregate to sub-group; watch base sizes.
- **"Statistically significant"** = unlikely to be chance at the stated confidence — not "big" or "meaningful".
- **"Representative"** = matches population on stated marks *only* (usually age/gender/region); silent on the marks it wasn't weighted to.
- **"Verbatims"** = respondents' exact words; the most fact-like part of qual — quote sparingly and accurately.
- **"Emergent theme"** = analyst's interpretation of qual; opinion, not count.
- **"n="** = base; the single most important number on any chart.

## 10. Red flags that should lower confidence in a number
- Percentages quoted with no base, or a base under ~30.
- Sub-group differences claimed with no significance test or overlapping intervals.
- Scale or wording changed between waves but trended as if comparable.
- Online/urban (or single-city, or all-national / all-expat) sample generalised to "the Saudi/Egyptian/Arab consumer".
- Leading, double-barrelled, or socially-desirable question wording.
- Claimed behaviour (recall, intent) presented as actual behaviour.
- A single wave presented as a trend.
- "Top-line" numbers that don't reconcile with the detailed tables.
- Weighting benchmark undisclosed or outdated.
- Causal language ("drove", "caused") on correlational data.
