# Common Pronunciation Mistakes — and Their Correct Articulation

The 25 most frequent Western pronunciation errors in ISKCON Sanskrit, with the correct śikṣā-grade articulation. Load when correcting someone's pronunciation, when auditing a recording, or when a beginner needs to be redirected.

For each entry: **The Error → The Correction → Why It Matters**.

---

## Names of the Lord

### 1. "Krish-nuh" or "Krish-nah" → *Kṛṣ-ṇa*

- **Wrong:** Flat tongue; final 'a' rhymes with English "uh" or "ah"
- **Right:** *Kṛṣ-ṇa* with **two retroflex events** (ṣ and ṇ both with curled tongue) and a **clean short *a* at the end** (schwa-like, never extended)
- **Why:** *Kṛṣṇa* = "all-attractive". The retroflex sounds are the ones that distinguish Sanskrit from English flat consonants. Without them, you are not pronouncing the Lord's name — you are pronouncing an English approximation.

### 2. "Krishn" (dropping the final 'a') → *Kṛṣ-ṇa*

- **Wrong:** Treating Sanskrit like Hindi where final 'a' is dropped (Krishn, Bhagvān, Ram)
- **Right:** Sanskrit IAST **always preserves final *a*** unless explicitly elided with avagraha (ʼ)
- **Why:** Sanskrit grammar requires the final vowel for the word's grammatical case to be intelligible. Hindi colloquial pronunciation drops it; Sanskrit (and Bhaktivedanta IAST) does not.

### 3. "Vish-noo" → *Viṣ-ṇu*

- **Wrong:** English "sh" (palatal *ś*) + English "n" (dental) + drawn-out "oo"
- **Right:** **Retroflex *ṣ*** (curled tongue) + **retroflex *ṇ*** (curled tongue) + clean short *u*
- **Why:** Both *ṣ* and *ṇ* are retroflex. The Lord's name has TWO tongue-curls in two syllables. "Vish-noo" with a flat tongue and elongated *u* is wrong on three counts.

### 4. "Ram" or "Raam" → *Rā-ma* (3 beats)

- **Wrong:** Hindi-style *Ram* (one beat, no final a) OR over-extended *Raaaam*
- **Right:** *Rā-ma* — *Rā* held for exactly 2 beats, *ma* for 1 beat
- **Why:** The long ā has a specific duration. Final 'a' must be present. *Rāma* is 3 mātrās, not 1 and not 4.

### 5. "Goh-vin-dah" → *Go-vin-da* (4 beats)

- **Wrong:** English long "oh" pulled into a drawl, elongated final "ah"
- **Right:** *Go* with the diphthong *o* (always long, 2 beats — clean "oh"), then short *vin*, then short *da*
- **Why:** *Go* is a true diphthong ending labial. *da* is short — not "dah" with stretched vowel.

### 6. "Hari" rhymed with "Mary" → *Ha-ri*

- **Wrong:** *Hair-ee* with long English vowels
- **Right:** *Ha-ri* — short *a* (like "ha!"), short *i* (like *bit*), 2 beats total
- **Why:** Both vowels are short. The whole word is 2 beats, not 4.

### 7. "Cait-an-ya" with English "ch-" → *Cai-tan-ya*

- **Wrong:** Treating *c* as English "k" or as English "ch" with a hard attack
- **Right:** Palatal *c* — same sound as *ch* in *church*, but **the IAST letter is *c*, not *ch***. In IAST, *c* alone means the unaspirated version, *ch* means the aspirated (with breath).
- **Why:** In IAST, *Caitanya* uses *c* (no aspiration). *Chandra* uses *c* (no aspiration) too — it's spelled *candra* in strict IAST! Many transliterations use *Chaitanya* or *Chandra* informally; this is a hybrid Hindi-English convention. **In Bhaktivedanta IAST, it's *Caitanya* and *candra*.**

## Sacred names of devotees

### 8. "Pra-bhoo-pah-dah" → *Pra-bhu-pā-da*

- **Wrong:** Long *bhoo* (drawing out the *u*); long *dah* (drawing out the final *a*)
- **Right:** *Pra-bhu-pā-da* — short *u* in *bhu*, long *ā* in *pā* (held 2 beats), short *a* in *da*
- **Why:** The vowel lengths matter. *Prabhu-pāda* literally = "at whose feet many masters [sit]". *Pā* (long) is the foot. Stretching the wrong vowel changes the rhythm.

### 9. "Bhakti-vedanta" mispronounced as "Bockty-vidanta" → *Bhak-ti Ve-dān-ta*

- **Wrong:** Losing the breath puff in *bh*, shortening the long *ā*
- **Right:** *Bha* (with breath) + *kti* + *Ve* (long *e*, 2 beats) + *dān* (long *ā*, 2 beats) + *ta*
- **Why:** *Bh* is a single aspirated consonant — labial *b* with a distinct puff of air. The long vowels distinguish *Vedānta* (the philosophical school) from any other word.

### 10. "Char-ya" or "Ah-cher-ya" for ācārya → *Ā-cār-ya*

- **Wrong:** English "ch" hard-attacked; missing the long ā at start
- **Right:** *Ā* (long, independent vowel, 2 beats) + *cār* (palatal *c* + long ā + cerebral *r*) + *ya*
- **Why:** Two long *ā* vowels and a palatal *c*. Total 5 beats. Native English speakers tend to compress this into 3 beats.

## Scripture names

### 11. "Bag-uh-vad Gee-tah" → *Bha-ga-vad Gī-tā*

- **Wrong:** Losing the *bh* breath; shortening *gī*; over-extending final *tā*
- **Right:** *Bha* (with breath puff) + *ga* + *vad* (halanta) + *Gī* (long *ī*, 2 beats) + *tā* (long *ā*, 2 beats)
- **Why:** "Bagavad Gee-tah" loses the *bh* and the proper vowel lengths. The correct form has the breath on *bh*, two long vowels (*ī* and *ā*), and proper rhythm.

### 12. "Shree-mahd Bah-gah-vah-tahm" → *Śrī-mad Bhā-ga-va-tam*

- **Wrong:** Losing *ś* (palatal soft "sh"); losing *bh* breath; English drawl on every "ah"
- **Right:** *Śrī* (palatal *ś* + cerebral *r* + long *ī*, 2 beats) + *mad* + *Bhā* (with breath, long ā) + *ga-va-tam*
- **Why:** *Śrīmad* uses palatal *ś*, NOT retroflex *ṣ* (the latter is the "Krishna sh"). Different "sh" entirely.

### 13. "Up-an-ish-add" → *U-pa-ni-ṣad*

- **Wrong:** English "sh" sound for *ṣ*; treating the word as 3 syllables
- **Right:** *U-pa-ni-ṣad* — 4 syllables, with retroflex *ṣ* (curled tongue, NOT flat English sh)
- **Why:** *Upaniṣad* = "to sit close beside" (the guru). The retroflex *ṣ* is what makes it the proper Sanskrit word, not an English approximation.

### 14. "Maha-bara-tuh" → *Ma-hā-bhā-ra-ta*

- **Wrong:** Losing the *bh* breath; shortening *hā* and *bhā*; mumbling the final *ta*
- **Right:** Five syllables with **two long ā vowels** and a clean *bh* breath
- **Why:** *Mahā-bhārata* literally = "great Bhārata (descendant of Bharata)". Both long vowels are essential.

## Philosophical terms

### 15. "Karma" rhymed with "drama" → *Kar-ma*

- **Wrong:** English "ar" (rhotacized); flat *r*
- **Right:** *Kar-ma* — guttural *k* + short *a* + cerebral *r* (tongue near hard palate, NOT rhotacized) + labial *m* + short *a*
- **Why:** The *r* in Sanskrit is cerebral — tongue tip near the hard palate, no rhotacization (no "rrr" coloring of the vowel). English "ar" (as in *car*) doesn't exist in Sanskrit.

### 16. "Dar-muh" → *Dhar-ma*

- **Wrong:** Loss of breath puff on *dh*; English-style flat *r*
- **Right:** *Dhar-ma* — dental *d* with a clear breath puff (*dh*, NOT just *d*) + cerebral *r* + labial *m* + short *a*
- **Why:** *dharma* uses **aspirated** dental *dh*. Without the breath, you are saying *darma* (no such word).

### 17. "Mox-uh" or "Mox-shah" → *Mok-ṣa*

- **Wrong:** English "x" sound; flat sibilant
- **Right:** *Mok-ṣa* — labial *m* + long *o* (2 beats — diphthong) + guttural *k* + retroflex *ṣ* (curled tongue) + short *a*
- **Why:** The conjunct *kṣ* is *k* + retroflex *ṣ*, not English "x". Two distinct articulations made smoothly together.

### 18. "Yo-guh" → *Yo-ga*

- **Wrong:** Schwa-like final "uh"
- **Right:** *Yo-ga* — palatal *y* + long *o* (2 beats) + guttural *g* + clean short *a*
- **Why:** The final *a* is preserved. *Yo* is a long diphthong (2 beats). *Yoga* is 3 mātrās total, not 2.

### 19. "Mantra" pronounced "Man-truh" → *Man-tra*

- **Wrong:** English "uh" final; flat *r*
- **Right:** *Man-tra* — labial *m* + short *a* + dental *n* + dental *t* + cerebral *r* + short *a*
- **Why:** Two distinct *a* vowels (preserved). *Tra* is its own syllable, with the *r* as cerebral.

### 20. "Gee-tuh" or "Geet-uh" → *Gī-tā*

- **Wrong:** Shortening *ī* or *ā*; or over-stretching *tah*
- **Right:** *Gī* (long *ī*, 2 beats) + *tā* (long *ā*, 2 beats) — total 4 beats
- **Why:** Both vowels are long. *Gītā* is a 4-beat word, not 2 or 3.

## Diacritic-specific errors

### 21. Saying *th* as English "th" (in *the*) → Sanskrit *th* = aspirated dental *t*

- **Wrong:** *Hari*-tha read as "Hari-thuh" with English *th* (interdental)
- **Right:** *th* = dental *t* + breath. Like "boat-house" run together quickly. **Never English "th" in *the*, *thin*, or *think*.**
- **Examples:** *ratha* (chariot) = *ra-tha* (with breath puff), NOT *ra-tha* (with English "th")
- **Why:** Sanskrit has no equivalent of English interdental "th". The aspirated dental is one of its 25 stop consonants.

### 22. Saying *ph* as English "f" → Sanskrit *ph* = aspirated labial *p*

- **Wrong:** *Phala* (fruit) read as *fala*
- **Right:** *ph* = labial *p* + breath. Like "up-hill" run together quickly. **Never English "f".**
- **Examples:** *phala* = *pa-la* with a breath puff after the *p* on the first syllable. Not *fala*.
- **Why:** Sanskrit has no *f*. What looks like *ph* is an aspirated *p*.

### 23. Ignoring visarga *ḥ* entirely → Read it as soft echo

- **Wrong:** *namaḥ* read as *namu* or *namah* (with hard h) or just *nama*
- **Right:** *namaḥ* = *na-ma-(h)a* — visarga is a **soft, voiceless echo** of the preceding vowel. Not a hard *h*, not silent.
- **Examples:** *hariḥ* = *ha-ri-(h)i* (echo colored by *i*); *guruḥ* = *gu-ru-(h)u* (echo colored by *u*)
- **Why:** The visarga has its own sound. Dropping it changes the grammatical form (and meaning) of the word.

### 24. Reading avagraha *ʼ* as a glottal stop or ignoring it → Pause or extend previous vowel

- **Wrong:** *so 'ham* read as *so-ham* (concatenated) or *so-ʼham* (glottal stop)
- **Right:** *so 'ham* = *so* held slightly longer + *ham*. The apostrophe marks where the *a* of *aham* was elided due to sandhi.
- **Why:** Avagraha is a **phonetic instruction**, not punctuation. It tells you sandhi has occurred — read with awareness of the elision.

### 25. Confusing the three "sh" sounds — *ś*, *ṣ*, and *s*

- **Wrong:** Pronouncing all three as English "sh", or treating *ś* and *ṣ* as identical
- **Right:**
  - **ś** (palatal) — soft "sh", tongue blade against soft palate. As in *Śiva*, *śānti*, *śrī*
  - **ṣ** (cerebral/retroflex) — hard "sh", tongue curled back to hard palate. As in *Kṛṣṇa*, *Viṣṇu*, *Pūṣṇa*
  - **s** (dental) — clean English "s", tongue at the teeth. As in *sāra*, *satya*, *sadā*
- **Why:** These are three different consonants at three different articulation points. They distinguish words. *śiva* (the Lord) is not *ṣiva* (no such word) is not *siva* (different word).

## Beat-length errors (mātrā mistakes)

### 26. Treating diphthongs (*e, ai, o, au*) as short

- **Wrong:** *deva* read with a short *e* (like *bed*)
- **Right:** *e* is **always long** (2 beats), like English "ay" in *say*. Same for *ai* (as in *aisle*), *o* (as in *go*), *au* (as in *cow*).
- **Why:** Sanskrit has no short *e* or *o*. They are diphthongs by definition. Shortening them is a fundamental phonetic error.

### 27. Treating macron vowels (*ā, ī, ū, ṝ*) as short

- **Wrong:** *Rāma* read as *Rama* (rhyming with *llama*)
- **Right:** Macron means **2 beats**, twice the length of the unmarked vowel
- **Why:** Vowel length is phonemic in Sanskrit — it changes meanings. *māra* (death) ≠ *mara* (would die).

### 28. Treating short vowels as long (over-pronouncing)

- **Wrong:** *Hari* read as *Haaree*; *Kṛṣṇa* with stretched final
- **Right:** Short vowels are 1 beat. Brisk and clean.
- **Why:** Symmetry of beats is what creates the rhythmic structure of Sanskrit poetry. Over-extending short vowels disrupts it.

## Special "ISKCON name" errors

### 29. "Pra-boo-pah-duh" → *Pra-bhu-pā-da*

- **Wrong:** Lose the *bh* breath; finish on *duh*
- **Right:** *Pra* + *bhu* (with breath) + *pā* (long, 2 beats) + *da* (short)
- **Why:** *Bh* without breath is just *b*. *Prabupāda* (no breath) is a different (non-)word.

### 30. Pronouncing every name with English stress patterns

- **Wrong:** Stressing the wrong syllable. English instinctively stresses the second-to-last syllable; Sanskrit doesn't have stress that way.
- **Right:** Sanskrit is largely **stress-neutral** with mātrā-based rhythm. Long vowels carry weight, but no syllable is "stressed" the way English stresses syllables.
- **Why:** English-stress overlay distorts the natural mātrā rhythm of Sanskrit. Better to read flat-and-rhythmic than stressed.

## Quick correction checklist

When auditing someone's pronunciation, scan for these in order:

- [ ] Is every retroflex sound (ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, ṣ, ṛ) made with curled tongue?
- [ ] Is every aspirated consonant (kh, gh, ch, jh, ṭh, ḍh, th, dh, ph, bh) made with a clear breath puff?
- [ ] Are long vowels (ā, ī, ū) held for 2 beats?
- [ ] Are diphthongs (e, ai, o, au) treated as long?
- [ ] Are final 'a' vowels preserved (no Hindi-style dropping)?
- [ ] Is *th* aspirated dental, not English "th"?
- [ ] Is *ph* aspirated labial, not English "f"?
- [ ] Is *c* palatal "ch" (not "k")?
- [ ] Are the three "sh" sounds (ś, ṣ, s) distinguished correctly?
- [ ] Is visarga (ḥ) given its soft echo?

If yes to all — the pronunciation is śikṣā-grade.
